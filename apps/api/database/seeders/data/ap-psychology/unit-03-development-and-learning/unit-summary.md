## The ideas that generate every question

1. **Development is studied with age-based designs (3.1).** Cross-sectional designs compare age groups at one time (fast, but open to cohort effects); longitudinal designs follow the same people (show individual change, but slow, costly and open to attrition); cross-sequential designs combine them. Age is never randomly assigned. Infants are studied with habituation and preferential looking. Children need parental permission plus their own assent.
2. **Three debates run through the unit (3.1).** Nature and nurture (they interact), continuity and stages, stability and change.
3. **The body changes on a maturational schedule shaped by experience (3.2).** Germinal, embryonic (most vulnerable to teratogens) and fetal stages; newborn reflexes (rooting, sucking, grasping, Moro, Babinski); motor milestones in a fixed order with variable timing; critical and sensitive periods; puberty with primary and secondary sex characteristics; a prefrontal cortex that matures after the limbic system; gradual physical decline in later adulthood.
4. **Sex is biological; gender is psychological and social (3.3).** Gender identity, roles, expression and typing develop through hormones, social learning and gender schemas. Most psychological sex and gender differences are small, with heavily overlapping distributions; report effect sizes. Sexual orientation is an enduring pattern of attraction; it is not a disorder, is not explained by parenting, and reflects many genes of small effect plus prenatal and other influences.
5. **Thinking develops through schemas and social support (3.4).** Piaget: assimilation and accommodation across four stages (sensorimotor, preoperational, concrete operational, formal operational). Later research found earlier competence and more continuity. Vygotsky: zone of proximal development and scaffolding. Theory of mind emerges around 4 or 5. Crystallized intelligence holds up in later life; fluid intelligence declines.
6. **Language is built from sounds to sentences on a biological timetable (3.5).** Phonemes, morphemes, semantics, syntax, pragmatics; babbling, one-word, telegraphic speech, overregularization. Skinner (learning), Chomsky (inborn grammar) and the interactionist middle ground; a sensitive period for language.
7. **Relationships shape social-emotional development (3.6).** Harlow (contact comfort), Ainsworth's Strange Situation (secure, avoidant, anxious, disorganized), temperament and goodness of fit, Baumrind's parenting styles (correlational evidence), Erikson's eight stages, identity statuses, emerging adulthood, Bronfenbrenner's systems.
8. **Classical conditioning pairs two stimuli (3.7).** NS + US → UR; then CS → CR. Acquisition, extinction, spontaneous recovery, generalization, discrimination, higher-order conditioning. Little Albert (and its ethics), counterconditioning, taste aversion and biological preparedness, and the need for the CS to predict the US.
9. **Operant conditioning links behavior with consequences (3.8).** Reinforcement increases behavior, punishment decreases it; positive adds, negative removes. Primary and secondary reinforcers, shaping, extinction bursts, the four partial schedules, the limits of punishment, instinctive drift, superstition and learned helplessness.
10. **People also learn by watching and thinking (3.9).** Bandura's Bobo doll studies, vicarious reinforcement, learning versus performance and the four modeling processes; insight (Köhler), latent learning and cognitive maps (Tolman); mirror neurons (debated), the amygdala, cerebellum, hippocampus, dopamine and long-term potentiation.

## Key facts and procedures

| Want | Do this |
|---|---|
| Name a developmental design | Same people measured repeatedly: longitudinal. Different ages at one time: cross-sectional. Several cohorts followed briefly: cross-sequential. |
| Explain an age difference in a cross-sectional study | Consider a cohort effect before concluding that age caused it. |
| Piaget's stage from an ability | Object permanence: sensorimotor. Pretend play but no conservation: preoperational. Conservation and reversibility: concrete operational. Abstract hypotheses: formal operational. |
| Assimilation or accommodation | Existing schema kept: assimilation. Schema changed or new one made: accommodation. |
| Count morphemes | Split into meaningful parts: "replayed" = re + play + ed (3). |
| Attachment pattern from reunion behavior | Comforted quickly: secure. Ignores caregiver: avoidant. Clings yet resists comfort: anxious. Contradictory: disorganized. |
| Parenting style | Warm + firm: authoritative. Cold + firm: authoritarian. Warm + lax: permissive. Cold + lax: uninvolved. |
| Erikson's crisis | Trust, autonomy, initiative, competence, identity, intimacy, generativity, integrity: in that order. |
| Label classical conditioning | What causes the response without learning (US, UR)? What was paired with it (NS to CS)? What does the CS trigger (CR)? |
| Classify an operant consequence | Behavior up = reinforcement, down = punishment; stimulus added = positive, removed = negative. |
| Name a schedule | Number of responses = ratio; time = interval; set = fixed; unpredictable = variable. |
| Most resistant to extinction | Variable ratio; least: continuous reinforcement. |
| Classical, operant or observational | Automatic response to a cue; voluntary behavior changed by its own consequence; copying a model without prior reinforcement. |
| Effect size | d = mean difference ÷ standard deviation; 0.2 small, 0.5 medium, 0.8 large. |
| Correlation versus causation | Only random assignment to conditions supports a causal claim; otherwise name reverse causation or a third variable. |

## Research methods and statistics this unit uses

- **Developmental designs and their threats:** cohort effects (cross-sectional) and attrition and practice effects (longitudinal).
- **Infant methods:** habituation and preferential looking; dependent variable = looking time, scored by blind observers.
- **Ethics:** parental permission and child assent; minimizing harm (brief separations in the Strange Situation); deception paired with debriefing; anonymity and confidentiality for sensitive topics such as gender identity and sexual orientation; the problems with the Little Albert study and with Harlow's and Seligman's animal research.
- **Statistics:** means and standard deviations (spread differs between groups), correlations (direction and strength), effect size versus statistical significance (large samples make tiny effects significant), twin concordance (genes plus non-genetic factors), reading conditioning and schedule data (acquisition, extinction, spontaneous recovery; response rates; resistance to extinction).

## Ten-question check

1. A researcher compares 10- and 70-year-olds on smartphone skill. What is the main confound?
2. Which prenatal stage is most vulnerable to structural damage from teratogens?
3. Two group means differ by 4 points with a standard deviation of 20. What is d?
4. A child says a tall, thin glass holds more juice than a short, wide one with the same amount. Which stage?
5. "Want cookie." Which stage of language?
6. A toddler clings to his mother at reunion but pushes her away. Which attachment pattern?
7. A smoke alarm stops when you open a window, and you open windows sooner next time. Classify the consequence.
8. Which schedule fits a slot machine?
9. A chimpanzee suddenly stacks boxes to reach fruit. Which kind of learning?
10. Children who saw a model punished could still reproduce the model's acts for a reward. What does this show?

**Answers.** 1. Cohort effects (the 70-year-olds grew up without smartphones). 2. Embryonic. 3. 4 ÷ 20 = 0.2, small. 4. Preoperational (no conservation). 5. Two-word (telegraphic) stage. 6. Insecure-anxious (resistant). 7. Negative reinforcement. 8. Variable ratio. 9. Insight. 10. Learning differs from performance; consequences affected whether they performed, not whether they learned.
