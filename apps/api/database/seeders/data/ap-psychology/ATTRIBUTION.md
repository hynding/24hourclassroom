# Attribution

Every study guide, unit summary, flashcard deck, quiz, unit exam, the exam-prep
guide and the practice final in this directory is **original writing** for this
course, organised around the College Board *AP Psychology Course and Exam
Description* (CED), revised and effective fall 2024: its five units, 35 topic
codes and topic titles, and its four science practices (Concept Application,
Research Methods and Design, Data Interpretation, Argumentation) organise the
material, and what each topic expects of students is paraphrased in the
course's own words, never copied. The May 2027 exam format (75 four-option
multiple-choice questions in 90 minutes, then an Article Analysis Question and
an Evidence-Based Question in 70 minutes, 7 points each, fully digital in
Bluebook) and the exam date (Friday 2027-05-14) are taken from AP Central. AP®
is a trademark of the College Board, which does not endorse this material.

Nothing here is adapted from a textbook, because no psychology text was found
under a licence this course can build on:

- OpenStax *Psychology 2e*, like every OpenStax book, is now CC BY-NC-SA 4.0
  (OpenStax's licensing help page), and its terms bar ingestion by language
  models. It was not used.
- The BCcampus *Introduction to Psychology* (1st Canadian edition) is
  CC BY-NC-SA; adapting it would force the non-commercial share-alike licence
  onto this course, which is CC BY 4.0. It was not used.

Nor was text taken from commercial textbooks (Myers, Kalat or others), Khan
Academy, commercial review books, study sites or College Board released exam
questions and scoring guidelines. The free-response scoring guides follow the
published shape of the AAQ and EBQ (which parts exist and what each is worth)
but are written for this course's own invented studies.

**Classic studies and theories.** Well-known research and theories (for
example Milgram's obedience studies, Asch's conformity studies, Harlow's
infant-monkey research, Loftus and Palmer's eyewitness study, Bandura's Bobo
doll research, Ainsworth's strange situation, Piaget's and Erikson's stages,
Festinger's cognitive dissonance, Ebbinghaus's forgetting curve, Selye's
general adaptation syndrome) are described in the course's own words: their
procedures and findings as facts, never quoted text. Where a date or detail
could not be confirmed, it was left out. Definitions and terms (including
DSM-5-TR disorder names) are standard in the field and were not copied from
any reference.

**Invented studies.** Every study used in a question (AAQ and EBQ sources,
data tables, study summaries and scenarios in stimulus sets) is invented for
this course and says so ("a hypothetical study", "invented for this course").
None reports real data, and none is attributed to a real researcher, journal
or university; invented researchers are unnamed ("a research team"). The
per-week tables below name each invented study.

**Sensitive topics.** Gender and sexual orientation (Topic 3.3) and
psychological disorders (Unit 5) are written in neutral, scientific,
person-first language with current DSM-5-TR names, and give no diagnosis or
treatment advice. The Unit 5 guides and the exam-prep guide point readers to
988, the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline in the U.S.

**Figures** are described in words or as tables of values; the app renders no
images, HTML or LaTeX.
