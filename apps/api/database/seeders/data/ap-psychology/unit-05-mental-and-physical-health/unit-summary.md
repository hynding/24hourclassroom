## The ideas that generate every question

1. **Health is biopsychosocial (5.1).** Biology, thinking and behavior, and social conditions interact in every illness and every recovery.
2. **Stress is appraisal plus response (5.1).** Stressors are catastrophes, significant life changes or daily hassles. Primary appraisal asks "threat or challenge?"; secondary appraisal asks "can I cope?" The sympathetic nervous system gives the fast response; the HPA axis (hypothalamus → pituitary → adrenal cortex → cortisol) gives the slower one. Selye's general adaptation syndrome runs alarm → resistance → exhaustion. Long-lasting stress suppresses immunity and strains the heart (hostility is the harmful part of Type A).
3. **Coping fits the stressor (5.1).** Problem-focused coping changes the situation; emotion-focused coping manages feelings. Social support and exercise buffer stress; tend-and-befriend is an alternative to fight-or-flight.
4. **Flourishing can be studied too (5.2).** Positive psychology studies positive emotions, character strengths (six virtues, 24 strengths) and enabling institutions. Broaden-and-build: positive emotions widen thinking and build resources. Resilience is bouncing back; posttraumatic growth is changing beyond where one was. Gains fade (adaptation-level phenomenon) and depend on comparison (relative deprivation).
5. **A disorder needs more than unusual behavior (5.3).** Look for clinically significant disturbance with distress or dysfunction, judged in cultural context.
6. **The DSM-5-TR describes; it does not explain or prescribe (5.3).** Criteria are symptoms and duration. Good diagnosis is reliable (raters agree) and valid (accurate). Comorbidity is common.
7. **Perspectives explain; the diathesis-stress model connects them (5.3).** Biological, psychodynamic, behavioral, cognitive, humanistic, evolutionary and sociocultural explanations each capture part of a disorder; a predisposition plus stress is the usual story.
8. **Labels help and harm (5.3).** They guide treatment but can bias perception and feed stigma. Use person-first language. Rosenhan's 1973 study raised the question, but it deceived staff and its accuracy has since been questioned.
9. **Each disorder has a signature (5.4).** Know the defining feature and the time frame that separate look-alikes (see the table).
10. **Treatments follow from explanations (5.5).** Insight therapies (psychodynamic, humanistic), behavior therapies (exposure, systematic desensitization, aversive conditioning, token economies), cognitive therapies (Beck, Ellis, CBT, DBT), and biomedical therapies (medications, ECT, rTMS).
11. **Treatments are judged by controlled research (5.5).** Placebo groups, random assignment, double-blind ratings, meta-analysis and effect sizes separate real effects from expectation, spontaneous remission and regression toward the mean. The therapeutic alliance predicts success across approaches.

## Telling look-alikes apart

| If you see | Think | Not |
|---|---|---|
| Steady, widespread worry, six months or more | Generalized anxiety disorder | Panic disorder (sudden attacks) |
| Unexpected panic attacks plus fear of the next one | Panic disorder | A single panic attack |
| Avoiding places where escape might be hard | Agoraphobia | Social anxiety disorder (fear of judgment) |
| Unwanted thoughts plus repeated acts to relieve them | Obsessive-compulsive disorder | Obsessive-compulsive personality disorder (rigid perfectionism) |
| Trauma symptoms for more than a month | PTSD | Acute stress disorder (three days to a month) |
| Two weeks or more of low mood or lost interest | Major depressive disorder | Persistent depressive disorder (two years or more) |
| A full manic episode | Bipolar I | Bipolar II (hypomania plus depression) |
| Delusions, hallucinations, disorganized speech | Schizophrenia | Dissociative identity disorder ("split personality") |
| Two or more identities plus memory gaps | Dissociative identity disorder | Schizophrenia |
| Health worry with no or few symptoms | Illness anxiety disorder | Somatic symptom disorder (distressing symptoms) |
| Real neurological symptoms with no matching disease | Functional neurological symptom disorder | Faking |
| Binges with loss of control, no regular compensatory behavior | Binge-eating disorder | Bulimia nervosa |
| Disregard for others' rights, no remorse, 18 or older | Antisocial personality disorder | Borderline (instability, fear of abandonment) |
| Inattention or hyperactivity in two settings before 12 | ADHD | Ordinary distraction |
| Social-communication differences plus repetitive interests | Autism spectrum disorder | Social anxiety disorder |

## Treatments at a glance

| Approach | Key figure | Techniques | Best-known use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Psychodynamic | Freud | Free association, resistance, transference, interpretation | Insight into recurring relationship patterns |
| Humanistic | Rogers | Active listening; genuineness, unconditional positive regard, empathy | Self-acceptance and growth |
| Behavioral | Wolpe, Jones | Exposure, systematic desensitization, flooding, aversive conditioning, token economies | Phobias, OCD, behavior change |
| Cognitive | Beck, Ellis | Challenging automatic thoughts; disputing irrational beliefs (ABC) | Depression, anxiety |
| CBT / DBT | Linehan (DBT) | Thought and behavior change; mindfulness and emotion regulation | Anxiety and depressive disorders; borderline personality disorder |
| Biomedical | | Antipsychotics, SSRIs, benzodiazepines, lithium, ECT, rTMS | Psychosis, depression, anxiety, bipolar disorders |

## Research methods that keep coming back

- **Stress and illness studies are mostly correlational**: watch for third variables and reverse causation.
- **Twin and adoption studies** separate genes from environment; concordance well under 100% means environment matters too.
- **Experiments** (gratitude exercises, expressive writing, therapy trials) need random assignment and a control group; **double-blind** procedures guard against placebo effects and experimenter bias.
- **Effect size** says how big a difference is; the **p-value** says how unlikely it is to be chance.
- **Ethics** recur: informed consent (parental consent and assent for minors), debriefing after deception, confidentiality and its safety limits, IRB review.

## Sentences that earn AP points

- **Applying a concept:** "Because Lena's nightmares and avoidance have lasted six months after the crash, her symptoms fit PTSD rather than acute stress disorder."
- **Explaining with a perspective:** "From a behavioral perspective, avoiding dogs is negatively reinforced by relief, so the fear never extinguishes."
- **Correlation caution:** "The study is correlational, so a third variable such as sleep could explain both high stress and frequent illness."
- **Generalizability:** "All participants were volunteer undergraduates at one university, so the findings may not apply to adolescents."
- **Using an effect size:** "An effect size of 0.58 shows a moderate difference: the drug helped, but far from every participant."

## Common mistakes

- Treating unusual behavior as automatically a disorder.
- Saying the DSM-5-TR explains causes or prescribes treatment; calling insanity a diagnosis.
- Mixing up reliability and validity.
- Confusing schizophrenia with dissociative identity disorder, or bipolar I with bipolar II.
- Reading "positive symptoms" as good.
- Mixing up transference and resistance, or systematic desensitization and flooding.
- Saying SSRIs add serotonin (they block reuptake).
- Claiming cause from correlational studies of stress, rumination or social support.

## Self-test

1. Name the three stages of the general adaptation syndrome.
2. Which hormone ends the HPA axis chain?
3. What is the difference between resilience and posttraumatic growth?
4. Two clinicians agree on 95% of diagnoses. What does that show, and what does it not show?
5. Classify: flat affect; hearing voices; believing you are being followed.
6. Which diagnosis requires a full manic episode?
7. What technique pairs relaxation with an anxiety hierarchy?
8. Why does a placebo group improve?

**Answers.** 1. Alarm, resistance, exhaustion. 2. Cortisol. 3. Resilience is bouncing back or staying well; growth is changing beyond where one was before. 4. Reliability; not validity. 5. Negative; positive (hallucination); positive (delusion). 6. Bipolar I. 7. Systematic desensitization. 8. Expectations (the placebo effect) and regression toward the mean, plus spontaneous remission.
