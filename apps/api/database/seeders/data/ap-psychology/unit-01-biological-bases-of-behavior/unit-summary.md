## The ideas that generate every question

1. **Heredity and environment interact (1.1).** Genes are expressed differently in different environments. Twin, adoption and family studies estimate genetic influence; heritability describes variation in a population, never one person, and can change with environment. Eugenics is the warning against treating heritable as unchangeable.
2. **The nervous system is a branching tree (1.2).** Central (brain, spinal cord) and peripheral; the peripheral splits into somatic (voluntary muscles, body senses) and autonomic (organs, glands), which splits into sympathetic (arouse) and parasympathetic (calm).
3. **Neurons fire all-or-nothing and talk chemically (1.3).** Electrical within the neuron, chemical across the synapse. Intensity is coded by firing rate. Drugs work as agonists, antagonists or reuptake inhibitors. Hormones are slower messengers in the blood.
4. **Brain regions specialize but cooperate (1.4).** Brainstem for survival, cerebellum for coordination, limbic structures for drives, memory and emotion, four cortical lobes for sensing, moving and thinking. Contralateral control, lateralization and plasticity. Every finding comes from a method with limits.
5. **Sleep is an active, cycling state (1.5).** A circadian clock set by light; 90-minute cycles of NREM 1–3 and REM, with deep sleep early and REM late; functions of restoration, consolidation and growth; two dream theories; five disorders.
6. **Sensation turns energy into neural signals (1.6).** Thresholds, Weber's law and signal detection; adaptation; the eye, color theories and visual differences; the ear, pitch theories and hearing loss; taste, smell, pain and the body senses.
7. **Every week is also a methods week.** Know the method, the variables, the operational definitions, sampling versus assignment, confounds, ethics and the statistics.

## Neurotransmitters, hormones and drugs at a glance

| Messenger | Role | Link |
|---|---|---|
| Acetylcholine | Muscle contraction, memory | Alzheimer's; myasthenia gravis |
| Dopamine | Movement, reward | Parkinson's (too little); schizophrenia (excess activity) |
| Serotonin | Mood, sleep, appetite | Depression; SSRIs block its reuptake |
| Norepinephrine | Alertness, arousal | Sympathetic response |
| Glutamate / GABA | Main excitatory / main inhibitory | Alcohol boosts GABA |
| Endorphins / substance P | Pain relief / pain signals | Opioids mimic endorphins |
| Adrenaline | Prolongs fight or flight | Adrenal glands |
| Leptin / ghrelin | Fullness / hunger | Fat cells / stomach |
| Melatonin | Sleepiness | Pineal gland, darkness |
| Oxytocin | Bonding and trust | Childbirth, nursing |

Drug classes: **stimulants** (caffeine, nicotine, cocaine, amphetamines), **depressants** (alcohol), **hallucinogens** (LSD, psilocybin; marijuana is usually grouped here), **opioids** (morphine, heroin, fentanyl). **Tolerance** means more drug is needed for the same effect; **withdrawal** is distress on stopping; **addiction** is compulsive use despite harm.

## The research toolkit

| Want to show | Use | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Cause and effect | Experiment: manipulated IV, random assignment, control group, placebo, blinding | Confounds, demand characteristics, experimenter bias |
| A relationship | Correlational study (scatterplot, r from −1 to +1) | Third variables; direction of cause |
| Rare cases in depth | Case study (Phineas Gage, split-brain patients) | Generalizing from one person |
| Brain timing | EEG | Poor location |
| Brain location | fMRI, PET | Correlational; costly |
| Brain structure | MRI, CT | No activity shown |

**Sampling and assignment.** A random **sample** supports generalizing to the population; random **assignment** supports causal claims. A convenience sample invites sampling bias.

**Ethics.** IRB approval; informed consent (and assent for minors); freedom to withdraw; protection from harm; confidentiality; deception only when justified and always followed by debriefing; animal research reviewed by its own committee with humane care.

**Statistics.** Mean, median, mode; range and standard deviation. Skew pulls the mean toward the tail: positive skew, mean above median. Normal curve: 68%, 95%, 99.7% within 1, 2 and 3 SD; +1 SD is about the 84th percentile, −2 SD about the 2nd. Significance (p less than .05) means unlikely due to chance; effect size means how big.

## Free response in this unit

**Article Analysis Question (7 points).** One study summary. Parts: A identify the method; B state an operational definition; C describe what a statistic shows (a mean difference, a correlation, an SD, a p-value); D identify an ethical guideline followed or violated, with evidence; E evaluate generalizability using specific features of the sample; F explain whether the findings support or refute a claim using a psychological concept (2 points).

**Evidence-Based Question (7 points).** Three study summaries. Parts: A a defensible claim; B(i) evidence from one source; B(ii) explanation with a psychological concept (2 points); C(i) evidence from a different source; C(ii) explanation with a different concept (2 points). Cite specific numbers, name the source, and use a concept that actually connects the evidence to the claim.

**Worked AAQ part.** "Describe what r = −0.52 shows." A full-credit answer: "A moderate negative relationship: as hours of late-night screen use increase, hours of sleep tend to decrease." It gives direction, strength and context, and makes no causal claim.

## Classic mix-ups

- **Hippocampus versus hypothalamus.** Memory versus drives and the pituitary.
- **Broca versus Wernicke.** Speaking versus understanding.
- **Sympathetic versus somatic.** Arousal of organs versus voluntary muscle.
- **Agonist versus antagonist.** Mimic or strengthen versus block.
- **Leptin versus ghrelin.** Fullness versus hunger.
- **Sleepwalking versus REM sleep behavior disorder.** NREM 3 versus REM.
- **Place versus frequency theory.** High pitch versus low pitch.
- **Conductive versus sensorineural loss.** Mechanical versus hair cells or nerve.
- **Random sample versus random assignment.** Generalizing versus causation.
- **Heritability.** About groups, not individuals.

## Last-minute checklist

- Can you trace a reflex arc and a voluntary movement through the right neurons and divisions?
- Can you predict the effect of a drug from how it acts at the synapse?
- Can you name the region from a symptom, and the hemisphere from the side of the body?
- Can you sketch a night's sleep cycles and say where dreams and sleepwalking happen?
- Can you explain a color afterimage and a high-pitch hearing loss with the right theories?
- For any study: method, IV, DV, operational definitions, sample, assignment, ethics, statistic, limit?
