## The ideas that generate every question

1. **An equation is a sentence about particles, and atoms are conserved.** Coefficients count particles and may be changed; subscripts define substances and may not. Balance metals, then other nonmetals, then H, then O; polyatomic ions as units; double to clear fractions. The seven diatomics: H₂ N₂ O₂ F₂ Cl₂ Br₂ I₂.
2. **Five patterns cover most reactions.** Synthesis (one product), decomposition (one reactant), single replacement (element + compound → element + compound), double replacement (two compounds swap ions), combustion (O₂ in, CO₂ + H₂O out).
3. **Whether a reaction happens is predictable.** A free metal replaces any metal, or hydrogen, below it in the activity series; a halogen replaces any halogen below it. A double replacement occurs only if a precipitate (solubility rules), a gas (carbonate + acid → CO₂) or water (acid + base) forms. Otherwise: no reaction.
4. **The mole is the hub.** 6.022 × 10²³ particles; molar mass in g/mol equals formula mass in u. Grams ↔ moles by molar mass; moles ↔ particles by Avogadro's number; never grams → particles directly.
5. **Formulas can be read from masses.** Percent composition from a formula; empirical formula from percents (100 g → moles → divide by smallest → clear fractions); molecular formula = n × empirical, n = M ÷ M(empirical).
6. **Coefficients are mole ratios.** Grams of given → moles → mole ratio → moles of wanted → grams. The equation enters once, at the ratio. Check direction and conservation of mass.
7. **One reactant runs out first.** Compare reactants in moles through the ratio (or divide each by its coefficient); the limiting reactant alone sets the theoretical yield. Percent yield = actual ÷ theoretical × 100%, and a value over 100% is a warning, not a success.

## Key terms and formulas

| Item | What to know |
|---|---|
| Balancing check | Count every element both sides; reduce common factors. 2 C₄H₁₀ + 13 O₂ → 8 CO₂ + 10 H₂O. |
| Atom counts | 3 Ca(NO₃)₂ = 3 Ca, 6 N, 18 O: coefficient × subscript × parentheses. |
| Activity series | Li K Ba Ca Na Mg Al Zn Fe Ni Sn Pb (H) Cu Hg Ag Au. Above H: react with acid. Li–Na: react with cold water. |
| Halogens | F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂. |
| Always soluble | Group 1, NH₄⁺, NO₃⁻, acetate. Mostly soluble: Cl⁻ Br⁻ I⁻ (not Ag⁺, Pb²⁺, Hg₂²⁺); SO₄²⁻ (not Ba²⁺, Sr²⁺, Pb²⁺). Mostly insoluble: CO₃²⁻, PO₄³⁻, S²⁻, OH⁻ (except group 1, NH₄⁺). |
| Gas formers | Carbonate + acid → CO₂ + H₂O; sulfite + acid → SO₂; sulfide + acid → H₂S; NH₄⁺ + OH⁻ → NH₃. |
| Net ionic | Remove spectators: Ag⁺ + Cl⁻ → AgCl(s); Ba²⁺ + SO₄²⁻ → BaSO₄(s). |
| Molar masses | H₂O 18.02; CO₂ 44.01; NaCl 58.44; CaCO₃ 100.09; NH₃ 17.03; H₂SO₄ 98.08; O₂ 32.00; Cl₂ 70.90; Fe₂O₃ 159.70; C₆H₁₂O₆ 180.16 g/mol. |
| Conversions | mol = g ÷ M; g = mol × M; particles = mol × 6.022 × 10²³. |
| Percent composition | (mass of element in 1 mol ÷ M) × 100%. Water 11.19% H; Fe₂O₃ 69.94% Fe. |
| Empirical formula | 100 g → g each → ÷ atomic mass → ÷ smallest → ×2 for .5, ×3 for .33/.67, ×4 for .25. |
| Molecular formula | n = M ÷ M(empirical); CH₂O (30.03) with M = 180 → n = 6 → C₆H₁₂O₆. |
| Stoichiometry chain | g A × (1 mol A ÷ M_A) × (mol B ÷ mol A) × (M_B ÷ 1 mol B) = g B. |
| Limiting reactant | Moles of each ÷ its coefficient; smaller limits. Product from limiting only. Excess left = present − needed. |
| Yield | Percent = actual ÷ theoretical × 100%. Theoretical needed = target ÷ (percent ÷ 100). |

## Common errors

- Changing subscripts to balance; writing O or Cl for a free element; forgetting to reduce coefficients.
- Copying subscripts into a replacement product (AlCl₂ from CuCl₂ + Al). Rebuild from ion charges.
- Calling a double replacement a reaction when every product is soluble.
- Multiplying grams by molar mass; using 35.45 for Cl₂; omitting the parentheses subscript in a molar mass.
- Using the percent as a mole number; rounding 1.5 to 1 or 2 in an empirical ratio.
- Applying a mole ratio to grams; inverting the ratio; using an unbalanced equation.
- Choosing the limiting reactant by smaller mass or by fewer moles when the ratio is not 1 : 1.
- Computing the yield from the excess reactant, or inverting the percent-yield fraction.

## Worked example

25.0 g of iron(III) oxide is heated with 10.0 g of carbon monoxide: Fe₂O₃ + 3 CO → 2 Fe + 3 CO₂ (Fe₂O₃ 159.70, CO 28.01, Fe 55.85, CO₂ 44.01 g/mol). 7.90 g of iron is recovered.

1. **Classify and check the equation.** Not one of the five simple patterns (it is a redox reaction you will meet in Unit 8), but balanced: Fe 2 = 2; C 3 = 3; O 3 + 3 = 6.
2. **Moles.** Fe₂O₃: 25.0 ÷ 159.70 = 0.157 mol. CO: 10.0 ÷ 28.01 = 0.357 mol.
3. **Limiting reactant.** Fe₂O₃ needs 3 × 0.157 = 0.470 mol CO; only 0.357 mol present → **CO limits**. (Shortcut: 0.157 ÷ 1 = 0.157; 0.357 ÷ 3 = 0.119; CO smaller.)
4. **Theoretical yield of iron.** 0.357 mol CO × (2 mol Fe ÷ 3 mol CO) = 0.238 mol Fe × 55.85 = **13.3 g**.
5. **Excess remaining.** Fe₂O₃ used = 0.357 ÷ 3 = 0.119 mol × 159.70 = 19.0 g; left = 25.0 − 19.0 = **6.0 g**.
6. **Percent yield.** 7.90 ÷ 13.3 × 100% = **59.4%**.
7. **Conservation check.** Reactants used: 19.0 g Fe₂O₃ + 10.0 g CO = 29.0 g. Products: 13.3 g Fe + 0.357 mol CO₂ × 44.01 = 15.7 g CO₂; total 29.0 g. ✓
8. **Percent composition cross-check.** Iron is 69.94% of Fe₂O₃; 19.0 g of oxide contains 13.3 g of iron, matching step 4.

## What the unit exam looks like

About 44 items in roughly 75 minutes. Two stimulus sets (a precipitation lab with masses leading to limiting reactant and percent yield; a table of three fuels leading to balancing and CO₂ per gram), multiple choice spanning all seven topics with the heaviest weight on balancing, predicting products and stoichiometry, select-all, true/false, fill-in-the-blank, four numeric items (a molar mass, a percent composition, a mass-to-mass conversion, a limiting-reactant mole count), two short answers (an empirical formula; a prediction with net ionic equation) and one long answer (a complete limiting-reactant and yield analysis). A periodic table is provided; the activity series and solubility rules are not.
