# Chemistry · Attribution

Every study guide, unit summary, flashcard deck, quiz item, unit exam item,
the exam prep guide and the practice final in this directory are **original
writing for this course**. No textbook, open or otherwise, was adapted: the
sequence of units, the explanations, the worked examples, the analogies, the
figure and graph descriptions and every question were written from scratch
for 24 Hour Classroom. The course is aligned to the Next Generation Science
Standards for high-school physical science (HS-PS1 Matter and Its
Interactions, HS-PS3 Energy) and uses the eight NGSS science and engineering
practices as its practice labels; NGSS is a public framework credited as the
alignment target only and is not quoted.

The only external content is **numerical data**: atomic weights, physical
constants, melting and boiling points, densities, specific heats,
electronegativities, isotopic masses and abundances, half-lives, enthalpies
of formation, average bond energies, equilibrium constants, standard
reduction potentials and nuclear masses. Every such value is taken from a
public-domain U.S. government source and rounded for classroom use, as the
attribution block at the end of every guide and summary states. Sources were
accessed on 2026-10-16.

Half-lives, bond energies, reduction potentials and formation enthalpies
are the standard rounded values taught in every first-year course and agree
with the NIST and NNDC compilations to the precision used here. Industrial
figures (Haber process conditions, reactor fuel enrichment, battery
voltages, plastics' properties) are general knowledge stated approximately.
Items that rest on no specific source carry `source: "general knowledge"` in
their YAML so a reviewer can find them. No image, map or diagram is
reproduced: every figure, graph and energy diagram is described in words.
