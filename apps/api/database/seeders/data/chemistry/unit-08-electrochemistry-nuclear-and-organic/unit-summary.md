## The ideas that generate every question

1. **Redox is electron transfer, tracked by oxidation numbers.** Oxidation is loss of electrons (number rises); reduction is gain (number falls). They always happen together. The oxidising agent is reduced; the reducing agent is oxidised. Assign numbers by the rules, and a change means redox.
2. **Half-reactions make the electrons visible.** Balance each half in atoms and charge (in acid: H₂O for oxygen, H⁺ for hydrogen, electrons for charge), scale so the electrons match, add, and check both atoms and charge.
3. **Separate the halves and the electrons do work.** In a voltaic cell the spontaneous reaction (E°cell > 0) pushes electrons from the anode (oxidation, negative) through a wire to the cathode (reduction, positive); the salt bridge carries ions. E°cell = E°(cathode) − E°(anode), never scaled by coefficients.
4. **Supply current and the reaction runs backwards.** Electrolysis forces a non-spontaneous reaction; anode and cathode keep their processes but swap signs. Q = I × t and 96,485 C per mole of electrons convert current and time into moles of product.
5. **Nuclear energy is mass turned to energy.** The mass defect of a nucleus, E = mc², is its binding energy. Moving nucleons toward iron-56 on the binding-energy curve releases energy: fission of heavy nuclei (about 200 MeV, with neutrons for a chain reaction) or fusion of light ones (17.6 MeV per D–T event, far more per gram, needing 100 million K).
6. **Carbon's four bonds build everything.** Alkanes CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ (saturated), alkenes CₙH₂ₙ and alkynes CₙH₂ₙ₋₂ (unsaturated); isomers share a formula; boiling points follow dispersion forces; combustion gives CO₂ and water (or CO and soot without enough oxygen).
7. **The functional group does the reacting, and monomers become polymers.** −OH alcohol, −COOH acid, −COO− ester, −NH₂ amine. Alkenes add to one another (addition polymerisation, nothing lost); two-ended monomers join with loss of water (condensation: polyesters, nylon, proteins). Structure sets properties and recyclability.

## Lists to memorise

- **Oxidation numbers to recognise:** S +6 in SO₄²⁻ and H₂SO₄; Mn +7 in MnO₄⁻; Cr +6 in Cr₂O₇²⁻; N +5 in HNO₃, +4 in NO₂, +2 in NO, −3 in NH₃; C −4 in CH₄, +4 in CO₂; O −1 in H₂O₂; Cl +5 in ClO₃⁻.
- **Always redox:** single replacement, combustion, synthesis or decomposition involving a free element. **Never redox:** double replacement, neutralisation, dissolving.
- **Two half-reactions to know by heart:** MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O; Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6e⁻ → 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O.
- **Cell vocabulary:** anode = oxidation (negative in voltaic, positive in electrolytic); cathode = reduction (positive in voltaic, negative in electrolytic); electrons in the wire anode → cathode; anions in the bridge to the anode, cations to the cathode; "an ox, red cat."
- **Batteries:** alkaline dry cell 1.5 V (Zn/MnO₂), lead-acid 2 V per cell (Pb/PbO₂/H₂SO₄, rechargeable), lithium-ion about 3.7 V (Li⁺ shuttles graphite ↔ metal oxide), fuel cell (H₂ + O₂ → H₂O).
- **Electrolysis products:** molten NaCl → Na at cathode, Cl₂ at anode; water → H₂ at cathode, O₂ at anode, 2:1 by volume; electroplating: object = cathode, plating metal = anode.
- **Reactor parts:** fuel (enriched UO₂), moderator (slows neutrons), control rods (absorb neutrons), coolant (carries heat), containment.
- **First ten alkanes:** methane, ethane, propane, butane, pentane, hexane, heptane, octane, nonane, decane.
- **Functional groups:** −OH alcohol (-ol), −COOH carboxylic acid (-oic acid), −COO− ester, −NH₂ amine, −Cl/−Br halide.
- **Addition polymers:** polyethene (ethene), polypropene (propene), PVC (chloroethene), PTFE (tetrafluoroethene), polystyrene (phenylethene). **Condensation polymers:** polyesters (PET, PLA), nylon, proteins, starch and cellulose.

## Common errors

- **Naming the atom, not the species, as the agent.** The oxidising agent is MnO₄⁻, not "Mn."
- **Agent and process swapped.** The oxidising agent is reduced; the reducing agent is oxidised.
- **Adding half-reactions with unequal electrons.** Scale first.
- **Multiplying E° by coefficients, or flipping the anode's sign and then subtracting too.** Use the table values in E°(cathode) − E°(anode), once.
- **Electrons through the salt bridge.** Ions in the bridge; electrons in the wire.
- **Electroplating the anode.** The object receives metal, so it is the cathode.
- **Minutes in Q = It, and forgetting electrons per ion.** Seconds; Cu²⁺ needs 2, Al³⁺ needs 3.
- **"Mass is destroyed."** Mass is converted to energy; E = mc² accounts for it exactly.
- **Moderator and control rods confused.** Moderator slows neutrons; control rods absorb them.
- **Wrong hydrogen count.** Hexane C₆H₁₄, not C₆H₁₂.
- **Polymers with double bonds.** Addition uses them up; polyethene is saturated.
- **Forgetting the water in condensation polymerisation.** It is the definition.

## Worked example: a cell from the table

A cell is built from nickel in Ni²⁺ and silver in Ag⁺. Silver has the higher E° (+0.80 V against −0.26 V), so silver is the cathode: Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag. Nickel is the anode: Ni → Ni²⁺ + 2e⁻. Double the silver half to match electrons: Ni + 2Ag⁺ → Ni²⁺ + 2Ag. E°cell = 0.80 − (−0.26) = 1.06 V (not 1.86 V; the coefficient does not change the voltage). Electrons flow nickel → silver through the wire; nitrate ions in the bridge drift toward the nickel; the silver electrode gains mass.

Now run it backwards as electrolysis for 20.0 minutes at 1.50 A: Q = 1.50 × 1,200 = 1,800 C; 1,800 ÷ 96,485 = 0.01866 mol e⁻; silver needs one electron per ion, so 0.01866 mol × 107.87 g/mol = 2.01 g of silver dissolves from what was the cathode and plates onto the nickel.

## Worked example: a nuclear equation and its energy

Uranium-235 absorbs a neutron and splits into xenon-140, strontium-94 and neutrons. Mass numbers: 236 = 140 + 94 + x, so x = 2. Atomic numbers: 92 = 54 + 38. If the mass defect for this fission is 0.200 u, the energy is 0.200 × 931.5 = 186 MeV = 186 × 1.602 × 10⁻¹³ J = 2.98 × 10⁻¹¹ J per fission. A gram of uranium-235 holds 2.56 × 10²¹ atoms, so about 7.6 × 10¹⁰ J per gram, two and a half million times the energy of burning a gram of coal.

## What to review from earlier units

- **Ions and electron configurations (2.6, 3.1):** cations form by losing electrons; that loss is oxidation. Periodic trends (2.8) say who gives and who takes.
- **Polyatomic ions (3.2):** permanganate, dichromate, sulfate, nitrate are the species whose oxidation numbers you assign.
- **Reaction types and the activity series (4.2, 4.3):** the series is a ranking of reducing agents and matches the order of reduction potentials.
- **Balancing and stoichiometry (4.1, 4.4, 4.6):** combustion equations, moles of electrons, grams of plated metal.
- **Nuclear notation, decay and half-life (2.2, 2.4):** A and Z bookkeeping, alpha and beta, fission products decaying for centuries.
- **Lewis structures, shape and intermolecular forces (3.3, 3.5, 3.6):** carbon's tetrahedron, alkane boiling points, why ethanol mixes with water.
- **Energy (7.1, 7.3):** combustion ΔH from bond energies; electrolysis as stored energy; nuclear energy per gram versus chemical.
- **Equilibrium (7.6):** esterification is reversible; a dead battery is at equilibrium.
