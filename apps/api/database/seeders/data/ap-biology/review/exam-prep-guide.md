# AP Biology · Exam Prep Guide

**Exam:** Monday 3 May 2027 · **Format:** two sections, three hours · **Built from:** the College Board AP Biology Course and Exam Description (effective Fall 2025) and the OpenStax texts this course was built on.

## The format

| Section | Items | Time | Weight | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I · Multiple choice | 60 questions, four options each | 90 minutes | 50% | About a third of the questions come in sets of 3 to 5 sharing one data set, passage or figure. No penalty for guessing: answer everything. |
| II · Free response | 6 questions | 90 minutes | 50% | Two long questions (8 to 10 points each) and four short questions (4 points each). |

The six free-response questions are always the same six types, in this order:

1. **Interpreting and Evaluating Experimental Results** (long). A data table or graph from an experiment. Describe the data, explain the biology, evaluate a claim, propose a change or prediction.
2. **Interpreting and Evaluating Experimental Results with Graphing** (long). The same kind of question, but one part asks you to describe or construct a graph: name the axes, units, scale, what is plotted, and error bars.
3. **Scientific Investigation** (short). Design an experiment: hypothesis, independent and dependent variables, controls, prediction.
4. **Conceptual Analysis** (short). Describe a process, explain a mechanism, predict an outcome.
5. **Analyze Model or Visual Representation** (short). Read a diagram or model; identify, explain, predict.
6. **Analyze Data** (short). Read a table or graph; describe the pattern, draw a conclusion, support it with numbers.

The free-response booklet gives you the full 90 minutes for all six. Budget about 22 minutes for each long question and 11 for each short one, and answer the easier short questions first if the long ones look slow.

**Calculators.** From the 2027 exam a graphing calculator is **not** permitted. A four-function calculator (with square root) is, and you will want it for Hardy-Weinberg, chi-square and standard error. The equation sheet is printed in the booklet.

## The equation sheet, and when each line is used

| Equation | Where it appears |
|---|---|
| Rate = Δ quantity ÷ Δ time | Enzyme assays, photosynthesis (leaf disks), respiration (respirometers), population growth. Read it from the slope of a graph. |
| Surface area and volume of a sphere, cube, cylinder; SA:V | Cell size (2.2): as a cell grows, volume outpaces surface, so exchange per unit volume falls. |
| Water potential Ψ = Ψp + Ψs; Ψs = −iCRT | Osmosis (2.7). Water moves from higher to lower Ψ. Pure water at atmospheric pressure is Ψ = 0; solutes make Ψs negative; pressure makes Ψp positive. R = 0.0831 L·bar/mol·K; T in kelvin. |
| Dilution: CiVi = CfVf | Preparing solutions in lab-based questions. |
| Hardy-Weinberg: p + q = 1; p² + 2pq + q² = 1 | Population genetics (7.5). The expected genotype frequencies if no evolution is occurring: the null hypothesis. Compare with observed to detect selection or drift. |
| Chi-square χ² = Σ (o − e)² ÷ e; the critical-value table at p = 0.05 | Genetics crosses (5.3), behaviour choice chambers (8.1), Hardy-Weinberg tests. Degrees of freedom = categories − 1. Reject the null when χ² exceeds the critical value. |
| Mean, standard deviation, standard error SE = s ÷ √n | Any data with replicates. Error bars of ±2 SE that do not overlap suggest a real difference. |
| Population growth dN/dt = B − D; exponential dN/dt = rmax N; logistic dN/dt = rmax N (K − N)/K | Population ecology (8.3, 8.4). K is carrying capacity; growth slows as N approaches K. |
| Simpson's diversity index 1 − Σ (n/N)² | Biodiversity (8.6). Higher is more diverse. |
| Gibbs free energy ΔG = ΔH − TΔS | Energetics (3.3): negative ΔG is spontaneous (exergonic). |
| pH = −log₁₀[H⁺] | Water (1.1), enzyme pH optima (3.2). |

Practise each with real numbers until the arithmetic is automatic; the exam rewards a correct set-up even when you run out of time to finish.

## The task verbs, and what each one requires

Points in the free-response section are awarded for doing what the verb says. Every verb below needs a separate sentence.

| Verb | What a scoring answer contains |
|---|---|
| **Identify** | Name it. One word or phrase: the variable, the structure, the process, the value read off the graph. |
| **Describe** | Say what it is or what the data show, in enough detail to distinguish it. For data: the direction of the trend plus at least two numbers or a comparison. |
| **Explain** | Give the mechanism: *how* or *why*, with the cause linked to the effect by a chain of reasoning ("because the kinked tails cannot pack, the membrane stays fluid, so…"). |
| **Predict** | State what will happen, directionally and specifically ("the rate will fall by about half"), under the condition given. |
| **Justify** | Give the reason your prediction or claim is right, using the biology or the data. A prediction without a justification earns the prediction point only. |
| **Calculate** | Show the set-up, the substitution and the answer with units. |
| **Construct a graph** | Correct graph type for the data; labelled axes with units; a sensible scale; the data plotted; a key if more than one series; error bars if standard error is given. |
| **Evaluate** | Say whether the claim or design holds, and why, with evidence from the data; name a limitation. |
| **Propose / Design** | A testable procedure: independent and dependent variables, at least two controlled variables, a control group, replication, and what result would support the hypothesis. |
| **Make a claim / Support with evidence** | A clear one-sentence claim, then the specific data or reasoning that backs it. |

Three habits that cost students points: answering "describe" with an explanation (you get no describe point), answering "explain" with a restatement of the data (no mechanism, no point), and giving two contradictory answers to hedge (the grader takes neither).

## How the multiple-choice section tests you

- **Read the whole stimulus before the first question of a set.** Note what was varied, what was measured, and what was held constant.
- **Prefer the explanation that fits every row**, not the one that fits a single striking number.
- **Eliminate on mechanism.** Distractors are usually a real concept applied in the wrong place: specific heat where heat of vaporization is meant, secondary where tertiary structure is meant, drift where selection is meant.
- **Watch the direction.** Lower pH means more H⁺; water moves toward lower Ψ; more negative ΔG is more spontaneous.
- **When a question says "best supported by the data", the answer is in the table, not in your memory.**

## Two-week review plan

The plan assumes about 90 minutes a day. Each day pairs a unit summary with the practice tools in this course and ends with free-response practice, because writing is the skill that decays fastest.

| Day | Content | Practice |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Unit 1 summary · Unit 2 summary | Re-take the Unit 1 and Unit 2 flashcard decks; 20 random multiple-choice items from the Unit 2 exam |
| 2 | Unit 3 summary (enzymes, photosynthesis, respiration) | Rate-from-graph items; write one Scientific Investigation FRQ on enzyme activity |
| 3 | Unit 4 summary (signalling, feedback, cell cycle) | Analyze Model FRQ on a signalling pathway; chi-square on mitotic index data |
| 4 | Unit 5 summary (meiosis, Mendel, non-Mendelian) | Twenty genetics problems including a test cross, a dihybrid chi-square and a linkage map |
| 5 | Unit 6 summary (DNA to protein, regulation, biotech) | Operon and transcription-factor model items; restriction-fragment gel reading |
| 6 | Unit 7 summary, part 1 (selection, population genetics, H-W) | Ten Hardy-Weinberg calculations; one long FRQ on selection data with a described graph |
| 7 | Rest day, or re-take any week quiz you scored under 70% on | — |
| 8 | Unit 7 summary, part 2 (evidence, phylogeny, speciation) | Build three cladograms from character tables; Analyze Data FRQ on molecular-clock data |
| 9 | Unit 8 summary, part 1 (behaviour, energy flow, cycles) | Draw the four biogeochemical cycles from memory; Conceptual Analysis FRQ on the nitrogen cycle |
| 10 | Unit 8 summary, part 2 (populations, communities, biodiversity, disruption) | Population-growth and Simpson's index calculations; Analyze Data FRQ on a trophic cascade |
| 11 | **Full-length practice exam, Section I** under timed conditions (90 minutes) | Mark it; list every item missed by topic code |
| 12 | **Full-length practice exam, Section II** under timed conditions (90 minutes) | Grade yourself against the acceptable-answer summaries; note which task verbs you dropped points on |
| 13 | Re-study the five topic codes you missed most | Re-take those weeks' quizzes |
| 14 | Equation sheet drill (one problem per line) · task-verb table · the "traps" section of every unit summary | Sleep. Calculator, pencils and ID ready. |

## The night before

- Re-read the eight unit summaries' "traps" sections. They are the exam's favourite distractors.
- Re-read the task-verb table once.
- Do not learn anything new.

## On the day

Section I: 90 seconds an item on average. Mark the stem's key words, answer, move on, come back. Section II: read all six questions in the first five minutes, start with the one you know best, write in sentences with the verb's requirement in each, label parts (a), (b), (c), and show every calculation. If you run short of time on a long question, write the describe and identify answers first; they are the cheapest points.
