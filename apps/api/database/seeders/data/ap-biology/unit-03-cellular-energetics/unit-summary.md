# Unit 3 · Cellular Energetics · The night-before sheet

**Exam weight:** 12–16% of the AP exam · **CED topics:** 3.1 to 3.5 · **Big Ideas:** ENE, SYI, EVO
**Built from:** OpenStax *Biology 2e* Chapter 6 (§6.1–6.5), Chapter 7 (§7.1–7.7) and Chapter 8 (§8.1–8.3)

## The six ideas that generate every question

1. **Enzymes change how fast, never how far.** They lower activation energy and leave ΔG, direction and equilibrium untouched. They are not consumed. Shape is everything, and shape is tertiary structure.
2. **Energy is conserved and every transfer leaks heat.** Organisms are open systems that stay ordered by importing free energy and exporting entropy. Spontaneous (ΔG negative) does not mean fast.
3. **ATP pays for endergonic work by coupling.** Its crowded phosphates make hydrolysis strongly exergonic; phosphorylation hands that energy to a reactant or a protein so the summed ΔG is negative.
4. **Energy moves as electrons.** Oxidation is loss, reduction is gain; NAD⁺, FAD and NADP⁺ carry electrons between pathways. Fuels are reduced carbon; products are oxidised carbon.
5. **Chemiosmosis is one machine in two mirrored rooms.** An electron transport chain pumps protons across a membrane; ATP synthase lets them back and makes ATP. Thylakoid and mitochondrion differ in direction, source and acceptor, not in principle.
6. **Matter cycles, energy flows.** Photosynthesis reduces CO₂ to sugar with light; respiration oxidises sugar to CO₂ for ATP; the atoms go round, the energy passes through once and leaves as heat.

## Enzymes (3.1) and their environment (3.2)

| Factor | Effect on rate | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Substrate concentration | Rises, then plateaus | Active sites become saturated; only more enzyme raises the plateau |
| Enzyme concentration | Proportional (substrate in excess) | Each molecule adds a site |
| Temperature | Rises to an optimum, then collapses | Collisions increase; then weak bonds of tertiary structure break (denaturation) |
| pH | Peak at the optimum, falls either side | R-group charges change; ionic and hydrogen bonds shift; active site deforms |
| Competitive inhibitor | Lower rate, same maximum | Competes for the active site; overcome by excess substrate |
| Noncompetitive inhibitor | Lower maximum, not rescued by substrate | Binds an allosteric site and changes shape |

Active-site specificity comes from R groups; **induced fit** tightens the fit on binding. **Cofactors** are ions (Mg²⁺, Zn²⁺); **coenzymes** are organic, often from vitamins (NAD⁺, FAD, coenzyme A). **Allosteric** activators and inhibitors switch the enzyme between shapes; **feedback inhibition** is the end product inhibiting an early enzyme (isoleucine, ATP on phosphofructokinase). Lab 13: peroxidase or catalase; initial rate = Δy/Δx from the straight early part; a falling rate late in a run is usually substrate depletion, not denaturation; the low plateau at 60 °C is denaturation.

## Cellular energy (3.3)

ΔG = ΔH − TΔS. **Exergonic**: negative, spontaneous, hydrolysis, respiration, diffusion down a gradient. **Endergonic**: positive, needs input, dehydration synthesis, photosynthesis, active transport. **ATP** = adenine + ribose + three phosphates; hydrolysis ΔG ≈ −7.3 kcal/mol standard, about −14 in a cell, because of repulsion between phosphates and the stability of ADP + Pi. **Coupling** via phosphorylated intermediates or phosphorylated proteins (Na⁺/K⁺ pump: 3 out, 2 in, 1 ATP). ATP is remade by **substrate-level phosphorylation** (direct transfer from an intermediate) and **oxidative phosphorylation** (gradient and synthase). Glycolysis and ATP are universal: evidence of common ancestry.

## Photosynthesis (3.4)

6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂. O₂ comes from water (¹⁸O proves it); carbon comes from CO₂.

| Stage | Where | In | Out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light-dependent reactions | Thylakoid membrane | Light, H₂O, ADP, NADP⁺ | ATP, NADPH (into stroma), O₂ |
| Calvin cycle | Stroma | CO₂, ATP, NADPH | G3P; ADP, NADP⁺ returned |

**Light reactions:** pigments (chlorophyll a at the reaction centres, chlorophyll b and carotenoids as accessories) absorb blue and red, reflect green; action spectrum is broader than any one absorption spectrum. Water → PSII (P680) → plastoquinone → cytochrome complex (pumps H⁺ into the lumen) → plastocyanin → PSI (P700) → ferredoxin → NADP⁺ reductase → NADPH. Protons flow lumen → stroma through ATP synthase. Photosystems are numbered by discovery; electrons start at II.

**Calvin cycle:** fix (RuBisCO: CO₂ + RuBP → 2 × 3-PGA), reduce (ATP + NADPH: 3-PGA → G3P), regenerate (ATP: 5 G3P → 3 RuBP). Three turns export one G3P at 9 ATP and 6 NADPH; one glucose costs 18 ATP and 12 NADPH. "Light-independent" means light is not a reactant; the cycle stops seconds after dark. Light off: 3-PGA up, RuBP down. CO₂ off: RuBP up, 3-PGA down. **Photorespiration**: O₂ competes with CO₂ at RuBisCO's active site (competitive inhibition). Lab 5: floating leaf disks, bicarbonate as CO₂ source, ET50 = time for half to float, rate = 1/ET50; dark and no-bicarbonate controls.

## Cellular respiration (3.5)

C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + about 30 ATP + heat.

| Stage | Where | Carbon | Carriers | ATP (direct) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Glycolysis | Cytosol, no O₂ | 6 C → 2 pyruvate (3 C) | 2 NADH | 2 net (4 made, 2 invested) |
| Pyruvate oxidation | Matrix | 2 CO₂, 2 acetyl CoA | 2 NADH | 0 |
| Citric acid cycle (2 turns) | Matrix | 4 CO₂ | 6 NADH, 2 FADH₂ | 2 |
| Oxidative phosphorylation | Inner membrane | none | reoxidised | about 26–28 |

**Chain:** NADH → complex I; FADH₂ → complex II (no pumping, hence 1.5 vs 2.5 ATP); ubiquinone → complex III → cytochrome c → complex IV → O₂ → H₂O. Protons pumped matrix → intermembrane space; return through ATP synthase into the matrix. Oxygen is used only at complex IV; all CO₂ leaves before the chain; the O₂ you breathe becomes water. **Cyanide** blocks complex IV: O₂ use, gradient and ATP all stop, cycle stops. **DNP** uncouples: O₂ use rises, ATP falls, heat. **Oligomycin** blocks the synthase: ATP stops, gradient builds, O₂ use stops. **Respiratory control:** without ADP the chain idles; add ADP and it races.

**Fermentation** regenerates NAD⁺ so glycolysis keeps its 2 ATP: lactate (muscle, red cells; no gas) or ethanol + CO₂ (yeast). **Anaerobic respiration** is a chain with an inorganic acceptor other than O₂ (some prokaryotes). **Regulation:** PFK (ATP, citrate inhibit; AMP activates), pyruvate dehydrogenase (acetyl CoA, NADH inhibit), cycle dehydrogenases (ADP activates), GLUT4 (insulin). Fats enter by **beta-oxidation** as acetyl CoA (9 kcal/g: more C–H, more electrons); proteins by **deamination**. Lab 6: respirometers, KOH absorbs CO₂ so the droplet measures O₂ uptake, bead vial is the control to subtract, germinating beats dormant, warm beats cool.

## Numbers to know

ΔG of ATP hydrolysis −7.3 kcal/mol (about −14 in vivo) · 9 ATP + 6 NADPH per G3P, 18 + 12 per glucose · glycolysis net 2 ATP, 2 NADH · 10 NADH + 2 FADH₂ per glucose · about 2.5 ATP per NADH, 1.5 per FADH₂ · about 30–32 ATP per glucose (36–38 in older texts), 2 by fermentation · about 34% efficiency, 90% of ATP from oxidative phosphorylation · visible light 380–750 nm, chlorophyll peaks about 430 and 660 nm · Na⁺/K⁺ pump 3 out, 2 in · rate roughly doubles per 10 °C below the optimum.

## Equations from the AP sheet that apply

Rate = Δy/Δx (enzyme assays, respirometers, ET50: use the early linear part and convert units). Photosynthesis and respiration equations as above. ΔG = ΔH − TΔS and the coupling arithmetic (sum the two ΔG values). For the labs: 1/ET50 for leaf disks; corrected volume = sample − control, then divide by time, for respirometers.

## Traps the exam sets

- Enzymes lower activation energy, never ΔG; they are not used up; they cannot make endergonic exergonic.
- A falling rate late in a run is substrate depletion unless the plateau is low; a cooler tube that slows too is the comparison.
- Competitive inhibition is overcome by substrate; noncompetitive is not. Decide from the high-substrate rows.
- Spontaneous is not fast. Breaking a bond costs energy; ATP's energy is in the strain and the stable products.
- O₂ from photosynthesis comes from water; the Calvin cycle makes G3P, not glucose, and runs by day.
- Glycolysis needs no oxygen and releases no CO₂; the cycle makes carriers, not most of the ATP; CO₂ is never made in the chain.
- Fermentation makes NAD⁺, not ATP; it is not anaerobic respiration.
- Uncouplers raise O₂ consumption and heat while ATP falls; inhibitors lower O₂ consumption. Opposite readings, same ATP result.
- Chloroplast and mitochondrion: same mechanism, mirrored compartments. Plants respire too.
- Extrapolating a temperature doubling past the optimum ignores denaturation.
