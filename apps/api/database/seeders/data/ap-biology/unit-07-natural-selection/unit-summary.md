**Built from:** OpenStax *Biology 2e* Chapter 18 (§18.1–18.3), Chapter 19 (§19.1–19.3), Chapter 20 (§20.1–20.3), §22.1, §23.1 and §1.2

## The six ideas that generate every question

1. **Variation comes first; selection sorts it.** Mutation and recombination make variants at random with respect to need. The environment (or a breeder) then decides which variants leave more offspring. Antibiotics do not create resistance; drought does not create deep beaks.
2. **Fitness is counted in offspring, relative to others.** Not strength, size or lifespan. A trait that lowers survival can still spread if it raises mating success.
3. **Populations evolve; individuals do not.** Evolution is a change in allele frequencies across generations. Hardy-Weinberg describes the population in which nothing is changing, so you can detect the one in which something is.
4. **Five forces change a gene pool.** Selection (the only one that adapts), drift (random, strong when small), gene flow (homogenises), mutation (the source, but slow), nonrandom mating (changes genotype frequencies only).
5. **Shared, arbitrary features mean shared ancestry.** DNA, the code and ribosomes for all life; nucleus, mitochondria, linear chromosomes and introns for eukaryotes; shared derived traits for every clade on a tree.
6. **Speciation is the end of gene flow.** Cut it (geography, ecology, polyploidy), let selection and drift diverge the halves, and barriers appear. Variation is what every step draws on; lose it and the population cannot respond.

## Natural selection (7.1, 7.2)

Darwin and Wallace's three conditions: heritable variation; overproduction with competition; differential survival and reproduction. **Adaptation** is a heritable trait that raises fitness in the present environment; favourable is environment-specific (large leaves in a wet climate, small in a dry one). Selection has no goal and cannot invent; it works under trade-offs and linkage, and drift and gene flow interfere.

| Mode | Mean | Spread | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stabilizing | stays | narrows | Mice matching the forest floor; birth weight |
| Directional | shifts | usually narrows | Finch beak depth after the 1977 drought |
| Disruptive | stays or splits | widens | Beach mice light on sand, dark in grass |

Variation is maintained by **negative frequency-dependent selection** (side-blotched lizard morphs: orange beats blue, blue beats yellow, yellow beats orange; rare wins) and **heterozygote advantage** (sickle-cell where malaria is common). **Sexual selection** produces dimorphism; the good-genes hypothesis and the handicap principle explain costly ornaments.

**Standard error.** SE = s / √n. Means whose ±2 SE bars do not overlap are probably different. A wider bar means a smaller sample, not more variation. **Relative fitness** = a genotype's offspring ÷ the best genotype's offspring.

## Artificial selection (7.3)

Same mechanism, different agent: the breeder chooses who reproduces. It is evidence because it changes populations on a human timescale, and its response depends on selection strength and **heritability**. It also reduces diversity (few founders, inbreeding), the start of 7.11. *Investigation 1*: Fast Plant trichomes, select one extreme, measure the next generation against an unselected control.

## Population genetics (7.4)

Count alleles, not individuals: for 245 YY, 210 Yy, 45 yy, p = (490 + 210)/1,000 = 0.7. **Drift** is sampling error; losing one of ten rabbits removes a tenth of the gene pool. Ends in **fixation**; a lost allele returns only by mutation or migration. **Bottleneck** (elephant seals: twenty survivors, no variation after recovery) and **founder effect** (Afrikaners: Huntington's and Fanconi alleles from a few colonists). **Gene flow** makes populations alike and adds alleles. **Nonrandom mating** (assortative, inbreeding) raises homozygosity and leaves p, q unchanged; inbreeding exposes recessives (**inbreeding depression**). Add variation: mutation, gene flow. Remove it: drift, stabilizing and directional selection. Keep it: disruptive, frequency-dependent, heterozygote advantage.

## Hardy-Weinberg (7.5)

**p + q = 1; p² + 2pq + q² = 1.** A Punnett square for the gene pool. Five conditions: large population, random mating, no gene flow, no selection, no mutation; each is the absence of a force. It is a **null hypothesis**: match it and you cannot reject "no evolution at this locus now" (random mating rebuilds the proportions every generation, so a fit does not prove the population never changed); fail to match it and a force is acting, though the test does not say which. Recessive phenotype gives q²; square root gives q; carriers are 2pq, huge when q is small (cystic fibrosis: q² = 1/2,500, q = 0.02, carriers ≈ 1/25). Heterozygote deficit with unchanged p, q: nonrandom mating. p changing over years: selection, drift or gene flow.

**Chi-square.** χ² = Σ (o − e)² / e; expected from p and q, never from the observed genotypes; df = categories − 1; critical values at p = 0.05: 3.84 (df 1), 5.99 (df 2), 7.82 (df 3). Above critical: reject the null. *Investigation 2*: a spreadsheet draws random gametes; vary N to watch drift, add fitness values to watch selection, replicate runs.

## Evidence and common ancestry (7.6, 7.7)

| Evidence | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Fossils in dated layers (radiometric half-lives) | Lineages change over time; transitional forms |
| Biogeography (Proteaceae across former Gondwana; island endemics) | Distributions record history |
| Homologous structures (tetrapod forelimb) | Same construction and origin: common ancestor |
| Analogous structures (insect and bird wings) | Same job, different parts: convergence, NOT relatedness |
| Vestigial structures (whale pelvis, cormorant wings) | Leftovers of ancestral function |
| Embryology (pharyngeal slits in all vertebrates) | Shared developmental programme |
| DNA and protein sequences; gene duplication | Differences accumulate with time since divergence |

Shared by all life: DNA, the genetic code, ribosomes, ATP, glycolysis. Shared by all eukaryotes: membrane-bound organelles, linear chromosomes, introns. Three domains (Woese, rRNA): Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya. Living species are tips, not ancestors; humans and chimpanzees are sisters.

## Continuing evolution (7.8)

Antibiotic, pesticide and herbicide resistance; influenza and HIV; the Grants' finches. Resistance pre-exists (Lederberg replica plating, 1952); the drug selects. Bacteria evolve fast: huge N, short generations, **horizontal gene transfer** (transformation, transduction, conjugation). Full courses, fewer prescriptions, drug rotation. **Molecular clock**: rate = differences ÷ fossil-dated time; time = differences ÷ rate; neutral changes tick most steadily; calibrate with several fossils; match the gene's speed to the depth of the question. Percent identity = matches ÷ positions × 100.

## Phylogeny (7.9)

Relatedness = recency of common ancestry: find the shared node. Nodes rotate freely; tips are not ancestors; branch length means nothing without a scale. **Outgroup** sets ancestral states; **shared derived characters** define **clades** (ancestor plus all descendants). Paraphyletic (reptiles without birds), polyphyletic (warm-blooded animals); shared absences define nothing. **Parsimony**: fewest changes. Build from a table: order derived traits from most to least shared; taxa sharing the rarest are sisters; n taxa, n − 1 nodes. **BLAST** (*Investigation 3*): rank by lowest E-value and highest identity; tiers of identity are nested clades. E-value = expected chance hits, not a percentage.

## Speciation (7.10)

Biological species: interbreed in nature, fertile offspring, isolated from others. **Allopatric**: geographic barrier, then founder effect, drift and different selection (spotted owls; honeycreeper adaptive radiation). **Sympatric**: niche specialisation plus assortative mating (Lake Victoria and Apoyeque cichlids), or **polyploidy** (instant: a 2n = 6 plant making 2n gametes gives 4n = 12; tetraploid × diploid = sterile triploid; allopolyploid 3 + 5 = 8 sterile, doubled to 16 fertile; over half of plants). **Prezygotic**: habitat, temporal (Rana aurora Jan–Mar, R. boylii Mar–May), behavioural (firefly flashes), mechanical, gametic. **Postzygotic**: hybrid inviability, hybrid sterility (mule). Hybrid zones: reinforcement, fusion, stability. Rates: gradualism or punctuated equilibrium; fast in small isolated populations after environmental change.

## Variation and persistence (7.11)

Selection chooses among what exists. High **heterozygosity** (2pq at equilibrium) means resistant variants are likely present; low means a stress that harms one harms all (cheetahs; monocultures: Irish potatoes, Cavendish bananas). Numbers do not restore lost alleles; **gene flow** does. Phenotypic variation that is environmental cannot evolve.

## Origins of life (7.12)

| Billion years ago | Event |
|---|---|
| 4.6 | Earth forms |
| 3.8 / 3.5 | Chemical traces / oldest stromatolites |
| 2.3 | Oxygen accumulates (cyanobacteria split water) |
| 2.1 | Oldest eukaryotes |
| 1.5–1.0 | Chloroplast endosymbiosis |
| 0.6 | Multicellular animals |

Steps: abiotic **monomers** (Oparin-Haldane; Miller-Urey 1953 made amino acids from CH₄, NH₃, H₂, H₂O and sparks: monomers, not life; vents are the alternative setting) → **polymers** on mineral surfaces → **self-replicators** (**RNA world**: RNA stores and catalyses; ribozymes, the ribosome's RNA core, RNA primers, ribonucleotide precursors are the relics; selection starts here) → **protocells** (amphipathic lipids self-assemble; vesicles make lineages). **Endosymbiosis** (Margulis): mitochondria from alpha-proteobacteria, chloroplasts from cyanobacteria; two membranes, circular DNA, bacterial ribosomes, binary fission; most genes moved to the nucleus; secondary endosymbiosis gives three or four membranes. LUCA already had DNA, the code and ribosomes. Evolution does not explain the origin of life.

## Traps the exam sets

- Individuals do not evolve; the environment does not cause the needed mutation; evolution has no goal.
- Fittest means most surviving offspring, not strongest.
- Count alleles (homozygotes twice) to get p; compute expected from p and q, not from observed genotypes.
- A Hardy-Weinberg fit is a failure to reject, not a proof of no evolution; a significant χ² names no force.
- Inbreeding changes genotype, not allele, frequencies.
- Analogous structures are not evidence of relatedness; shared absences and shared ancestral traits define no clade.
- Read nodes, not neighbours; tips are not ancestors; branch length needs a scale.
- Classify barriers by whether fertilisation happened.
- Lab-producible hybrids do not make one species; polyploids are species in one generation.
- Large numbers are not large diversity; phenotypic variation is not heritable variation.
- Miller-Urey made monomers; natural selection does not explain the first replicator.
