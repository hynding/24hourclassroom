# Unit 2 · Cells · The night-before sheet

**Exam weight:** 10–13% of the AP exam · **CED topics:** 2.1 to 2.10 · **Big Ideas:** SYI, ENE, EVO
**Built from:** OpenStax *Biology 2e* Chapter 4 (§4.1–4.6), Chapter 5 (§5.1–5.4) and §23.1 Eukaryotic Origins

## The five ideas that generate every question

1. **The hydrophobic core decides what crosses.** Phospholipids self-assemble into a bilayer because water excludes their tails (Unit 1). Small nonpolar molecules dissolve through; polar molecules struggle; ions cannot cross without a protein. Polarity and charge matter, size hardly does.
2. **Proteins give the cell control.** Channels and carriers let chosen things through down their gradients for free; pumps spend ATP to move things uphill and so make the inside different from the outside; cotransporters spend those gradients to move still more things uphill.
3. **Water follows solutes.** Water is never pumped. It moves by osmosis from higher to lower water potential, Ψ = Ψs + Ψp, and the cell manages water by managing solutes, pressure (plant walls) or bailing (contractile vacuoles).
4. **Surface area limits volume.** SA:V falls as a cell grows (6/*s* for a cube, 3/*r* for a sphere), so cells stay small, flatten, grow projections or fold membranes inside. Organelles are folded membrane.
5. **Compartments have histories.** Membranes let incompatible chemistries coexist and build gradients. Two compartments, mitochondria and chloroplasts, carry bacterial genomes, ribosomes and membranes because they were engulfed bacteria.

## Cell structure (2.1)

| Structure | Key feature | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | double envelope with pores; nucleolus | holds DNA; assembles ribosome subunits |
| Ribosome | rRNA + protein, two subunits; free or on rough ER | translation; free = cytosolic proteins, bound = membrane and secreted proteins |
| Rough ER | ribosome-studded sheets | folds and glycosylates export proteins; makes phospholipid |
| Smooth ER | no ribosomes | lipid and steroid synthesis; detoxification; Ca²⁺ storage |
| Golgi | stacked sacs, cis to trans | modifies, tags and sorts cargo into vesicles |
| Lysosome | single membrane, pH 5 hydrolases | digestion and recycling (animal cells) |
| Peroxisome | single membrane | oxidises fatty acids; detoxifies; handles H₂O₂ |
| Mitochondrion | double membrane; cristae; matrix; own DNA and ribosomes | ATP by respiration |
| Chloroplast | double envelope; thylakoids in grana; stroma; own DNA and ribosomes | photosynthesis |
| Central vacuole | plant; large | water and solute store; turgor |
| Cell wall | cellulose (plant), chitin (fungi), peptidoglycan (bacteria) | shape, pressure resistance; porous |
| Cytoskeleton | microfilaments 7 nm, intermediate 8–10 nm, microtubules 25 nm | shape, movement, vesicle tracks, spindle, cilia (9+2) |

Every cell: membrane, cytoplasm, DNA, ribosomes. Prokaryotes 0.1–5 μm, nucleoid, no organelles. Eukaryotes 10–100 μm. Secretory path: rough ER → vesicle → Golgi cis → trans → vesicle → membrane or lysosome. Lumen of ER/Golgi/vesicle = outside of cell, so sugars face out.

## Cell size (2.2)

Cube: SA = 6*s*², V = *s*³, SA:V = 6/*s*. Sphere: SA = 4π*r*², V = (4/3)π*r*³, SA:V = 3/*r*. Double the size, halve the ratio. Diffusion covers a fixed distance in a fixed time, so a big cell's centre is unreachable. Solutions: stay small or divide; be flat or long; microvilli and root hairs; cristae and thylakoids.

## The membrane (2.3, 2.4)

**Fluid mosaic model** (Singer and Nicolson, 1972): phospholipid bilayer 5–10 nm thick, ~50% protein, 40% lipid, 10% carbohydrate by mass (varies: myelin 76% lipid). Fluid because no covalent bonds between lipids; fluidity raised by temperature and unsaturated (kinked) tails, buffered by cholesterol. **Integral** proteins span the bilayer with 20–25 nonpolar residues; **peripheral** proteins sit on a face. **Glycoproteins and glycolipids** face out only (glycocalyx; blood groups).

| Crosses a bare bilayer | Examples |
|---|---|
| Freely | O₂, CO₂, N₂, steroids, fat-soluble vitamins |
| Slowly | water, glycerol, urea |
| Barely | glucose, amino acids |
| Not at all | Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻, Ca²⁺, ATP, proteins |

## Transport (2.5, 2.6, 2.8)

| Mechanism | Protein | ATP | Direction | Saturates |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Simple diffusion | no | no | down | no |
| Facilitated diffusion (channel / carrier) | yes | no | down | carrier yes |
| Osmosis | aquaporin optional | no | toward lower Ψ | no |
| Primary active (pump) | yes | directly | up | yes |
| Secondary active (symporter, antiporter) | yes | via a gradient | up | yes |
| Endocytosis, exocytosis | yes | yes | any | no |

**Na⁺/K⁺ pump:** 3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in, 1 ATP; electrogenic; inside about −70 mV. Cell holds ~140 mM K⁺ in / 5 out, ~15 mM Na⁺ in / 145 out. **Secondary:** Na⁺ down its gradient drags glucose up through a symporter; block the pump and it stops. **Channels** are pores (tens of millions/s, some gated); **carriers** bind and flip (10³–10⁶/s, specific, saturate). **Endocytosis:** phagocytosis (particles, then lysosome), pinocytosis (fluid), receptor-mediated (specific, clathrin; LDL). **Exocytosis:** secretion, neurotransmitters. Membrane area balanced by the two.

## Tonicity and water potential (2.7)

| Solution | Animal cell | Plant cell |
|---|---|---|
| Hypotonic (fewer solutes) | swells, lyses | turgid (healthy) |
| Isotonic | normal | flaccid |
| Hypertonic (more solutes) | crenates | plasmolyses, wilts |

**Ψ = Ψs + Ψp.** **Ψs = −iCRT**: *i* = 1 sucrose, 2 NaCl; *C* in mol/L; *R* = 0.0831 L·bar/(mol·K); *T* in kelvin. Pure open water Ψ = 0. Ψp = 0 in an open beaker, positive in a turgid cell. **Water moves to lower Ψ.** Add components before comparing. Zero crossing of a mass-change graph gives the isotonic molarity; its Ψs is the tissue's Ψ. Incipient plasmolysis: Ψp = 0, Ψcell = Ψs. Osmoregulation: contractile vacuole (ATP); freshwater fish dilute urine and absorb salt; marine fish drink and pump salt out; ~5% of fish energy; kidney and ADH insert aquaporins.

## Compartmentalization and its origin (2.9, 2.10)

Compartments allow incompatible chemistry (lysosome pH 5 vs cytosol 7.2), concentrate enzymes and substrates, hold gradients across internal membranes, and multiply membrane surface. Endomembrane system: nuclear envelope, ER, Golgi, lysosomes, vesicles, plasma membrane; **not** mitochondria or chloroplasts. Prokaryotes specialise one membrane (infoldings) rather than making organelles.

**Endosymbiosis evidence:** two membranes (inner bacterial, outer host); own circular histone-free DNA; bacterial-type ribosomes (antibiotic-sensitive); division by fission, never built from scratch. Mitochondria (aerobic alpha-proteobacterium) came first, in all eukaryotes; chloroplasts (cyanobacterium) later, in some. Eukaryote fossils ~2.1 billion years ago. Secondary endosymbiosis: an alga engulfed, plastid with 3–4 membranes, sometimes a nucleomorph. Nucleus and ER arose by infolding, a different route.

## Traps the exam sets

- Polarity decides permeability, not size: Na⁺ is tiny and excluded; a steroid is large and passes.
- Facilitated diffusion uses a protein but no ATP; it is passive. Endocytosis uses no gradient but needs ATP; it is active.
- Secondary active transport uses no ATP directly but stops when ATP stops.
- NaCl has *i* = 2; *T* is in kelvin; living tissue has negative Ψ; compare total Ψ, not Ψs.
- Plant cells do not burst because of the wall, not because of a less permeable membrane.
- Plant cells have mitochondria too. The nucleus does not make proteins. Mitochondria are not in the endomembrane system.
- A membrane and enzymes are not evidence of endosymbiosis; DNA, ribosomes and fission are.
- More total surface is not more SA:V. Double the size, halve the ratio.
- Describe data with direction and two numbers before explaining.
