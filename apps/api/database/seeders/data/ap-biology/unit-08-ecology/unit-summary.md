# Unit 8 · Ecology · The night-before sheet

**Exam weight:** 10–15% of the AP exam · **CED topics:** 8.1 to 8.7 · **Big Ideas:** ENE, SYI, EVO, IST
**Built from:** OpenStax *Biology 2e* §44.5, Chapter 45 (§45.1, §45.3–45.7), Chapter 46 (§46.1–46.3) and Chapter 47 (§47.1–47.4)

## The six ideas that generate every question

1. **Energy flows; matter cycles.** Energy enters as light (or inorganic chemical energy), passes up the food web, and leaves as heat. Carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus and water are used over and over. Decomposers recycle matter, never energy.
2. **Every transfer loses most of the energy.** Respiration at every level turns assimilated energy into heat, so only 5–20% (call it 10%) reaches the next level. That is why food chains are short and why persistent toxins concentrate upward.
3. **Growth is births minus deaths, scaled by what is left.** Unlimited, dN/dt = rmax N; limited, the factor (K − N)/K slows it. Crowding is the biology inside that factor.
4. **Only density-dependent factors regulate.** Competition, predation, disease and waste strengthen per individual as density rises and push a population toward K. Weather, fire and floods take the same proportion at any density and only reset N.
5. **Interactions are selective pressures.** Predation, competition and symbiosis are classified by who gains and who loses, and each shapes the traits of both partners. A few species (keystones) hold a community together far out of proportion to their numbers.
6. **Diversity is insurance; disruption sorts variation.** Richer, more even communities resist disturbance and recover faster. A disruption does not create the variation it selects; the survivors are the ones whose existing traits suit the new conditions.

## Responses to the environment (8.1)

Ask two questions of every behaviour: **proximate** (what cue triggers it, what nerves and hormones carry it out) and **ultimate** (why individuals who did it left more offspring).

| Kind | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Kinesis | Undirected change in speed or turning | Woodlice speed up in dry air, linger in damp |
| Taxis | Directed movement toward or away from a stimulus | Phototaxis, chemotaxis in *Tetrahymena* |
| Fixed action pattern | Stereotyped sequence released by a sign stimulus | Stickleback attacks anything red-bellied |
| Habituation | Learned loss of response to a harmless repeated stimulus | Prairie dogs ignore familiar footsteps |
| Imprinting | Learning in a critical period | Ducklings follow the first moving object |
| Classical / operant conditioning | Cue paired with outcome / behaviour shaped by consequence | Pavlov's dogs / Skinner's rats |
| Signals | Pheromones, song, dance | Ant trails; the honeybee waggle dance |
| Altruism | Lowers actor's fitness, raises another's | Worker bees (kin selection), grooming monkeys (reciprocity) |

Plants respond without nerves: **phototropism** (auxin on the shaded side) and **photoperiodism** (night length read by phytochrome). Migration and hibernation are timed by day length. Communication is a signal plus a response, handled in the receiver by the same receptor-transduction-response machinery as Unit 4. **Investigation 12** (choice chamber) is tested with chi-square against a null of no preference.

## Energy flow and the four cycles (8.2)

**GPP** is the rate producers fix energy; **NPP = GPP − producer respiration**, the only energy consumers can eat. Silver Springs: GPP 20,810 and NPP 7,633 kcal/m²/yr. Energy pyramids are always upright; biomass and number pyramids can invert (English Channel phytoplankton 4 g/m² under zooplankton 21 g/m²: a snapshot, not a rate). **Net production efficiency** is about ten times higher in ectotherms (caterpillar 18%) than endotherms (squirrel 1.6%), because endotherms burn assimilated energy to keep warm. **Biomagnification**: DDT, PCBs and mercury are not respired or excreted, so concentration rises at each level (walleye in Saginaw Bay held over four times the PCB concentration of phytoplankton).

| Cycle | Main reservoirs | Key processes and organisms | Human disruption |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water | Ocean (97.5%); fresh water 2.5%, of which 69% ice, 31% groundwater, 0.3% lakes and rivers | Evaporation, transpiration, condensation, precipitation, infiltration, runoff | Deforestation (less transpiration, more runoff), overdrawn aquifers |
| Carbon | Atmosphere CO₂; ocean bicarbonate (over 90% of ocean carbon); limestone; fossil fuels | Photosynthesis in, respiration and decomposition out (fast loop); shells to limestone, burial to fossil fuel (slow loop) | Burning fossil fuels and forests: CO₂ from about 280 ppm before 1750 to over 400 ppm |
| Nitrogen | Atmosphere N₂ (78%), unusable because of the triple bond | Fixation (Rhizobium, Azotobacter, cyanobacteria) → ammonification (decomposers) → nitrification (Nitrosomonas) → denitrification (Pseudomonas, Clostridium) back to N₂ | Industrial fixation as large as natural; fertiliser runoff, eutrophication, acid rain, N₂O |
| Phosphorus | Rock and ocean sediment; **no atmospheric phase** | Weathering → soil → plants → consumers → decomposers → water → sediment; returns to land only by uplift | Fertiliser and sewage runoff; usually the limiting nutrient in fresh water |

**Eutrophication**: nutrients in, algal bloom, bloom dies, decomposer respiration strips oxygen, dead zone (Gulf of Mexico, over 8,000 square miles).

## Population ecology (8.3)

dN/dt = B − D (add immigration, subtract emigration). **Exponential** (J-curve): dN/dt = rmax N, unlimited resources, briefly real. **Logistic** (S-curve): dN/dt = rmax N (K − N)/K; growth is fastest at **N = K/2**; above K the factor is negative and N falls. Real populations overshoot and oscillate because K shifts and feedback is delayed.

| Survivorship | Pattern | Life history |
|---|---|---|
| Type I | Most survive to old age | Few offspring, much care (humans, Dall sheep) |
| Type II | Constant death rate | Many birds |
| Type III | Most die young | Huge numbers of offspring, no care (most fish, trees) |

Estimate N for mobile animals by **mark-recapture**: N = (marked first × caught second) ÷ marked in second; it assumes no tag loss, full mixing and equal catchability.

## Effect of density (8.4)

| Density-dependent | Density-independent |
|---|---|
| Per-capita effect rises with crowding | Same proportion at any density |
| Competition, predation, parasites, disease, waste | Frost, drought, fire, flood, eruption |
| Negative feedback; can hold N near K | Resets N; cannot regulate |
| *Ascaris* eggs per female fall with worm number | A storm killing 30% of a population whatever its size |

Predator-prey cycles (lynx peaks 1–2 years after hares on a ten-year cycle) are density-dependent feedback with a lag. **K-selected** species (elephants, oaks) live near K with few, well-provisioned offspring; **r-selected** species (dandelions, insects) exploit disturbed habitats with many cheap ones. Dispersion is uniform, random or clumped.

## Community ecology (8.5)

| Interaction | Signs | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Competition | −/− | *Paramecium aurelia* excludes *P. caudatum* |
| Predation, herbivory | +/− | Lynx and hare; defences, aposematism, mimicry |
| Mutualism | +/+ | Rhizobium and legumes; termites and gut protozoa; lichens |
| Commensalism | +/0 | A bird nesting in a tree |
| Parasitism | +/− | Tapeworm; *Plasmodium* |

**Competitive exclusion**: two species cannot share one niche indefinitely; coexisting competitors partition resources, so the **realised niche** is narrower than the **fundamental niche**. **Batesian** mimicry is a harmless bluff (works while mimics are rare); **Mullerian** is several harmful species sharing one warning. **Foundation species** build habitat (kelp, coral); **keystone species** have effects out of proportion to their abundance (*Pisaster*: richness 15 to 8 when removed; Yellowstone wolves set off a **trophic cascade** to willows and streams). **Primary succession** starts without soil (lava, pioneer lichens); **secondary** starts with soil (fire, old fields; oak-hickory climax after about 150 years).

## Biodiversity (8.6)

Diversity is **richness** plus **evenness**, measured with **Simpson's index = 1 − Σ (n/N)²**: 0 for a monoculture, approaching 1 for many equally common species; the maximum for S species is 1 − 1/S. A dominant species drags it down hard: 90% in one species caps it at 0.19. Diverse communities are more **resistant** (stay put) and more **resilient** (recover fast), because several species can fill each role; genetic diversity does the same within a species (the Irish potato famine). **Ecosystem services**: pollination, water purification, pest control, soil, carbon storage. 34 **hotspots** on 2.3% of land hold 42% of terrestrial vertebrates, most of them endemic.

## Disruptions (8.7)

| Category | Examples | Mechanism to name |
|---|---|---|
| Invasive species | Kudzu (7 million acres), brown tree snake on Guam, Nile perch in Lake Victoria, chytrid fungus, white-nose syndrome | Released from enemies; natives never selected to resist |
| Human activity | Habitat loss (the leading cause), overharvesting (Atlantic cod), pollution, eutrophication | Removes habitat or individuals faster than replacement |
| Climate change | CO₂ above the 180–300 ppm ice-core range; earlier flowering; range shifts north and upslope | Phenological mismatch; habitat squeezed off mountaintops and poles |
| Geological and meteorological | Eruptions, the K-Pg impact, El Niño, continental drift | Resets communities; El Niño collapses upwelling and productivity |

Every disruption selects among existing variation; mass extinctions are followed by **adaptive radiation** into emptied niches.

## Numbers to know

Transfer efficiency 5–20% (10% rule) · ectotherm net production efficiency about 10 × endotherm (18% versus 1.6%) · 4–6 transfers before energy runs out · fresh water 2.5% of Earth's water, 0.3% of that in lakes and rivers · N₂ 78% of air · CO₂ about 280 ppm before 1750, over 400 ppm now · ocean surface pH 8.2 to 8.1, about a 26% rise in H⁺ · logistic growth fastest at K/2 · chi-square critical values at p = 0.05: 3.84 (df 1), 5.99 (df 2), 7.81 (df 3).

## Equations from the AP sheet that apply

- Exponential growth: **dN/dt = rmax N**.
- Logistic growth: **dN/dt = rmax N (K − N)/K**.
- Simpson's diversity index: **1 − Σ (n/N)²**.
- Chi-square for behaviour or count data: **χ² = Σ (o − e)²/e**, df = categories − 1, expected values from the null.
- The 10% rule (not on the sheet as an equation): energy at the next level ≈ energy at this level × 0.1; transfer efficiency = upper level ÷ lower level × 100.
- Population growth from rates: dN/dt = B − D.

## Traps the exam sets

- Decomposers recycle **matter**, not energy.
- The 10% rule is an estimate; calculate from the data when numbers are given.
- Biomass pyramids can invert; energy pyramids cannot.
- Per-capita growth is highest at low N; total growth (dN/dt) peaks at K/2.
- K belongs to a species *and* its environment; it changes.
- Proportion killed decides density dependence, not number killed.
- A keystone is not the most abundant species; that is usually the dominant competitor or a foundation species.
- Simpson's index is 1 *minus* the sum of squares; more species does not guarantee a higher index.
- Plants take up nitrate and ammonium, never N₂; denitrification removes usable nitrogen.
- Phosphorus has no gaseous phase.
- Burning fossil fuels raises CO₂ because it moves carbon out of a long-term reservoir; respiration returns recently fixed carbon.
- A significant chi-square shows a non-random pattern, not its mechanism (kinesis or taxis?).
- Disruptions do not cause the mutations survivors carry.

## The exam

Weeks 28 to 32, every topic 8.1 to 8.7, about 70 minutes, 49 items: multiple choice (three data stimulus sets: a duckweed growth table, a lake energy and methylmercury table, a stream-invertebrate table for Simpson's index), select-all, true/false, fill-in-the-blank, two numeric items, one short free response (4 points) on a competition experiment and one long free response (8 points) interpreting a density experiment. About a fifth of the items are cumulative review from Units 1 to 7, because ecology is where the course closes its loops: photosynthesis and respiration are the carbon cycle, tonicity decides who survives salt intrusion, enzymes decide where species live. In every data item, read the increments or proportions before naming a model, show each calculation, and in free response answer every verb (identify, describe, calculate, explain, evaluate, predict) with its own sentence and a number from the table.
