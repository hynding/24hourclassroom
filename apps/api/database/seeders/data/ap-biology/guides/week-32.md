## What you need to be able to do

This week has no new content. Its job is the synthesis the exam rewards: taking a data set about an ecosystem and bringing to it the right tool from the formula sheet, the right concept from Unit 8, and the right mechanism from Units 1 to 7. The learning objectives are all seven of the unit's, and the Science Practices are the ones that carry most of the marks: representing and describing data, performing statistical tests, and building an argument from evidence.

In plain language: when you see a table or graph you must (1) say what it shows with numbers, (2) pick the calculation it invites, (3) explain the mechanism, (4) predict what changes if a variable changes, and (5) justify with a concept. Each of those is a point on a free-response item.

## Key vocabulary

| Tool or idea | When it applies |
|---|---|
| dN/dt = B − D | Any population given births and deaths (add immigration, subtract emigration if open). |
| dN/dt = rmax N | A population with resources not yet limiting; a J-curve. |
| dN/dt = rmax N (K − N)/K | A population approaching a carrying capacity; an S-curve; growth peaks at K/2. |
| 1 − Σ (n/N)² | Comparing the diversity of two communities from counts; sensitive to dominance. |
| χ² = Σ (o − e)²/e | Comparing observed counts with counts expected under a null hypothesis; df = categories − 1; 3.84 / 5.99 / 7.81. |
| SE = s/√n | Deciding whether two means differ; non-overlapping 2-SE bars suggest a real difference. |
| Transfer efficiency | Upper level ÷ lower level × 100; about 10%, range 5–20%. |
| Density-dependent test | Does the per-capita effect change with crowding? |
| Keystone test | Does removal change the community far out of proportion to abundance? |
| Control | What rules out everything except the treatment. |
| Proximate / ultimate | How the behaviour happens / why it raised fitness. |
| Reservoir / flux | Where an element sits / the rate it moves; which flux has a human addition? |

## The links the exam will test

Unit 8 is where the course closes its loops. The links below are the ones that appear in multi-unit questions.

**Energy.** Gross primary productivity is photosynthesis at ecosystem scale (Unit 3); the gap between gross and net is the producers' respiration (Unit 3); the 10% rule exists because every organism's respiration releases most of its assimilated energy as heat, as the laws of thermodynamics require (Unit 3); small endotherms lose heat fastest because of surface-to-volume ratio (Unit 2), which is why net production efficiency is lowest in warm-blooded consumers; evaporative cooling, the way that heat leaves, depends on water's hydrogen bonding (Unit 1).

**Matter.** The carbon cycle's two biological arrows are photosynthesis and respiration (Unit 3); the nitrogen cycle needs bacteria because of the N≡N triple bond (Unit 1) and those bacteria express nitrogenase only when ammonium is scarce (Unit 6, operon logic); nitrate and phosphate enter roots by active transport (Unit 2); biomagnifying toxins are nonpolar and partition into lipid (Unit 1); ocean acidification is pH arithmetic (Unit 1), a 0.1-unit fall being a 26% rise in hydrogen ions.

**Populations.** Hardy-Weinberg is the null hypothesis for whether a population is evolving (Unit 7), and a density-independent crash that bottlenecks a population makes drift likely (Unit 7); mutation is the source of the variation a disruption sorts (Unit 6), and the disruption does not cause the mutations; the chi-square used on a choice chamber or on seedling counts is the one used on Mendelian ratios (Unit 5), with the expected values supplied by a different hypothesis.

**Interactions.** Predator-prey cycles are negative feedback with a delay (Unit 4); a pheromone is a ligand and the receiver's response is signal transduction (Unit 4); aposematic coloration, mimicry and plant toxins are products of natural selection by predators (Unit 7); invasive species succeed because native species were never selected to resist them (Unit 7); the cichlids of Lake Victoria are a clade produced by adaptive radiation (Unit 7); coral bleaching is the loss of a photosynthetic partner (Unit 3); which species tolerates an estuary is decided by tonicity (Unit 2); where a species lives on a temperature gradient is decided by its enzymes (Unit 3).

## Worked example 1: a deer exclosure read with two tools

| Plant group | Inside fence | Outside fence |
|---|---|---|
| Oak seedlings | 40 | 8 |
| Maple seedlings | 30 | 12 |
| Wildflowers | 25 | 5 |
| Grasses | 20 | 60 |
| Ferns | 15 | 15 |
| N | 130 | 100 |

*Describe:* the same five groups occur in both plots; outside the fence grasses are 60% of plants and seedlings and wildflowers are scarce. *Simpson's index:* outside, squares 0.0064 + 0.0144 + 0.0025 + 0.36 + 0.0225 = 0.406, index **0.59**; inside, 0.78. Equal richness, lower evenness where deer browse. *Chi-square on oak seedlings:* null of equal numbers gives 24 and 24; (16²/24) × 2 = **21.3**; df 1; 21.3 > 3.84, so reject. *Mechanism:* selective herbivory removes the browsed species and releases the grasses, a keystone effect in reverse. *Predict:* the fenced plot follows secondary succession to oak-maple forest; the browsed plot stays an opening as long as deer are abundant. *Control:* the unfenced plot experienced the same weather and years, so the fence is the only difference.

## Worked example 2: a nutrient-addition experiment

A lake is split by a curtain. Both basins receive carbon and nitrogen; one also receives phosphorus. After eight summers, chlorophyll is 4 μg/L in the C + N basin and 38 in the C + N + P basin; late-summer oxygen near the bottom is 7.5 mg/L versus 0.8; zooplankton species 14 versus 7; the phosphorus basin is dominated by nitrogen-fixing cyanobacteria.

*Limiting nutrient:* phosphorus, because the basins differ only in P and the response is tenfold. *Why phosphorus:* no atmospheric reservoir, only rock weathering. *Why anoxia:* the bloom died and decomposer respiration consumed the oxygen. *Why cyanobacteria:* they fix their own nitrogen, so phosphorus alone released them from limitation, which means a policy that cuts nitrogen but not phosphorus will not stop blooms. *Why fewer zooplankton:* dominance by one inedible group and anoxic deep water. *Limitation:* one lake, no replication; improve by repeating on several lakes or swapping treatments between basins.

## Worked example 3: a life table into a survivorship curve

Of 1,000 Dall sheep lambs, about 200 die in year one, 10–20 die in each year from 1 to 7, and more than 100 die in each year from 8 to 14. *Mortality rate at age 3–4:* 12 die of 776 alive, 12 ÷ 776 × 1,000 ≈ 15.5 per 1,000. *Type:* mostly I: a dip for vulnerable lambs, a long flat stretch, then death in old age. *Life history implied:* few offspring, parental care, K-selected. *Contrast:* a cod lays millions of eggs and loses nearly all in the first interval, Type III, r-selected. The shape of the curve is the life-history strategy drawn as a graph.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Choose the biggest number."** Describe the pattern across all rows and the control before you choose an explanation.
- **"Significant means important."** A chi-square above the critical value means the difference is unlikely to be chance; it says nothing about size or cause. Mechanism is a separate point.
- **"Expected values come from the data."** They come from the null hypothesis: equal chambers, 9:3:3:1, p² + 2pq + q².
- **"K is fixed."** Humans have raised theirs repeatedly; overgrazers lower theirs; it is a property of the environment and the species together.
- **"Energy is recycled by decomposers."** Matter cycles. Energy enters as light, leaves as heat.
- **"A hybrid is a new species."** The grizzly-polar hybrid is gene flow between two species brought into contact by climate, not speciation.
- **"Disruptions cause the mutations populations need."** Mutation is random; disruption selects among variation already present.

## Self-check

1. Counts of 60, 25, 10 and 5 in one plot and 25 each in another. Calculate both Simpson's indices and state which plot would resist the loss of its commonest species better.
2. Forty seedlings inside a fence, 8 outside. Run the chi-square against equal numbers and interpret.
3. A population of 2,000 has rmax 0.3 per year and K 5,000. Calculate dN/dt now, and state at what N it would be largest.
