# Unit 6 · Gene Expression and Regulation · The night-before sheet

**Exam weight:** 12–16% of the AP exam · **CED topics:** 6.1 to 6.8 · **Big Ideas:** IST, EVO, SYI, ENE
**Built from:** OpenStax *Biology 2e* Chapter 14 (§14.1–14.6), Chapter 15 (§15.1–15.5), Chapter 16 (§16.1–16.7), Chapter 17 (§17.1–17.3), with §13.2 and §22.2

## The five ideas that generate every question

1. **Strands have direction, and enzymes obey it.** Every polymerase reads 3′ → 5′ and builds 5′ → 3′ by joining a 5′ triphosphate to a 3′-OH. That one fact produces primers, leading and lagging strands, Okazaki fragments, telomere shortening, the direction of transcription, and the way Sanger sequencing stops a chain.
2. **Base pairing is the universal targeting system.** Replication, transcription, codon-anticodon reading, splice-site recognition, miRNAs, PCR primers, probes and CRISPR guide RNAs all find their targets by complementarity.
3. **Same genome, different reading.** A neuron and a liver cell carry identical DNA. Regulation at five levels, from chromatin to protein turnover, decides what is made where, and the environment acts by changing that reading.
4. **Mutation is the only source of new alleles, and it is random with respect to need.** Its effect depends on where it falls (coding, regulatory, splice site, chromosome) and what it changes (frame, residue, dose). Selection, not the environment, decides which survive.
5. **The code is universal, so the tools are universal.** A human gene works in a bacterium and a bacterial gene in a plant; biotechnology is the cell's own enzymes and pairing rules run in a tube.

## DNA and RNA structure (6.1)

| Evidence | Question | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Griffith | Can a trait transfer? | Heat-killed S + live R killed mice; live S recovered |
| Avery, MacLeod, McCarty | What transfers? | Only destroying DNA stopped transformation |
| Hershey-Chase | What enters the host? | ³²P (DNA) in cells; ³⁵S (protein) outside |
| Chargaff | What rules do bases obey? | A = T, G = C; A+T : G+C varies by species |

Helix: 0.34 nm per bp, 3.4 nm and 10 bp per turn, 2 nm wide, purine always with pyrimidine, antiparallel, A–T two H-bonds, G–C three. **Prokaryotes**: one circular chromosome in the nucleoid, supercoiled, plus **plasmids**. **Eukaryotes**: linear chromosomes; **histone coiling** from nucleosome (147 bp on a histone octamer) to 30-nm fibre to looped scaffold to metaphase chromosome; histones bind because lysine and arginine are positive. Euchromatin open, heterochromatin silent. Some viruses use RNA.

## DNA replication (6.2)

**Meselson-Stahl**: generation 1 all intermediate (kills conservative); generation 2 half intermediate, half light (kills dispersive). Semiconservative.

| Enzyme | Job |
|---|---|
| Helicase | breaks H-bonds, unzips (ATP) |
| SSB proteins | hold strands apart |
| Topoisomerase | nicks and reseals ahead of the fork to relieve twist |
| Primase | lays a 5-10 nt RNA primer (the first 3′-OH) |
| DNA polymerase III | adds dNTPs 5′ → 3′; proofreads with 3′ exonuclease |
| DNA polymerase I | removes primers, fills with DNA |
| Ligase | seals the backbone between fragments |

Leading strand continuous toward the fork; lagging strand in Okazaki fragments away from it. E. coli: 4.6 × 10⁶ bp, one origin, two forks at 1,000 nt/s, about 40 min. Eukaryotes: many origins (up to 100,000), 50-100 nt/s, Pol α/δ/ε, telomeres (TTAGGG) and **telomerase** in germ, stem and cancer cells. Mismatch repair uses methylation to pick the old strand; excision repair removes thymine dimers.

## Transcription and RNA processing (6.3)

RNA polymerase reads the **template** 3′ → 5′, makes RNA 5′ → 3′ identical to the **coding strand** with U for T, needs **no primer**. Bacteria: sigma factor at −10/−35, hairpin or rho termination, polycistronic mRNA, coupled with translation, mRNA lasts seconds. Eukaryotes: Pol I (rRNA), **Pol II (mRNA)**, Pol III (tRNA); TATA box at −25 to −35 bound by general transcription factors; then **cap** (7-methylguanosine, 5′ protection and ribosome landing), **splicing** (spliceosome of snRNPs removes GU…AG introns; **alternative splicing** gives several proteins per gene), **poly-A tail** (about 200 A after AAUAAA; stability and export). mRNA lasts hours.

## Translation (6.4)

4³ = 64 codons: 61 sense, 3 stop (UAA, UAG, UGA), AUG = start = Met. Degenerate, mostly at the third position; nearly universal. Read the mRNA 5′ → 3′ in threes from AUG. **tRNA** anticodon pairs antiparallel with the codon; **aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase** (one per amino acid, ATP) loads it, and that is where the code is enforced. Ribosome 70S (30S + 50S) or 80S (40S + 60S), sites **A, P, E**. Initiation at Shine-Dalgarno (bacteria) or the cap (eukaryotes); elongation with GTP and **peptidyl transferase, an rRNA**; termination by a release factor. Polypeptide grows N → C; signal sequence sends it to the ER.

## Regulation (6.5)

| System | Switch | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| lac operon | allolactose inactivates repressor | on (inducible, catabolic) |
| lac operon | low glucose → cAMP → CAP | boosts to high |
| trp operon | tryptophan activates repressor (corepressor) | off (repressible, anabolic, negative feedback) |

Four lac states: Glc+/Lac− off; Glc+/Lac+ low; Glc−/Lac− off; Glc−/Lac+ high.

Eukaryotic levels: **epigenetic** (histone acetylation opens, DNA methylation of CpG silences and is inherited), **transcriptional** (general factors at the promoter; activators on distant **enhancers** loop to it; cis elements versus trans factors), **post-transcriptional** (splicing, stability, **miRNA/RISC**), **translational** (eIF-2 phosphorylation), **post-translational** (phosphorylation; ubiquitin → proteasome). Diagnose the level from which measure changes: transcription, mRNA or protein.

## Cell specialisation (6.6)

Every nucleated cell has the whole genome (nuclear transfer, cloning). Housekeeping genes everywhere; cell-specific genes hundreds-fold higher in one type. Development is a transcription-factor cascade; a **master regulator** (MyoD) imposes a fate. Signals end at transcription factors: steroid receptors directly, surface receptors through kinases. Environment changes expression (temperature, nutrients, diet and methylation).

## Mutations (6.7)

| Type | Effect |
|---|---|
| Silent | synonymous codon, no change |
| Missense | one residue changed; matters by position and R-group chemistry |
| Nonsense | new stop, truncated |
| Frameshift | indel not ÷ 3; everything downstream scrambled; earlier is worse |
| In-frame indel | residues added or removed, rest intact |
| Regulatory | amount, timing or splicing changed, sequence not |
| Chromosomal | deletion, duplication, inversion, translocation; nondisjunction → aneuploidy |

Spontaneous (replication, decay) or induced (UV, X-rays, chemicals). Germline inherited, somatic not (cancer). Replica plating: resistance precedes exposure, so mutation is random and selection follows. Bacteria also share genes by **transformation, transduction, conjugation** (pilus, plasmid); viruses recombine and reassort.

## Biotechnology (6.8)

**PCR**: 95 °C denature (replaces helicase), 50-65 °C anneal primers (replace primase, define the target), 72 °C extend with Taq; 2ⁿ copies. **Gel**: negative DNA to the positive pole, smaller farther, read against a ladder, semi-log plot. **Restriction enzymes**: palindromic sites, sticky ends, linear n sites → n + 1 fragments, circular → n; RFLPs. **Cloning**: same enzyme, ligase, vector with origin + marker + cloning site, cDNA for eukaryotic genes, expression vector with bacterial promoter and Shine-Dalgarno. **Transformation**: CaCl₂ and heat shock; select on ampicillin; pGLO glows only with arabinose; efficiency = colonies ÷ μg DNA plated. **Sequencing**: ddNTPs lack 3′-OH and stop chains; read shortest to longest; next-generation reads assembled by overlap. **CRISPR-Cas9**: guide RNA targets, Cas9 cuts, repair gives a frameshift knockout or copies a template. Applications: insulin from bacteria, Bt crops, gene therapy, DNA profiling by STRs.

## Numbers to know

0.34 nm per bp, 10 bp per turn · A–T 2 bonds, G–C 3 · E. coli 4.6 × 10⁶ bp, 1,000 nt/s, one origin · eukaryotes 50-100 nt/s, up to 100,000 origins · primer 5-10 nt · 64 codons, 61 sense, 3 stop · 70S and 80S ribosomes · 0.05 s per amino acid in E. coli · cap = 7-methylguanosine, tail about 200 A · miRNA about 22 nt · about 70% of human genes alternatively spliced · PCR 2ⁿ · linear fragments = sites + 1.

## Equations and arithmetic that apply

No AP formula-sheet equation is specific to this unit. The arithmetic you will be asked for: base-pair percentages from Chargaff (if A = 30%, C = 20%); length from 0.34 nm per bp; replication time = (bp ÷ forks) ÷ rate; amino acids = (coding nucleotides ÷ 3) − 1 for the stop; water released = residues − 1; PCR copies = 2ⁿ; transformation efficiency = colonies ÷ (μg added × fraction plated); fold change = ratio, not difference. Chi-square (from Unit 5) may appear in a transformation or expression data set.

## Traps the exam sets

- No polymerase ever builds 3′ → 5′; the lagging strand is 5′ → 3′ in pieces.
- Generation 1 kills conservative; generation 2 kills dispersive.
- The mRNA matches the coding strand, not the template.
- The stop codon adds no amino acid; the release factor is a protein.
- Lactose lifts the repressor; glucose absence adds CAP; both are needed for high.
- Tryptophan is a corepressor, not an inducer.
- Unchanged transcription with less mRNA is post-transcriptional control.
- A one-base deletion is worse than a three-base deletion.
- Antibiotics select resistant mutants; they do not create them.
- Smaller fragments run farther; count sites from fragments with the linear/circular rule.
- Only the ampicillin plate selects; arabinose only switches GFP on.
- A ddNTP is incorporated; it is the next nucleotide that cannot be.

Weeks 18 to 22, every topic 6.1 to 6.8, about 60 minutes, 45 items: multiple choice (two data-based stimulus sets), select-all, true/false, fill-in-the-blank, two numeric items, one short free response (4 points) and one long free response (8 points) on an experimental result.
