# Unit 5 · Heredity · The night-before sheet

**Exam weight:** 8–11% of the AP exam · **CED topics:** 5.1 to 5.5 · **Big Ideas:** IST, EVO, SYI
**Built from:** OpenStax *Biology 2e* Chapter 11 (§11.1–11.2), Chapter 12 (§12.1–12.3) and Chapter 13 (§13.1–13.2)

## The five ideas that generate every question

1. **One replication, two divisions.** S phase copies every chromosome once. Meiosis I separates homologues (2n → n, chromatids still paired); meiosis II separates sister chromatids (n → n). Homologues apart in I, sisters apart in II.
2. **Mendel's laws are meiosis seen from outside.** Segregation is anaphase I; independent assortment is metaphase I. Every Punnett square is a picture of gametes meeting at random.
3. **Variation is shuffled, not invented.** Mutation makes alleles. Crossing over (prophase I), independent assortment (2ⁿ) and random fertilisation make combinations. That variation is what the Red Queen demands and what Unit 7's selection acts on.
4. **Every non-Mendelian pattern is one assumption removed.** Not fully dominant (incomplete, codominant), not two alleles (ABO), not autosomal (X-linked), not independent (linked), not one gene per trait (epistasis), not nuclear (mitochondria, chloroplasts).
5. **Phenotype = genotype + environment.** The same allele gives a white trunk and black ears on a Himalayan rabbit; the same hydrangea is blue in acid and pink in neutral soil. Hold genotype constant to prove it.

## Meiosis (5.1)

| Stage | What happens | Chromosomes (2n = 4 example) |
|---|---|---|
| Prophase I | Synapsis, synaptonemal complex, crossing over between non-sister chromatids, chiasmata | 4 chromosomes, 8 chromatids, 2 tetrads |
| Metaphase I | Tetrads at the plate, each oriented at random | 4, 8 |
| Anaphase I | Homologues separate; sisters stay joined at the centromere | 2 per pole, each doubled |
| Telophase I | Two haploid cells; no S phase in interkinesis | 2 chromosomes, 4 chromatids per cell |
| Metaphase II | Chromosomes line up singly | 2, 4 |
| Anaphase II | Sister chromatids separate | 2 single-chromatid chromosomes per pole |
| Products | Four haploid cells, all different | 2 chromosomes, 2 chromatids each |

Mitosis: no synapsis, no crossing over, sisters separate, two identical 2n cells. Meiosis II is mitosis on a haploid set. Count chromosomes by centromeres; DNA content doubles in S and halves at each anaphase.

Life cycles all have one meiosis and one fertilisation: animals (diploid body, gametes by meiosis), fungi (haploid body, spores by meiosis), plants (sporophyte makes spores by meiosis, gametophyte makes gametes by mitosis).

**Nondisjunction.** Meiosis I failure: all four gametes abnormal (two n + 1, two n − 1). Meiosis II failure in one cell: two normal, one n + 1, one n − 1.

## Genetic diversity (5.2)

| Source | Stage | Arithmetic |
|---|---|---|
| Crossing over | Prophase I | Recombinant chromatids; more likely the farther apart two genes are |
| Independent assortment | Metaphase I | 2ⁿ combinations per parent; 2²³ ≈ 8.4 million in humans |
| Random fertilisation | Fertilisation | (2ⁿ)² zygote combinations; ≈ 70 trillion in humans |

**Sordaria (AP Investigation 7).** 4:4 ascus = no crossover between gene and centromere; 2:2:2:2 or 2:4:2 = crossover. Map distance = (crossover asci ÷ total × 100) ÷ 2, because only two of four chromatids take part in any one crossover.

**Why sex?** Asexual lineages gain variation only by mutation; sexual populations reshuffle every generation. The Red Queen: coevolving parasites track the common genotype, so a clone is a sitting target.

## Mendelian genetics (5.3)

Peas: self-fertilising, true-breeding, seven two-form traits, thousands counted. F1 uniform, F2 about 3 : 1 (705 : 224 for flower colour). Recessive traits vanish and return unchanged, so inheritance is particulate, not blending.

| Cross | Gametes | Ratio |
|---|---|---|
| Aa × Aa | A, a | 1 AA : 2 Aa : 1 aa; 3 : 1 by phenotype |
| Aa × aa (test cross) | A, a / a | 1 : 1; any recessive offspring proves the parent was Aa |
| AaBb × AaBb | AB, Ab, aB, ab | 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 (= 3 : 1 × 3 : 1) |
| AaBb × aabb | four types / ab | 1 : 1 : 1 : 1 if unlinked |

**Probability.** Product rule for "and" (independent events together): P(two aa children in a row) = 1/16. Sum rule for "or" (mutually exclusive routes): P(exactly one dominant trait in a dihybrid) = 3/16 + 3/16 = 6/16. Multigene crosses: multiply monohybrid probabilities (AaBbCc self: P(aabbcc) = 1/64; P(all dominant) = 27/64). Each birth is independent; nobody is "due".

**Pedigrees.** Unaffected parents with an affected child → recessive, both carriers. Affected parents with an unaffected child → dominant, both heterozygous. Affected daughter with an unaffected father → not X-linked recessive. Work backwards from the affected (aa), then forwards with a square.

**Chi-square.** χ² = Σ (o − e)²/e, with e = ratio fraction × total (counts, never percentages); df = classes − 1; critical values at p = 0.05: df 1 = 3.84, df 2 = 5.99, df 3 = 7.81, df 4 = 9.49. Above the critical value, reject the null (the ratio does not fit: suspect linkage, lethality, epistasis). Below it, fail to reject; never "prove". Worked: 176 : 64 : 58 : 22 against 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 of 320 gives χ² = 0.62 < 7.81.

## Non-Mendelian genetics (5.4)

| Pattern | Heterozygote | Signature |
|---|---|---|
| Incomplete dominance | Intermediate (pink snapdragon) | F2 1 : 2 : 1 |
| Codominance | Both products in full (MN, AB blood, roan cattle) | F2 1 : 2 : 1 |
| Multiple alleles | Iᴬ = Iᴮ > i; rabbit C⁺ > cᶜʰ > cʰ > c | A × B parents can have an O child |
| X-linked recessive | Males hemizygous | Mostly males; carrier mothers; never father to son; affected female needs affected father |
| Linked genes | Parental classes in excess | Recombination frequency < 50%; 1% = 1 map unit |
| Epistasis | One gene masks another | 9 : 3 : 4 (mouse C over A), 12 : 3 : 1 (squash W over Y); still sums to 16 |
| Recessive lethal | Aa × Aa survivors 2 : 1 | One class missing |
| Non-nuclear | Mitochondria, chloroplasts | Follows the egg; no Mendelian ratio |

**Mapping.** Recombination frequency = recombinants ÷ total × 100; recombinants are the two small classes of a test cross. Morgan: (206 + 185) ÷ 2,300 = 17%. Order three genes by putting the largest pairwise distance on the outside: a–b 6, b–c 14, a–c 20 → a–b–c. Fifty percent means unlinked, not "different chromosome". Double crossovers make long intervals read short.

**X inactivation.** One X per cell becomes a Barr body at random; tortoiseshell cats are heterozygous females.

## Environmental effects (5.5)

Himalayan rabbit and Siamese cat: temperature-sensitive tyrosinase active below about 35 °C, so pigment on cool extremities; chill a shaved patch and it regrows dark. Hydrangea: acid soil (soluble aluminium) blue, neutral pink. Arctic fox: day length switches fur colour. Turtles and crocodilians: incubation temperature sets sex. Height: polygenic, continuous, shaped by nutrition. The proof is always the same design: clones, cuttings, twins or the same animal, environment varied, genotype held constant.

## Numbers to know

## Equations from the AP sheet that apply

χ² = Σ (o − e)²/e with the critical-value table (df = classes − 1). Recombination frequency = recombinants ÷ total × 100 (not on the sheet, but expected). Product rule P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B); sum rule P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) for exclusive events. Hardy-Weinberg (p² + 2pq + q² = 1) arrives in Unit 7 and is tested with the same chi-square.

## Traps the exam sets

- Sister chromatids separate in meiosis II, not I. Homologues are not identical; sisters are.
- No DNA replication between the two divisions. Same DNA content does not mean same ploidy.
- The exponent in 2ⁿ is the haploid number.
- Expected counts come from the hypothesis; use counts, not percentages; df = classes − 1; "fail to reject", not "prove".
- Dominant does not mean common. A 3 : 1 ratio is a probability, not a guarantee.
- A father cannot give an X-linked allele to a son. Daughters of an affected man are all carriers.
- Recombinants are the two smallest classes, whatever the question lists first.
- 50% recombination means unlinked, not a different chromosome.
- Epistasis keeps independent assortment; the ratio still sums to 16.
- The environment changes expression and enzyme activity, never the DNA sequence.
