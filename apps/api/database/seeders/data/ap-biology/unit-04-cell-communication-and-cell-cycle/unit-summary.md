# Unit 4 · Cell Communication and Cell Cycle · The night-before sheet

**Exam weight:** 10–15% of the AP exam · **CED topics:** 4.1 to 4.6 · **Big Ideas:** IST, ENE, SYI
**Built from:** OpenStax *Biology 2e* Chapter 9 (§9.1–9.4), Chapter 10 (§10.1–10.4) and §33.3 Homeostasis

## The five ideas that generate every question

1. **A signal is a shape change passed from protein to protein.** The ligand stops at the receptor; what travels inward is conformational change, phosphates and second messengers. Receptor specificity is tertiary structure; which ligands cross the membrane is permeability.
2. **Every pathway has an on switch and an off switch, and both can break.** G proteins hydrolyse GTP, phosphatases remove phosphates, phosphodiesterase destroys cAMP, pumps clear Ca²⁺, cyclins are degraded. A mutation that disables an off switch (Ras, cholera-modified G protein, non-degradable cyclin) leaves the pathway stuck on.
3. **Cascades amplify, so hormones work at nanomolar concentrations.** Each catalytic step acts on many molecules of the next; the gain is the product of the steps.
4. **Feedback is a pathway whose response acts on its own stimulus.** Negative loops stabilise a variable around a set point; positive loops drive a one-way event to completion.
5. **The cell cycle is a sequence of irreversible steps, each guarded by a checkpoint and driven by a timed kinase.** Cyclin-Cdk complexes push; p53, p21 and Rb hold; cancer is the loss of enough of those controls that division no longer waits for permission.

## Cell communication (4.1)

| Mode | Route | Distance | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct contact | gap junctions (animals), plasmodesmata (plants): cytoplasm to cytoplasm | adjacent | heart muscle contraction |
| Paracrine | diffusion through extracellular matrix; ligand degraded fast | local | growth factors |
| Synaptic | neurotransmitter across a 20–40 nm cleft; cleared by enzyme or reuptake | adjacent | nerve to muscle |
| Endocrine | hormones in blood, diluted, high-affinity receptors | whole body | insulin, estradiol |
| Autocrine | cell signals itself | self | apoptosis signals |
| Quorum sensing | bacterial autoinducers accumulate with density; threshold switches on group genes | population | *Vibrio fischeri* light, biofilms |

Yeast mating factor uses kinases and GTP-binding proteins: signalling predates multicellularity.

## Introduction to signal transduction (4.2)

**Reception → transduction → response.** Hydrophobic ligands (steroids, thyroid hormone, NO) cross the membrane to **internal receptors** that regulate transcription. Hydrophilic ligands (peptides, epinephrine, neurotransmitters) bind **surface receptors** with extracellular, transmembrane and intracellular domains.

| Surface receptor class | Structure | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Ion channel-linked | receptor is a gated pore | ligand opens it; ions flow down their gradient; milliseconds |
| G protein-coupled | seven transmembrane segments | G protein swaps GDP for GTP, splits, activates enzyme or channel; GTP hydrolysis is the off switch |
| Enzyme-linked (receptor tyrosine kinase) | kinase domain inside | ligand causes dimerisation; partners cross-phosphorylate tyrosines; phosphotyrosines are docking sites |

**Kinase** adds phosphate from ATP to Ser, Thr or Tyr; **phosphatase** removes it. Upstream is nearer the receptor; a block stops everything downstream, an added intermediate bypasses everything upstream.

## Signal transduction pathways (4.3)

| Second messenger | Made by | Acts on | Removed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| cAMP | adenylyl cyclase from ATP (G protein-activated) | protein kinase A | phosphodiesterase → AMP |
| Ca²⁺ | released from ER or outside through channels; resting level 10⁴-fold lower than stores | contraction, secretion, kinases | ATP-driven pumps |
| IP₃ and DAG | phospholipase C splits PIP₂ | IP₃ opens ER Ca²⁺ channels; DAG activates protein kinase C | metabolism |

**Epinephrine → liver:** GPCR → G protein → adenylyl cyclase → cAMP → PKA → phosphorylates the glycogen-breaking enzyme on and the glycogen-making enzyme off. The same cAMP in heart cells speeds contraction: **response depends on the receiving cell's target proteins.** Responses: enzyme activity, gene expression (freed transcription factors), protein synthesis, growth and division (growth factor → RTK → Ras → MAP kinase), apoptosis (digit webbing, self-reactive T cells, damaged cells). **Mutations:** a receptor that cannot dimerise or bind is silent; a GTPase-dead Ras (about 30% of cancers) or a receptor overexpressed like HER2 (about 25% of breast cancers) is stuck on.

## Feedback (4.4)

Receptor senses, control centre (hypothalamus, gland) compares with the **set point**, effector responds.

| Loop | Stimulus | Signal | Response | Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Glucose after a meal | high glucose | insulin | uptake and glycogen storage | negative |
| Glucose between meals | low glucose | glucagon | glycogen breakdown | negative |
| Calcium | low Ca²⁺ | PTH | absorption, bone release | negative |
| Too warm | high temperature | hypothalamus | vasodilation, sweating | negative |
| Too cold | low temperature | hypothalamus | vasoconstriction, shivering, goose bumps | negative |
| Labour | cervical stretch | oxytocin | contractions → more stretch | positive, ends at birth |
| Clotting | wound | thrombin | more thrombin, fibrin | positive, ends when sealed |
| Ripening | ethylene | ethylene | more ripening, more ethylene | positive, ends when ripe |

Fever is a raised set point, not a failed loop. Endotherms use metabolic heat; ectotherms use behaviour, same loop logic. Both insulin and glucagon are negative because the response removes the stimulus, whatever its direction.

## Cell cycle (4.5)

**Interphase** (about 90%): G₁ growth and stockpiling; S replication (each chromosome → two sister chromatids held by **cohesin**; centrosome duplicated); G₂ energy, spindle proteins. **G₀**: resting; permanent for neurons and heart muscle. Cultured human cells: 24 h, G₁ 9, S 10, G₂ 4.5, M 0.5.

| Stage | Diagnostic feature | Molecular event |
|---|---|---|
| Prophase | chromosomes condense, nucleolus gone, envelope fragments | centrosomes separate, spindle grows |
| Prometaphase | envelope gone | kinetochores form, capture microtubules from opposite poles |
| Metaphase | chromosomes on the plate | every chromatid attached; maximum condensation |
| Anaphase | chromatids move apart, cell elongates | cohesin degraded; polar microtubules slide |
| Telophase | two nuclei re-form | spindle depolymerises; chromatin decondenses |
| Cytokinesis | animal: cleavage furrow (actin ring); plant: cell plate (Golgi vesicles) | cytoplasm divides |

**Bookkeeping:** count centromeres to count chromosomes. Human G₁: 46 chromosomes, 46 chromatids. G₂ and metaphase: 46 and 92. Each daughter: 46 and 46. Mitosis keeps the chromosome number; meiosis halves it. **Compaction:** DNA around eight histones = nucleosome (7×), 30 nm fibre (50×), folded chromosome; two metres in a 10 μm nucleus. **Counting to time:** fraction of cells in a stage × cycle length = time in that stage (needs asynchronous cells).

## Regulation of the cell cycle (4.6)

| Checkpoint | Checks | Guards |
|---|---|---|
| G₁ (restriction point) | size, reserves, growth signals, DNA damage | commitment to S phase |
| G₂ | complete, undamaged replication | entry to mitosis |
| M (spindle) | every kinetochore attached to opposite poles | anaphase, which is irreversible |

**Engine:** Cdk level constant, cyclin level oscillates; Cdk active only with cyclin; complex phosphorylates targets (Rb) to pass a checkpoint; cyclin degradation ends the phase. **Brakes:** Rb binds E2F until phosphorylated; p53 on DNA damage halts (via p21 inhibiting Cdk-cyclin), repairs, or orders apoptosis; p21 enforces. **External signals:** growth factors (→ cyclin D), hormones, cell death nearby, falling SA:V push; crowding and loss of anchorage stop. **Cancer:** proto-oncogene (positive regulator) → oncogene by gain of function (stuck accelerator); tumour suppressor (p53, p21, Rb) lost by loss of function (failed brakes); mutated p53 in over half of tumours because one loss removes halt, repair and apoptosis; usually several mutations accumulate across generations of a cell that divides when it should not.

**Investigation 7:** mitotic index = cells in mitosis ÷ total. Null hypothesis: treatment has no effect, so expected counts for the treated sample come from the control's proportions. χ² = Σ (o − e)²/e; df = categories − 1; reject at p = 0.05 if χ² exceeds the critical value. Rejecting says the counts differ by more than chance; it says nothing about mechanism.

## Numbers to know

Synaptic cleft 20–40 nm · cytoplasmic Ca²⁺ about 10⁴-fold below stores · Ras mutated in about 30% of cancers · HER2 overexpressed in about 25% of breast cancers · p53 mutated in over 50% of tumours · human body cells 46 chromosomes, gametes 23 · G₁ 9 h, S 10 h, G₂ 4.5 h, M 0.5 h of 24 · nucleosome 7×, 30 nm fibre 50× · chi-square critical values at p = 0.05: df 1 → 3.84, df 2 → 5.99, df 3 → 7.81, df 4 → 9.49 · enzyme activity roughly doubles per 10 °C within range.

## Equations from the AP sheet that apply

χ² = Σ (o − e)²/e, with degrees of freedom = number of categories − 1. Amplification (not on the sheet) = product of the per-step gains. Time in a stage = fraction of cells in it × cycle length. SA:V = 3/r for a sphere (Unit 2) is the size signal at G₁.

## Traps the exam sets

- The ligand never enters the cell unless it is hydrophobic; the receptor's intracellular domain carries the message.
- A second messenger is intracellular and small; it is not a second hormone.
- Kinase adds, phosphatase removes; neither digests.
- The direction of a hormone's effect does not decide the loop type; whether the response removes the stimulus does.
- Fever is a reset set point; glucagon is negative feedback; positive loops end at a terminal state.
- Chromosome number is constant from G₁ to metaphase; chromatids double in S, chromosomes double in anaphase.
- Cytokinesis is not a stage of mitosis.
- Cdk is constant; cyclins oscillate.
- Oncogenes are gain of function; tumour suppressors fail by loss of function; a proto-oncogene is a normal gene.
- A significant chi-square rejects the null; it does not prove why.
- Cells pile up just before the step a drug blocks.

## The exam

Weeks 12 to 14, every topic 4.1 to 4.6, about 65 minutes, 49 items: multiple choice (a signalling-pathway dissection and a drug-arrest table as stimulus sets), select-all, true/false, fill-in-the-blank, two numeric items (a phase-duration calculation and a chi-square), one short free response on a feedback loop (4 points) and one long free response linking a growth-factor pathway to the cell cycle (8 points), with about a fifth of the items reviewing Units 1 to 3. For any pathway table, locate each block and reason upstream and downstream; for any feedback question, ask whether the response removes the stimulus; for any cell-cycle count, convert fractions to times and state the null before you calculate.
