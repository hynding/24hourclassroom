# Unit 1 · Chemistry of Life · The night-before sheet

**Exam weight:** 8–11% of the AP exam · **CED topics:** 1.1 to 1.7 · **Big Ideas:** SYI, ENE, IST
**Built from:** OpenStax *Biology 2e* Chapter 2 (§2.1–2.3) and Chapter 3 (§3.1–3.5)

## The five ideas that generate every question

1. **Oxygen and nitrogen pull electrons; carbon and hydrogen do not.** That gives O–H and N–H bonds partial charges (polar) and leaves C–H bonds nonpolar. Everything about water, solubility, membranes and protein folding follows.
2. **Hydrogen bonds are weak alone and strong in numbers.** They make water cohesive, give it a high specific heat and heat of vaporization, make ice float, hold DNA strands together and shape proteins, yet can be broken locally with little energy.
3. **Water excludes what it cannot bond to.** Nonpolar molecules cluster; amphipathic molecules form bilayers; nonpolar R groups bury themselves in a protein's core.
4. **One reaction builds every polymer and its reverse breaks it.** Dehydration synthesis removes water and costs energy; hydrolysis adds water and releases energy. Glycosidic, peptide and phosphodiester bonds are all the same story.
5. **Structure determines function, at every scale.** α versus β linkage decides starch versus cellulose; cis versus trans decides oil versus solid fat; R-group chemistry decides a protein's fold; base pairing decides the helix.

## Water (1.1)

| Property | Molecular cause | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Cohesion, surface tension | H-bonds between water molecules | Water column in xylem; insects on ponds |
| Adhesion, capillary action | H-bonds to polar surfaces | Water climbs narrow tubes and cell walls |
| High specific heat (1 cal/g·°C) | Heat breaks H-bonds before raising temperature | Stable body and ocean temperatures |
| High heat of vaporization (586 cal/g) | All H-bonds must break to escape | Evaporative cooling, sweating |
| Ice floats | Frozen lattice is more open than liquid | Lakes freeze from the top; life survives below |
| Universal solvent | Hydration shells around ions and polar molecules | Cytoplasm, blood, transport |

**pH = −log₁₀[H⁺].** Pure water 10⁻⁷ M, pH 7. One unit = tenfold. Acids add H⁺, bases remove it. A **buffer** (carbonic acid/bicarbonate in blood, pH 7.4) absorbs or releases H⁺ to resist change.

## Elements of life (1.2)

Body by mass: O 65%, C 18%, H 10%, N 3%. Air: N 78%, O 21%, trace C. Organisms **select** elements and spend energy rearranging them. Carbon (four bonds: chains, rings, branches) is every backbone; nitrogen is in amino groups and nucleotide bases; phosphorus in nucleic acids, ATP and phospholipid heads; sulfur in two amino acids. N₂'s triple bond makes air's nitrogen unusable without fixation.

**Functional groups:** hydroxyl, carbonyl, carboxyl (acidic), amino (basic), phosphate (acidic), sulfhydryl are polar; methyl is nonpolar. **Isomers:** structural, geometric (cis/trans), enantiomers.

## Macromolecules (1.3)

Monomer → polymer by **dehydration synthesis** (water out, energy in, enzyme). Polymer → monomer by **hydrolysis** (water in, energy out; digestion). Carbohydrates, proteins and nucleic acids are true polymers; lipids are not.

## Carbohydrates (1.4)

(CH₂O)ₙ. Glucose, fructose and galactose are C₆H₁₂O₆ isomers. Disaccharides: maltose (Glc+Glc), lactose (Glc+Gal), sucrose (Glc+Fru), all joined by **glycosidic linkages**.

| Polymer | Linkage | Shape | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amylose, amylopectin (starch) | α 1-4 (+ α 1-6 branches) | coil / branched | plant store |
| Glycogen | α 1-4, many α 1-6 | highly branched: many ends, fast release | animal store |
| Cellulose | β 1-4 | straight chains, H-bonded fibres | plant wall; humans lack cellulase |
| Chitin | β-linked N-containing glucose | straight | exoskeleton, fungal wall |

About 4 kcal/g.

## Lipids (1.5)

Hydrophobic, not polymers. **Triglyceride** = glycerol + 3 fatty acids (ester bonds); about 9 kcal/g, no bound water, so the light long-term store. **Saturated** = no C=C, straight, packs, solid. **Cis-unsaturated** = kinked, cannot pack, liquid. **Trans** = straight despite the double bond, behaves as saturated. **Phospholipid** = 2 tails + phosphate head, **amphipathic** → bilayer self-assembles in water. **Steroid** = four fused rings; cholesterol tunes membrane fluidity and is the precursor of steroid hormones and vitamin D. Waxes waterproof.

## Nucleic acids (1.6)

Nucleotide = pentose + phosphate (5′) + base (1′). **Phosphodiester bonds** (3′-OH to 5′-phosphate) make a directional backbone: 5′ end has the phosphate, 3′ end the hydroxyl. Strands are **antiparallel** and **complementary**: A–T two H-bonds, G–C three. Purine (A, G, two rings) always pairs with pyrimidine (C, T, U, one ring), so the helix is a constant width. More G–C, higher melting temperature. RNA: ribose (2′-OH), uracil, single-stranded and foldable; mRNA carries, rRNA builds and catalyses, tRNA delivers.

## Proteins (1.7)

Amino acid = α carbon + amino + carboxyl + H + **R group** (nonpolar, polar, +, −, aromatic). **Peptide bonds** (dehydration synthesis) make a polypeptide with N- and C-termini.

| Level | What | Held by |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | sequence | peptide bonds; set by the gene |
| Secondary | α helix, β sheet | H-bonds between backbone C=O and N–H |
| Tertiary | 3-D fold of one chain | R-group interactions: hydrophobic core, H-bonds, ionic bonds, disulfide bridges |
| Quaternary | several chains | the same weak interactions between subunits |

**Denaturation**: heat, pH or salt break the weak interactions; shape and function are lost, the sequence survives, refolding is sometimes possible. **Sickle cell**: glutamate → valine at β6, one residue in about 600, creates a hydrophobic patch; deoxygenated haemoglobin polymerises.

## Numbers to know

Specific heat of water 1 cal/g·°C · heat of vaporization 586 cal/g · pH 7 = 10⁻⁷ M H⁺ · blood pH 7.4 · carbohydrate 4 kcal/g, fat 9 kcal/g · A–T 2 bonds, G–C 3 · twenty amino acids, nine essential · carbon-14 half-life 5,730 years.

## Equations from the AP sheet that apply

pH = −log₁₀[H⁺]. Heat = mass × specific heat × ΔT (not on the sheet, but used in data items). Water potential comes in Unit 2.

## Traps the exam sets

- Hydrogen bonds are *between* molecules; the O–H bond *inside* water is polar covalent.
- Dehydration builds, hydrolysis breaks.
- Same formula is not the same sugar (isomers), and same monomer is not the same polymer (linkage).
- Unsaturated fats are liquid because of the *kink*, not because of "fewer hydrogens"; trans fats prove it.
- Secondary structure is backbone hydrogen bonds; tertiary is R groups.
- DNA backbones are covalent; the rungs are hydrogen bonds; the strands are antiparallel.
- Denatured is not destroyed: the primary structure survives.
- A single substitution matters according to *where* it is and *what chemistry* it changes.
