# Unit 5 Summary · Solid Geometry

**Lessons covered:** 5.1–5.18 (weeks 18–21): solids of rotation, cross sections, dilated cross sections, scaling area (5.1–5.5); scaling solids, cube roots, size in the real world, cylinder volumes, Cavalieri (5.6–5.10); prisms, pyramids, the ⅓ proofs, slant heights (5.11–5.14); all solids together, surface area and volume, density, graphing (5.15–5.18).

This is the night-before sheet. Every formula below was derived in class; on the exam you may use any of them.

## The ideas that generate every question

1. **Scaling:** lengths × k, areas × k², volumes × k³. Undo with √ (area) or ∛ (volume). Surface-to-volume ratio × 1/k.
2. **A cross section parallel to the base of a pyramid or cone is a dilation of the base from the apex**, k = (distance from apex)/height, area × k².
3. **Cavalieri's principle:** equal cross-section areas at every height ⇒ equal volumes. It makes oblique solids match right ones and makes ⅓Bh universal.
4. **V = Bh for prisms and cylinders; V = ⅓Bh for pyramids and cones** (three congruent pyramids fill a cube; Cavalieri spreads the result). Height is always perpendicular.
5. **Sphere: V = 4/3 πr³, A = 4πr²** (hemisphere = cylinder minus cone by Cavalieri). Density = mass/volume ties geometry to the real world.

## Scaling

| k | area × | volume × |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 4 | 8 |
| 3 | 9 | 27 |
| 1/2 | 1/4 | 1/8 |
| 1.5 | 2.25 | 3.375 |
| 10 | 100 | 1000 |

- Volume × 8 ⇒ k = 2 ⇒ area × 4. Area × 6.25 ⇒ k = 2.5 ⇒ volume × 15.625. Doubling a volume needs k = ∛2 ≈ 1.26.
- Weight ∝ k³, bone strength ∝ k²: big animals need thick legs. Heat loss ∝ surface, heat made ∝ volume: small animals eat more per gram.
- Doubling only a cylinder's radius: × 4. Only its height: × 2. Both: × 8.

## Formulas

| Solid | Volume | Surface area |
|---|---|---|
| prism | Bh | 2B + Ph |
| cylinder | πr²h | 2πr² + 2πrh |
| pyramid | ⅓Bh | B + ½Pℓ (regular) |
| cone | ⅓πr²h | πr² + πrℓ |
| sphere | 4/3 πr³ | 4πr² |
| hemisphere | 2/3 πr³ | 2πr² (+ πr² flat) |

Slant height of a square pyramid: ℓ² = h² + (s/2)². Edge to a corner: e² = h² + s²/2. Cone: ℓ² = h² + r². Frustum: whole minus the removed top (removed fraction = k³).

**Units:** 1 L = 1000 cm³; 1 m³ = 1000 L; 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³. Water 1 g/cm³, steel 7.85, gold 19.3, aluminium 2.7.

## Graphs of volume

- One dimension varies, the rest fixed: linear if it appears once (height), quadratic if squared (radius of a cylinder or cone), cubic if the whole solid scales (cube edge, sphere radius).
- Table test: dimension doubles, V × 2 (linear), × 4 (quadratic), × 8 (cubic).
- A cylinder fills at a steady level; an inverted cone fills fast at the bottom, slowly at the top (V ∝ d³).

## Common errors

- Area scaled by k, volume by k or k². Going backwards without a root.
- Measuring the dilation of a cross section from the base instead of the apex.
- Using a slant or tilted length as a height, or the height as a slant height.
- Forgetting the ⅓ for pyramids and cones; counting a pyramid's base twice or a prism's once.
- Using 4/3 πr³ for a hemisphere; counting glued faces in a composite surface area.
- Mixing g/cm³ with m³.

## Worked examples

**1. Scaling.** A model at 1 : 25 has surface 0.4 m² and volume 0.002 m³. Real: 0.4 × 625 = 250 m², 0.002 × 15,625 = 31.25 m³.

**2. Cross section.** Cone r = 9, h = 24, cut 8 below the apex: k = 1/3, radius 3, area 9π.

**3. Cylinder.** r = 1.2 m, h = 2.5 m: V = 3.6π ≈ 11.31 m³ ≈ 11,310 L.

**4. Pyramid.** Base 8 × 8, h = 3: V = 64; ℓ = 5; lateral 80; total 144.

**5. Cone.** r = 5, h = 12: V = 100π ≈ 314.2; ℓ = 13; lateral 65π; total 90π.

**6. Composite.** Silo r = 5, h = 12 with hemisphere: 300π + 250π/3 ≈ 1204.3.

**7. Density.** Lead sphere (11.3 g/cm³), r = 2 cm: V = 32π/3 ≈ 33.5 cm³, mass ≈ 378.7 g.

**8. Graph.** V = 6 at d = 1, 48 at d = 2, 162 at d = 3: cubic, V = 6d³.
