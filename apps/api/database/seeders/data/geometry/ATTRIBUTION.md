## What was adapted and what is original

**Adapted from IM:** the unit sequence and unit titles; the lesson sequence
and lesson titles (our topic codes are IM lesson numbers, `"6.14"` = Unit 6,
Lesson 14); the learning goals each lesson works toward, paraphrased; the
order in which ideas are introduced and proved (constructions before rigid
motions, transformations before congruence, dilations before similarity, the
altitude-to-the-hypotenuse proof of the Pythagorean Theorem, Cavalieri's
principle for volumes, the radian defined through arc length, two-way tables
before conditional probability).

**Original writing for this course:** every study guide, every unit summary,
the exam prep guide, every flashcard, every worked example and every test
item, option and rationale, including all figures, which are described in
words rather than drawn. No IM task text, problem set or lesson narrative is
reproduced; where a phrase from the curriculum is echoed it is shorter than
25 words. The quiz, exam and practice-final scenarios (the coordinates, the
measurements, the two-way tables, the games) were made up for this course
and checked by hand.

**Not used:** OpenStax (its current licence is CC BY-NC-SA with a no-LLM
clause), Khan Academy, commercial textbooks and review books, and released
exam questions from any testing body.

**Other sources:**

- Euclid, *Elements*, in the public-domain translation by Sir Thomas L.
  Heath (Cambridge University Press, 1908). Week 1 quotes Definition 15 (the
  circle) from Book I; the course elsewhere refers to Euclid's first
  proposition (the equilateral triangle on a given segment) in its own words.
- No text was taken from Wikipedia or any other encyclopaedia; the few
  historical remarks (Euclid, Cavalieri, the origin of the radian) are
  common knowledge stated in our own words.

Items that rest on no specific lesson carry `source: "general knowledge"` in
their YAML so a reviewer can find them; every other item names its IM unit
and lesson.
