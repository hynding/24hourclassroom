# Unit 1 · Introducing Multiplication · Unit summary

**Weeks:** 1 to 4
**Standards:** 3.MD.B.3 · 3.OA.A.1 · 3.OA.A.3 · 3.OA.A.4 · 3.OA.B.5 · 3.OA.D.9
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 1, in our own words

## The big ideas

1. **A picture or a line can stand for more than 1.** A picture graph's key and a bar graph's scale tell what each picture or line is worth. Count by that number.
2. **Multiplication means equal groups.** 4 × 3 means 4 groups with 3 in each group. The numbers you multiply are **factors**. The answer is the **product**.
3. **Equal groups can be drawn many ways:** circles with dots, arrays, equal jumps on a number line, tape diagrams.
4. **Story problems are about groups.** Find how many groups and how many in each group, then multiply.
5. **A missing number can be found.** In 5 × ? = 30, ask "5 groups of what make 30?"
6. **Order does not change the product.** 3 × 8 = 8 × 3. Turning an array on its side shows why.
7. **Multiplication is full of patterns.** Any number times 2 is even. The 5s end in 5 or 0. The 10s end in 0.

## Strategies to know

| Strategy | Example |
|---|---|
| Read the key or scale first | Key: one star = 5. Three stars: 5, 10, 15. |
| Subtract to compare on a graph | Dogs 20, cats 15: 20 − 15 = 5 more dogs. |
| Draw equal groups | 3 × 4: draw 3 circles with 4 dots in each. Count 12. |
| Skip-count | 6 × 5: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30. |
| Use an array | 2 rows of 7 is 2 × 7 = 14. |
| Make equal jumps | 3 jumps of 6 from 0 land on 18. |
| Find the unknown factor | 4 × ? = 32: count by 4s to 32. That is 8 counts, so ? = 8. |
| Use a turn-around fact | Know 2 × 9 = 18, so 9 × 2 = 18. |
| Look for a pattern | 7, 14, 21, 28: add 7 each time. Next is 35. |

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Key | The part of a picture graph that tells what one picture stands for. |
| Scale | The numbers along the side of a bar graph. |
| Equal groups | Groups that all have the same number of things. |
| Factor | A number you multiply. |
| Product | The answer to a multiplication. |
| Array | Things set out in equal rows and equal columns. |
| Unknown | A number we do not know yet, written as ? or a box. |
| Turn-around fact | The same two factors in the other order, like 4 × 5 and 5 × 4. |
| Even, odd | Even numbers split into 2 equal groups. Odd numbers have 1 left over. |

## Watch out for

- **Counting pictures instead of what they stand for.** If one circle = 2 and a row has 4 circles, the row shows 8, not 4.
- **Counting lines instead of reading the scale.** A bar three lines up on a scale of 5s shows 15, not 3.
- **Adding the two numbers in a story.** 3 bags of 6 is 3 × 6 = 18, not 3 + 6 = 9.
- **Counting the start on a number line.** 0 is where you start. Count the jumps, not the numbers you land on.
- **Subtracting to find an unknown factor.** For 4 × ? = 24 the answer is not 20. Check: 4 × 6 = 24, so ? = 6.
- **Thinking the order changes the product.** 8 × 3 and 3 × 8 are both 24.

## Quick practice (answers at the end)

1. One apple = 10 votes. A row has 4 apples. How many votes?
2. A bar graph counts by 2s. A bar stops at the 6th line above 0. What does it show?
3. Write a multiplication for 7 bikes with 2 wheels each, and find the product.
4. An array has 3 rows of 9. How many in all?
5. Solve ? × 5 = 45.
6. Write the turn-around fact for 6 × 7 = 42.
7. Is 5 × 9 even or odd?

*Answers:* (1) 40 votes. (2) 12. (3) 7 × 2 = 14 wheels. (4) 3 × 9 = 27. (5) 9, because 9 groups of 5 make 45. (6) 7 × 6 = 42. (7) Odd: 45, and odd times odd is odd.
