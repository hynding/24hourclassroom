# Unit 7 · Two-dimensional Shapes and Perimeter · Unit summary

**Weeks:** 25 to 28
**Standards:** 3.G.A.1 · 3.MD.D.8 · 3.MD.C.7
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 7, in our own words

## The big ideas

1. **Shapes are sorted by attributes.** Count the sides, compare the side lengths and check the corners for right angles. Color, size and which way a shape points do not change its name.
2. **Special quadrilaterals have rules.** A quadrilateral has 4 sides. A rhombus has 4 equal sides. A rectangle has 4 right angles. A square has both, so every square is also a rhombus and a rectangle. Not every rectangle or rhombus is a square.
3. **Perimeter is the distance around.** Add every side length, or count unit lengths along the outside edge on a grid. It is measured in units like cm, m, in or ft.
4. **A missing side can be found.** Add the sides you know and subtract from the perimeter. On a plan with square corners, short sides add up to the long side across from them.
5. **Perimeter and area are different.** Rectangles with the same perimeter can cover different areas, and rectangles with the same area can have different perimeters. The rectangle closest to a square has the most area for its perimeter and the least perimeter for its area.

## Strategies at a glance

| Strategy | When to use it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Check attributes | Naming a shape | 4 sides of 5 cm, no right angles: rhombus |
| Add every side | Perimeter of any polygon | Sides 3, 6, 4, 5 m: 3 + 6 + 4 + 5 = 18 m |
| Double length + width | Perimeter of a rectangle | 7 by 2: 7 + 2 = 9, 9 + 9 = 18 |
| Multiply a side by 4 | Perimeter of a square or rhombus | Side 8 in: 4 × 8 = 32 in |
| Subtract the known sides | One side is missing | Perimeter 22, sides 5 + 6 + 4 = 15, missing 7 |
| Share what is left | Rectangle, one side known | Perimeter 18, side 5: 18 − 10 = 8, 8 ÷ 2 = 4 |
| Balance opposite sides | Hidden sides on a plan | Bottom 11, top part 7: hidden part 4 |
| Split into rectangles | Area of an L-shape | 5 × 3 + 2 × 2 = 15 + 4 = 19 square units |
| List in order | All rectangles for a perimeter or area | Perimeter 12: 1 by 5, 2 by 4, 3 by 3 |

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Attribute | A feature you can check, like the number of sides or right angles. |
| Right angle | A square corner. |
| Quadrilateral | A polygon with exactly 4 sides. |
| Rhombus | A quadrilateral with 4 sides of the same length. |
| Rectangle | A quadrilateral with 4 right angles. |
| Square | A quadrilateral with 4 equal sides and 4 right angles. |
| Perimeter | The total length of all the sides of a shape. |
| Area | How many unit squares cover a shape. |

## Worked example: one plan, three questions

A reading nook is a big rectangle with one corner cut out. All corners are square corners. The bottom is 7 ft, the left side is 6 ft, the top is 4 ft and the short right side is 2 ft.

- **Hidden sides.** Across: 7 − 4 = 3 ft. Up: 6 − 2 = 4 ft.
- **Perimeter.** 7 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 4 + 6 = 26 ft.
- **Area.** Split it into a 4 by 6 rectangle (24 square feet) and a 3 by 2 rectangle (6 square feet). 24 + 6 = 30 square feet. Not 7 × 6 = 42: that counts the cut-out corner too.

## Watch out for

- Calling a shape by only one name. A square is also a rectangle, a rhombus and a quadrilateral.
- Thinking a rhombus must have right angles, or that a rectangle must have equal sides.
- Mixing up side length with number of sides: a rhombus with 6 cm sides is not a hexagon.
- Counting squares inside a shape when the question asks for perimeter.
- Adding only the labeled sides of a rectangle. All four sides count.
- Taking away only one side when finding the missing side of a rectangle.
- Thinking the same perimeter means the same area.
- Writing perimeter in square units, or area in plain units.

## Quick practice (answers at the end)

1. A shape has 4 right angles and sides of 9 cm, 4 cm, 9 cm and 4 cm. Name it.
2. True or false: every rhombus is a square.
3. What is the perimeter of a square with 9 m sides?
4. A triangle has a perimeter of 30 cm. Two sides are 8 cm and 12 cm. How long is the third side?
5. A rectangle has a perimeter of 22 in. One side is 8 in. How long is the side next to it?
6. List the rectangles with whole-number sides and an area of 10 square units. Which has the smaller perimeter?

*Answers:* (1) A rectangle (not a square). (2) False: a rhombus needs 4 equal sides, but only a rhombus with 4 right angles is a square. (3) 4 × 9 = 36 m. (4) 30 − 20 = 10 cm. (5) 22 − 16 = 6, 6 ÷ 2 = 3 in. (6) 1 by 10 (perimeter 22) and 2 by 5 (perimeter 14); the 2 by 5 rectangle.
