## The big ideas

1. **Every fact can be built from one you know.** If 7 × 8 does not come to mind, split it: 5 × 8 = 40 and 2 × 8 = 16, so 7 × 8 = 56. Doubling, adding one more group and using a ten all work too.
2. **Division is a missing factor.** 56 ÷ 7 asks "7 times what is 56?" Since 7 × 8 = 56, the answer is 8. Three numbers make a fact family of four facts.
3. **Tens behave like ones.** 6 × 40 is 6 × 4 tens = 24 tens = 240. Say the word "tens" out loud so you do not lose the zero.
4. **Place value makes big numbers easy.** Add or subtract hundreds, tens and ones. Regroup when a place gets 10 or more, or when the top digit is too small.
5. **Patterns help you check.** Odd + odd is even. A product with an even factor is even. If your answer breaks a pattern, look again.
6. **Two-step problems need a plan.** Read, draw, write an equation with a letter for the unknown, solve, and check with an estimate.

## Strategies at a glance

| Strategy | When to use it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Add one more group | A fact just past one you know | 6 × 7 = 5 × 7 + 7 = 35 + 7 = 42 |
| Double | One factor is 4, 6 or 8 | 8 × 6 is double 4 × 6: 24 + 24 = 48 |
| Use a ten | A 9s fact | 9 × 7 = 70 − 7 = 63 |
| Missing factor | Any division fact | 63 ÷ 9: 9 × 7 = 63, so 7 |
| Think in tens | A one-digit number times 20, 30, 40 ... | 7 × 30 = 21 tens = 210 |
| Place by place | Adding three-digit numbers | 356 + 228 = 500 + 70 + 14 = 584 |
| Count up | Subtracting, especially across zeros | 600 − 258: 2 + 40 + 300 = 342 |
| Check with the opposite | After any division or subtraction | 600 − 258 = 342, and 342 + 258 = 600 |
| Estimate | Checking a word problem | 3 × 9 + 48 is about 30 + 50 = 80 |

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Product | The answer when you multiply. |
| Quotient | The answer when you divide. |
| Fact family | The four × and ÷ facts made from the same three numbers. |
| Multiple of 10 | A number you say when you count by 10s, like 30 or 70. |
| Regroup | Trade 10 ones for 1 ten or 10 tens for 1 hundred, or the other way. |
| Even and odd | Even numbers split into 2 equal groups; odd numbers have 1 left over. |
| Estimate | A close answer with rounded numbers. |
| Two-step problem | A word problem that needs two calculations. |

## Watch out for

- **Counting the picture, not the key.** On a graph where each picture stands for 6, 4 pictures are 24, not 4.
- **Losing the tens.** 4 × 30 is 120, not 12.
- **Adding instead of multiplying.** 8 racks of 20 is 8 × 20, not 8 + 20.
- **Off by one group.** 6 × 8 is 48, and 7 × 8 is one more 8: 56. Check by counting the groups.
- **Smaller-from-bigger subtraction.** In 503 − 278 you cannot take 3 − 8 as 8 − 3. Regroup, or count up.
- **Forgetting the traded hundred.** After regrouping, the hundreds digit on top is one less.
- **Stopping after step one.** Read the question again before you write the answer.
- **Too many minutes.** 10:35 plus 40 minutes is not 10:75. An hour has 60 minutes, so it is 11:15.

## Practice set

1. 8 × 7
2. 72 ÷ 8
3. 5 × 60
4. 458 + 367
5. 800 − 526
6. Is 5 × 7 odd or even? Explain without multiplying.
7. Jonah has 5 packs of 6 cards. He gives 8 cards to a friend. Write an equation with a letter and find how many cards he has left.
8. A rectangle is 9 feet by 4 feet. Find its perimeter and its area.

*Answers:* (1) 56. (2) 9, because 8 × 9 = 72. (3) 5 × 6 tens = 30 tens = 300. (4) 825. (5) 274; check 274 + 526 = 800. (6) Odd, because both factors are odd (it is 35). (7) c = 5 × 6 − 8; 30 − 8 = 22 cards. (8) Perimeter 9 + 4 + 9 + 4 = 26 feet; area 9 × 4 = 36 square feet.
