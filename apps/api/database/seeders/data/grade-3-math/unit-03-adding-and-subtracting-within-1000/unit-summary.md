# Unit 3 · Wrapping Up Addition and Subtraction Within 1,000 · Summary

**Weeks:** 8 Adding Within 1,000 · 9 Subtracting Within 1,000 · 10 Rounding to the Nearest 10 and 100 · 11 Two-Step Problems and Answers That Make Sense
**Standards:** 3.NBT.A.2, 3.NBT.A.1, 3.OA.D.8, 3.OA.D.9 · **Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 3, in our own words

## The big ideas

1. **Place value does the work.** A three-digit number is hundreds, tens and ones. Add or subtract the same kind: hundreds with hundreds, tens with tens, ones with ones.
2. **Ten of one place make one of the next.** 10 ones make 1 ten, and 10 tens make 1 hundred. When adding, a place with 10 or more **regroups** into the next place. When subtracting, a place without enough **decomposes** a ten or a hundred to get more.
3. **Subtraction is the distance between two numbers.** You can take away, or count up from the smaller number. Addition checks subtraction, because each one undoes the other.
4. **Rounding finds the closer friendly number.** On a number line, a number is closer to one ten (or hundred) than the other. A number exactly halfway rounds up.
5. **Estimate, then check.** Rounded numbers give a quick estimate that tells you if an exact answer makes sense.
6. **Two-step problems need a plan.** Find what you need first, then use it. A tape diagram and an equation with a letter for the unknown keep the steps straight.

## Strategies with an example

| Strategy | Example |
|---|---|
| Base-ten blocks | 263 + 159: 12 cubes make a rod, 12 rods make a flat, so 4 flats, 2 rods, 2 cubes = 422 |
| Adding by place | 437 + 285 → 600 + 110 + 12 = 722 |
| Open number line, adding | 316 + 243: 316 → 516 → 556 → 559 |
| Friendly numbers | 199 + 357 → 200 + 356 = 556 |
| Standard algorithm, adding | 649 + 274: 13 ones, 12 tens, 9 hundreds → 923 |
| Decomposing to subtract | 542 − 318: 12 − 8 = 4, 3 − 1 = 2, 5 − 3 = 2 → 224 |
| Counting up | 704 − 389: 389 → 400 → 700 → 704, so 11 + 300 + 4 = 315 |
| Subtracting across zeros | 900 − 572: 8 hundreds, 9 tens, 10 ones → 328 |
| Rounding on a number line | 683 → nearest ten 680, nearest hundred 700 |
| Estimating | 512 + 279 ≈ 500 + 300 = 800 |
| Two-step with a letter | 7 bags of 5 balloons, 9 pop: 7 × 5 − 9 = b, b = 26 |

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Sum / difference | The answer to an addition / a subtraction problem. |
| Regroup | Trade 10 of one place for 1 of the next place. |
| Decompose | Break 1 ten into 10 ones, or 1 hundred into 10 tens. |
| Round | Change a number to the nearest ten or hundred. |
| Estimate | A close answer made with rounded numbers. |
| Equation | A math sentence with an equal sign. |
| Unknown | The number we do not know yet, often shown by a letter. |

## Watch out for

- **Forgetting to regroup.** 263 + 159 is not 312. The 12 ones make an extra ten and the 12 tens make an extra hundred.
- **Subtracting the wrong way.** In 542 − 318, the ones are 2 − 8, not 8 − 2. Decompose a ten first.
- **Zeros.** In 900 − 572, after trading there are 8 hundreds and 9 tens, not 9 hundreds and 10 tens.
- **Rounding twice.** 449 rounds to 400 for the nearest hundred, even though it rounds to 450 for the nearest ten.
- **Stopping after step one.** Read the question again: does it ask for the total, or what is left?
- **Trusting an answer without an estimate.** If 512 + 279 gave 691, the estimate 800 shows a mistake.

## Practice (answers at the end)

1. 346 + 287
2. 812 − 457
3. 600 − 238
4. Round 752 to the nearest ten and to the nearest hundred.
5. Estimate 389 + 304 to the nearest hundred.
6. A team has 8 boxes of 6 balls and gives away 13 balls. Write an equation and solve it.
7. Is 35 + 47 odd or even?

*Answers:* (1) 633. (2) 355. (3) 362. (4) 750 and 800. (5) 400 + 300 = 700. (6) 8 × 6 − 13 = b, so b = 35. (7) Even (82): odd + odd is even.
