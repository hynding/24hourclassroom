# Unit 2 · Area and Multiplication · Summary

## The big ideas

1. **Area is how much flat space a shape covers.** We measure it by covering the shape with same-size squares, with no gaps and no overlaps, and counting them.
2. **A unit square is 1 unit on each side and covers 1 square unit.** Areas are said in square units: square centimeters, square inches, square feet, square meters.
3. **A rectangle full of squares is an array.** Every row has the same number of squares, so the area is rows × squares in each row. That is the same as multiplying the two side lengths.
4. **A rectangle can be split.** The areas of the parts add up to the area of the whole, so 6 × 7 = 6 × 5 + 6 × 2. This helps with facts you do not know yet.
5. **Shapes made of rectangles can be split too.** Cut the shape into rectangles that do not overlap, find each area and add.

## Strategies at a glance

| Strategy | When to use it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Count the squares | A shape drawn on a grid | Rows of 2, 4, 5 and 2 squares: 2 + 4 + 5 + 2 = 13 square units |
| Put half squares together | A shape with triangles that are half a square | 6 whole squares and 2 halves: 6 + 1 = 7 square units |
| Multiply rows by squares in a row | A tiled or partly tiled rectangle | Top row of 7, left column of 5: 5 × 7 = 35 square units |
| Multiply the side lengths | A rectangle with its sides labeled | 7 feet by 3 feet: 7 × 3 = 21 square feet |
| Find a missing side | You know the area and one side | Area 30, one side 5: 5 × 6 = 30, so the other side is 6 |
| Split a factor | A fact you do not know yet | 4 × 9 = 4 × 5 + 4 × 4 = 20 + 16 = 36 |
| Split the shape and add | A shape made of rectangles | An L made of 8 by 3 and 3 by 3: 24 + 9 = 33 square units |

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Area | How much flat space a shape covers. |
| Unit square | A square 1 unit long on each side. |
| Square unit | The unit for area; one unit square covers 1 square unit. |
| Side length | How long one side of a shape is. |
| Square centimeter, square inch | Small units of area, for things like cards and stickers. |
| Square foot, square meter | Bigger units of area, for rugs, gardens and rooms. |
| Decompose | Break a number or a shape into smaller parts. |

## Watch out for

- **Counting the empty squares.** On a grid, count only the squares that belong to the shape.
- **Gaps and overlaps.** A gap makes the area too small; an overlap counts space twice and makes it too big.
- **Adding the side lengths.** A 4 by 6 rectangle is 4 × 6 = 24 square units, not 4 + 6 = 10. Adding all the sides finds the distance around, not the area.
- **Leaving off "square".** "12 centimeters" is a length. "12 square centimeters" is an area.
- **Forgetting to multiply one part of a split.** 5 × 8 is 5 × 4 + 5 × 4, not 5 × 4 + 4.

## Practice (answers at the end)

1. A shape on a grid has rows of 3, 5, 5 and 1 squares. What is its area?
2. What is the area of a rectangle 9 inches by 3 inches?
3. A rectangle has an area of 40 square meters. One side is 8 meters. How long is the other side?
4. Finish: 7 × 6 = 7 × 5 + 7 × ___. Then find 7 × 6.
5. A shape is made of a 6 by 2 rectangle and a 3 by 5 rectangle that do not overlap. What is its area?
6. Which is bigger: a 4 by 8 rectangle or a 5 by 6 rectangle? By how much?

*Answers:* (1) 3 + 5 + 5 + 1 = 14 square units. (2) 9 × 3 = 27 square inches. (3) 8 × 5 = 40, so 5 meters. (4) 1; 35 + 7 = 42. (5) 12 + 15 = 27 square units. (6) 4 × 8 = 32 and 5 × 6 = 30, so the 4 by 8 rectangle, by 2 square units.
