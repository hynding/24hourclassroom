# Grade 3 Math · Year Review Guide

**Built from:** the eight units of this course (IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Units 1–8, in our own words) · **Use with:** the eight unit summaries and the End-of-Year Check (week 33)

You learned a lot of math this year. This guide walks through each unit, tells you how the End-of-Year Check is set up, and gives you a two-week plan to get ready. Read it with a grown-up if you can.

## How the End-of-Year Check works

The check has 37 questions and takes about 75 minutes. The questions go in course order: Unit 1 first, Unit 8 last. Bigger units have more questions.

| Unit | Questions | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Introducing Multiplication | 5 | Scaled graphs, equal groups, arrays, the order of factors, missing numbers |
| 2 · Area and Multiplication | 4 | Unit squares, area of rectangles, splitting a rectangle |
| 3 · Adding and Subtracting Within 1,000 | 5 | Adding and subtracting with regrouping, rounding, two-step problems |
| 4 · Relating Multiplication to Division | 7 | Sharing and grouping, fact families, multiples of 10, word problems |
| 5 · Fractions as Numbers | 5 | Unit fractions, fractions on a number line, equal fractions, comparing |
| 6 · Measuring Length, Time, Liquid Volume, and Weight | 5 | Time to the minute, elapsed time, grams, kilograms, liters, half and quarter inches |
| 7 · Two-dimensional Shapes and Perimeter | 4 | Quadrilaterals, perimeter, unknown sides, area and perimeter together |
| 8 · Putting It All Together | 2 | Fact fluency and a two-step problem |

**Kinds of questions.** Most questions are multiple choice or ask for a number. There are also true/false questions, fill-in-a-word questions, one select-all question (choose every right answer) and two short answers. Five questions have a picture, such as a bar graph, a clock or a number line. Three pairs of questions share one picture, so read the picture carefully the first time.

**Points.** Most questions are worth 1 point. The select-all question is worth 2 points, and you get part of the points for part of the right answers. Each short answer is worth 3 points, one for each part. The check is out of 42 points. Your teacher checks the short answers, so show your work in words, numbers or a drawing.

## What each unit asks you to do

**Unit 1 · Introducing Multiplication.** Read a picture graph where one picture stands for 2, 5 or 10, and a bar graph whose scale counts by 2s or 5s. Remember: count what each picture stands for, not the pictures. See multiplication as equal groups and arrays: 4 bags of 6 oranges is 4 × 6. The order of the factors does not change the product. Find a missing number in an equation like 6 × ___ = 42.

**Unit 2 · Area and Multiplication.** Area is how many unit squares cover a shape with no gaps or overlaps. For a rectangle, multiply the side lengths: 6 cm by 5 cm is 30 square centimeters. A big rectangle can be split into two smaller ones, so 7 × 6 = (5 × 6) + (2 × 6).

**Unit 3 · Adding and Subtracting Within 1,000.** Add and subtract three-digit numbers by place value. Regroup 10 ones as a ten or 10 tens as a hundred. Subtract by counting up or by trading. Round to the nearest ten or hundred to estimate: 362 rounds to 360 to the nearest ten, and 851 rounds to 900 to the nearest hundred. Solve two-step problems and ask whether the answer is reasonable.

**Unit 4 · Relating Multiplication to Division.** Division shares into equal groups or makes groups of a size. Every division fact has a multiplication partner: 56 ÷ 8 = 7 because 8 × 7 = 56. Multiply by multiples of 10: 6 × 30 = 18 tens = 180. Solve word problems with equal groups, arrays and measurements.

**Unit 5 · Fractions as Numbers.** A fraction names equal parts of one whole. The denominator (bottom) tells how many equal parts make the whole; the numerator (top) tells how many parts you count. Find fractions on a number line by counting spaces, not tick marks. Some fractions are equal, like 1/2 = 2/4 = 4/8, and whole numbers can be fractions: 3 = 6/2. With the same numerator, more parts means smaller parts, so 1/4 is more than 1/8.

**Unit 6 · Measuring.** Tell time to the minute: the short hand is the hour and the long hand counts by 5s for each number. Find elapsed time by jumping to the next hour, and remember an hour is 60 minutes. Estimate mass in grams and kilograms and liquid volume in liters. Measure lengths to the nearest half or quarter inch and read line plots.

**Unit 7 · Shapes and Perimeter.** Sort shapes by sides and corners. A square is a rectangle and also a rhombus. Perimeter is the distance around a shape: add all the sides. Find an unknown side when you know the perimeter. Two rectangles with the same area can have different perimeters.

**Unit 8 · Putting It All Together.** Know your facts within 100, build any fact you forget from one you know, add and subtract within 1,000, and solve two-step problems with a letter for the unknown.

## A two-week practice plan

Each day takes about 20 to 30 minutes. Read that unit's summary first, then do its practice set, then look back at quiz questions you missed during the year.

| Day | Focus | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Unit 1 | Read two graphs out loud using the key. Write 5 multiplication facts as equal groups and as arrays. |
| 2 | Unit 2 | Find the area of 4 rectangles. Split one rectangle into two parts and add the areas. |
| 3 | Unit 3, adding | Add 5 pairs of three-digit numbers. Circle every place where you regrouped. |
| 4 | Unit 3, subtracting and rounding | Subtract 5 problems, 2 by counting up. Round 6 numbers to the nearest 10 and 100. |
| 5 | Unit 4, facts | Write 4 fact families. Play a card game: flip two cards and multiply. |
| 6 | Unit 4, problems | Solve 4 word problems, 2 multiplication and 2 division. Do 5 multiples-of-10 facts. |
| 7 | Rest, or redo your hardest day | Look back at the mistakes from days 1 to 6. |
| 8 | Unit 5, fractions | Fold paper strips into halves, fourths and eighths. Mark 5 fractions on a number line. |
| 9 | Unit 5, equal and compare | Find 3 fractions equal to 1/2. Compare 4 pairs of fractions and explain. |
| 10 | Unit 6, time | Read 5 clocks at home. Find 3 elapsed times, like the start and end of a show. |
| 11 | Unit 6, measuring | Weigh or hold things to estimate grams and kilograms. Measure 5 objects to the nearest half inch. |
| 12 | Unit 7 | Draw 3 quadrilaterals and name them. Find 3 perimeters and one unknown side. |
| 13 | Unit 8 | Make up 3 two-step stories, solve them, and check each with an estimate. |
| 14 | End-of-Year Check | Take the check in one sitting or in two halves. Then go over every question you missed. |

## Tips for the day of the check

- **Read the question twice.** Underline what it asks you to find.
- **Look at the key or scale** before you read a graph.
- **Draw a picture** when a problem feels hard: an array, a tape diagram or a number line.
- **Check with the opposite operation.** Check subtraction with addition and division with multiplication.
- **Estimate.** If your answer is far from your estimate, look again.
- **Label your answers** in the short answers: 35 cards, 7 bags, 30 square centimeters.
- **Do not leave a question blank.** If you are stuck, make your best try and come back to it later.

## For grown-ups at home

Short, friendly practice every day helps more than one long session. Let your child explain how they got an answer, even when it is right. Praise the strategy, not just the answer. Cooking, shopping, clocks and car trips all have math in them: "We need 3 bags of 6 rolls. How many rolls is that?" If a topic is still hard, the week's guide and flashcards from that unit are a good place to start again.
