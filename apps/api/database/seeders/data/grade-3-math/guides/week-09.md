# Week 09 · Subtracting Within 1,000

**Standard:** 3.NBT.A.2 Add and subtract fluently within 1,000
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 3, Section B, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Subtract hundreds from hundreds, tens from tens and ones from ones.
- **Decompose** a ten or a hundred when a place does not have enough.
- Count up or count back on an open number line.
- Subtract from numbers with zeros, like 500 or 800.
- Check a subtraction with addition.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Difference | The answer to a subtraction problem. |
| Decompose | Break a bigger unit into smaller ones: 1 ten into 10 ones, or 1 hundred into 10 tens. |
| Count up | Start at the smaller number and jump to the bigger one; the jumps add to the difference. |
| Count back | Start at the bigger number and jump back by the number you take away. |

## Taking away by place

When every top digit is big enough, take away place by place:

789 − 352 → 700 − 300 = 400, 80 − 50 = 30, 9 − 2 = 7. The difference is **437**.

## When a place does not have enough

Show 352 with blocks: 3 flats, 5 rods and 2 cubes. Now take away 128. You need to take away 8 cubes, but there are only 2.

So **decompose** a ten: break 1 rod into 10 cubes. Now there are 3 flats, 4 rods and 12 cubes.

- Cubes: 12 − 8 = 4.
- Rods: 4 − 2 = 2.
- Flats: 3 − 1 = 2.

352 − 128 = **224**.

Sometimes you need to decompose a hundred too. For 425 − 160, there are 2 tens but you need to take 6 tens, so break 1 hundred into 10 tens: 3 hundreds and 12 tens. 12 − 6 = 6 tens, and 3 − 1 = 2 hundreds. The answer is 265.

**Watch out.** A very common mistake is to take the smaller digit from the bigger one in every place. For 463 − 128, some students do 8 − 3 = 5 in the ones and get 345. But you are taking 8 *away from* 3, so you must decompose a ten first. The real answer is 335.

## Subtracting across zeros

For 700 − 254 there are no tens and no ones to break. Trade 1 hundred for 10 tens, then trade 1 of those tens for 10 ones. Now 700 is 6 hundreds, 9 tens and 10 ones:

10 − 4 = 6, 9 − 5 = 4, 6 − 2 = 4. So 700 − 254 = **446**.

## Counting up and counting back

Subtraction finds how far apart two numbers are, so you can count up on an open number line.

**Try it.** 614 − 375. Start at 375. Jump 25 to 400, then 200 to 600, then 14 to 614. The jumps add to 25 + 200 + 14 = **239**.

You can also count back. For 634 − 210, start at 634, jump back 200 to 434, then back 10 to 424.

A shortcut for 99: subtract 100, then add 1 back. 512 − 99 = 412 + 1 = 413.

## Checking with addition

Addition and subtraction undo each other. To check 800 − 425 = 375, add 375 + 425. If you get 800, your answer is right.

## For grown-ups at home

Your child may subtract in ways that look new: counting up on a number line, or crossing out and rewriting digits to show a decomposed ten. All of them rest on the same idea, that 1 hundred is 10 tens and 1 ten is 10 ones. If your child gets stuck, ask "Are there enough ones to take away?" Change from a purchase or pages left in a book are good real examples.

## Check yourself

1. What is 968 − 523?
2. What is 900 − 461?
3. Count up to find 403 − 278.
4. Check: is 641 − 275 = 366 correct?

*Answers:* (1) 445. (2) 439. (3) 2 + 20 + 103 = 125. (4) Yes, because 366 + 275 = 641.
