# Week 05 · What Is Area? Covering Shapes With Unit Squares

**Standards:** 3.MD.C.5 Understand area and unit squares · 3.MD.C.6 Measure area by counting unit squares
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 2, Section A, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Say what **area** means: how much flat space a shape covers.
- Measure area by covering a shape with **unit squares**, with no gaps and no overlaps.
- Count square units on a grid, including half squares.
- Name an area in **square units**, such as square centimeters or square inches.
- Compare the areas of two shapes.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Area | How much flat space a shape covers. |
| Unit square | A square that is 1 unit long on each side. |
| Square unit | The unit we use to say an area. One unit square covers 1 square unit. |
| Tiling | Covering a shape with same-size tiles, edge to edge. |
| Gap | A space you did not cover. |
| Overlap | Two tiles covering the same space. |

## Area is about covering

Think about a placemat on a table. The placemat covers part of the table. **Area** tells how much space it covers.

To measure area, we cover the shape with tiles that are all the same size. Squares work best, because they fit together with no spaces between them.

Two rules keep the count fair:

1. **No gaps.** Every bit of the shape must be covered. A gap would not get counted, so the area would come out too small.
2. **No overlaps.** Tiles must not sit on top of each other. An overlap would be counted twice, so the area would come out too big.

**Try it.** Sam covers a book with 2 rows of 4 square sticky notes, with no gaps or overlaps. The area of the book is 4 + 4 = 8 sticky notes.

## Unit squares and square units

A **unit square** is 1 unit long on each side. It covers **1 square unit**. If a shape is covered by 10 unit squares, its area is 10 square units.

Some unit squares have special names:

- A **square centimeter** is 1 centimeter on each side. It is about as big as the nail on your smallest finger.
- A **square inch** is 1 inch on each side. It is a little bigger, about the size of a stamp.

Be careful with the words. "9 centimeters" is a length: how long something is. "9 square centimeters" is an area: how much space something covers.

## Counting squares on a grid

When a shape is drawn on a grid, count only the squares that are part of the shape. The empty squares around it do not count.

A tidy way to count is one row at a time. Write the number for each row, then add. A shape with rows of 3, 5 and 2 squares has an area of 3 + 5 + 2 = 10 square units.

Sometimes a shape has **half squares**. A square cut from corner to corner makes two triangles, and each triangle is half of the square. Two half squares make 1 whole square unit.

**Try it.** A shape has 4 whole squares and 2 half squares. The two halves make 1 more square, so the area is 4 + 1 = 5 square units.

## Comparing areas

To compare two shapes, count the squares in each one. A long, thin shape and a short, wide shape might look very different, but if both cover 12 squares, they have the **same area**.

The size of the tile matters too. If you cover the same desk with big tiles and then with small tiles, you need more small tiles. The desk did not change size. You just used a smaller unit.

A common mistake is to count every square on the grid, or to forget a row. Point to each square as you count it, and check your rows add up.

## For grown-ups at home

This week starts a unit that ties area to multiplication. For now, children are learning what area is by covering and counting. Let your child cover a cereal box side or a notebook with sticky notes or paper squares and count them. Ask, "Did we leave any gaps? Did any squares overlap?" Then try bigger squares and ask whether you will need more or fewer.

## Check yourself

1. A shape on a grid has rows of 4, 4 and 3 squares. What is its area?
2. A shape has 6 whole squares and 4 half squares. What is its area?
3. Is "15 inches" an area? Why or why not?

*Answers:* (1) 4 + 4 + 3 = 11 square units. (2) Four halves make 2 whole squares, so 6 + 2 = 8 square units. (3) No. Inches measure length; an area would be in square inches.
