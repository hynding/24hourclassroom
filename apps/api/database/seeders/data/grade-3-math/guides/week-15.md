# Week 15 · Dividing Larger Numbers Using Facts You Know

**Standard:** 3.OA.C.7 Multiply and divide fluently within 100
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 4, Section D, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Use multiplication facts to divide quickly.
- Divide a number like 48 by sharing its **tens** and **ones**.
- Split a total into **friendly parts** to divide numbers like 52 or 84.
- Skip-count on a number line, and check every answer with multiplication.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Quotient | The answer to a division. |
| Divide | Split a total into equal groups. |
| Friendly parts | Parts of a number that are easy to divide, like 40 and 12 for 52 ÷ 4. |
| Area diagram | A rectangle where the area is the total and the sides are the factors. |

## Use the facts you know

Every division fact has a multiplication partner. If you know 7 × 8 = 56, you also know 56 ÷ 7 = 8 and 56 ÷ 8 = 7. The more multiplication facts you know, the faster you can divide.

**Try it.** 72 ÷ 8. Think "8 times what is 72?" 8 × 9 = 72, so the answer is 9.

## Share the tens, then the ones

Hana has 48 beads to share equally into 4 bags. 48 is 4 tens and 8 ones.

- Share the tens: 4 tens into 4 bags is 1 ten in each bag.
- Share the ones: 8 ones into 4 bags is 2 ones in each bag.

Each bag gets 1 ten and 2 ones, which is **12 beads**. So 48 ÷ 4 = 12.

**Try it.** 66 ÷ 3. 6 tens ÷ 3 = 2 tens. 6 ones ÷ 3 = 2 ones. The answer is 22.

## Split into friendly parts

Some numbers do not share so neatly. Try 52 ÷ 4. 5 tens cannot be shared equally into 4 groups, so choose different parts.

Think of a rectangle with an area of 52 and one side of 4. We want the other side. Cut the rectangle into a part we know and a part that is left:

- 4 × 10 = 40, so one part has an area of 40 and a top side of 10.
- 52 − 40 = 12 is left. 4 × 3 = 12, so the other part has a top side of 3.

The whole top side is 10 + 3 = **13**. So 52 ÷ 4 = 13.

**Try it.** 75 ÷ 5. Split 75 into 50 and 25. 50 ÷ 5 = 10 and 25 ÷ 5 = 5. 10 + 5 = 15.

Good friendly parts are often 10 times the number you divide by: 40 when dividing by 4, 50 when dividing by 5, 60 when dividing by 6.

## Skip-count on a number line

To find 54 ÷ 6, start at 0 and jump by 6: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42, 48, 54. Count the jumps. There are 9, so 54 ÷ 6 = 9.

## A common mistake

On a number line, count the **jumps**, not the tick marks. From 0 to 54 by 6s there are 10 tick marks (counting 0) but only 9 jumps. Put your finger on 0 and say "one" only when you land on 6.

## Check with multiplication

Always multiply back. Is 84 ÷ 4 = 21? 4 × 20 = 80 and 4 × 1 = 4, so 4 × 21 = 84. Yes!

## For grown-ups at home

Your child now has three ways to divide: recall a fact, split the number into parts, or skip-count. Ask which way they chose and why; there is no single right path. Keep practising multiplication facts a few minutes a day, because quick facts make division quick. A deck of cards works well: flip two cards, multiply them, then say the two division facts that go with it.

## Check yourself

1. 63 ÷ 7 = ?
2. Use tens and ones: 69 ÷ 3 = ?
3. Use friendly parts: 96 ÷ 8 = ?
4. How many jumps of 7 go from 0 to 42?

*Answers:* (1) 9, because 7 × 9 = 63. (2) 6 tens ÷ 3 = 2 tens and 9 ones ÷ 3 = 3 ones, so 23. (3) 96 = 80 + 16; 80 ÷ 8 = 10 and 16 ÷ 8 = 2, so 12. (4) 6 jumps, so 42 ÷ 7 = 6.
