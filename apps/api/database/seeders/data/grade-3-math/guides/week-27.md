# Week 27 · Same Perimeter, Different Area (and the Other Way Around)

**Standards:** 3.MD.D.8 Solve problems about perimeter · 3.MD.C.7 Connect area to multiplication and addition
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 7, Section C, in our own words

## What you will learn

- See that rectangles with the **same perimeter** can cover **different areas**.
- See that rectangles with the **same area** can have **different perimeters**.
- List every rectangle with whole-number sides for a given perimeter or area.
- Pick the rectangle with the most room, or the shortest fence.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Perimeter | The length all the way around a shape, in units like cm or ft. |
| Area | How many unit squares cover a shape, in square units. |
| Square unit | A square that is 1 unit long on each side. |
| Factor pair | Two numbers that multiply to make a number, like 3 and 4 for 12. |

## Same perimeter, different area

Take a piece of string 14 cm long and make rectangles with it. Every rectangle uses the whole string, so every one has a perimeter of 14 cm. But they do not cover the same space.

Half the perimeter is one length plus one width. Half of 14 is 7, so the length and width must add to 7:

| Rectangle | Perimeter | Area |
|---|---|---|
| 1 cm by 6 cm | 14 cm | 6 square cm |
| 2 cm by 5 cm | 14 cm | 10 square cm |
| 3 cm by 4 cm | 14 cm | 12 square cm |

The long, skinny rectangle covers the least. The 3 by 4 rectangle covers the most. With the same perimeter, the rectangle closest to a square has the most area.

**Try it.** Lena has 16 m of fence for a garden. Length + width = 8. A 7 by 1 garden covers 7 square meters, a 5 by 3 garden covers 15, and a 4 by 4 garden covers 16. The 4 by 4 square gives the most room.

## Same area, different perimeter

Now take 18 square tiles and make rectangles that use all of them. Every rectangle has an area of 18 square units. The sides must be a **factor pair** of 18:

| Rectangle | Area | Perimeter |
|---|---|---|
| 1 by 18 | 18 square units | 38 units |
| 2 by 9 | 18 square units | 22 units |
| 3 by 6 | 18 square units | 18 units |

This time the long, skinny rectangle has the **greatest** perimeter, and the one closest to a square has the least. That matters if you are buying a border: the 3 by 6 rectangle needs the least trim.

A 3 by 6 rectangle and a 6 by 3 rectangle are the same rectangle turned on its side, so they count once in a list.

## Making a list without missing any

Work in order so you do not skip one.

- **For a perimeter:** halve it, then start the width at 1 and count up. Perimeter 16 means length + width = 8: 1 and 7, 2 and 6, 3 and 5, 4 and 4. Stop when the pairs start to repeat.
- **For an area:** find the factor pairs. Area 20: 1 × 20, 2 × 10, 4 × 5. (3 does not divide 20 evenly.)

A common mistake is to think that the same perimeter means the same area. It does not. Always work out both: multiply for area, add the four sides for perimeter.

Another mistake is to use half the perimeter as one side. A perimeter of 14 does not mean a 7 by 7 square: that square goes 28 units around.

## For grown-ups at home

Give your child 12 or 24 small square crackers, coins or sticky notes and ask for every rectangle that uses all of them. For each one, count the squares (area) and count the edges around the outside (perimeter). Then use a piece of string tied in a loop and stretch it into different rectangles: the string never changes length, but the space inside does. Talking about "fence" and "grass" helps keep the two ideas apart.

## Check yourself

1. List the rectangles with whole-number sides and a perimeter of 10 units. Which has the most area?
2. List the rectangles with an area of 8 square units. Which has the greatest perimeter?
3. True or false: a 6 by 2 rectangle and a 4 by 4 square have the same perimeter.

*Answers:* (1) 1 by 4 (area 4) and 2 by 3 (area 6); the 2 by 3 rectangle has the most area. (2) 1 by 8 (perimeter 18) and 2 by 4 (perimeter 12); the 1 by 8 rectangle has the greatest perimeter. (3) True: both are 16 units around, but their areas are 12 and 16 square units.
