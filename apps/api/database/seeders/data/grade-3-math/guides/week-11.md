# Week 11 · Two-Step Problems and Answers That Make Sense

**Standards:** 3.OA.D.8 Solve two-step word problems with the four operations · 3.OA.D.9 Find and explain patterns in numbers
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 3, Section D, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Solve story problems that take **two steps**.
- Use a **tape diagram** to see how the numbers fit together.
- Write an **equation** with a letter for the unknown number.
- Use an estimate to decide if an answer is **reasonable**.
- Spot patterns with odd and even numbers.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Two-step problem | A story problem that needs two calculations to answer. |
| Equation | A math sentence with an equal sign, like 3 × 8 + 5 = 29. |
| Unknown | The number we do not know yet. A letter, like n, can stand for it. |
| Reasonable | Makes sense; close to what an estimate says it should be. |
| Even / odd | Even numbers split into pairs with none left; odd numbers have 1 left over. |

## Two steps, one at a time

Read the story and ask: what do I need to find **first**?

A class wants to read 400 pages. They read 135 pages on Monday and 170 on Tuesday. How many more pages do they need to read?

- Step 1: How many so far? 135 + 170 = 305.
- Step 2: How many more? 400 − 305 = 95.

A tape diagram shows this well. Draw one long bar for 400. Split it into parts for 135, 170 and the unknown part. Call the unknown part **p**. The equation is 135 + 170 + p = 400, and p = 95.

## Mixing multiplication with adding or subtracting

Juice boxes come in 4 packs of 6, and there are 3 loose ones. How many juice boxes?

- Step 1: 4 × 6 = 24 in packs.
- Step 2: 24 + 3 = 27 juice boxes.

As one equation: 4 × 6 + 3 = j, so j = 27. When an equation has multiplication and addition with no parentheses, **multiply first**. If there are parentheses, do what is inside them first: (20 − 5) × 2 = 15 × 2 = 30.

**Try it.** Leo has 5 boxes of 8 crayons and gives away 12. Step 1: 5 × 8 = 40. Step 2: 40 − 12 = 28 crayons.

## Is my answer reasonable?

After solving, estimate to see if your answer is close.

A park has 615 tulips, and 278 are picked for a festival. Sam says 437 are left. Estimate: 600 − 300 = 300. 437 is far from 300, so something went wrong. The exact answer is 337.

**Watch out.** A common mistake is to stop after the first step. In the story "a farm picked 226 peaches in the morning and 348 in the afternoon, then sold 300", 574 is only how many were picked. The question asks how many are **left**: 574 − 300 = 274.

## Odd and even patterns

- Even + even = even (14 + 6 = 20).
- Odd + odd = even (7 + 5 = 12). The two leftover ones make a pair.
- Even + odd = odd (8 + 3 = 11).

Counting patterns help too. In 208, 218, 228, 238, each number is 10 more, so the tens digit goes up by 1 and the next number is 248.

## For grown-ups at home

Two-step problems ask children to plan, not just compute. Encourage your child to say the first question out loud ("First I need to know how many they made"), draw a quick bar, and then check with a rounded estimate. Everyday stories work well: snacks bought and eaten, pages read over two days, seats left on a bus.

## Check yourself

1. 3 shelves hold 7 books each, and a teacher adds 10 more books. How many books?
2. Find n: 245 + n = 400.
3. Is 27 + 15 odd or even?
4. Is 198 + 305 = 803 reasonable?

*Answers:* (1) 3 × 7 + 10 = 31 books. (2) n = 155. (3) Even (42). (4) No: 200 + 300 = 500, so the sum should be near 500; it is 503.
