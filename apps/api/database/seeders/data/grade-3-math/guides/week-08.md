# Week 08 · Adding Within 1,000 Using Place Value

**Standard:** 3.NBT.A.2 Add and subtract fluently within 1,000
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 3, Section A, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Show three-digit numbers with **base-ten blocks** and in **expanded form**.
- Add hundreds to hundreds, tens to tens and ones to ones.
- **Regroup** 10 ones as 1 ten, and 10 tens as 1 hundred.
- Add with jumps on an open number line and with friendly-number shortcuts.
- Write addition in columns, the standard way.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Place value | What a digit is worth because of its place. In 352, the 3 is worth 300. |
| Expanded form | A number written as the sum of its places: 352 = 300 + 50 + 2. |
| Addend | One of the numbers you add. |
| Sum | The answer to an addition problem. |
| Regroup | Trade 10 of one place for 1 of the next place, like 10 ones for 1 ten. |

## Base-ten blocks

A small **cube** is 1. A **rod** is 10 cubes stuck together, so it is 10. A **flat** is 10 rods, so it is 100.

To show 246, use 2 flats, 4 rods and 6 cubes. To add 178, put out 1 flat, 7 rods and 8 cubes more. Now push the same kinds together:

- Cubes: 6 + 8 = 14. That is more than 9, so trade 10 cubes for 1 rod. Now there are 4 cubes.
- Rods: 4 + 7 + 1 = 12. Trade 10 rods for 1 flat. Now there are 2 rods.
- Flats: 2 + 1 + 1 = 4.

So 246 + 178 = **424**.

**Try it.** Show 136 + 245 with blocks. Cubes: 6 + 5 = 11, so trade for a rod and keep 1 cube. Rods: 3 + 4 + 1 = 8. Flats: 1 + 2 = 3. The sum is 381.

## Adding place by place

You can do the same thing with numbers. Break each addend into hundreds, tens and ones, add each place, then add the parts:

368 + 254 → 300 + 200 = 500, 60 + 50 = 110, 8 + 4 = 12.
500 + 110 + 12 = **622**.

These parts are called partial sums. They are a good way to see where regrouping happens: 110 has an extra hundred, and 12 has an extra ten.

## Jumps on an open number line

An open number line has no tick marks for every number. You draw only the jumps you need. For 327 + 251, start at 327, jump 200 to 527, jump 50 to 577 and jump 1 to 578.

**Friendly-number shortcuts.**

- To add 99, add 100 and take 1 away: 572 + 99 = 672 − 1 = 671.
- To add 397 + 128, move 3 from 128 to 397: 400 + 125 = 525. Nothing was added or lost, so the sum stays the same.

## The standard way

Write the numbers in columns, ones under ones and tens under tens. Add the ones first. If they make 10 or more, write the ones digit and put a small 1 above the tens column. That 1 is the new ten. Then add the tens the same way, and then the hundreds.

538 + 275: ones 8 + 5 = 13 (write 3, carry 1 ten), tens 3 + 7 + 1 = 11 (write 1, carry 1 hundred), hundreds 5 + 2 + 1 = 8. The sum is **813**.

**Watch out.** A common mistake is to write every place total side by side, like "3 11 15", or to drop the extra ten. If a place adds to 10 or more, it always sends 1 to the next place.

## For grown-ups at home

Children learn several ways to add this week before the column method, because the blocks and partial sums show *why* the little 1 moves to the next place. Ask your child to explain a sum out loud ("8 ones and 7 ones make 15, so that is one more ten"). Adding scores, pages read or steps walked in a day are good everyday practice.
