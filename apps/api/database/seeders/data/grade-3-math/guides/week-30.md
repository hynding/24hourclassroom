# Week 30 · Adding and Subtracting Within 1,000, and Number Patterns

**Standards:** 3.NBT.A.2 Add and subtract fluently within 1,000 · 3.OA.D.9 Find and explain patterns in numbers
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 8, Section C, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Add and subtract three-digit numbers with a strategy you trust.
- Regroup tens and hundreds when you need to.
- Count up on an open number line to subtract.
- Check an answer with the opposite operation.
- Spot and explain odd and even patterns in the addition and multiplication tables.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Sum | The answer when you add. |
| Difference | The answer when you subtract. |
| Regroup | Trade 10 ones for 1 ten, or 10 tens for 1 hundred (and back again). |
| Open number line | A line with jumps drawn on it to add or subtract. It has no fixed scale. |
| Even number | A number that splits into 2 equal groups with none left over. |
| Odd number | A number that has 1 left over when you split it into 2 equal groups. |

## Adding three-digit numbers

You can add place by place. For 356 + 228:

- Hundreds: 300 + 200 = 500
- Tens: 50 + 20 = 70
- Ones: 6 + 8 = 14
- Put them together: 500 + 70 + 14 = 584

When you line the numbers up and add in columns, the 14 ones do not fit in the ones place. **Regroup**: 14 ones is 1 ten and 4 ones. Write the 4 and add the new ten to the tens.

Sometimes a friendly number is faster. For 298 + 145, move 2 from 145 to 298. Now it is 300 + 143 = 443.

**Try it.** 467 + 158. Hundreds 400 + 100 = 500, tens 60 + 50 = 110, ones 7 + 8 = 15. 500 + 110 + 15 = 625.

## Subtracting three-digit numbers

**Count up.** To find 600 − 258, start at 258 and jump to friendly numbers: + 2 is 260, + 40 is 300, + 300 is 600. Add the jumps: 2 + 40 + 300 = 342.

**Regroup.** For 524 − 263, the ones are fine (4 − 3 = 1), but 2 tens take away 6 tens does not work. Trade 1 hundred for 10 tens. Now there are 4 hundreds and 12 tens. 12 − 6 = 6 tens and 4 − 2 = 2 hundreds. The difference is 261.

**Zeros on top.** For 700 − 248, trade 1 hundred for 10 tens, then 1 ten for 10 ones. You get 6 hundreds, 9 tens and 10 ones. 10 − 8 = 2, 9 − 4 = 5, 6 − 2 = 4. The difference is 452.

**Check with addition.** Add your answer to the number you took away. For 700 − 248 = 452, check 452 + 248 = 700. It works.

**A common mistake.** In each column, some students take the smaller digit from the bigger one, even when the bigger digit is on the bottom. For 524 − 263 they write 4 − 3 = 1, 6 − 2 = 4, 5 − 2 = 3 and get 341. Always subtract the bottom digit from the top digit, and regroup when the top is too small. Another slip is forgetting that a hundred was traded, so the hundreds digit is one too big.

## Patterns in the tables

Patterns help you check your work.

- **Odd and even sums.** Even + even is even. Odd + odd is even, because the two leftovers make a pair. Even + odd is odd.
- **The addition table.** Moving one square to the right, the sum goes up by 1. The doubles (4 + 4, 5 + 5, 6 + 6 ...) sit on a diagonal and go up by 2.
- **The multiplication table.** Any fact with an even factor has an even product, like 4 × 7 = 28. Only odd × odd is odd, like 3 × 9 = 27. The 5s end in 5, 0, 5, 0.

**Try it.** Is 37 + 45 odd or even? Both are odd, so the sum is even. Check: 37 + 45 = 82, which is even.

## For grown-ups at home

Ask your child to explain one subtraction two ways, such as counting up and regrouping, and to check it with addition. Shop and kitchen numbers are useful: "We had 500 sheets of paper and used 135. How many are left?" Odd and even patterns are fun to test with house numbers on a walk.
