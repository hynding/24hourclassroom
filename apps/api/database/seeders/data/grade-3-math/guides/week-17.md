# Week 17 · Equal Parts and Unit Fractions

**Standards:** 3.NF.A.1 Understand unit fractions and fractions made of unit fractions · 3.G.A.2 Split shapes into parts with equal areas
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 5, Section A, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Split a shape into **equal parts**: halves, thirds, fourths, sixths and eighths.
- Name one equal part with a **unit fraction**, such as 1/4.
- Build fractions such as 3/4 out of unit fractions.
- Use the words **numerator** and **denominator**.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Whole | The one thing you are splitting, such as one sandwich or one rectangle. |
| Equal parts | Parts that are all the same size. |
| Fraction | A number that names part of a whole, like 1/2 or 3/8. |
| Unit fraction | A fraction with 1 on top. It names one equal part, like 1/6. |
| Numerator | The top number. It tells how many parts we are counting. |
| Denominator | The bottom number. It tells how many equal parts make the whole. |

## Equal parts

Imagine you and a friend share a granola bar. If one piece is big and one is tiny, that is not fair. Fair shares are **equal parts**: every part is the same size.

A whole cut into equal parts has a name for its parts:

- 2 equal parts are **halves**.
- 3 equal parts are **thirds**.
- 4 equal parts are **fourths** (also called quarters).
- 6 equal parts are **sixths**.
- 8 equal parts are **eighths**.

Parts can be equal even when they look different. A square cut corner to corner both ways makes 4 triangles that are all the same size. A square cut into 4 thin strips also makes fourths. Both ways split the square's **area** into 4 equal parts.

**Try it.** A rectangle is cut into 4 parts. One part is wide and three are thin. Is it cut into fourths? No. The parts must be equal to be fourths.

## Unit fractions

When a whole is cut into 4 equal parts, one part is **1/4** of the whole. We read it "one fourth". A fraction with 1 on top is a **unit fraction**. It is the building block for all the other fractions.

- One of 2 equal parts is 1/2.
- One of 3 equal parts is 1/3.
- One of 6 equal parts is 1/6.
- One of 8 equal parts is 1/8.

The bottom number, the **denominator**, tells how many equal parts make the whole.

**Try it.** A garden bed is split into 6 equal parts. What fraction of the bed is one part? It is 1/6.

## Building fractions from unit fractions

What if you shade more than one part? Count the unit fractions.

A strip is cut into 8 equal parts and 3 are shaded. Each part is 1/8. Three of them make **3/8**: 1/8 + 1/8 + 1/8.

The top number, the **numerator**, counts how many parts you are talking about. The denominator still tells the size of each part.

To name the fraction of a shape:

1. Count all the equal parts in the whole. That is the denominator.
2. Count the parts you want, such as the shaded ones. That is the numerator.

If all the parts are shaded, the numerator and denominator are the same. 4/4 is the whole thing.

**Try it.** A rectangle has 6 equal parts and 5 are shaded. What fraction is shaded? 5/6. What fraction is white? 1/6.

## A common mistake

Some students count the shaded parts and the white parts and write 3/5 for a strip with 3 shaded and 5 white. But the denominator must count **every** part of the whole: 3 + 5 = 8 parts. So the shaded part is 3/8.

## For grown-ups at home

This week begins fractions. The big idea is that 1/4 means "one of 4 equal parts", and 3/4 means "three of those parts". Fold paper napkins or cut a sandwich and ask: "Are the parts equal? What is one part called? What fraction did you eat?" Use the words numerator and denominator when you can.

## Check yourself

1. A pizza is cut into 8 equal slices. What fraction is one slice?
2. A square is cut into 4 parts that are not the same size. Is each part 1/4?
3. A strip has 6 equal parts and 4 are shaded. What fraction is shaded?
4. How many copies of 1/3 make 2/3?

*Answers:* (1) 1/8. (2) No, fourths must be equal parts. (3) 4/6. (4) 2 copies.
