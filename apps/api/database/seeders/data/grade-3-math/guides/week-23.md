# Week 23 · Telling Time to the Minute and Finding Elapsed Time

**Standard:** 3.MD.A.1 Tell time to the minute and find elapsed time
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 6, Section C, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Read an analog clock to the exact minute.
- Explain why the hour hand often points between two numbers.
- Find **elapsed time**: how much time has passed.
- Find when something ends, or when it started, using jumps on a number line, even across the hour.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Hour hand | The short hand. It shows the hour. |
| Minute hand | The long hand. It goes around the clock once every hour. |
| Minute | A short unit of time. There are 60 minutes in 1 hour. |
| Elapsed time | How much time passes from the start of something to its end. |
| a.m. and p.m. | a.m. is from midnight to noon. p.m. is from noon to midnight. |

## Reading the minute hand

The clock has 12 numbers and 60 small marks. The minute hand moves one small mark every minute.

Each number on the clock stands for 5 minutes when you read the minute hand. So count by 5s to the last number the minute hand has passed, then count on by 1s.

**Try it.** The minute hand is 3 small marks past the 4. Count 5, 10, 15, 20 to the 4, then 21, 22, 23. It is 23 minutes after the hour.

## Reading the hour hand

The hour hand does not jump from number to number. It moves slowly all hour long. At 6:00 it points right at the 6. At 6:30 it is halfway between the 6 and the 7. At 6:50 it is almost at the 7.

So when the hour hand is between two numbers, the hour is the number it has **passed**, the smaller one (between 12 and 1, it is 12). This is the most common clock mistake: at 6:50 the hour hand is very close to the 7, but it is still 6:50, not 7:50.

**Try it.** The hour hand is between the 10 and the 11, close to the 11. The minute hand is one small mark past the 9. The time is 10:46, not 11:46.

## Finding elapsed time on a number line

A number line for time works like a ruler for minutes. Mark the start time, then make jumps to the end time. Add up the jumps.

Football practice goes from 3:10 to 3:55. Jump from 3:10 to 3:50 (40 minutes), then to 3:55 (5 minutes). 40 + 5 = 45 minutes.

**Across the hour**, jump to the next o'clock first. Lena reads from 7:40 to 8:15.

- 7:40 to 8:00 is 20 minutes.
- 8:00 to 8:15 is 15 minutes.
- 20 + 15 = **35 minutes**.

**Common mistake:** subtracting the times as if they were normal numbers. 815 − 740 = 75, but Lena did not read for 75 minutes. An hour has 60 minutes, not 100, so always count on with jumps.

## Finding an end time or a start time

To find an **end time**, start at the start time and jump forward. A puppet show starts at 10:35 and lasts 45 minutes. Jump 25 minutes to 11:00. That uses 25 of the 45 minutes, so 20 are left. Jump 20 more: the show ends at **11:20**.

To find a **start time**, start at the end time and jump backward. Art ended at 3:20 and lasted 35 minutes. Jump back 20 minutes to 3:00, then back 15 more to **2:45**.

## For grown-ups at home

If you have an analog clock at home, ask your child to read it at odd moments: "What time is it right now, to the minute?" Ask them to tell you where the hour hand is and why. When you set a timer for cooking or screen time, ask, "If we start at 4:50 and it takes 20 minutes, when will it be done?" A quick sketch of a number line with jumps is the best tool for these questions.

## Check yourself

1. The minute hand points at the 8. How many minutes after the hour is it?
2. Swim class goes from 9:40 to 10:15. How long is it?
3. Dinner starts at 5:50 and takes 30 minutes. When does it end?

*Answers:* (1) 40 minutes. (2) 20 minutes to 10:00, then 15 more: 35 minutes. (3) 10 minutes to 6:00, then 20 more: 6:20.
