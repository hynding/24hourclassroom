# Week 10 · Rounding to the Nearest 10 and 100

**Standard:** 3.NBT.A.1 Round whole numbers to the nearest 10 or 100
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 3, Section C, in our own words

## What you will learn

- **Round** a number to the nearest ten and to the nearest hundred.
- Use a number line to see which ten or hundred is closer.
- Know what to do with a number that is exactly halfway.
- **Estimate** sums and differences and decide if an answer makes sense.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Round | Change a number to a nearby ten or hundred that is easier to use. |
| Nearest ten | The multiple of 10 that is closest, like 530 for 528. |
| Nearest hundred | The multiple of 100 that is closest, like 700 for 730. |
| Halfway | The middle point between two tens or two hundreds, like 525 or 750. |
| Estimate | A close answer found with rounded numbers. |

## Rounding to the nearest ten

Draw a number line from the ten below the number to the ten above it. For 528, that is 520 to 530. Mark the halfway point, 525.

528 sits past 525. It is 2 steps from 530 and 8 steps from 520, so 528 rounds to **530**.

A quick way: look at the **ones digit**. If it is 0, 1, 2, 3 or 4, round down. If it is 5, 6, 7, 8 or 9, round up.

**Try it.** Round 174 to the nearest ten. It is between 170 and 180, and 4 ones means round down: 170.

## Rounding to the nearest hundred

Now the ends of the number line are hundreds. For 730, draw 700 to 800 with ticks every 10. The halfway point is 750.

730 has not reached 750, so it is closer to 700. It rounds to **700**.

For hundreds, look at the **tens digit**. 438 has 3 tens, so it rounds down to 400. 762 has 6 tens, so it rounds up to 800.

## Exactly halfway

A number exactly in the middle is not closer to either end. Mathematicians agreed to **round it up**. So 285 rounds to 290, and 650 rounds to 700.

**Watch out.** Always round the number you started with. 349 rounds to 350 for the nearest ten, but to **300** for the nearest hundred, because 349 is less than 350. Do not round 349 to 350 and then round 350 again.

## Estimating

An estimate tells you *about* how much. Round each number, then add or subtract.

- 296 + 518 → 300 + 500 = 800. (The exact sum is 814.)
- 615 − 382 → 600 − 400 = 200. (The exact difference is 233.)

Estimates help you check work. Mira said 804 − 397 = 507. Estimate: 800 − 400 = 400. 507 is far from 400, so Mira should check again. The real answer is 407.

## For grown-ups at home

Rounding is about which number is *closer*, so the number line matters more than a rule. Ask your child to sketch the two tens or hundreds and the halfway mark. Real-life estimating is everywhere: "We drove 187 miles and have about 200 to go," or "There are about 300 seats in the hall."

## Check yourself

1. Round 63 to the nearest ten.
2. Round 149 to the nearest hundred.
3. Round 655 to the nearest ten and to the nearest hundred.
4. Estimate 421 + 365 to the nearest hundred.

*Answers:* (1) 60. (2) 100. (3) 660 and 700. (4) 400 + 400 = 800 (the exact sum is 786).
