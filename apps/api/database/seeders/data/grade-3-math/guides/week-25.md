# Week 25 · Sorting Shapes: Quadrilaterals, Rhombuses, Rectangles and Squares

**Standard:** 3.G.A.1 Sort shapes by their attributes, including quadrilaterals
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 7, Section A, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Describe a shape by its **attributes**: how many sides, how long the sides are, and whether it has **right angles**.
- Know that a **quadrilateral** is any shape with exactly 4 straight sides.
- Tell a **rhombus**, a **rectangle** and a **square** apart.
- Explain why every square is a rhombus and a rectangle at the same time.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Polygon | A flat, closed shape made only of straight sides. |
| Vertex | A corner, where two sides meet. More than one are called vertices. |
| Right angle | A square corner, like the corner of a sheet of paper. |
| Attribute | A feature you can check, like the number of sides or right angles. |
| Quadrilateral | Any polygon with exactly 4 sides. |

## Looking at attributes

Shapes are sorted by attributes, not by color, size or which way they point. A tiny blue triangle and a huge red triangle are both triangles, because each one has 3 straight sides.

Here are the questions to ask about any shape:

1. Is it closed, with only straight sides? Then it is a polygon. A circle is not a polygon, because its edge is curved.
2. How many sides does it have? 3 sides make a triangle, 4 a quadrilateral, 5 a pentagon and 6 a hexagon.
3. Are any sides the same length? Measure them, or count grid squares.
4. Does it have right angles? Hold the corner of an index card in each corner of the shape. If the corners match, it is a right angle. Pictures often mark a right angle with a tiny square.

**Try it.** A shape has 5 straight sides and is closed. What is it? It is a pentagon, and it is not a quadrilateral.

## Three special quadrilaterals

All of these have 4 sides. What makes each one special is one more attribute.

- A **rhombus** has 4 sides that are all the same length. Its corners can lean over, like a square that someone pushed sideways.
- A **rectangle** has 4 right angles. Two sides can be long and two short, like a door.
- A **square** has 4 sides of the same length **and** 4 right angles.

Many quadrilaterals have none of these. A shape with sides of 7 cm, 6 cm, 2 cm and 4 cm and no right angles is still a quadrilateral. It just has no special name in this unit.

**Try it.** Kofi drew a shape with sides 8 cm, 3 cm, 8 cm and 3 cm and 4 right angles. It is a rectangle. It is not a rhombus or a square, because its sides are not all equal.

## A square belongs to two groups

Look at the rules again. A rhombus needs 4 equal sides. A square has 4 equal sides, so a square is a rhombus. A rectangle needs 4 right angles. A square has 4 right angles, so a square is a rectangle too.

This only works one way. A rectangle that is long and thin is not a square. A rhombus that leans over is not a square. Think of a family: every square is in the rhombus family and in the rectangle family, but not everyone in those families is a square.

A common mistake is to think a shape can have only one name. A square can be called a square, a rectangle, a rhombus, a quadrilateral and a polygon, and every one of those names is true. "Square" is just the most exact one.

Another mistake is to mix up side lengths with the number of sides. A rhombus with sides of 6 cm is not a hexagon: it has 4 sides, and each one is 6 cm long.

## For grown-ups at home

Go on a shape hunt around the house: windows, tiles, books, cereal boxes, a kite. For each shape, ask, "How many sides? Are they equal? Are the corners square?" Then ask which names fit. Let your child use an index card to test corners. The goal is to reason from attributes, so "it looks like a diamond" should become "it has 4 equal sides and no square corners, so it is a rhombus."

## Check yourself

1. How many sides does a quadrilateral have?
2. A shape has 4 sides of 3 cm and 4 right angles. Name three names that fit it.
3. True or false: a rhombus always has right angles.
4. Is a shape with sides of 5 cm, 5 cm, 2 cm and 2 cm and 4 right angles a square?

*Answers:* (1) 4. (2) Square, rectangle and rhombus (also quadrilateral). (3) False: a rhombus needs equal sides, not right angles. (4) No. It is a rectangle, because its sides are not all equal.
