# Week 16 · Multiplication and Division Word Problems

**Standard:** 3.OA.A.3 Solve multiplication and division word problems within 100
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 4, Section D, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Find the three numbers in every equal-groups story: **groups**, **size of each group** and **total**.
- Decide whether a story needs multiplication or division.
- Draw a **tape diagram** or an array to show a story.
- Write an equation with a symbol for the **unknown**, solve it, and check that the answer makes sense.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Equal groups | Groups that each have the same number of things. |
| Total | How many there are altogether. |
| Unknown | The number the story asks you to find. Show it with ?, a box or a letter. |
| Equation | A math sentence with an equal sign, such as 6 × 4 = 24. |
| Tape diagram | A bar for the total, cut into equal parts for the groups. |

## Three numbers in every story

Every equal-groups story has the same three numbers. The story tells you two and asks for the third.

| What is unknown | Example | Equation |
|---|---|---|
| The total | 9 vans hold 7 students each. How many students? | 9 × 7 = ? (63) |
| The size of each group | 35 chairs in 5 equal rows. How many in each row? | 35 ÷ 5 = ? or 5 × ? = 35 (7) |
| The number of groups | 48 treats, 6 for each dog. How many dogs? | 48 ÷ 6 = ? or ? × 6 = 48 (8) |

If the total is unknown, multiply. If the total is given, divide, or find the missing factor.

## Draw it

A quick drawing keeps you from guessing. A garden has 7 rows with 8 carrots in each row. Draw a bar with 7 equal parts. Write 8 in each part. Put a question mark over the whole bar. Now you can see the equation: 7 × 8 = ?, which is 56.

For a division story, the total goes over the whole bar. "32 inches of ribbon cut into 4 equal pieces" is a bar labeled 32, cut into 4 parts, with ? in each part. 32 ÷ 4 = 8 inches.

Stories about rows of chairs, seats or plants can also be drawn as **arrays**: rows are the groups, and the number in each row is the group size.

## Write an equation with an unknown

Use a symbol for the number you do not know. All of these say the same thing:

- 24 ÷ 6 = ?
- 6 × □ = 24
- 6 × b = 24

**Try it.** Imani has 24 beads and puts 6 on each bracelet. 6 × b = 24, and b = 4. She makes 4 bracelets.

## Check that it makes sense

When you finish, ask two questions:

1. **Did I answer the question?** If the story asks "how many on each page?", the answer is a number on one page, not the total.
2. **Is it reasonable?** If 30 children ride in vans of 6, there must be fewer vans than children. Multiply back to check: 5 vans × 6 = 30.

## Common mistakes

- **Adding instead of multiplying.** "7 boxes of 6 crayons" is not 7 + 6. It is 7 × 6 = 42.
- **Stopping one step early.** If a story has two steps, such as finding the muffins on 3 trays and then sharing them, do both steps.
- **Mixing up the numbers in a division.** The total always comes first: 28 ÷ 4, not 4 ÷ 28.

## For grown-ups at home

Ask your child to retell a story problem in their own words before solving it: "There are 7 rows, 8 in each row, and we want all of them." Then ask, "Which number is missing: the groups, the size, or the total?" Everyday moments work well, such as "The recipe needs 3 eggs. How many eggs for 4 batches?" or "We have 20 grapes for 5 of us."

## Check yourself

1. 6 shelves hold 9 books each. How many books?
2. 45 students make teams of 5. How many teams?
3. 28 cards are shared equally by 4 friends. Write an equation with an unknown and solve it.
4. Is 12 a reasonable answer for "8 bikes with 2 wheels each, how many wheels"? Why or why not?

*Answers:* (1) 6 × 9 = 54 books. (2) 45 ÷ 5 = 9 teams. (3) 28 ÷ 4 = ? or 4 × ? = 28; each friend gets 7 cards. (4) No. 8 × 2 = 16 wheels; 12 wheels would be only 6 bikes.
