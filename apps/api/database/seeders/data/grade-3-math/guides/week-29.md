# Week 29 · Multiplication and Division Facts You Can Trust

**Standards:** 3.OA.C.7 Multiply and divide fluently within 100 · 3.NBT.A.3 Multiply one-digit numbers by multiples of 10
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 8, Section C, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Find any multiplication fact up to 10 × 10, even one you forget, by building it from a fact you know.
- Solve a division fact by asking "what times this number makes that one?"
- Write the four facts in a **fact family**.
- Multiply a one-digit number by 20, 30, 40 and other multiples of 10.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Factor | A number you multiply. In 6 × 7 = 42, the factors are 6 and 7. |
| Product | The answer when you multiply. In 6 × 7 = 42, the product is 42. |
| Quotient | The answer when you divide. In 42 ÷ 6 = 7, the quotient is 7. |
| Fact family | The multiplication and division facts made from the same three numbers. |
| Multiple of 10 | A number you say when you count by 10s: 10, 20, 30, 40 and so on. |
| Fluency | Getting the right answer quickly, with a strategy you can explain. |

## Build a fact from one you know

You have learned many facts this year. Some you just know, like 2 × 8 or 5 × 6. When a fact will not come to mind, build it from a fact you know.

- **Add one more group.** 6 × 7 is 5 sevens and one more seven. 5 × 7 = 35, and 35 + 7 = 42.
- **Double.** 8 × 6 is double 4 × 6. 4 × 6 = 24, and 24 + 24 = 48. The 6s are double the 3s, and the 8s are double the 4s.
- **Use a ten.** 9 × 7 is one 7 less than 10 × 7. 10 × 7 = 70, and 70 − 7 = 63.
- **Split an array.** A 7 × 9 array can be cut into 5 rows of 9 and 2 rows of 9. That is 45 + 18 = 63.

**Try it.** Find 7 × 6 by splitting the 7 into 5 and 2. 5 × 6 = 30 and 2 × 6 = 12. 30 + 12 = 42.

Remember the easy rules too. Any number times 1 stays the same. Any number times 0 is 0. Swapping the factors does not change the product, so if you know 3 × 9 = 27 you also know 9 × 3 = 27.

## Division is a missing factor

Every division fact hides a multiplication fact. To find 56 ÷ 7, ask: "7 times what is 56?" Since 7 × 8 = 56, the quotient is 8.

Three numbers make a whole **fact family**. With 6, 9 and 54 you can write:

- 6 × 9 = 54 and 9 × 6 = 54
- 54 ÷ 6 = 9 and 54 ÷ 9 = 6

To check a division answer, multiply. If you think 42 ÷ 6 = 7, check that 6 × 7 = 42. It does, so you are right.

**Try it.** Omar has 32 stickers and puts 4 on each page. How many pages? Ask "4 times what is 32?" 4 × 8 = 32, so 8 pages.

## Multiplying by multiples of 10

To find 4 × 30, think of 30 as 3 tens. Then 4 × 3 tens = 12 tens. Twelve tens is 120. Base-ten blocks show this well: 4 groups of 3 ten-rods make 12 rods, and 12 rods are 120 ones.

The order does not matter. 60 × 8 is the same as 8 × 6 tens, which is 48 tens, or 480.

**Try it.** 9 × 80. You know 9 × 8 = 72, so 9 × 8 tens = 72 tens = 720.

**A common mistake.** Some students find 4 × 3 = 12 and stop, writing 12 for 4 × 30. Say the unit out loud: "4 times 3 **tens** is 12 **tens**." Saying "tens" reminds you the answer is 120. Another mistake is adding when the problem has equal groups. 6 shelves of 30 books is 6 × 30 = 180, not 6 + 30 = 36.

## For grown-ups at home

This week is about speed with understanding, not memorising alone. When your child gets stuck on a fact, ask "What fact do you know that is close?" and let them build it. Short games help: flip two playing cards and multiply, or call out a product (like 24) and ask for every fact that makes it. Five minutes a day does more than one long session.

## Check yourself

1. Build 6 × 8 from 5 × 8.
2. What is 63 ÷ 9? Which multiplication fact did you use?
3. What is 5 × 70?
4. Write the fact family for 4, 7 and 28.

*Answers:* (1) 5 × 8 = 40, and one more 8 makes 48. (2) 7, because 9 × 7 = 63. (3) 5 × 7 tens = 35 tens = 350. (4) 4 × 7 = 28, 7 × 4 = 28, 28 ÷ 4 = 7, 28 ÷ 7 = 4.
