# Week 24 · Solving Measurement Word Problems

**Standards:** 3.MD.A.2 Measure and estimate liquid volumes and masses · 3.OA.D.8 Solve two-step word problems with the four operations
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 6, Section D, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Solve word problems about kilograms, grams, liters and time.
- Choose the right operation: add, subtract, multiply or divide.
- Draw a tape diagram or a number line to see the problem.
- Write an equation with a letter for the unknown number.
- Check that an answer is reasonable with an estimate.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Tape diagram | A long box split into parts that shows how amounts in a story fit together. |
| Unknown | The number you are trying to find. |
| Equation | A math sentence with an equal sign, like 4 × 5 = m. |
| Two-step problem | A problem where you find one number first, then use it to answer the question. |
| Reasonable | An answer that makes sense for the story. |

## Four steps for every word problem

1. **Read and picture it.** What is happening? What is the question?
2. **Draw it.** A tape diagram for amounts, or a number line for time.
3. **Write an equation and solve.** Use a letter for the unknown number.
4. **Check it.** Does the answer make sense? Did you write the unit?

## Choosing the operation

The story tells you what to do.

- Amounts are **put together**: add. 7 L and 4 L make 11 L.
- An amount is **taken away** or you **compare** two amounts: subtract. 15 kg take away 4 kg is 11 kg.
- There are **equal groups**: multiply. 5 bags of 3 kg is 5 × 3 = 15 kg.
- A total is **shared equally**, or you ask **how many groups** fit: divide. 20 L shared into 4 jugs is 20 ÷ 4 = 5 L each. How many 3 L buckets fill 12 L? 12 ÷ 3 = 4 buckets.

**Try it.** The garden club splits 30 kilograms of soil equally into 5 pots. A tape diagram shows a total of 30 kg cut into 5 equal parts. Each part is 30 ÷ 5 = **6 kg**.

## Two-step problems

Some problems hide a number you must find first.

A rain barrel has 30 liters of water. Juan waters 4 plants with 5 liters each. How much water is left?

- **Step 1:** water used. 4 × 5 = 20 liters.
- **Step 2:** water left. 30 − 20 = 10 liters.

We can write this as one equation with a letter for the unknown: 30 − (4 × 5) = w. The parentheses tell us to find 4 × 5 first. Then w = 10.

**Common mistake:** doing only one step. "20 liters" is how much water was used, not how much is left. Read the question again before you finish.

## Time problems

For time, a number line with jumps is the best drawing. A bus to the zoo leaves at 8:35 and the ride takes 45 minutes. Jump 25 minutes to 9:00. That leaves 20 more minutes, so the bus arrives at **9:20**. Remember, an hour has 60 minutes, so 8:80 is not a real time.

## Is my answer reasonable?

Round the numbers to estimate, then compare. Four bottles of 8 liters hold 32 liters, and a tub holds 41 liters. The exact total is 32 + 41 = 73 liters. To check: 32 is about 30 and 41 is about 40, and 30 + 40 = 70. 73 is close to 70, so 73 makes sense. If you got 173, the estimate tells you something went wrong.

Also check the size of the units. A pet rabbit of 2 grams is too light (that is 2 paper clips). A rabbit of 2 kilograms makes sense.

## For grown-ups at home

Real life is full of these problems. While cooking, shopping or packing, ask your child two-step questions: "We have 3 bottles of 2 liters and we drink 1 liter. How much is left?" Ask them to tell you which operation they used and why, and to say whether the answer makes sense. Drawing a quick box or line on paper is a great habit, not a sign that the problem is too hard.

## Check yourself

1. 4 bags of potatoes are 5 kg each. How many kilograms in all?
2. A jug has 18 L of water. 3 classes each take 4 L. How much is left?
3. Recess starts at 10:45 and lasts 30 minutes. When does it end?

*Answers:* (1) 4 × 5 = 20 kg. (2) 3 × 4 = 12 L taken, and 18 − 12 = 6 L left. (3) 15 minutes to 11:00, then 15 more: 11:15.
