# Week 18 · Fractions on the Number Line

**Standard:** 3.NF.A.2 Show fractions on a number line
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 5, Section B, in our own words

## What you will learn

- See that fractions are **numbers**, with their own places on the number line.
- Split the space from 0 to 1 into equal parts.
- Mark and name fractions such as 1/3, 3/4 and 5/8.
- Keep counting past 1, to fractions such as 4/3 and 5/3.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Number line | A line with numbers in order, spaced evenly. |
| Tick mark | A short line across the number line that marks a point. |
| Whole | On a fraction number line, the space from 0 to 1. |
| Interval | The space between two tick marks next to each other. |
| Jump | One move from a tick mark to the next one. |

## Splitting 0 to 1 into equal parts

Think of a fraction strip laid along a number line. The strip starts at 0 and ends at 1. If the strip is cut into 4 equal pieces, each piece is 1/4 long, and the edge of each piece is a tick mark.

To show fourths on a number line:

1. Mark 0 and 1.
2. Split the space between them into 4 **equal** parts. That takes 3 tick marks in between.
3. Name the marks by counting jumps of 1/4: 1/4, 2/4, 3/4, and 4/4 at 1.

The spaces must be equal. If one space is wider than the others, the marks do not show fourths.

**Try it.** Show 1/3. Split 0 to 1 into 3 equal parts. 1/3 is the end of the first part after 0.

## Naming a point

A dot sits on a number line. What fraction is it?

- **Denominator:** count the equal parts between 0 and 1. Do not count the tick marks: 4 parts have 5 tick marks.
- **Numerator:** count the jumps from 0 to the dot.

**Try it.** A line from 0 to 1 has 6 equal parts. A dot is 2 jumps from 0. The dot is at 2/6.

Fractions near 0 have only a few jumps. Fractions near 1 are only a jump or two away from 1. On a line in eighths, 1/8 is close to 0 and 7/8 is close to 1.

## Past 1

The number line does not stop at 1, and neither do fractions. Keep splitting each whole into the same number of parts and keep counting.

On a line from 0 to 2 split into thirds, count jumps of 1/3 from 0: 1/3, 2/3, 3/3 (which is at 1), 4/3, 5/3, 6/3 (which is at 2).

So **5/3** is two jumps past 1. Always start counting at 0, not at 1.

**Try it.** On a line from 0 to 2 in fourths, where is 5/4? One jump of 1/4 past 1.

## A common mistake

When counting, some students use the tick marks for the denominator. A line from 0 to 1 with 5 tick marks has only 4 parts, so it shows fourths, not fifths. Another mistake is counting parts all the way to 2. The denominator counts the parts in **one** whole.

## For grown-ups at home

Placing fractions on a number line helps children see that 3/4 is a number, just like 3, only smaller than 1. A ruler, a tape measure or a hallway floor with tiles can be a number line. Ask: "If this hallway is 1 whole, where is halfway? Where is 1/4 of the way?"

## Check yourself

1. How many tick marks between 0 and 1 do you need to show sixths?
2. A line from 0 to 1 is in eighths. A dot is 5 jumps from 0. What fraction is it?
3. On a line in thirds, which tick mark is 4/3?

*Answers:* (1) 5 tick marks in between (7 counting 0 and 1). (2) 5/8. (3) The first tick mark after 1.
