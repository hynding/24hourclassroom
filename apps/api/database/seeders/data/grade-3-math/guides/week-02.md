# Week 02 · Equal Groups and Writing Multiplication

**Standard:** 3.OA.A.1 Understand multiplication as equal groups
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 1, Section B, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Tell **equal groups** from groups that are not equal.
- Say "groups of" for a picture or a story, like "4 groups of 5".
- Write a **multiplication** expression such as 4 × 5.
- Use the words **factor** and **product**.
- Find a product by skip-counting or by adding the same number again.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Equal groups | Groups that all have the same number of things. |
| Multiplication | A fast way to find the total of equal groups. |
| × | The times sign. 3 × 4 is read "3 times 4". |
| Factor | A number you multiply. In 3 × 4 = 12, the factors are 3 and 4. |
| Product | The answer to a multiplication. In 3 × 4 = 12, the product is 12. |

## Equal groups

Last week, a picture on a graph stood for 2, 5 or 10 things. Counting by 2s, 5s or 10s was really counting **equal groups**.

Equal groups all have the same number of things in them. Picture 3 pencil cups with 4 pencils in every cup. Each cup is a group. Every group has 4. These are equal groups.

Now picture one cup with 4 pencils, one with 2 and one with 6. The groups are not the same size, so they are not equal groups. To find the total, you have to add each group: 4 + 2 + 6.

**Try it.** A box has 5 markers, another has 5, and another has 5. Are these equal groups? Yes: 3 groups of 5.

## From "groups of" to multiplication

When the groups are equal, we can say it in a short way. "3 cups with 4 pencils in each cup" is **3 groups of 4**.

We write 3 groups of 4 as **3 × 4**. In this course, the first number tells how many groups, and the second number tells how many are in each group.

- 3 × 4 → 3 groups, 4 in each group
- 6 × 2 → 6 groups, 2 in each group
- 2 × 6 → 2 groups, 6 in each group

The numbers we multiply are the **factors**. The answer is the **product**. In 3 × 4 = 12, the factors are 3 and 4, and the product is 12.

**Try it.** 5 dogs each have 4 legs. Write the multiplication. It is 5 groups of 4, so 5 × 4.

## Finding the product

There are a few ways to find how many in all.

1. **Draw it.** Draw a circle for each group. Put the same number of dots in each circle. Count the dots.
2. **Skip-count.** Count by the group size, once for each group. For 5 × 4, count by 4s five times: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20.
3. **Add the same number.** 5 × 4 is 4 + 4 + 4 + 4 + 4 = 20.

Each way gives the same product: 20.

**Try it.** Jun has 3 packs of stickers with 6 stickers in each pack. Skip-count by 6s three times: 6, 12, 18. Jun has 18 stickers. The multiplication is 3 × 6 = 18.

A common mistake is to add the two numbers. For 3 packs of 6, some students write 3 + 6 = 9. But 9 only counts one pack and the number of packs. Ask yourself, "How many groups? How many in each group?" Then multiply.

## Stories and pictures

Any story with equal groups can be a multiplication.

- 4 vases with 3 tulips in each vase → 4 × 3
- 2 hands with 5 fingers on each hand → 2 × 5
- 7 bikes with 2 wheels on each bike → 7 × 2

You can also go the other way. For 6 × 3, you might tell a story about 6 lunch tables with 3 friends at each table.

## For grown-ups at home

This week your child learns that multiplication means equal groups. Look for groups together: eggs in a carton, wheels on cars, socks in pairs. Ask, "How many groups? How many in each group? How many in all?" Let your child skip-count out loud. It is fine if they still add or draw to find the total; fast facts come later in the year.

## Check yourself

1. Write a multiplication for 5 groups of 3.
2. What is the product of 4 × 2?
3. Name the factors in 7 × 6 = 42.
4. Are 3 bags with 2, 3 and 4 apples equal groups?

*Answers:* (1) 5 × 3. (2) 8, because 2, 4, 6, 8. (3) 7 and 6. (4) No, each bag has a different number.
