# Week 14 · Multiplying by Multiples of 10 and Larger Products

**Standards:** 3.NBT.A.3 Multiply one-digit numbers by multiples of 10 · 3.OA.B.5 Use properties of operations to multiply and divide
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 4, Section C, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Multiply a one-digit number by 10, 20, 30 … 90 by thinking in **tens**.
- Use base-ten pictures to show products like 4 × 30.
- Find a product like 6 × 14 by **splitting** one factor into tens and ones.
- Spot the most common slip: forgetting that you are counting tens.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Multiple of 10 | A number you reach counting by 10s, like 20, 50 or 90. |
| Ten | A group of 10 ones. A base-ten rod shows 1 ten. |
| Factor | A number you multiply. |
| Product | The answer to a multiplication. |
| Distributive property | Splitting a factor into parts, multiplying each part, then adding. |

## Thinking in tens

Every multiple of 10 is a number of tens. 30 is 3 tens. 70 is 7 tens.

So 4 × 30 means 4 groups of 3 tens. Picture 4 boxes with 3 rods in each. Count the rods: 4 × 3 = 12 rods. Each rod is a ten, so there are **12 tens**. Ten tens make 100, and 2 more tens make 20, so 12 tens is **120**.

4 × 30 = 120

You already know your facts. Now use them with tens:

| Fact with ones | Same fact with tens |
|---|---|
| 3 × 2 = 6 | 3 × 20 = 6 tens = 60 |
| 6 × 5 = 30 | 6 × 50 = 30 tens = 300 |
| 9 × 6 = 54 | 9 × 60 = 54 tens = 540 |
| 8 × 8 = 64 | 8 × 80 = 64 tens = 640 |

**Try it.** 7 × 40. 40 is 4 tens. 7 × 4 tens = 28 tens = 280.

## Splitting a factor

What about 6 × 14? 14 is not a multiple of 10, but it is 10 and 4.

Draw a rectangle 6 units tall and 14 units long. Cut it with a line after 10 units. Now there are two smaller rectangles:

- Left part: 6 × 10 = 60
- Right part: 6 × 4 = 24

Add the parts: 60 + 24 = **84**. So 6 × 14 = 84.

This is the **distributive property**: split one factor into easier parts, multiply each part by the other factor, then add.

**Try it.** 4 × 18. Split 18 into 10 and 8. 4 × 10 = 40 and 4 × 8 = 32. 40 + 32 = 72.

Splitting works on facts you are still learning, too. 8 × 7 is 8 × 5 plus 8 × 2: 40 + 16 = 56.

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting the tens.** 3 × 90 is not 27. It is 27 tens, which is 270. Say the word "tens" out loud while you work.
- **Multiplying only one part.** 6 × 14 is not 6 × 10 + 4. The 4 is a part of the factor, so it must be multiplied by 6 too.

## For grown-ups at home

Children are building the idea of place value into multiplication: 6 × 50 is "six fives, but tens". Please avoid teaching "just add a zero" for now; it hides the reason and causes trouble later with decimals. Instead ask, "How many tens is that?" Bundles of 10 (straws, pencils, craft sticks) make the idea easy to see.

## Check yourself

1. 5 × 30 = ?
2. 9 × 20 = ?
3. Split 7 × 13 into two easier products and find the answer.
4. Mia says 4 × 60 = 24. What would you tell her?

*Answers:* (1) 15 tens = 150. (2) 18 tens = 180. (3) 7 × 10 + 7 × 3 = 70 + 21 = 91. (4) 60 is 6 tens, so 4 × 60 is 24 tens, which is 240, not 24.
