# Week 01 · Picture Graphs and Bar Graphs With a Scale

**Standard:** 3.MD.B.3 Draw and read scaled picture graphs and bar graphs
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 1, Section A, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Read a **picture graph** where one picture stands for more than one thing.
- Read a **bar graph** whose scale counts by 2s, 5s or 10s.
- Use a graph to answer "how many more?", "how many fewer?" and "how many in all?"
- Choose a good scale when you draw your own graph.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Graph | A picture that shows numbers so they are easy to compare. |
| Category | One of the groups being counted, like "apple" or "banana". |
| Title | The name of the graph. It tells what the graph is about. |
| Key | The part of a picture graph that tells how many one picture stands for. |
| Scale | The numbers up the side of a bar graph, like 0, 5, 10, 15. |

## Picture graphs with a key

Imagine your class votes for a favorite fruit. You could draw one circle for every student, but with lots of students the rows get very long. Instead, the graph has a **key**: "one circle = 2 students".

Now 4 circles do not mean 4 students. Count by 2s, one count for each circle: 2, 4, 6, 8. Four circles mean **8 students**.

If the key says one picture = 5, count by 5s. If it says one picture = 10, count by 10s. Always find the key before you count.

**Try it.** One star = 5 votes. Pizza has 3 stars. How many votes? Count 5, 10, 15. Pizza has 15 votes.

## Bar graphs with a scale

A bar graph uses a bar for each category. To find how many, look at where the top of the bar lines up with the **scale**.

Read the scale carefully. If it says 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, then every line is worth 5, not 1. A bar that stops on the third line above 0 stands for 15.

If a bar stops halfway between two lines, its value is halfway between the two numbers. Halfway between 10 and 20 is 15.

## Answering questions from a graph

- **How many in all?** Add every category. 8 + 6 + 12 = 26.
- **How many more?** Find the two amounts and subtract: bigger − smaller. 20 dogs and 15 cats: 20 − 15 = 5 more dogs.
- **How many fewer?** It is the same subtraction. 15 cats is 5 fewer than 20 dogs.

A common mistake is to count pictures or lines instead of what they stand for. If grape has 3 more circles than banana and each circle is 2 students, then grape has 6 more students, not 3.

## Drawing your own graph

1. Write a title.
2. Name each category.
3. Choose a scale or key. Pick an easy number to count by (2, 5 or 10) that reaches past your biggest amount.
4. Draw the bars or pictures and check each one against your data.

If your numbers are 30, 25, 40 and 15, counting by 5s up to 40 works well: every number lands on a line.

## For grown-ups at home

This week starts the multiplication unit. Counting by 2s, 5s and 10s to read a graph is really adding equal groups, which is what multiplication is. Ask your child to read a graph out loud: "Each picture is 5, so 5, 10, 15." Graphs in the news, on cereal boxes or in sports pages are good practice.

## Check yourself

1. One heart = 2 votes. A row has 5 hearts. How many votes?
2. A bar stops at 25 and another at 10. How many more is the first?
3. Which key would you use for the numbers 20, 40 and 60: one picture = 2 or one picture = 10?

*Answers:* (1) 10 votes. (2) 25 − 10 = 15 more. (3) One picture = 10, so the rows have 2, 4 and 6 pictures.
