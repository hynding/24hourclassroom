# Week 22 · Grams, Kilograms and Liters

**Standard:** 3.MD.A.2 Measure and estimate liquid volumes and masses
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 6, Section B, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Tell what **mass** and **liquid volume** mean.
- Know about how heavy a **gram** and a **kilogram** are, and how much a **liter** holds.
- Choose a sensible unit and make a good estimate.
- Read a scale, a pan balance and a container with liter marks.
- Add and subtract amounts that use the same unit.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Mass | How heavy something is. |
| Gram (g) | A small unit of mass. A paper clip is about 1 gram. |
| Kilogram (kg) | A bigger unit of mass. A full 1-liter water bottle is about 1 kilogram. |
| Liquid volume | How much liquid a container holds or has in it. |
| Liter (L) | A unit of liquid volume. A large water bottle holds about 1 liter. |
| Estimate | A careful guess, made by comparing with something you know. |

## Grams and kilograms

Hold a paper clip in your hand. You can hardly feel it. That is about **1 gram**. Grams are good for light things: a strawberry, a pencil, a crayon.

Now hold a full 1-liter bottle of water, or a big textbook. That is about **1 kilogram**. Kilograms are good for heavy things: a cat, a bag of potatoes, you.

To choose a unit, ask: is it light like a paper clip, or heavy like a bottle of water or heavier? Light things use grams. Heavy things use kilograms.

**Try it.** Would you measure a puppy in grams or kilograms? A puppy is heavier than a water bottle, so use kilograms.

## Estimating

To estimate, compare with something you know. A melon feels like about 2 water bottles, so it is about 2 kilograms. A pencil feels like a few paper clips, so it is a few grams. Estimates do not need to be exact, but they should make sense. A rabbit of "2 grams" does not make sense: that is only 2 paper clips.

## Measuring mass

**A pan balance** compares two sides. If the pans are level, the two sides have the same mass. If one pan is lower, that side is heavier. Put an object on one side and add 1-kilogram blocks to the other side until it is level. If 3 blocks balance a pumpkin, the pumpkin is about 3 kilograms.

**A scale** has a pointer and numbers, like a number line. Before reading it, work out what each mark is worth. If the labels go 0, 100, 200 and there is one mark between each pair, that mark is halfway, so the marks count by 50s. A pointer on the mark between 300 and 400 shows 350 grams.

## Liquid volume in liters

Containers for measuring have marks up the side. Read them the same way as a scale. If only 0, 2, 4, 6 are written but there is a mark for every liter, then the mark between 4 and 6 is 5 liters.

**Try it.** Water comes up to the mark between 2 and 4. It is 3 liters.

**Common mistake:** reading the nearest labeled number. Count the marks first to see what each one is worth.

## Adding and subtracting amounts

When two amounts use the **same unit**, you can add or subtract them like any numbers. Always write the unit in the answer.

- A jug has 5 L and a bowl has 2 L. Poured together: 5 + 2 = 7 L.
- How many more liters in the jug? 5 − 2 = 3 L more.
- A 15 kg bag of sand, and 4 kg is used: 15 − 4 = 11 kg left.

## For grown-ups at home

Kitchens are full of this week's math. Look at food labels together for grams, kilograms and liters (don't worry about other units). Let your child hold a paper clip and a full 1-liter bottle and then guess the mass of fruit, a shoe or a book before checking on a kitchen scale. Ask, "Is that closer to 10 grams or 10 kilograms?" This week does not ask children to change grams into kilograms; we only compare and choose.

## Check yourself

1. Which unit fits the mass of a bicycle: grams or kilograms?
2. A jug has 6 L of lemonade. Ana pours in 3 L more. How much lemonade is there?
3. A scale is labeled 0, 10, 20, 30 with one mark between each pair. What is the mark between 20 and 30 worth?

*Answers:* (1) Kilograms, because a bicycle is heavy. (2) 6 + 3 = 9 L. (3) 25, halfway between 20 and 30.
