# Week 19 · Equivalent Fractions and Whole Numbers as Fractions

**Standard:** 3.NF.A.3 Find equivalent fractions and compare fractions
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 5, Section C, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Find **equivalent fractions**: different fractions that name the same amount.
- Use fraction strips and number lines to check.
- Write whole numbers as fractions, such as 1 = 4/4 and 3 = 6/2.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Equivalent fractions | Fractions that name the same amount, like 1/2 and 2/4. |
| Fraction strip | A strip of paper, one whole long, cut into equal parts. |
| Whole number | 0, 1, 2, 3 and so on, with no fraction part. |
| Same size whole | Two wholes you can fairly compare, like two equal strips. |

## Same amount, different names

Take two strips of paper the same length. Cut one into 2 equal pieces and the other into 4. Lay them one above the other.

One half covers exactly the same length as two fourths. So **1/2 = 2/4**. They are **equivalent fractions**: different names for the same amount.

What happened? Each half was cut into 2 smaller pieces. That made twice as many parts in the whole (4 instead of 2) and twice as many parts in our piece (2 instead of 1).

Here are some equivalent fractions with our denominators:

- 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6 = 4/8
- 1/3 = 2/6, and 2/3 = 4/6
- 1/4 = 2/8, and 3/4 = 6/8

**Try it.** How many eighths are equal to 1/4? Line up the strips: one fourth covers two eighths, so 1/4 = 2/8.

## Equivalent fractions on the number line

Draw two number lines from 0 to 1, one above the other, with 0 and 1 lined up. Split the top one into thirds and the bottom one into sixths.

The mark for 1/3 on top sits straight above 2/6 on the bottom. The mark for 2/3 sits above 4/6. Fractions at the **same point** are equivalent.

**Try it.** On lines in fourths and eighths, which eighth sits under 3/4? 6/8.

## Whole numbers as fractions

When all the parts of a whole are there, you have 1 whole. So **2/2 = 1**, **3/3 = 1**, **4/4 = 1**, **6/6 = 1** and **8/8 = 1**.

More than one whole works the same way. Three circles, each cut into fourths, have 4 + 4 + 4 = 12 fourths. So **12/4 = 3**.

A denominator of 1 means each whole is just 1 part. So 5/1 means 5 wholes: **5/1 = 5**.

**Try it.** Write 2 as halves. Each whole has 2 halves, so 2 = 4/2.

## A common mistake

Some students think 3/4 and 3/8 are equivalent because they both have a 3. But eighths are smaller pieces than fourths. Three eighths cover much less of a strip than three fourths. Equivalent fractions must cover the **same length** of the **same size** whole.

## For grown-ups at home

Equivalence is a big idea that comes back in later grades. Fold a sheet of paper in half, then in half again, and show that 1 of the 2 halves is the same as 2 of the 4 fourths. Measuring cups also help: two 1/4 cups of water fill a 1/2 cup.

## Check yourself

1. Name a fraction in sixths that is equal to 1/2.
2. Is 2/3 equivalent to 2/6?
3. Write 1 whole as a fraction in eighths.
4. What whole number is 8/2?

*Answers:* (1) 3/6. (2) No, 2/6 = 1/3, which is less than 2/3. (3) 8/8. (4) 4, because 2 halves make each whole and 8 halves make 4 wholes.
