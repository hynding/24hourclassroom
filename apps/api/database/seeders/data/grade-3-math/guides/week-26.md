# Week 26 · Perimeter: The Distance All the Way Around

**Standard:** 3.MD.D.8 Solve problems about perimeter
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 7, Section B, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Say what **perimeter** means: the distance all the way around a shape.
- Find perimeter by counting unit lengths on a grid and by adding side lengths.
- Use shortcuts for rectangles and squares.
- Find a **missing side** when you know the perimeter.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Perimeter | The total length of all the sides of a shape. |
| Side length | How long one side is, such as 6 cm. |
| Unit length | One step of length, like 1 centimeter or 1 foot. |
| Area | How many unit squares cover a shape. It is not the same as perimeter. |

## Walking around a shape

Imagine an ant walking along the edge of a sticker, starting at one corner and going all the way around until it gets back. The distance it walks is the **perimeter**.

Perimeter is a length. We measure it in length units: centimeters, meters, inches or feet. A square sticker with 3 cm sides has a perimeter of 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 12 cm.

## Perimeter on a grid

On a grid, every square is 1 unit on each side. To find the perimeter, count the unit lengths along the **outside edge** of the shape.

Picture a shape made from a row of 5 squares with 2 more rows under it, so it is 5 squares long and 3 squares tall. Count around: 5 along the top, 3 down the side, 5 along the bottom and 3 up the other side. 5 + 3 + 5 + 3 = 16 units.

The shape covers 15 squares, but its perimeter is 16 units. Those are different questions. Counting the squares inside gives the area. Counting the edges around the outside gives the perimeter.

**Try it.** A shape on a centimeter grid has sides of 4, 2, 4 and 2 units. Its perimeter is 4 + 2 + 4 + 2 = 12 cm.

## Adding side lengths

When the sides are labeled, add them all. A triangle with sides of 6 m, 8 m and 9 m has a perimeter of 6 + 8 + 9 = 23 m.

A good habit: put your finger on one corner and go around the shape, touching each side once as you add it. Then you will not skip a side or add one twice.

## Shortcuts for rectangles and squares

A rectangle's opposite sides are the same length. So a picture often shows only two labels. If a rectangle is 8 cm long and 5 cm wide, its sides are 8, 5, 8 and 5 cm:

- Add them all: 8 + 5 + 8 + 5 = 26 cm.
- Or add one length and one width, then double: 8 + 5 = 13, and 13 + 13 = 26 cm.

A square has 4 equal sides, so multiply one side by 4. A square with 6 inch sides has a perimeter of 4 × 6 = 24 inches.

## Finding a missing side

Sometimes you know the perimeter and all the sides except one. Add the sides you know, then subtract from the perimeter.

**Try it.** A playground with five sides has a perimeter of 30 meters. Four sides are 9, 4, 5 and 7 meters. 9 + 4 + 5 + 7 = 25, and 30 − 25 = 5. The missing side is 5 meters.

For a rectangle, remember there are two of each side. A rectangle with a perimeter of 24 m and one side of 8 m: the two 8 m sides make 16 m, which leaves 24 − 16 = 8 m for the other two sides. Each one is 8 ÷ 2 = 4 m.

A common mistake is to subtract only one 8 m side and say the other side is 16 m. Draw the rectangle and label all four sides to check: 8 + 4 + 8 + 4 = 24. It works.

## For grown-ups at home

Perimeter is everywhere at home: the trim around a door, the edge of a placemat, the fence around a yard. Ask, "If an ant walked all the way around this, how far would it go?" Measure a book cover together with a ruler and add the four sides, then try the "add one length and one width, then double" shortcut and see that it gives the same answer. Keep asking whether a question is about the edge (perimeter) or the inside (area).

## Check yourself

1. A triangle has sides of 5 cm, 5 cm and 7 cm. What is its perimeter?
2. What is the perimeter of a rectangle 8 m long and 3 m wide?
3. A square has a perimeter of 24 inches. How long is one side?
4. A rectangle has a perimeter of 14 feet and one side of 4 feet. How long is the side next to it?

*Answers:* (1) 5 + 5 + 7 = 17 cm. (2) 8 + 3 + 8 + 3 = 22 m. (3) 24 ÷ 4 = 6 inches. (4) 14 − 4 − 4 = 6, and 6 ÷ 2 = 3 feet.
