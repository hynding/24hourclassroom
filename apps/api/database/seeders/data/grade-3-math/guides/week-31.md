# Week 31 · Solving Problems With Everything We Know

**Standard:** 3.OA.D.8 Solve two-step word problems with the four operations
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 8, Sections A, B and D, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Solve word problems that need two steps.
- Choose the right operation: add, subtract, multiply or divide.
- Write an equation with a letter for the number you do not know.
- Check an answer with an estimate.
- Use area, perimeter, time, line plots and fractions inside a story.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Two-step problem | A word problem that needs two calculations to answer. |
| Unknown | The number you are trying to find. |
| Equation | A math sentence with an equal sign, like 3 × 8 − 5 = m. |
| Estimate | A close answer made with rounded numbers, used to check your work. |
| Reasonable | An answer that makes sense for the story and is close to your estimate. |

## Four steps for any word problem

1. **Read and say what you need.** "I need to find how many muffins are left."
2. **Draw it.** A tape diagram, an array, a number line or a quick sketch shows the parts and the whole.
3. **Write an equation and solve.** Use a letter for the unknown.
4. **Check.** Does the answer fit the story? Is it close to your estimate? Did you label it?

**Try it.** Mia has 4 bags of 6 oranges. She uses 9 oranges for juice. How many are left?

- Step 1: 4 × 6 = 24 oranges in all.
- Step 2: 24 − 9 = 15 oranges left.
- Equation: n = 4 × 6 − 9, so n = 15.

## Choosing the operation

Listen for what is happening in the story.

| In the story | Operation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Putting amounts together | Add | 245 boys and 230 girls: 245 + 230 |
| Taking away or comparing | Subtract | 500 sheets, 135 used: 500 − 135 |
| Equal groups, find the total | Multiply | 6 tables with 4 chairs each: 6 × 4 |
| Sharing equally or making groups | Divide | 48 cards, 6 per pack: 48 ÷ 6 |

In a two-step problem you often use two different operations. Find the first answer, then use it in the second step.

## Using the whole year

Many problems this week mix in ideas from earlier units.

- **Area and perimeter.** Fence goes around the edge, so it needs the perimeter. Grass or tiles cover the inside, so they need the area. A rectangle 7 feet by 3 feet has perimeter 7 + 3 + 7 + 3 = 20 feet and area 7 × 3 = 21 square feet.
- **Time.** To add minutes, jump to the next hour first. From 9:50, 30 minutes is 10 minutes to 10:00 and 20 more to 10:20.
- **Line plots.** Each X is one thing measured. "Longer than" means only the Xs to the right of that number.
- **Fractions.** 1/2, 2/4, 3/6 and 4/8 all name the same amount.

**Try it.** A rug is 6 feet by 4 feet. Half of it is blue. How many square feet are blue? Area 6 × 4 = 24 square feet. Half of 24 is 12 square feet.

## Is it reasonable?

Round and estimate. If a problem asks for 3 × 9 + 48 and someone gets 175, think: 3 × 9 is about 30, 48 is about 50, so the answer is about 80. 175 is much too big. The exact answer is 27 + 48 = 75.

**A common mistake.** Stopping after the first step. In the orange problem, 24 is how many oranges Mia had, not how many are left. Read the question again before you write your answer. Another mistake is adding numbers just because they appear in the story. Ask "what is happening?" before you choose.

## For grown-ups at home

Make up two-step problems from your day and let your child invent some for you: "We have 3 packs of 8 juice boxes and drink 5. How many are left?" Ask them to draw it, write it with a letter for the unknown, and say why the answer makes sense. Talking through the plan matters more than speed.

## Check yourself

1. 7 boxes of 5 crayons, and 12 more crayons. How many crayons in all?
2. 56 stickers shared by 7 friends, then each friend gives away 3. How many stickers does each friend have now?
3. Is 230 a reasonable answer for 48 + 52 + 31? Why or why not?

*Answers:* (1) 7 × 5 = 35, and 35 + 12 = 47 crayons. (2) 56 ÷ 7 = 8, and 8 − 3 = 5 stickers. (3) No. The numbers are about 50 + 50 + 30 = 130, so 230 is too big. The exact sum is 131.
