# Week 20 · Comparing Fractions

**Standard:** 3.NF.A.3 Find equivalent fractions and compare fractions
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 5, Section D, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Compare two fractions with the **same denominator**.
- Compare two fractions with the **same numerator**.
- Write comparisons with the words "is greater than", "is less than" and "is equal to".
- Explain why we only compare fractions of the same size whole.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Compare | Decide which amount is more, or if they are the same. |
| Is greater than | Is more than. 5/8 is greater than 3/8. |
| Is less than | Is a smaller amount than. 1/6 is less than 1/2. |
| Is equal to | Is the same amount as. 2/4 is equal to 1/2. |
| Same denominator | Both fractions have the same bottom number, like 2/6 and 5/6. |
| Same numerator | Both fractions have the same top number, like 3/4 and 3/8. |

## Same denominator: count the parts

When the denominators match, the parts are the **same size**. So just count them.

Compare 2/6 and 5/6. Both are sixths. Five sixths is more than two sixths. So **5/6 is greater than 2/6**.

On a number line, the fraction farther to the right is greater. 5/6 is to the right of 2/6.

**Try it.** Compare 7/8 and 4/8. Both are eighths, and 7 is more than 4, so 7/8 is greater than 4/8.

## Same numerator: look at the size of the parts

When the numerators match, you have the **same number** of parts. Now ask: whose parts are bigger?

Cut two same-size strips: one into 4 parts and one into 8. Each fourth is bigger than each eighth, because the strip is cut into fewer pieces. So 3 fourths reach farther than 3 eighths: **3/4 is greater than 3/8**.

The unit fractions, from greatest to least, are 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/6, 1/8. The bigger the denominator, the smaller the parts.

**Try it.** Zara runs 2/3 of a mile and Eli runs 2/4 of a mile on the same track. Who runs farther? Both run 2 parts, but thirds are bigger than fourths, so Zara runs farther.

## The same whole

Half of a small pancake is not as much as half of a big pancake. Both are 1/2, but the wholes are different sizes. Fractions can only be compared fairly when they are parts of the **same size whole**: the same strip, the same number line, two pans of the same size.

## Explaining a comparison

A good explanation gives a reason, not just an answer:

- "The parts are the same size, and 5/6 has more of them."
- "Both have 3 parts, but fourths are bigger parts than eighths."
- "On the number line, 7/8 is farther to the right."

## A common mistake

Many students think 1/8 is greater than 1/4 "because 8 is greater than 4". It is the other way around. The 8 means the whole is cut into 8 pieces, so each piece is small. **1/8 is less than 1/4.**

## For grown-ups at home

Share a snack and compare: "Would you rather have 1/4 of this orange or 1/8?" Children often pick 1/8 at first because 8 sounds bigger. Cutting two same-size oranges, one into fourths and one into eighths, settles it fast. Ask your child to say *why*, using the size of the pieces.

## Check yourself

1. Compare 3/6 and 4/6.
2. Compare 1/3 and 1/8.
3. Compare 2/4 and 2/6.
4. Which unit fraction is the greatest: 1/6, 1/2 or 1/4?

*Answers:* (1) 3/6 is less than 4/6. (2) 1/3 is greater than 1/8. (3) 2/4 is greater than 2/6, because fourths are bigger than sixths. (4) 1/2.
