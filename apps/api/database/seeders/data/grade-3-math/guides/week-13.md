# Week 13 · Division as an Unknown Factor and Fact Families

**Standards:** 3.OA.B.6 Understand division as finding an unknown factor · 3.OA.A.4 Find the unknown number in a multiplication or division equation
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 4, Section B, in our own words

## What you will learn

- See that every division is a multiplication with one **factor** missing.
- Use arrays and tape diagrams to show a multiplication and a division at the same time.
- Write the four equations of a **fact family**.
- Find the **unknown number** in a multiplication or division equation.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Factor | A number you multiply. In 4 × 6 = 24, the factors are 4 and 6. |
| Product | The answer to a multiplication. In 4 × 6 = 24, the product is 24. |
| Unknown | The number we do not know yet. It can be shown with ?, a box or a letter. |
| Fact family | Multiplication and division equations that use the same three numbers. |

## Division is a missing factor

Last week you divided by sharing and by making groups. Here is a shortcut: every division asks a multiplication question.

35 ÷ 5 = ? asks: **5 times what number makes 35?**

You may already know 5 × 7 = 35. So 35 ÷ 5 = 7. The answer to the division is the missing factor.

**Try it.** 42 ÷ 6 = ? Think: 6 × ? = 42. Since 6 × 7 = 42, the answer is 7.

If you do not know the fact yet, count by the number you are dividing by. For 42 ÷ 6, count by 6s: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42. That is 7 sixes.

## Arrays show both

Mr. Adebayo planted 28 tomato plants in 4 rows of 7. Look at the array in two ways:

- Rows: 4 rows of 7 make 28, so **4 × 7 = 28**.
- Split into rows: 28 plants in 4 equal rows gives 7 in each row, so **28 ÷ 4 = 7**.

The same picture also shows 7 columns of 4, so 7 × 4 = 28 and 28 ÷ 7 = 4.

A **tape diagram** works the same way. A bar with 6 equal parts and a total of 42 shows 6 × ? = 42 and also 42 ÷ 6 = ?. Each part is 7.

## Fact families

The numbers 4, 7 and 28 make a fact family of four equations:

| Multiplication | Division |
|---|---|
| 4 × 7 = 28 | 28 ÷ 4 = 7 |
| 7 × 4 = 28 | 28 ÷ 7 = 4 |

The biggest number, the product, is always the total. In a division it comes first.

**Try it.** Write the fact family for 3, 9 and 27. Answer: 3 × 9 = 27, 9 × 3 = 27, 27 ÷ 3 = 9, 27 ÷ 9 = 3.

## Finding the unknown number

The unknown can be in any spot. Ask: **is the unknown a factor or the total?**

- 8 × ? = 48. A factor is missing. 8 × 6 = 48, so the unknown is 6.
- 36 ÷ ? = 4. Think 4 × ? = 36. The unknown is 9.
- ? ÷ 5 = 6. Here the total is missing. 5 groups of 6 make 30, so the unknown is 30.

Always check by putting your number back in. Does 36 ÷ 9 = 4? Yes, because 4 × 9 = 36.

## A common mistake

Some students subtract or add when they see an unknown. In 36 ÷ ? = 4, the answer is not 32 (36 − 4) and not 40 (36 + 4). Division and multiplication go together, so turn it into a multiplication question first.

## For grown-ups at home

Knowing multiplication facts makes division much easier, because each division fact is a multiplication fact read backwards. Practise in pairs: say "6 × 8 is 48" and ask "So what is 48 ÷ 6?" Fact family triangles (three numbers at the corners of a triangle, cover one and name it) are a quick game for the car or the dinner table.

## Check yourself

1. What multiplication fact helps you find 56 ÷ 8?
2. Find the unknown: ? × 9 = 63.
3. Write all four equations in the fact family for 5, 6 and 30.
4. Find the unknown: ? ÷ 3 = 7.

*Answers:* (1) 8 × 7 = 56, so 56 ÷ 8 = 7. (2) 7. (3) 5 × 6 = 30, 6 × 5 = 30, 30 ÷ 5 = 6, 30 ÷ 6 = 5. (4) 21, because 3 groups of 7 make 21.
