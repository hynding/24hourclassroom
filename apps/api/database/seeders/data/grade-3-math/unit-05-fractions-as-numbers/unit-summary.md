# Unit 5 · Fractions as Numbers · Summary

**Weeks:** 17 Equal Parts and Unit Fractions · 18 Fractions on the Number Line · 19 Equivalent Fractions and Whole Numbers as Fractions · 20 Comparing Fractions
**Standards:** 3.NF.A.1 · 3.G.A.2 · 3.NF.A.2 · 3.NF.A.3 · **Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 3, Unit 5, in our own words

## The big ideas

1. **A fraction names equal parts of a whole.** The whole must be split into parts that are all the same size. A shape cut into 4 parts of different sizes is not cut into fourths.
2. **Unit fractions are the building blocks.** 1/4 is one of 4 equal parts. 3/4 is three copies of 1/4. The **denominator** (bottom) tells how many equal parts make the whole; the **numerator** (top) tells how many parts we are counting.
3. **Fractions are numbers on the number line.** Split the space from 0 to 1 into equal parts, then count jumps from 0. Fractions keep going past 1: 4/3 is one third past 1.
4. **Different fractions can name the same amount.** 1/2, 2/4, 3/6 and 4/8 all sit at the same point. They are **equivalent fractions**.
5. **Whole numbers can be fractions.** 4/4 = 1, 6/2 = 3 and 5/1 = 5.
6. **Compare fractions of the same size whole.** Same denominator: count the parts. Same numerator: the bigger parts win.

## Strategies with an example

| Strategy | When to use it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Count all parts, then the parts you want | Naming the fraction of a shape | 8 equal slices, 3 eaten: 3/8 eaten |
| Count jumps from 0 | Naming a point on a number line | Line in fourths, dot 3 jumps from 0: 3/4 |
| Line up fraction strips | Finding equivalent fractions | One 1/4 piece covers two 1/8 pieces: 1/4 = 2/8 |
| Line up two number lines | Finding equivalent fractions | 2/3 on top sits above 4/6 below |
| Count the wholes in parts | Writing a whole number as a fraction | 3 wholes in halves: 2 + 2 + 2 = 6 halves, 6/2 |
| Same denominator: compare numerators | Comparing like 2/6 and 5/6 | 5/6 is greater than 2/6 |
| Same numerator: compare part sizes | Comparing like 3/4 and 3/8 | Fourths are bigger, so 3/4 is greater than 3/8 |

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Equal parts | Parts that are all the same size. |
| Unit fraction | A fraction with 1 on top, like 1/6. |
| Numerator | The top number: how many parts we are counting. |
| Denominator | The bottom number: how many equal parts make one whole. |
| Tick mark | A short line that marks a point on a number line. |
| Equivalent fractions | Fractions that name the same amount, like 2/3 and 4/6. |
| Is greater than / is less than | Words for comparing two amounts. |

## Watch out for

- **Counting unequal parts.** If the parts are not the same size, you cannot name them with a fraction like 1/4.
- **Leaving out some parts.** A strip with 3 shaded and 5 white is 3/8 shaded, not 3/5. The denominator counts every part.
- **Counting tick marks instead of parts.** A line from 0 to 1 with 5 tick marks is in fourths.
- **Counting from 1 instead of 0.** On a line from 0 to 2 in thirds, the second mark after 1 is 5/3, not 2/3.
- **Same top number means equal.** 3/4 and 3/8 are not equivalent: eighths are smaller.
- **Bigger denominator means bigger fraction.** It is the opposite: 1/8 is less than 1/2.
- **Different wholes.** Half of a small pancake is less than half of a big one.

## Practice set

1. A rectangle is split into 6 equal parts and 1 is shaded. What fraction is shaded? What fraction is not?
2. How many copies of 1/8 make 5/8?
3. A number line from 0 to 1 is in thirds. Name the fraction at each tick mark between 0 and 1.
4. On a line from 0 to 2 in fourths, a dot is 2 jumps past 1. What fraction is it?
5. Name a fraction in eighths equal to 3/4.
6. Write 2 as a fraction in thirds.
7. Compare 4/6 and 1/6.
8. Compare 2/3 and 2/8.

*Answers:* (1) 1/6 shaded, 5/6 not shaded. (2) 5 copies. (3) 1/3 and 2/3. (4) 6/4. (5) 6/8. (6) 6/3. (7) 4/6 is greater than 1/6. (8) 2/3 is greater than 2/8, because thirds are bigger parts than eighths.
