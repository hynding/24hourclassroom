# Unit 2 · Networks of Exchange, c. 1200–1450 · The night-before sheet

**Exam weight:** about 8–10% of the AP exam · **CED topics:** 2.1 The Silk Roads · 2.2 The Mongol Empire and the Making of the Modern World · 2.3 Exchange in the Indian Ocean · 2.4 Trans-Saharan Trade Routes · 2.5 Cultural Consequences of Connectivity · 2.6 Environmental Consequences of Connectivity · 2.7 Comparison of Economic Exchange
**Themes:** ECN, TEC, ENV, CDI, GOV, SIO · **Reasoning processes:** Causation above all; comparison; continuity and change
**Built from:** Marco Polo, *The Travels of Marco Polo* (Yule–Cordier edition, 1903); John of Plano Carpini in *The Journey of William of Rubruck* (trans. W. W. Rockhill, 1900); Giovanni Boccaccio, *The Decameron* (trans. J. M. Rigg, 1903); the described travels of Ibn Battuta and the Ming records of Zheng He

## The ideas that generate every question

1. **Three networks, one logic.** Silk Roads, Indian Ocean and trans-Saharan routes each grew because demand rose, transport technology improved and some power protected the route. Know the specific version of each cause for each network.
2. **The Mongols are a cause, not just an event.** Their conquests destroyed cities; their peace moved merchants, missionaries, technologies and the plague. Every effect in 2.5 and 2.6 can be traced partly to them.
3. **Islam travelled with trade.** Muslim merchants dominated all three networks, settled as diasporic communities, and were followed by Sufis; rulers at Malacca, Kilwa and in Mali adopted the faith of the people who paid their customs dues.
4. **Connection made and unmade cities.** Hangzhou, Samarkand, Kashgar, Kilwa, Calicut, Malacca, Timbuktu grew on routes; Baghdad and Constantinople declined when routes, rulers or irrigation failed.
5. **Environment moved too.** Bananas, Champa rice, sugar, citrus and cotton changed diets and landscapes; the Black Death followed the Mongol routes and killed perhaps a third of Europe and the Middle East.
6. **Difference is in cargo and control.** Camels carry luxuries; ships carry bulk; the desert carried two necessities. The Mongols ran the Silk Roads; no one ran the ocean; Mali policed the Sahara's southern end.

## The Mongols in six lines

- 1206 Temüjin becomes Chinggis Khan; army in tens, hundreds, thousands, tumens.
- Conquests: Jin China, Khwarazm (1219–1221), Russia (1237–1240), Baghdad (1258), Song (1279); stopped at Ain Jalut (1260), Japan, Vietnam.
- Four khanates by the 1260s; each adapted (Kublai as Chinese emperor; Ilkhans and Golden Horde Muslim; Chagatai nomadic).
- Pax Mongolica c. 1250–1350: yam relay posts, passes for merchants, security.
- Transfers: gunpowder, printing, compass west; Persian astronomy east; artisans moved by force; paper money tried in Persia.
- Costs: Nishapur, Merv, Baghdad depopulated; Persian irrigation collapsed; then the plague.

## Consequences in one list

**Cultural (2.5):** syncretic Islam in Southeast Asia and the Sahel; Theravada Buddhism in mainland Southeast Asia; Neo-Confucianism in Korea, Japan, Vietnam; Greek and Islamic learning into Europe via Spain and Sicily; Persian literary culture from Anatolia to Delhi; Chinese motifs in Persian painting; Djenné's mud-brick mosque; travellers Ibn Battuta, Polo, Kempe.

**Environmental (2.6):** bananas to Africa; Champa rice to China; sugar, citrus, cotton to the Mediterranean; the Black Death (Central Asia 1330s, Black Sea 1346, Mediterranean 1347, Florence and Cairo 1348) with labour shortages, wage rises, weakening serfdom, religious panic and persecution, the end of the Mongol-era overland boom.

**Economic (2.7):** more cities, more state revenue from tolls, more production for export (Chinese porcelain in export styles, Indian cottons), credit and paper money, the Ming withdrawal from the sea in 1433 leaving the ocean to its merchants and, soon, the Portuguese.

## Sources you have read

- **Polo on paper money and on the Mongol army** (Yule–Cordier, 1903): a merchant impressed by efficiency; good for institutions, inflated on numbers.
- **Polo on Sumatra** (Book III ch. 9): "great abundance of treasure, with costly spices"; evidence of why Southeast Asia drew traders.
- **Carpini on Güyük's enthronement** (Rockhill, 1900): a papal envoy who would not kneel; evidence of Mongol ceremony and of how outsiders saw it.
- **Boccaccio on Florence, 1348** (Rigg, 1903): the gavoccioli; "brother was forsaken by brother"; a literary witness, strong on social breakdown, loose on numbers.
- **Ibn Battuta** (described): junks at Calicut, Kilwa's sultan, salt houses at Taghaza, Damascus praying against the plague.

## Comparison and causation phrases that earn points

- "Because the Mongols secured the overland routes, merchants such as Polo and envoys such as Carpini could cross Eurasia, which transferred gunpowder and the compass westward and later enabled European exploration."
- "Unlike the Silk Roads, which carried luxuries by camel, the Indian Ocean moved bulk goods by ship, so its trade was larger and survived the plague."
- "Islam spread along all three networks for the same reason: Muslim merchants dominated them and settled where they traded."
- "The Black Death was an environmental consequence of the same connectivity that produced the cultural ones."

## Mistakes the exam punishes

- Saying the Mongols invented paper money or the compass (they spread them).
- Saying Zheng He founded colonies or that China "closed" after 1433 (the state withdrew; trade continued).
- Treating the Indian Ocean trade as a European creation or the trans-Saharan trade as gold only.
- Naming the Black Death as only European or only an effect, not also a cause (of wage rises, religious change, the Pax's end).
- Comparing without explaining: a difference needs a reason.

## The unit exam

Forty-two items, about 72 minutes, every topic from 2.1 to 2.7 with review of Unit 1. Four source-based multiple-choice sets (Polo on the Mongol army, Boccaccio on the plague, a described account of Ibn Battuta at Calicut and Kilwa, and the three-network comparison table), stand-alone multiple choice, select-all, true/false and fill-in-the-blank items, two SAQs (one on Polo's Sumatra passage, one with no stimulus on causation) and one LEQ on the extent to which the Mongols changed Afro-Eurasian exchange. For the SAQs, label each part and give one specific example with a reason. For the LEQ, state a position on extent, give context before the thesis, and treat both change and continuity.
