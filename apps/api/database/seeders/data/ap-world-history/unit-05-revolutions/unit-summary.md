## The ideas that generate every question

1. **The Enlightenment moved the source of authority.** From God and tradition to reason and consent: Locke's natural rights and revocable trust; Montesquieu's separated powers; Rousseau's general will; Smith's laissez-faire; deism and toleration. Most philosophes were not democrats, and enlightened absolutists used the language without the politics.
2. **Revolutions had material causes and Enlightenment justifications.** Taxation after 1763 (America); fiscal crisis, privilege and bread (France); slavery and the split of the free population (Haiti); creole exclusion and Napoleon in Spain (Latin America). The declarations supplied the words; the grievances supplied the people.
3. **Who led a revolution decided what it changed.** Gentry (America): political change, slavery kept. Lawyers then Jacobins then Napoleon (France): privilege abolished, the Code, empire. The enslaved (Haiti): slavery and the planter class destroyed. Creoles (Latin America): independence, hierarchy kept.
4. **The nation became the unit of politics.** Popular sovereignty required a people; nationalism defined it by language and history, made Greece, Italy and Germany, shook the Ottoman and Habsburg empires, and was used from above by Cavour and Bismarck. The excluded (women at Seneca Falls, the enslaved, Filipinos and Cubans) used the revolutions' own principles.
5. **Britain industrialised first because several things coincided.** Coal near iron and water; an agricultural revolution that fed towns; Atlantic capital, cotton and markets; protected property and patents; high wages and cheap coal. Cotton machines, Cromford, Watt, coke iron, then railways.
6. **Industry spread where its conditions recurred and destroyed handicraft where they did not.** Belgium, Germany (Zollverein, Ruhr), the United States; deindustrialisation of India, Egypt and the Ottoman lands. Steam, rail, steamship and telegraph joined the world's markets; steel, chemicals, electricity and oil (Germany, the United States) made the second industrial revolution.
7. **States that lacked industry tried to build it from above.** Muhammad Ali (stopped by Balta Liman and occupation), the Tanzimat (debt and bankruptcy), Meiji Japan (success: sovereignty, tariffs, Tokugawa literacy, zaibatsu), Witte's Russia (half-success on an unreformed peasantry), Self-Strengthening China (provincial, conservative, beaten in 1895).
8. **Industrial capitalism built institutions and provoked reactions.** Corporations, stock exchanges, the gold standard, transnational firms, free trade for the leader and tariffs for the followers. Luddites, unions, Chartism, Owen, Marx, anarchists; Factory Acts and Bismarck's insurance; Smiles's self-help.
9. **Society was remade by class and city.** Middle and working classes replaced estates; women and children in mills, then separate spheres and schooling; Manchester and cholera, then sewers; real wages flat to 1840, rising after 1850.
10. **Extent depends on where you stand.** The fastest change in history in a few regions; cheap cloth, new taxes and foreign power for most of humanity. Aristocracies, patriarchy, hand farming and unequal exchange persisted.

## The four Atlantic revolutions on one page

| | American (1776) | French (1789) | Haitian (1791–1804) | Latin American (1808–1825) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cause | Imperial taxes after the Seven Years' War | Fiscal crisis; orders; bread; Enlightenment | Slavery; 1789 splits the free population | Creole exclusion; Bourbon reforms; Napoleon removes the king |
| Leaders | Colonial gentry | Third Estate lawyers; Jacobins; Napoleon | Toussaint, Dessalines | Bolívar, San Martín; Hidalgo, Iturbide |
| Text | Declaration of Independence | Rights of Man | Haitian Declaration (described) | Jamaica Letter (described) |
| Social outcome | Slavery kept | Privilege abolished; Code | Slavery abolished; planters destroyed | Castes repealed; hierarchy kept; slavery ended slowly |
| Political outcome | Federal republic | Republic, Terror, Empire, restoration | Independent Black republic; indemnity | Creole republics; caudillos; Brazilian empire |

## State-led industrialisation on one page

| State | Programme | Result | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Egypt (Muhammad Ali, 1805–48) | State cotton, mills, arsenals, conscript army | Failed; cotton supplier; British occupation 1882 | Balta Liman (1838) removed tariffs and monopolies |
| Ottoman Tanzimat (1839–76) | Legal equality, conscription, schools, codes, constitution 1876 | Modern bureaucracy; bankruptcy 1875; constitution suspended 1878 | European debt and control of revenue |
| Meiji Japan (1868–1912) | Charter Oath; samurai abolished; conscription; model mills sold to zaibatsu; Iwakura mission | Only non-Western industrial power by 1900; beat China 1895, Russia 1905 | Sovereignty, tariffs (1899), literacy, commercial base |
| Russia (Witte, 1892–1903) | Gold standard, tariffs, French loans, Trans-Siberian Railway | Heavy industry and factory cities; peasantry unreformed | Autocracy kept; 1905 and 1917 follow |
| China (Self-Strengthening, 1861–95) | Arsenals, shipyards, telegraphs, language schools | Discredited by defeat in 1895 | Provincial, decentralised, court conservatism |

## Sources you have read

- **Locke (1690):** a "state of perfect freedom"; reason teaches that "no one ought to harm another in his life, health, liberty, or possessions." Natural rights and the revocable trust.
- **Rousseau (1762):** "Man is born free; and everywhere he is in chains"; the association in which each "may still obey himself alone"; the general will.
- **Wollstonecraft (1792):** women "rendered weak and wretched by a variety of concurring causes"; must "acquire strength, both of mind and body." Universal reason, universal rights.
- **Smith (1776):** improvements in productive power "the effects of the division of labour"; ten pin-makers, "forty-eight thousand pins in a day."
- **Declaration of Independence (1776):** self-evident truths, unalienable rights, "consent of the governed," the right "to alter or to abolish."
- **Rights of Man (1789):** "born and remain free and equal in rights"; sovereignty "in the nation"; law "the expression of the general will"; office by "virtues and talents."
- **Declaration of Sentiments (1848):** "all men and women are created equal"; "He has compelled her to submit to laws, in the formation of which she had no voice."
- **Bentley (1832):** began at six; "From 5 in the morning till 9 at night"; "Strap us."
- **Manifesto (1848):** "the history of class struggles"; "callous 'cash payment'"; "WORKING MEN OF ALL COUNTRIES, UNITE!"
- **Engels (1845):** "the unfeeling isolation of each in his private interest"; "the war of each against all, is here openly declared."
- **Smiles (1859):** "Heaven helps those who help themselves"; "no laws, however stringent, can make the idle industrious."
- **Described:** Hobbes, Montesquieu, Voltaire, de Gouges, the Haitian Declaration, the Jamaica Letter, the Charter Oath, the Tanzimat, Muhammad Ali, Owen, Chartism, Bismarck's laws, Self-Strengthening.

## Sentences that earn points

- "The American and French revolutions shared a fiscal cause in the debts of the Seven Years' War and a vocabulary in Locke and Rousseau, but differed in outcome because gentry led the first and the Third Estate and then the Paris crowd drove the second."
- "The Haitian Revolution was the only one of the four made by the enslaved, which is why it alone abolished slavery at its foundation and why slaveholding states refused to recognise it."
- "Britain industrialised first not because of coal alone but because coal lay near iron and water in a country with an agricultural surplus, Atlantic capital and markets, secure property, and wages high enough to make machines pay."
- "Meiji Japan succeeded where Muhammad Ali's Egypt failed because Japan kept its sovereignty and tariffs, built on Tokugawa literacy and commerce, and sold its model factories to private firms, while Egypt lost its tariffs in 1838 and its government in 1882."
- "Although industrialisation created new classes, cities and goods in Western Europe and a few other regions, most of the world's people in 1900 still farmed by hand, aristocracies and patriarchal law persisted, and the global division of labour continued the unequal exchange of the Atlantic economy."

## Mistakes the exam punishes

- Treating the Enlightenment as the cause of the revolutions rather than their justification.
- Saying Rousseau and Locke agree, or that either was a democrat in the modern sense.
- Writing that France freed Haiti's slaves; they freed themselves and France ratified it.
- Calling Latin American independence a social revolution outside Mexico's first phase.
- Naming the steam engine as the cause of British industrialisation; it came after cotton, coal, agriculture and capital.
- Forgetting deindustrialisation: India and Egypt are part of the story of industrial spread.
- Saying Japan copied the West or that China rejected Western technology; both borrowed, and the state made the difference.
- Claiming workers' living standards rose from the start, or that laissez-faire meant no state.

## The unit exam

Forty-two items, about 72 minutes, every topic from 5.1 to 5.10 with review of Units 1 to 4. Four source-based multiple-choice sets (Rousseau's Social Contract, the Declaration of the Rights of Man, Smith's pin factory, and the Communist Manifesto), stand-alone multiple choice, select-all, true/false and fill-in-the-blank items, two SAQs (one on the Declaration of Sentiments, one with no stimulus comparing state-led industrialisation) and one LEQ on the extent to which the revolutions of 1750–1900 changed political and social structures. For the SAQs, label each part and give one specific example with a reason. For the LEQ, take a position on extent, give context before the thesis, and address both the political and the industrial revolutions.
