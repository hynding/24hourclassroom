# Attribution

Every study guide, unit summary, flashcard deck and test item in this
directory is **original writing** for this course, organised around the
College Board *AP World History: Modern Course and Exam Description* (topic
codes and titles; learning objectives paraphrased, not copied). The exam
format and the May 2027 changes come from AP Central (the AP World History:
Modern exam page and the AP History Exam Updates page, accessed 2026-10-16).
AP® is a trademark of the College Board, which does not endorse this
material.

Nothing here is adapted from a textbook. OpenStax *World History* is not
used: its current licence is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and its terms bar ingestion by
language models. Nor was text taken from Khan Academy, commercial review
books or College Board released exam questions.

**Quotations** are limited to the public domain: works and translations
published before 1931, and U.S. federal government works (statutes,
treaties, presidential messages and speeches made in office, federal
reports). Each was checked word for word against the host named in the
tables below (Project Gutenberg, Wikisource, the Internet Archive, the
Avalon Project at Yale Law School, the U.S. National Archives, the American
Presidency Project, the Miller Center, the presidential libraries, the
National Diet Library of Japan, the University of Tokyo's World and Japan
database and similar). Excerpts are under 120 words. Where a text could not
be verified it is paraphrased without quotation marks. Copyrighted
post-1930 private writing (Churchill, Mao, Nehru, Nkrumah, Ho Chi Minh,
Fanon, Mandela, Gorbachev, King, Carson, bin Laden and others) is
described, never quoted; the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948)
is quoted only in a phrase of Article 1 and otherwise described.

**Interpretations** used as secondary-source stimuli are written for this
course and labelled so; none puts words in a real historian's mouth.
**Images, maps, posters and photographs** are described in words, not
reproduced. **Data tables** are rounded figures compiled from standard
published estimates (United Nations, World Bank, the Trans-Atlantic Slave
Trade Database, the Maddison Project and standard economic and demographic
histories); they are listed below so a reviewer can spot-check them.

Items that rest on no specific source carry `source: "general knowledge"` in
their YAML so a reviewer can find them.

## Sources deliberately not quoted in weeks 1–18

The following were considered and are described in our own words because no
pre-1931 English translation could be verified, or the text was out of reach
of the tools used: Ibn Battuta's *Rihla* (Gibb's translation, 1929, was not
accessible to check; the Hakluyt volumes are post-1958); Ibn Khaldun's
*Muqaddimah* (Rosenthal, 1958); *The Secret History of the Mongols* (Cleaves,
1982); Ma Huan's *Ying-yai Sheng-lan* (Mills, 1970); the *Ain-i-Akbari*
(Blochmann, 1873, exists but was not reached); the Tokugawa edicts; the Meiji
Charter Oath (standard translation in Tsunoda, de Bary and Keene, 1958);
Bolívar's Jamaica Letter (Bierck and Lecuna, 1951); the Haitian Declaration of
Independence (1804); Olympe de Gouges (1791); Montesquieu (Nugent translation,
1750, exists but was not reached); Hobbes (1651, not reached); Busbecq (Forster
and Daniell, 1881, Project Gutenberg #53629–53630, beyond the fetch limit);
the *Baburnama* (Beveridge, 1922, beyond the fetch limit); the journal of da
Gama's voyage (Ravenstein, 1898, not reached); Bernal Díaz (Maudslay, 1908,
not reached). Any of these could be quoted in a later revision once its
pre-1931 edition has been checked word for word.
