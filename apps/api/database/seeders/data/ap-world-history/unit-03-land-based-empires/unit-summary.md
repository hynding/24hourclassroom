# Unit 3 · Land-Based Empires, c. 1450–1750 · The night-before sheet

**Exam weight:** about 12–15% of the AP exam · **CED topics:** 3.1 Empires Expand · 3.2 Empires: Administration · 3.3 Empires: Belief Systems · 3.4 Comparison in Land-Based Empires
**Themes:** GOV, TEC, CDI, ECN, SIO · **Reasoning processes:** Comparison above all; causation; continuity and change
**Built from:** Niccolò Machiavelli, *The Prince*, ch. XVII (trans. W. K. Marriott, 1908); Martin Luther, the Ninety-five Theses (Holman, 1915); the described Turkish letters of Busbecq, memoirs of Babur, *Ain-i-Akbari* and chronicles of Akbar's court

## The ideas that generate every question

1. **Gunpowder made the empires.** Cannon ended the defensibility of walls and musketeers needed pay, so power concentrated in rulers who could afford both. Songhai, without guns, fell to an army that had them (1591).
2. **Every empire answered three questions: who serves, why obey, how paid.** Dependent elites (devshirme, mansabdars, salaried samurai, intendants, service nobility); legitimacy through religion, ceremony and buildings (caliphate, Mandate of Heaven, divine right, Versailles, Taj Mahal, Qing portraits); and reorganised revenue (tax farming, zamindars, silver tax, serfdom).
3. **Rivalry shaped everything.** Ottoman–Safavid (Sunni–Shia), Safavid–Mughal (Kandahar), Ottoman–Habsburg (Vienna), Qing–Russia (Nerchinsk). No one empire dominated Eurasia as the Mongols had.
4. **Belief systems split and were remade.** Western Christianity divided (Luther, Calvin, Henry VIII) and the Catholic Church reformed (Trent, Jesuits); the Sunni–Shia line became a state frontier; Sikhism arose; Akbar included, Aurangzeb excluded.
5. **Religious policy decided stability.** Millets and Akbar's tolerance held diverse empires together; Safavid conversion made Persia Shia for good; Aurangzeb's orthodoxy and Louis XIV's revocation of Nantes cost their states allies and skills.
6. **Comparison is the skill.** Two empires, two columns, one similarity, one difference, each with a reason.

## Sources you have read

- **Machiavelli, *The Prince* ch. XVII (Marriott, 1908):** safer to be feared than loved, but avoid hatred. A Florentine humanist's advice; use it as a lens, not as evidence for Asia.
- **Luther, Ninety-five Theses (Holman, 1915):** "so soon as the penny jingles into the money-box, the soul flies out"; the pope "whose wealth is to-day greater than the riches of the richest". An academic disputation that printing made a revolution.
- **Busbecq** (described): janissary discipline, promotion without regard to birth; a hostile admirer urging his own side to reform.
- **Babur** (described): wagons chained, matchlocks and cannon, flanking cavalry at Panipat.
- ***Ain-i-Akbari*** (described): mansabdar ranks, surveyed revenue, Akbar as ideal ruler; record and propaganda.
- **Abu'l Fazl and Badauni on Akbar** (described): the same policies praised and condemned; point of view in action.

## Comparison sentences that earn points

- "Both the Ottomans and the Mughals built elites of outside origin (devshirme slaves; Persian and Central Asian mansabdars) because both dynasties ruled populations larger and older than themselves."
- "Where the Safavids forced one faith on their subjects, Akbar sought to stand above all faiths, because Persia's population could be converted while India's Hindu majority could not."
- "The Qing and the Ottomans both ruled through institutions of the conquered (examinations; Byzantine and Islamic administration) while keeping the ruling group separate (banners; devshirme)."
- "Tax farming and zamindars both delegated collection to intermediaries who eroded central control over time."

## Mistakes the exam punishes

- Confusing Safavid (Persia, Shia, Isfahan) with Ottoman (Anatolia and Balkans, Sunni, Istanbul).
- Saying the Manchus were Chinese, or that the Qing abolished the examinations.
- Calling divine right universal; the Mandate of Heaven could be lost and caliphs served God's law.
- Treating Luther as the founder of a church rather than a reformer whose protest split one.
- Describing Sikhism as "a mix"; say a new, syncretic religion drawing on Bhakti and Sufism.
- Comparing without a reason.

## The unit exam

Forty-two items, about 72 minutes, every topic from 3.1 to 3.4 with review of Units 1 and 2. Four source-based multiple-choice sets (Luther's theses, Machiavelli on fear and love, a described passage of Busbecq, and the six-empire comparison table), stand-alone multiple choice, select-all, true/false and fill-in-the-blank items, two SAQs (one on a described account of Panipat, one with no stimulus on religious policy) and one LEQ on the extent to which religion served as a tool of state-building. For the SAQs, label each part and give one specific example with a reason. For the LEQ, take a position on extent, give context before the thesis, and compare at least two empires.
