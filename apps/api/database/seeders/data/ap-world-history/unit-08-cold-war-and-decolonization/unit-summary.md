# Unit 8 · Cold War and Decolonization, 1900–present · The night-before sheet

**Exam weight:** about 8–10% of the AP exam · **CED topics:** 8.1 Setting the Stage for the Cold War and Decolonization · 8.2 The Cold War · 8.3 Effects of the Cold War · 8.4 Spread of Communism After 1900 · 8.5 Decolonization After 1900 · 8.6 Newly Independent States · 8.7 Global Resistance to Established Order After 1900 · 8.8 End of the Cold War · 8.9 Causation in the Age of the Cold War and Decolonization
**Themes:** GOV, ECN, SIO, TEC, CDI · **Reasoning processes:** Causation (the unit's capstone), comparison, continuity and change
**Built from:** the Truman Doctrine (1947); Eisenhower's Chance for Peace (1953), domino press conference (1954) and farewell address (1961); Kennedy's inaugural (1961); Nixon's addresses of 1969 and 1970; the Carter Doctrine (1980); Reagan's speeches of 1983 and 1987; Du Bois, *The Souls of Black Folk* (1903)

## The ideas that generate every question

1. **1945 left two powers and a ruined world.** The United States had half the world's industry and the bomb; the Soviet Union had the largest army and 27 million dead; Europe and its empires were broke. The UN (with the great-power veto) and Bretton Woods (IMF, World Bank, the dollar) were built on the lessons of 1919 and 1929.
2. **The Cold War had three causes and one rule.** Ideology (capitalist democracy against one-party communism), security (Stalin's buffer; the Western fear of a vacuum) and the structure of two powers facing each other. The rule: deterrence made direct war suicidal, so the contest was fought by alliances, arms, aid, propaganda and proxies.
3. **Its structure was built in 1947–49 and tested in crises.** Truman Doctrine, Marshall Plan, Berlin blockade, NATO, the Soviet bomb and China's revolution (1949); then Korea, Hungary and Suez (1956), the Berlin Wall (1961), Cuba (1962), Vietnam, Afghanistan (1979). The Non-Aligned Movement (Bandung 1955) was the third position.
4. **Communism spread where land and nation were at stake.** China (1949) by peasant war; Cuba (1959) by guerrilla war and American hostility; Vietnam (1954, 1975) by anti-colonial and then proxy war. Mao's Great Leap and Cultural Revolution show its costs; the Sino-Soviet split shows it was never one bloc.
5. **Decolonization had similar causes and different processes.** Imperial exhaustion, nationalist mobilisation, superpower and UN pressure everywhere; negotiation where there were no settlers and a credible party (India, Ghana, French West Africa), war where settlers or a stubborn power stood in the way (Algeria, Kenya, Vietnam, Angola). Partition (India, Palestine) made negotiated independence deadly.
6. **New states faced inherited borders, export economies and the Cold War.** Nehru's democracy and plans; Nasser's Suez; Israel and the Arab wars; the Congo's collapse; Nkrumah's Pan-Africanism and coup; Nyerere's ujamaa; the Green Revolution; migration to the old metropoles; coups and one-party states.
7. **Resistance took every form.** Non-violence (Gandhi to King to the early ANC), youth revolt (1968), feminism and environmentalism, religion (Liberation Theology, Iran 1979), and violence (the IRA, the PLO, the Shining Path, al-Qaeda). Compare methods, conditions and results.
8. **The Cold War ended from inside.** A stagnant command economy, the arms burden, Afghanistan, Chernobyl and nationalism made reform necessary; Gorbachev's glasnost and perestroika and his refusal to use force dissolved the bloc (1989) and the Union (1991); Western pressure raised the cost. China's Tiananmen shows the road not taken.

## Sample SAQ sketch

*Using the Truman Doctrine, (a) describe the two ways of life Truman contrasts; (b) explain one way the speech shaped American policy; (c) explain one limit of the policy.* (a) Majority rule with free institutions and elections against minority rule by terror, a controlled press and fixed elections. (b) Containment became a global pledge: Marshall aid, NATO, Korea, Vietnam. (c) The United States backed dictators who were anti-communist (Iran 1953, Guatemala 1954, Chile 1973), contradicting the speech's free institutions; or the pledge led to the Vietnam quagmire.

## Sample LEQ sketch

*Evaluate the extent to which the Cold War shaped the process of decolonization after 1945.* Thesis: the Cold War did not cause decolonization, whose roots lay in nationalism and imperial exhaustion, but it shaped its course decisively: it gave nationalists patrons and weapons (Vietnam, Angola), turned some independence struggles into proxy wars, led both superpowers to overthrow or prop up new governments (Iran, the Congo, Cuba), and offered the Non-Aligned Movement leverage; the extent was great in strategic regions and slight in others (Ghana, French West Africa). Context: the Second World War and the Atlantic Charter. Evidence: Dien Bien Phu and the domino principle; Suez; Lumumba; Bandung; Cuba; Angola. Complexity: cases with little Cold War role; the Cold War's end accelerating apartheid's fall.

## The unit exam

Forty-five items, about 75 minutes, every topic from 8.1 to 8.9 plus review of units 6 and 7. Four source-based multiple-choice sets (the Truman Doctrine against Kennedy's inaugural, Eisenhower's domino principle, the Carter Doctrine and Reagan at the Brandenburg Gate, and a table of decolonization and proxy wars), stand-alone multiple choice, select-all, true/false and fill-in-the-blank items, one SAQ on a described photograph of the Berlin Wall's opening, one SAQ without a source, and one LEQ on causation. For the SAQ, describe what the source shows before explaining it. For the LEQ, rank causes and give a reason for the ranking, contextualize before the thesis, and use evidence from both the Cold War and decolonization.
