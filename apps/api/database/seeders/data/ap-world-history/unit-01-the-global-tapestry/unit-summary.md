# Unit 1 · The Global Tapestry, c. 1200–1450 · The night-before sheet

**Exam weight:** about 8–10% of the AP exam · **CED topics:** 1.1 Developments in East Asia · 1.2 Developments in Dar al-Islam · 1.3 Developments in South and Southeast Asia · 1.4 State Building in the Americas · 1.5 State Building in Africa · 1.6 Developments in Europe · 1.7 Comparison in the Period from c. 1200 to c. 1450
**Themes:** GOV, CDI, ECN, SIO, ENV, TEC · **Reasoning processes:** Comparison above all; continuity and change; causation
**Built from:** Marco Polo, *The Travels of Marco Polo* (Yule–Cordier edition, 1903); Magna Carta (1215); the described accounts of Ibn Battuta, Bernal Díaz and the Codex Mendoza

## The ideas that generate every question

1. **Every region had states; none had the same kind.** The unit asks how rulers gained and kept power in six regions, and the answer is always a mix of bureaucracy, soldiers, religion and revenue in different proportions.
2. **Continuity with innovation.** Song China expanded an old examination system and remade Confucianism; Dar al-Islam kept sharia and scholarship while Turks replaced Arabs as rulers; Hinduism absorbed Bhakti; Europe's Church endured while kings grew.
3. **Religion legitimised rulers everywhere; its institutions differed.** A pope could excommunicate; the ulama could disapprove; Aztec and Inca priesthoods served the ruler.
4. **Revenue shaped the state.** Farmland produced bureaucracies (Song, Inca); trade routes produced ports and tolls (Mali, Kilwa, Malacca); land grants produced feudal independence (Europe, Japan).
5. **Religions spread by trade and preaching more than by armies.** Islam reached West Africa, the Swahili coast and Southeast Asia with merchants and Sufis; Buddhism and Hinduism had reached Southeast Asia the same way earlier.
6. **The Americas built large states in isolation.** Aztec tribute and Inca labour were different solutions to ruling without iron, horses or contact with Afro-Eurasia.

## Comparisons the exam likes

- **Aztec vs Inca:** tribute in goods through subject rulers vs labour (mit'a) through direct administration; markets and cacao vs storehouses and no money; codices vs quipu. Reason: Mesoamerica's existing city-states and markets vs the Andean storehouse economy.
- **Song China vs feudal Europe:** salaried examined officials vs land-holding lords; emperor commanding currency vs king needing consent to tax. Reason: ancient unified state and monetised agriculture vs post-Roman fragmentation and land as pay.
- **Mali vs Delhi Sultanate:** both Muslim elites over non-Muslim majorities using Islam for legitimacy and officials; Mali taxed caravan trade, Delhi taxed farmers.
- **Sea-based vs land-based states:** Srivijaya, Majapahit, Malacca, Kilwa, Venice vs Khmer, Song, Delhi, Inca. Reason: where the revenue was.
- **Church vs ulama:** both judged rulers; one a hierarchy with courts and taxes, the other scholars with learning and reputation.
- **Bhakti, Sufism, lay piety:** devotion from below in three religions, in local languages, bypassing clerical elites.

## Chains to have ready

- **Causation:** Champa rice → population growth → markets and canals → iron, porcelain, paper money → Song commercialisation → goods for Silk Road and Indian Ocean trade (Unit 2).
- **Causation:** Abbasid weakness → Turkic soldiers and migrants take power → Seljuks, Mamluks, Delhi → Mongols destroy Baghdad (1258) → Mamluks hold Egypt → Cairo replaces Baghdad as centre of learning.
- **Causation:** gold and salt on opposite sides of the Sahara → caravan trade → Mali taxes it → rulers adopt Islam → Timbuktu's scholars → Mansa Musa's hajj advertises it.
- **Continuity and change (Europe):** feudal fragmentation c. 1200 → Magna Carta (consent to taxation) → Hundred Years' War forces taxes and armies → parliaments → stronger kings by 1450; Black Death → serfdom declines in the west, tightens in the east.

## Sourcing reminders

- **Marco Polo (1903 edition of a 1298 text):** a Venetian merchant writing to astonish; admires scale and state power; good on trade and money, prone to big numbers.
- **Magna Carta (1215):** barons' demands in a moment of royal weakness; "freemen" are the propertied; evidence of feudal limits, not democracy.
- **Ibn Battuta (described):** a Moroccan jurist judging Mali, India and the Maldives by North African Islamic norms; excellent on law and courts, disapproving of local custom and women's freedom.
- **Codex Mendoza and Spanish accounts (described):** post-conquest copies and memoirs; separate pre-conquest content from Spanish purposes.

## Sample SAQ sketch

*Prompt:* "Identify one way religion supported state power in two regions; explain one difference; explain one reason for the difference."
*Answer in three labelled sentences:* (a) Mali's mansas and European kings both drew legitimacy and literate officials from a universal religion. (b) In Europe the Church was an independent hierarchy that could excommunicate a king, whereas in Mali Islamic scholars served the court and the countryside kept local religion. (c) The Church had grown for a thousand years as the one institution surviving Rome's fall, while Islam reached Mali recently and through trade, so it stayed a court and merchant religion.

## Common errors

- Giving the Inca markets, money or writing; giving the Aztecs direct provincial rule.
- Treating 1258 as the end of Islamic civilisation.
- Saying Islam conquered Southeast Asia or that Mali conquered the Swahili coast.
- Calling serfs slaves or Magna Carta a democratic document.
- Writing "Europe was advanced" for 1200.

## The unit exam

Forty items, about 70 minutes, every topic from 1.1 to 1.7. Four source-based multiple-choice sets (Marco Polo on Hangzhou, a described Spanish account of Inca administration, Magna Carta, and an interpretation of devotional religion written for this course), stand-alone multiple choice, select-all, true/false and fill-in-the-blank items, two SAQs (one on a described Ethiopian church, one with no stimulus on comparison) and one LEQ on the extent to which religion shaped state-building. For the SAQs, answer each part in a separate labelled sentence with one specific example. For the LEQ, take a position on extent, contextualise before the thesis, and compare at least two regions.
