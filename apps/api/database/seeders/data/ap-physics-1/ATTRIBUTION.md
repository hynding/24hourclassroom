# Attribution

Every study guide, unit summary, flashcard deck, quiz, unit exam, the exam-prep
guide and the practice final in this directory is **original writing** for this
course, organised around the College Board *AP Physics 1: Algebra-Based Course
and Exam Description* (CED, effective fall 2024, with the clarifications for
2026-27): its eight units, topic codes and topic titles organise the material,
and what each topic expects of students is paraphrased in the course's own
words, never copied. The May 2027 exam format (42 multiple-choice questions in
85 minutes and four free-response questions in 95 minutes, hybrid digital) and
the exam date are taken from AP Central; the free-response point values come
from the College Board's 2026 AP Physics 1 scoring guidelines (accessed
2026-11-15). AP® is a trademark of the College Board, which does not endorse
this material.

Nothing here is adapted from a textbook, because no algebra-based physics text
was found under a licence this course can build on: OpenStax *College Physics*
and *College Physics for AP Courses* are now CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (OpenStax's
licensing help page) and their terms bar ingestion by language models, and the
other open physics texts the project checked carry non-commercial or
share-alike terms that conflict with this course's CC BY 4.0 licence.

Nor was text taken from Khan Academy, The Physics Classroom, commercial review
books or College Board released exam questions and scoring guidelines. Laws,
definitions and equations (Newton's laws, the work–energy theorem,
conservation of energy, momentum and angular momentum, τ = rF sin θ, the
periods of a mass–spring system and a pendulum, Archimedes' principle, the
continuity and Bernoulli equations) are standard physics and were not copied
from any reference.

**Data.** Every situation and lab data set was invented for this course and
computed to fit the model it illustrates, with g = 9.8 m/s² as on the AP table
of information. None of it is a real measurement, and items that might be
mistaken for real data say "a student" or "a lab group". The only real values
are standard physical constants and the published masses and radii of the
Earth, Moon and Mars (weeks 8 and 26), which are facts, not anyone's
expression. The per-week table below names each invented data set.

**Figures** are described in words or given as tables of values (motion
graphs, free-body diagrams, energy bar charts); the app renders no images or
LaTeX, so mathematics is written in plain Unicode.
