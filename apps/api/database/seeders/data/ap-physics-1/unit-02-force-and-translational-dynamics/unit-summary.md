## The ideas that generate every question

1. **Choose the system first (2.1).** Forces from outside the system are external; forces between parts of it are internal and cancel in pairs. Only external forces change the velocity of the system's centre of mass, x_cm = Σmx/Σm. A system can be treated as a single object when its internal structure does not matter to the question.
2. **A force is an interaction, and a free-body diagram shows every force on one object (2.2).** Each arrow names the object exerting it: Earth (F_g), a surface (F_N and F_f), a rope (T), a spring (F_s). Never draw "force of motion", "inertia" or ma.
3. **Third law: interactions come in pairs (2.3).** The force of A on B equals in size and opposes the force of B on A, at every instant, whatever the masses or the motion. The two forces act on different objects, so they never cancel on one diagram.
4. **First law: zero net force means constant velocity (2.4).** At rest or moving steadily in a straight line, the forces balance. A moving object needs no forward force to keep moving.
5. **Second law: the net force sets the acceleration (2.5).** a = ΣF/m, in the direction of the net force, applied axis by axis. Acceleration, not velocity, points along the net force.
6. **Gravity is universal (2.6).** F_g = Gm₁m₂/r² between any two masses; near a planet's surface F_g = mg, with g = GM/R² the gravitational field strength (9.8 N/kg on Earth). A scale reads the normal force, the apparent weight, which differs from mg whenever the person accelerates vertically.
7. **Friction is the parallel part of a contact force (2.7).** Static friction takes any value up to μ_s F_N to prevent sliding; kinetic friction is μ_k F_N opposite the sliding. Both use the normal force, not the weight.
8. **Springs push back in proportion to their deformation (2.8).** F_s = k\|Δx\|, measured from the relaxed length and directed back toward it. Parallel springs add (k₁ + k₂); series springs add as reciprocals.
9. **Moving in a circle needs a net inward force (2.9).** a_c = v²/r toward the centre; ΣF_in = mv²/r, supplied by real forces. There is no centrifugal force.

## Equations and what each symbol means

| Relation | When to use it |
|---|---|
| x_cm = (m₁x₁ + m₂x₂ + …)/(m₁ + m₂ + …) | locating the centre of mass of a system |
| ΣF = ma (per axis) | any object or system; a = 0 for equilibrium |
| F_g = Gm₁m₂/r², g = GM/R² | gravity between bodies; field strength at a planet's surface |
| F_g = mg | weight near a surface |
| F_f,k = μ_k F_N; F_f,s ≤ μ_s F_N | sliding; gripping (equality only at the point of slipping) |
| F_s = −kΔx | ideal spring |
| k_eq = k₁ + k₂ (parallel); 1/k_eq = 1/k₁ + 1/k₂ (series) | combined springs |
| a_c = v²/r, v = 2πr/T | uniform circular motion |

## Situations to recognise instantly

| Situation | The move |
|---|---|
| Elevator or scale | F_N − mg = ma (up positive): reading above mg when the acceleration is up, below when down, equal at constant velocity |
| Angled pull on a box | F_N = mg − T sin θ; then F_f = μ_k F_N |
| Incline | Axes along and perpendicular to the slope: mg sin θ along, F_N = mg cos θ; slips when tan θ exceeds μ_s |
| Two blocks joined by a string, or touching | System for the acceleration (internal forces cancel), then one block for the string or contact force |
| Hanging mass over a pulley | Tension is less than the hanging weight while the system accelerates |
| Flat curve | Static friction supplies mv²/r; v_max = √(μ_s g r) |
| Hilltop, dip, loop | Inward positive: hilltop mg − F_N = mv²/r; dip F_N − mg = mv²/r; top of loop F_N + mg = mv²/r, minimum speed √(gr) |
| Spring lab | Graph mg against stretch; slope k |

## Representations the exam asks for

- **Free-body diagram:** a dot for the object, one arrow per force starting on the dot, labelled with its source, lengths showing relative sizes. If the object accelerates, the arrows must not balance, and the net force must point along the acceleration.
- **Force-sensor graph:** a rising section (static friction growing), a peak (μ_s F_N), a lower plateau (μ_k F_N).
- **Linearised graphs:** friction against normal force (slope μ_k); spring force against stretch (slope k); inward force against v² (slope m/r) or against 1/r (slope mv²); acceleration against 1/m at fixed force (slope F).
- **Derivations with checks:** derive a symbolic answer, then test it: does it have the right units, does it give the familiar result when a quantity goes to zero (μ → 0, θ → 0 or 90°), and does it change the right way when a quantity doubles?

## Reasoning moves that earn AP points

- Name the system and say which forces are external before using it.
- State Newton's law by name when you use it: "Since the crate moves at constant velocity, the net force is zero by Newton's first law, so friction equals the pull."
- Find the normal force from the perpendicular direction before you write friction.
- For a static-friction question, compute the friction needed and compare it with μ_s F_N.
- In functional-dependence questions, write the expression, then say what happens to the quantity and why ("v_max = √(μ_s g r), so the mass cancels and a heavier car has the same limit").
- In qualitative/quantitative translation questions, make the paragraph and the equation say the same thing, and say explicitly that they agree.

## Common errors

- Third-law pairs drawn on the same object, or claimed to cancel; "the truck pushes harder on the car in a collision".
- A forward force on a thrown or sliding object after contact ends; a net force in the direction of the velocity rather than the acceleration.
- F_N = mg on inclines, in elevators, with angled forces and on curved paths.
- Static friction set to μ_s F_N when nothing is slipping; friction always pointing backward.
- "Heavier objects fall faster"; weight and mass confused (mass in kg, weight in N).
- Spring stretch measured from the wrong reference; series and parallel rules swapped.
- Zero acceleration claimed for constant-speed circular motion; centrifugal force on a diagram; released objects flying outward.
