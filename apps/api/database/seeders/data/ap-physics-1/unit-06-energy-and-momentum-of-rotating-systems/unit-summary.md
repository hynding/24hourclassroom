## The ideas that generate every question

1. **Spinning objects store kinetic energy (6.1).** K = ½Iω². Mass far from the axis moves faster and stores more: at the same M, R and ω a hoop has twice a disk's energy. K ∝ ω², so doubling ω quadruples K.
2. **A torque through an angle does work (6.2).** W = τΔθ with Δθ in radians. The net work by external torques equals ΔK_rot. Forces through the axis, and torques on objects that do not turn, do no work; friction and brake torques do negative work.
3. **Rotational energy belongs in the energy accounting.** A massive pulley, a flywheel or a rolling ball takes a share of the energy, so translational speeds end lower than they would without it. Check any result with the limiting case I → 0.
4. **Angular momentum is the rotational momentum (6.3).** L = Iω for a rigid object; L = mvr⊥ for a point object, even one moving in a straight line. The net external angular impulse τΔt (the area under a τ–t graph) equals ΔL.
5. **Zero net external torque conserves L (6.4).** A spinner pulling in their arms speeds up (I down, ω up) and gains kinetic energy from muscle work; objects that stick in a rotational collision conserve L and lose kinetic energy.
6. **Rolling couples the two motions (6.5).** Without slipping, v_cm = Rω, the contact point is at rest and the top moves at 2v_cm. K = ½M(1 + β)v², with I = βMR². Static friction does no work, so mechanical energy is conserved; only shape (β) decides a rolling race.
7. **Satellites obey gravity, circular motion and both conservation laws (6.6).** Circular orbit: GMm/r² = mv²/r, v = √(GM/r), T = 2π√(r³/GM). U_g = −GMm/r and E = −GMm/(2r). In an elliptical orbit gravity exerts no torque about the planet, so vr is the same at the nearest and farthest points, and mechanical energy is conserved.

## The linear–rotational dictionary

| Translational | Rotational | Link |
|---|---|---|
| mass m | rotational inertia I = Σmr² | I = βMR² for a symmetric shape |
| velocity v | angular velocity ω | v = rω |
| K = ½mv² | K = ½Iω² | rolling: both at once |
| W = FΔx | W = τΔθ | τ = rF sin θ |
| p = mv | L = Iω, or mvr⊥ for a point object | |
| impulse FΔt = Δp | angular impulse τΔt = ΔL | area under F–t or τ–t |
| p conserved if F_ext,net = 0 | L conserved if τ_ext,net = 0 | choose the system to make forces internal |
| inelastic collision: K lost | rotational collision: K lost | |

## Representations to know

- **Energy bar charts (as tables)** with separate rows for K_trans, K_rot, U_g, U_s and energy transferred in or out. A falling block on a massive pulley: U_g at the start becomes K_block plus K_pulley at the end.
- **Torque–time graphs:** the area is ΔL; area below the axis is negative.
- **Before/after tables for angular momentum:** list I, ω and L for each state; L matches, K usually does not.
- **Orbit tables:** r, v and vr at the nearest and farthest points; ½v² − GM/r per kilogram is the same at both.

## Sentences that earn AP points

- **Conservation of angular momentum:** "The axle's force acts at the axis and exerts no torque about it, and the force between the child and the deck is internal, so the angular momentum of the child–carousel system about the axle is conserved."
- **Kinetic energy is not conserved:** "The child slides on the deck before moving with it, so some kinetic energy becomes internal energy, as in a perfectly inelastic collision."
- **Spinner:** "With L fixed, K = L²/(2I) increases as I decreases; the energy comes from the work the skater's muscles do pulling her arms in."
- **Rolling energy:** "The contact point is at rest, so static friction does no work; the ball–Earth system's mechanical energy is conserved."
- **Rolling race:** "Each object's v = √(2gh/(1 + β)) depends only on its shape, so the solid sphere, with the smallest β, wins regardless of mass or radius."
- **Orbit:** "Gravity is the only force, so GMm/r² = mv²/r; the satellite's mass cancels."
- **Elliptical orbit:** "Gravity points at the planet's centre and exerts no torque about it, so mvr is the same at the nearest and farthest points."

## Classic question types and how to attack them

**"Find the speed of a block hanging from a massive pulley."** System: block + pulley + Earth. mgh = ½mv² + ½Iω² with ω = v/R. Check with I → 0.

**"How many turns before the wheel stops?"** Work–energy: τΔθ = ΔK, then divide radians by 2π.

**"A skater pulls in her arms / a child walks inward / clay lands on a turntable."** L_i = L_f; find ω; then compare K with K = ½Iω² and say where the energy came from or went.

**"Rank the objects in a rolling race."** Compare β; the smallest wins. Mass and radius cancel.

**"How high does the rolling ball climb?"** Mgh = ½M(1 + β)v²; it beats a sliding block because its spin energy also becomes height.

**"Compare two orbits."** Proportional reasoning: v ∝ 1/√r, T ∝ r^(3/2), F ∝ 1/r², E = −GMm/(2r). Always measure r from the planet's centre.

**"Derive and check" (QQT and MR).** Write the symbolic expression, then check units, the limiting cases (I → 0, v equal to the rim speed, β → 0) and the direction of each dependence against your paragraph.

## Common mistakes

- Revolutions or degrees in W = τΔθ or v = rω.
- ½Mv² for a spinning or rolling object; forgetting the pulley's energy.
- The wrong rotational inertia for the axis (rod about its end versus its centre).
- Using the distance r instead of the moment arm r⊥ for a straight-line mover.
- Conserving kinetic energy when only angular momentum is conserved, or linear momentum for a pivoted system.
- "Friction does negative work on a rolling ball", or "heavier balls roll down faster".
- Orbit radius measured from the surface; "no gravity in orbit"; "a satellite needs thrust to keep moving"; "a higher orbit is faster".

## Self-test

1. A solid disk (M = 4.0 kg, R = 0.25 m) spins at 10 rad/s. K?
2. A 2.5 N·m torque turns a wheel through 4.0 revolutions. Work?
3. A torque rises from 0 to 6.0 N·m over 2.0 s, then stays at 6.0 N·m for 1.0 s. ΔL?
4. A spinner's rotational inertia drops from 2.4 to 0.80 kg·m² while spinning at 1.0 rad/s. Final ω and the ratio K_f/K_i?
5. A solid cylinder rolls without slipping from rest down a ramp 0.90 m high. Speed at the bottom?
6. Satellite A orbits at r, satellite B at 9r. Compare their speeds and periods.

**Answers.** 1. I = ½(4.0)(0.25)² = 0.125 kg·m²; K = ½(0.125)(100) ≈ 6.3 J. 2. W = 2.5 × 8π ≈ 63 J. 3. ½(2.0)(6.0) + (1.0)(6.0) = 12 kg·m²/s. 4. ω = 3.0 rad/s; K triples (K_f/K_i = 3). 5. v = √(2gh/1.5) = √(2 × 9.8 × 0.90/1.5) ≈ 3.4 m/s. 6. v_B = v_A/3; T_B = 27 T_A.
