## The ideas that generate every question

1. **Vectors carry direction; in one dimension, the sign does it (1.1).** Choose an axis and a positive direction and keep them. Distance, speed and time are scalars; displacement, velocity and acceleration are vectors. A magnitude is never negative.
2. **Velocity is the rate of change of position; acceleration is the rate of change of velocity (1.2).** v_avg = Δx/Δt, a_avg = Δv/Δt. Average speed (distance/time) differs from the size of average velocity whenever the path doubles back.
3. **Signs decide speeding up or slowing down (1.2).** Velocity and acceleration with the same sign: speeding up. Opposite signs: slowing down. Zero velocity does not mean zero acceleration.
4. **Graphs are slopes and areas (1.3).** Slope of x–t = velocity; slope of v–t = acceleration; area under v–t = displacement (below the axis counts negative); area under a–t = change in velocity.
5. **Constant acceleration has four equations (1.3).** They come from the straight-line v–t graph and apply only while a is constant. Free fall is the case a = 9.8 m/s² downward, for every mass, all the way up, at the top, and down.
6. **Velocity depends on the observer; acceleration does not (1.4).** v_AC = v_AB + v_BC. Frames moving at constant velocity relative to each other agree on accelerations and time intervals.
7. **Two dimensions are two one-dimensional problems sharing a clock (1.5).** Resolve vectors with sine and cosine. A projectile moves at constant velocity horizontally and in free fall vertically; the time links them.

## Equations and what each one is for

| Relation | Use it for |
|---|---|
| Δx = x_f − x_i | displacement from positions |
| v_avg = Δx/Δt | average velocity; slope of an x–t graph |
| a_avg = Δv/Δt | average acceleration; slope of a v–t graph |
| v = v₀ + at | constant a, no displacement involved |
| x = x₀ + v₀t + ½at² | constant a, final velocity unknown and unwanted |
| v² = v₀² + 2a(x − x₀) | constant a, no time: stopping distances, maximum heights |
| x − x₀ = ½(v₀ + v)t | constant a, acceleration unknown |
| v_AC = v_AB + v_BC | relative velocity, boats, planes, walkways |
| A_x = A cos θ, A_y = A sin θ | components of any vector |
| A = √(A_x² + A_y²), θ = tan⁻¹(A_y/A_x) | rebuilding a vector |

Projectile on level ground: T = 2v₀ sin θ/g, H = (v₀ sin θ)²/(2g), R = (v₀ cos θ)T. Horizontal launch from height h: t = √(2h/g), landing distance v₀t.

## Representations you must be able to produce and read

- **Motion graphs.** Given one of x–t, v–t, a–t (or a description), produce the others. Straight x–t = constant velocity; curving steeper = speeding up; peak or valley = turnaround. A v–t line crossing the axis = reversal. Constant-acceleration motion has a straight v–t line and a flat a–t line.
- **Tables of data.** Equal steps in position = constant velocity; steps that grow by equal amounts = constant acceleration. Compute interval velocities from position tables and accelerations from velocity tables.
- **Motion diagrams.** Dots at equal times: spacing growing (speeding up), shrinking (slowing), even (constant velocity).
- **Vector diagrams.** Component tables for adding displacements or velocities; right triangles for boats in currents and planes in crosswinds.
- **Trajectory tables.** For a projectile, x grows by equal steps, y rises and falls symmetrically, v_x is constant, v_y drops by 9.8 m/s each second.

## Reasoning moves that earn AP points

- **State the frame and the positive direction** before writing any signed quantity.
- **Functional dependence.** Write the symbolic relation, then scale it: stopping distance ∝ v₀² (double the speed, four times the distance); fall time ∝ √h (four times the height, twice the time); level-ground range ∝ v₀²; horizontal-launch distance ∝ v₀ at fixed height; crossing time of a boat aimed straight across is independent of the current.
- **Limiting cases.** Check symbolic answers: an average speed formula must give v when both speeds equal v; a range formula must give zero at θ = 90°; a crossing time must blow up when the current matches the boat's speed.
- **Linearise lab data.** From rest with constant acceleration, d = ½at²: plot d against t², slope = a/2. For a horizontal launch at fixed height, landing distance against launch speed is a straight line through the origin with slope √(2h/g).
- **Claim, evidence, reasoning.** "The acceleration is not zero at the top, because the velocity changes from positive to negative across that instant at the same rate as everywhere else (evidence from the table)."
- **Show that representations agree.** The slope of your v–t sketch should equal the value on your a–t sketch; the area under v–t should match the displacement you calculated.

## Classic question types and how to attack them

**"Describe the motion from this graph."** Identify what is plotted. Read slopes for rates, areas for accumulations, axis crossings for reversals. Say "speeding up" or "slowing down" from the signs.

**"How far, how fast, how long?" (constant acceleration).** List v₀, v, a, Δx, t; mark the unknown and the unwanted; pick the equation missing the unwanted one. For multi-stage trips, solve each stage, carrying the final velocity of one into the next.

**"Thrown up, dropped, or launched."** Up positive, a = −9.8 m/s². At the top v_y = 0 (but v_x may not be), and the time down to the launch height equals the time up.

**"Two observers" or "boat in a river."** Write the subscript chain. In one dimension use signs; for perpendicular velocities use a right triangle. Time to cross depends only on the across component.

**"Projectile from a height or at an angle."** Resolve v₀. Find the time from the vertical motion; use it horizontally. Use the level-ground formulas only when launch and landing heights match.

## Common errors

- Distance for displacement; averaging two speeds instead of total distance over total time.
- "Negative acceleration means slowing down"; "zero velocity means zero acceleration".
- Reading the height of an x–t graph as speed; forgetting negative area below the v–t axis.
- Dropping the ½ in ½at²; using kinematic equations across a change in acceleration.
- "Heavier objects fall faster"; "the acceleration is zero at the top".
- Adding perpendicular velocities as numbers; reversing subscripts in relative velocity.
- Sine for cosine; a changing horizontal velocity for a projectile; "the velocity is zero at the top" of an angled flight.

## Self-test

1. A car's velocity goes from −15 m/s to −5 m/s in 2.0 s. Find a, and say whether it speeds up or slows down.
2. A v–t graph is a straight line from +6 m/s at t = 0 to −6 m/s at t = 4 s. Find the displacement and the distance.
3. A ball is dropped from 45 m. How long does it fall?
4. A swimmer at 1.5 m/s relative to the water heads straight across a 30 m river with a 0.5 m/s current. How long to cross, and how far downstream?
5. A ball rolls off a 0.80 m table at 2.5 m/s. How far from the table does it land?
6. A projectile launched at 30° lands 50 m away on level ground. Which other angle gives the same range at the same speed?

**Answers.** 1. a = +5.0 m/s²; slowing down (opposite signs). 2. Displacement 0 (equal areas above and below); distance 12 m. 3. √(90/9.8) ≈ 3.0 s. 4. 20 s; 10 m. 5. t = √(1.6/9.8) ≈ 0.40 s, distance ≈ 1.0 m. 6. 60°.
