## The ideas that generate every question

1. **Kinetic energy is a scalar that grows with speed squared (3.1).** K = ½mv² is never negative and has no direction. Double the speed, four times the K; double the mass, twice the K. Its value depends on the reference frame.
2. **Work is energy transferred by a force over a displacement (3.2).** W = Fd cos θ uses only the force component along the displacement. Perpendicular forces do no work; forces against the motion do negative work. For a varying force, work is the area under the force–position graph.
3. **Net work changes kinetic energy (3.2).** W_net = ΔK. It is Newton's second law multiplied by distance, and it never needs the time.
4. **Potential energy belongs to a system (3.3).** It is stored in the configuration of interacting objects through conservative forces: ΔU_g = mgΔy near Earth, U_s = ½kx² for a spring, U_G = −Gm₁m₂/r in general. A single object has none. Only changes in U are physical.
5. **Energy is conserved; the system decides the bookkeeping (3.4).** K_i + U_i + W_ext = K_f + U_f + ΔE_int. With no external work and no friction inside, mechanical energy K + U is constant. Friction inside the system converts mechanical energy into internal energy; nothing is destroyed.
6. **Power is the rate of energy transfer (3.5).** P_avg = ΔE/Δt; for a force, P = Fv cos θ. The same work done faster takes more power. A kilowatt-hour is energy.

## Equations and what each symbol means

| Relation | Use it when |
|---|---|
| K = ½mv² | finding the energy of motion, or a speed from an energy |
| W = Fd cos θ | a constant force acts over a straight displacement |
| W = area under F_∥ versus x | the force changes with position (springs, pushes that ramp up) |
| W_net = ΔK | forces and distances are known, time is not |
| ΔU_g = mgΔy | height changes are small compared with Earth's radius |
| U_s = ½kx² | x measured from the spring's natural length |
| U_G = −Gm₁m₂/r | satellites, planets, any large change in separation |
| K_i + U_i + W_ext = K_f + U_f + ΔE_int | the general energy equation for a chosen system |
| ΔE_int = F_f d | kinetic friction inside the system over a sliding distance d |
| P = ΔE/Δt, P = Fv cos θ | average power; instantaneous power of a force |

Useful results: speed after a frictionless drop h from rest, v = √(2gh); stopping distance against friction, d = v²/(2μ_k g); launch speed from a spring, v = x√(k/m); height of a swing's drop, h = L(1 − cos θ).

## Representations to know cold

**Free-body diagrams, read for work.** For each force, compare its direction with the displacement: along it (positive), against it (negative), perpendicular (zero). Normal forces on level ground and centripetal forces in uniform circular motion do zero work.

**Force–position graphs.** Area = work, counted negative below the axis. For a spring, a straight line through the origin: slope k, triangle area ½kx².

**Energy bar charts (as tables).** One row per state, one column each for K, U_g, U_s, ΔE_int, and W_ext if the system is open. The totals must match (plus any external work). State the system and the zero of U_g first.

**Potential energy versus position graphs.** The force points toward lower U, with size equal to the steepness. Flat means zero force (equilibrium); a minimum of U is where the object moves fastest.

**Energy–time and power–time graphs.** The slope of energy against time is power; the area under power against time is energy.

## Moves that earn AP points

- **Name the system and say what is internal.** "The ball–Earth system is closed, because the only force doing work is gravity, which is internal; the normal force is perpendicular to the motion."
- **Justify conservation.** Do not just write mgh = ½mv²; say why no external work is done and why no energy becomes internal energy.
- **Derive before you compute.** Write v = √(2gh) or d = v²/(2μ_k g) symbolically, then substitute. Partial credit lives in the symbolic step.
- **Check functional dependence and limiting cases.** Mass cancels for drops and sliding distances; d ∝ v²; v ∝ x for a spring launcher; h ∝ 1/m for a vertical spring launch; mgh is the small-height limit of GMm(1/R − 1/(R + h)); at θ = 0 a swing has no speed.
- **Make representations agree.** If your bar chart shows K growing, your equation and your graph must too; if a qualitative answer says "four times as far", the derivation must give d ∝ v².
- **Linearise lab data.** Ramp heights and speeds: graph v² against h, slope 2g. Spring compressions and launch speeds: v against x, slope √(k/m). Lifting a mass at constant motor power: time against mass, slope gh/P. A slope below the prediction points to friction converting energy into internal energy.

## Classic question types

**"How fast at the bottom?"** Closed system, energy conservation, v = √(2gh), independent of mass and path; check whether friction or a spring is involved.

**"How far does it slide or compress?"** Kinetic energy against friction (μ_k mg d) or against a spring (½kx²); watch for energy lost twice when an object crosses a rough patch both ways.

**"Rank the work, the energy or the power."** Write each in symbols, then compare ratios. Same task, less time: same work, more power.

**"Which system has potential energy / is mechanical energy conserved?"** Two or more interacting objects with a conservative force between them; no external work; no friction inside.

**"Draw a bar chart, then write the equation, then sketch the graph."** The three must agree, and the system must be the same in all three.

**"Design an experiment and analyse the data."** Measure vertical heights and speeds (photogate or motion sensor), repeat trials, choose a straight-line graph, compute the slope with units, compare with the prediction and explain the difference physically.

## Common mistakes

- Using the whole force instead of its parallel component; sin where cos belongs.
- Doubling K when the speed doubles; treating K or W as vectors.
- Saying someone holding a box still does work on it.
- Giving potential energy to one object; counting gravity both as U_g and as external work.
- ½kx with no square, or x as the spring's total length.
- Using mgh for satellites; losing the minus sign in −Gm₁m₂/r.
- "Energy is lost to friction" (it becomes internal energy); "a heavier skier reaches the bottom faster".
- Confusing power with energy; assuming a more powerful machine always does more work; using mgv as the power for a car on level ground.
- Treating P = Fv at the final speed as the average power while speeding up.

## Self-test

1. A 0.50 kg ball is thrown at 12 m/s. Its kinetic energy?
2. A 40 N force at 25° above horizontal pulls a box 6.0 m. Work by the force?
3. A spring with k = 120 N/m is stretched 0.25 m. Stored energy?
4. A child slides from rest down a frictionless slide 2.5 m high. Speed at the bottom?
5. A 900 kg car at constant 30 m/s faces 450 N of resistance. Power from the driving force?
6. A 70 kg climber gains 300 m in 20 min. Average power to raise himself?

**Answers.** 1. 36 J. 2. (40)(6.0) cos 25° ≈ 218 J. 3. ½(120)(0.25)² = 3.75 J. 4. √(2 × 9.8 × 2.5) = 7.0 m/s. 5. (450)(30) = 13,500 W. 6. (70)(9.8)(300)/1,200 ≈ 172 W.
