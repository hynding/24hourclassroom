# Unit 7 · Shapes on the Coordinate Plane · Unit summary

**Weeks:** 24 to 27
**Standards:** 5.G.A.1 · 5.G.A.2 · 5.G.B.3 · 5.G.B.4 · 5.OA.B.3
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Unit 7, in our own words

## The big ideas

1. **A coordinate grid names every point with two numbers.** The x-axis runs across, the y-axis runs up, and they meet at the origin, (0, 0). In an ordered pair (x, y), the first number tells how far right and the second tells how far up. (4, 1) and (1, 4) are different points.
2. **Points solve problems.** On a map, points that share a coordinate sit on one grid line, so you subtract to find the distance between them. Rectangle corners line up, so a missing corner borrows one coordinate from each neighbor. On a data graph, the axis titles tell what each coordinate means.
3. **Shapes sit in a hierarchy.** Quadrilaterals, then parallelograms, then rectangles and rhombuses, then squares. Every attribute of a category belongs to all its subcategories, so a square has everything a rectangle has and everything a rhombus has.
4. **Triangles sort the same way.** Every equilateral triangle is isosceles. A right triangle can be isosceles or scalene.
5. **Two patterns make ordered pairs.** Line up matching terms of two rules, use them as (x, y), and graph them. When both start at 0, each term of one pattern is the same number of times the matching term of the other.

## Strategies at a glance

| Strategy | When to use it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Across first, then up | Plotting a point | (6, 2): right 6, up 2 |
| Look down, then across | Reading a point | Above 3, across from 8: (3, 8) |
| Subtract matching coordinates | Distance along a grid line | (2, 7) to (9, 7): 9 − 2 = 7 |
| Add the two legs | A walk that turns a corner | (1, 1) to (5, 4): 4 + 3 = 7 |
| Borrow from each neighbor | Missing corner of a rectangle | (1, 3), (7, 3), (7, 6): fourth is (1, 6) |
| Read the axis titles | Data graphs | (5, 10) on Week/Height: 10 cm in week 5 |
| Go down the hierarchy | Most specific name | 4 equal sides, no right angles: rhombus |
| Always down, sometimes up | Always, sometimes, never | Every square is a rhombus; some rhombuses are squares |
| Match terms in columns | Two patterns | Add 3 and add 6 from 0: (3, 6), (6, 12), (9, 18) |
| Compare with "times" | Relationship between patterns | 6 = 2 × 3, 12 = 2 × 6: B is 2 times A |

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| x-axis, y-axis | The horizontal and vertical number lines of the grid. |
| Origin | The point (0, 0), where the axes meet. |
| Ordered pair | (x, y): how far right, then how far up. |
| Attribute | A feature of a shape you can check, like equal sides. |
| Hierarchy | Categories from most general to most specific. |
| Parallelogram | A quadrilateral with 2 pairs of parallel sides. |
| Rhombus | A parallelogram with 4 equal sides. |
| Rectangle | A parallelogram with 4 right angles. |
| Term | One number in a pattern. |

## Watch out for

- Going up first. In (7, 2), go right 7, then up 2.
- Counting grid lines from 1. The origin is 0, and distances are jumps, not lines.
- Thinking a square is not a rectangle. A rectangle only needs 4 right angles.
- Reading the hierarchy upward. Every square is a rectangle, but only some rectangles are squares.
- Forgetting that equilateral triangles are isosceles.
- Saying two patterns differ by "the same amount more". From 0, the gap grows; the "times" stays the same.
- Putting the Pattern B term first in an ordered pair when Pattern A is on the x-axis.

## Quick practice (answers at the end)

1. Which point is on the x-axis: (0, 5) or (5, 0)?
2. How far apart are (4, 3) and (4, 11)?
3. Three corners of a rectangle are (2, 2), (9, 2) and (9, 6). Where is the fourth corner?
4. "A rhombus is ____ a parallelogram." Always, sometimes or never?
5. A quadrilateral has 4 equal sides and 4 right angles. Give every name that fits.
6. Pattern A starts at 0 and adds 4; Pattern B starts at 0 and adds 12. Write the ordered pair from the 4th terms and the relationship.

*Answers:* (1) (5, 0). (2) 11 − 3 = 8 units. (3) (2, 6). (4) Always. (5) Square, rectangle, rhombus, parallelogram, quadrilateral. (6) The 4th terms are 12 and 36, so (12, 36); each B term is 3 times the A term.
