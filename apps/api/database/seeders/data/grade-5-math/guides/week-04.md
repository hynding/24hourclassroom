# Week 04 · Fractions as Quotients: Sharing Equally

**Standard:** 5.NF.B.3 Understand a fraction as division of the numerator by the denominator
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Unit 2, Section A, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Share things equally and write each person's share as a fraction.
- See that a fraction like 3/4 also means a division, 3 ÷ 4.
- Write any division as a fraction, and any fraction as a division.
- Tell which two whole numbers a share is between when it is more than 1.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Quotient | The answer to a division. In 12 ÷ 4 = 3, the quotient is 3. |
| Share equally | Split an amount so that every person gets the same. |
| Numerator | The top number of a fraction. In a sharing story it is the amount being shared. |
| Denominator | The bottom number of a fraction. In a sharing story it is the number of people sharing. |
| Mixed number | A whole number and a fraction together, like 2 1/3. |

## Sharing makes a fraction

Four friends share 3 granola bars equally. How much does each friend get?

There are not enough bars for each friend to get a whole one. So cut every bar into 4 equal pieces, one piece for each friend. Each piece is 1/4 of a bar.

Now hand out the pieces. From each of the 3 bars, every friend gets 1 piece. So each friend gets 3 pieces, and each piece is 1/4 of a bar. That is **3/4 of a bar**.

We started with a division: 3 bars shared by 4 friends is 3 ÷ 4. We ended with a fraction: 3/4. So **3 ÷ 4 = 3/4**.

**Try it.** Five students share 2 sheets of poster paper equally. How much paper does each student get? *Cut each sheet into 5 equal parts. Each student gets 1 part from each sheet: 2/5 of a sheet. So 2 ÷ 5 = 2/5.*

## Any division can be a fraction

The granola bars show a rule that always works. **A fraction is the numerator divided by the denominator.**

- 5 ÷ 8 = 5/8
- 7 ÷ 3 = 7/3
- 1 ÷ 6 = 1/6

It works the other way too. The fraction 9/4 means 9 ÷ 4.

You already know some of these from whole numbers. 12 ÷ 4 = 3, and 12/4 is also 3. The fraction bar and the division sign do the same job.

Be careful about the order. The amount being shared comes first in the division and goes on top of the fraction. The number of people sharing comes second and goes on the bottom.

**Try it.** Write 4 ÷ 9 as a fraction, and write 10/7 as a division. *4/9 and 10 ÷ 7.*

## When each share is more than 1

Two hikers share 9 liters of water equally. Each hiker gets 9 ÷ 2 = 9/2 liters.

Is that a lot or a little? Find the whole numbers on either side. 8/2 = 4 and 10/2 = 5, so 9/2 is between 4 and 5. Each hiker gets 4 liters and half of another liter: **4 1/2 liters**.

You can also think of the division with a remainder. 9 ÷ 2 = 4 R 1. Each hiker gets 4 whole liters. The leftover 1 liter is shared by 2 people, so each gets 1/2 more. That gives 4 1/2 again.

A number line helps. Mark 0 to 5 and cut each whole into halves. Count 9 halves from 0. You land halfway between 4 and 5.

**Try it.** Three cousins share 7 pancakes. How much does each cousin get, and between which two whole numbers is it? *7 ÷ 3 = 7/3 pancakes. 6/3 = 2 and 9/3 = 3, so it is between 2 and 3. It is 2 1/3 pancakes.*

## More people, smaller shares

If the amount stays the same and more people share it, each share gets smaller. 3 pizzas shared by 4 people is 3/4 of a pizza each. The same 3 pizzas shared by 5 people is only 3/5 of a pizza each.

When the amount is bigger than the number of people, each share is more than 1. When it is smaller, each share is less than 1. When they are the same, each person gets exactly 1.

## A common mistake

Some students flip the numbers. For 3 ÷ 4 they write 4/3. But 4/3 is more than 1 whole, and 4 friends sharing only 3 bars cannot each get more than a whole bar. Always put the amount being shared on top.

Another mistake is to answer with the number of pieces instead of the size of a share. If 3 bars are each cut into 4 pieces, there are 12 pieces. Each friend gets 3 of them, and each piece is 1/4 of a bar, so the share is 3/4 of a bar, not 3 bars.

## For grown-ups at home

This week links two ideas your child already knows: division and fractions. Next time you split a snack, ask: "If 4 of us share 3 muffins, how much does each person get?" Let your child cut and hand out the pieces, then say the share as a fraction (3/4 of a muffin) and as a division (3 ÷ 4). Try a case where each share is more than one, like 5 muffins for 2 people.

## Check yourself

1. Write 6 ÷ 7 as a fraction.
2. Write 11/5 as a division.
3. Eight children share 5 oranges equally. How much does each child get?
4. Between which two whole numbers is 13/4?
5. Who gets more: each of 3 people sharing 2 pies, or each of 6 people sharing 2 pies?

*Answers:* (1) 6/7. (2) 11 ÷ 5. (3) 5 ÷ 8 = 5/8 of an orange. (4) Between 3 and 4, because 12/4 = 3 and 16/4 = 4. It is 3 1/4. (5) Each of the 3 people, who gets 2/3 of a pie. Each of the 6 people gets only 2/6 of a pie.
