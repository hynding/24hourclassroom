# Week 11 · Problem Solving With Fractions and Line Plots

**Standards:** 5.NF.B.6 Solve real-world problems by multiplying fractions and mixed numbers · 5.MD.B.2 Make line plots with fractions and solve problems with them
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Unit 3, Section C, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Decide whether a fraction story needs **multiplication** or **division**.
- Multiply with **mixed numbers** by splitting them into wholes and parts.
- Make and read a **line plot** of measurements in eighths.
- Use a line plot to find totals and to **share a total equally**.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Mixed number | A whole number and a fraction together, like 2 1/2. |
| Line plot | A number line with an X above it for each measurement. |
| Data | The measurements we collect. |
| Total | All the amounts added together. |
| Share equally | Split a total so every part gets the same amount. |

## Multiply or divide?

Ask yourself what you know and what you need.

| The story says | Operation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A fraction **of** an amount | Multiply | 2/3 of 12 eggs: 2/3 × 12 = 8 eggs |
| Some groups, each the **same size** | Multiply | 5 bags of 3/4 pound: 5 × 3/4 = 15/4 pounds |
| A fraction **shared** among people | Divide | 1/2 pizza for 4 kids: 1/2 ÷ 4 = 1/8 pizza each |
| How many pieces **fit** | Divide | 3 feet of ribbon in 1/4-foot pieces: 3 ÷ 1/4 = 12 pieces |

**Try it.** Liam has 4 pounds of clay. He gives 1/2 of it to his sister. Multiply or divide? *1/2 of 4 pounds means multiply: 1/2 × 4 = 2 pounds.*

## Multiplying with mixed numbers

A bike path is 2 1/4 miles long, and Rosa rides it 4 times. How far does she ride?

Split the mixed number into its whole part and its fraction part, multiply each, and add:

- 4 × 2 = 8
- 4 × 1/4 = 4/4 = 1
- 8 + 1 = **9 miles**

The same idea works for area. A patio is 3 1/2 meters by 2 meters. Split it into a 3 by 2 rectangle (6 square meters) and a 1/2 by 2 strip (1 square meter). The area is 6 + 1 = **7 square meters**.

When the other factor is a fraction, it is often easier to write the mixed number as a fraction first. To find 2/3 of 1 1/2 cups, write 1 1/2 as 3/2. Then 2/3 × 3/2 = **6/6 = 1 cup**.

**Is it reasonable?** 2 1/4 × 4 should be a little more than 2 × 4 = 8, and 9 is. 2/3 of 1 1/2 should be less than 1 1/2, and 1 is.

**Try it.** What is 3 × 1 2/3? *3 × 1 = 3 and 3 × 2/3 = 6/3 = 2, so 5.*

## Line plots with eighths

Ms. Bello's class measured how much juice was left in 10 cups, in eighths of a cup:
1/8, 3/8, 3/8, 2/8, 3/8, 5/8, 2/8, 5/8, 3/8, 5/8.

To make a line plot, draw a number line from 0 to 1 and mark every eighth: 0, 1/8, 2/8 and so on to 8/8 = 1. Put one X above the mark for each cup. The plot has 1 X at 1/8, 2 Xs at 2/8, 4 Xs at 3/8 and 3 Xs at 5/8.

Now use it:

- **How many cups?** 1 + 2 + 4 + 3 = 10.
- **Total juice at 3/8?** 4 × 3/8 = 12/8 = 1 4/8 cups.
- **Total juice in all the cups?** 1/8 + 4/8 + 12/8 + 15/8 = 32/8 = **4 cups**.
- **Share it equally?** If all 4 cups of juice were poured back and shared equally among the 10 cups, each would hold 4 ÷ 10 = **4/10 cup**.

That last question is a favorite: find the total first, then divide by the number of Xs.

## A common mistake

When multiplying a mixed number, some students multiply only the whole part. For 3 × 2 1/2 they write 6 1/2, forgetting that each of the 3 groups has its own 1/2. Always multiply **both** parts: 3 × 2 = 6 and 3 × 1/2 = 1 1/2, so 7 1/2.

On a line plot, remember that each X is one thing measured. To find a total, multiply the value by how many Xs are above it.

## For grown-ups at home

Measure something together in eighths of an inch (crayons, leaves or pasta shapes), write down the lengths and build a line plot on paper. Then ask: "If we lined them all up, how long would the row be?" and "If they were all the same length, how long would each one be?"

## Check yourself

1. What is 5 × 1 1/2?
2. What is 1/2 × 2 1/2?
3. Ben has 2 cups of nuts and puts 1/4 cup in each bag. How many bags can he fill?
4. A line plot has 3 Xs at 5/8 inch. What is their total length?
5. Four ribbons total 3 feet. If they were all the same length, how long would each be?

*Answers:* (1) 5 + 2 1/2 = 7 1/2. (2) 1/2 × 5/2 = 5/4, which is 1 1/4. (3) 2 ÷ 1/4 = 8 bags. (4) 3 × 5/8 = 15/8 = 1 7/8 inches. (5) 3 ÷ 4 = 3/4 foot.
