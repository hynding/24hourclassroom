# Week 26 · The Hierarchy of Shapes

**Standards:** 5.G.B.3 Know that a shape has every attribute of the categories it belongs to · 5.G.B.4 Sort two-dimensional figures in a hierarchy
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Unit 7, Section B, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Describe shapes by their **attributes**: sides, parallel sides, equal sides and angles.
- Read a **hierarchy** diagram of quadrilaterals from most general to most specific.
- Explain why a shape has every attribute of each category it belongs to.
- Decide whether a statement like "A rhombus is a square" is always, sometimes or never true.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Attribute | A feature you can check, like 4 equal sides or a right angle. |
| Category | A group of shapes that all share certain attributes. |
| Subcategory | A smaller group inside a category, with more attributes. |
| Hierarchy | Categories arranged from most general at the top to most specific at the bottom. |
| Parallelogram | A quadrilateral with 2 pairs of parallel sides. |
| Rhombus | A parallelogram with 4 sides of equal length. |
| Rectangle | A parallelogram with 4 right angles. |

## Attributes and categories

When we sort shapes, we look only at attributes that can be checked: how many sides, which sides are equal, which are parallel, and what kinds of angles there are. Color, size and which way a shape is turned do not count. A square turned on its corner is still a square.

A **category** is a group of shapes that share some attributes. "Quadrilaterals" is a category: every shape in it has 4 straight sides. Inside it is a **subcategory**, "parallelograms", whose shapes have 4 sides **and** 2 pairs of parallel sides.

## The quadrilateral hierarchy

Here is the hierarchy, from the top down. Each line is a special kind of the line above it.

1. **Quadrilaterals:** 4 sides.
2. **Parallelograms:** quadrilaterals with 2 pairs of parallel sides (and opposite sides equal).
3. **Rectangles:** parallelograms with 4 right angles. **Rhombuses:** parallelograms with 4 equal sides.
4. **Squares:** shapes that are both a rectangle and a rhombus, with 4 right angles and 4 equal sides.

The big rule: **every attribute of a category passes down to all its subcategories.** All parallelograms have opposite sides parallel. Rectangles are parallelograms, so all rectangles have opposite sides parallel too. Squares are rectangles, so they have 4 right angles. Squares are rhombuses, so they have 4 equal sides.

**Try it.** Name every name that fits a square. Square, rectangle, rhombus, parallelogram and quadrilateral: five names.

## Always, sometimes or never

The arrows in a hierarchy work in one direction.

- Going **down** is **always** true: every square is a rectangle, every rhombus is a parallelogram.
- Going **up** is only **sometimes** true: some rectangles are squares, some parallelograms are rhombuses.
- Shapes on different branches are **sometimes** each other: a rectangle is a rhombus only when it is a square.

**Try it.** "A quadrilateral is ____ a parallelogram." Sometimes: only the ones with 2 pairs of parallel sides.

**Try it.** A quadrilateral has 2 pairs of parallel sides, 4 equal sides and no right angles. Its **most specific name** is the lowest box it fits: rhombus.

## Triangles have a hierarchy too

Triangles can be sorted by sides: **equilateral** (3 equal sides), **isosceles** (at least 2 equal sides) and **scalene** (no equal sides). Because isosceles means *at least* 2, every equilateral triangle is also isosceles. Equilateral triangles are a subcategory of isosceles triangles.

Triangles can also be sorted by angles: right, acute and obtuse. The two ways mix. A right triangle with sides 3, 4 and 5 cm is right and scalene. A right triangle whose two shorter sides are both 6 cm is right and isosceles.

**Watch out.** Many people think a square is not a rectangle "because a rectangle is long". A rectangle only needs 4 right angles; nothing says its sides must be different. The other common mistake is reading the hierarchy upward: "every rectangle is a square" is false.

## For grown-ups at home

Cut a few shapes from paper or cereal boxes: a square, a long rectangle, a leaning rhombus, a leaning parallelogram, and a four-sided shape with no parallel sides. Ask your child to sort them into piles for "parallelograms", then "rectangles", then "rhombuses". Notice how the square belongs in every pile. Then try "always, sometimes or never" sentences at dinner: "A rectangle is ____ a square."

## Check yourself

1. What is the most general category in the quadrilateral hierarchy?
2. Is every rhombus a parallelogram?
3. "A rectangle is ____ a rhombus." Always, sometimes or never?
4. A quadrilateral has 4 right angles and sides of 5, 2, 5 and 2 cm. What is its most specific name?
5. True or false: an equilateral triangle is isosceles.

*Answers:* (1) Quadrilaterals. (2) Yes: rhombuses are a subcategory of parallelograms. (3) Sometimes: only when it is a square. (4) Rectangle. (5) True: it has at least 2 equal sides.
