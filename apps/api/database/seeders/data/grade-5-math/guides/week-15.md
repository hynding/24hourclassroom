# Week 15 · Decimals to Thousandths

**Standards:** 5.NBT.A.3 Read, write and compare decimals to thousandths · 5.NBT.A.1 Know a digit is worth 10 times the place to its right and 1/10 of the place to its left
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Unit 5, Section A, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Name a new place after the hundredths: the **thousandths place**.
- Read and write decimals to thousandths in **standard form**, **word form** and **expanded form**.
- Find thousandths on a number line.
- Explain why a digit is worth **10 times** as much as the same digit one place to its right, and **1/10** as much as the same digit one place to its left.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Thousandth | One of 1,000 equal parts of a whole: 1/1000, written 0.001. |
| Thousandths place | The third place after the decimal point. In 0.274, the 4 means 4 thousandths. |
| Standard form | A number written with digits, like 3.405. |
| Word form | A number written in words, like "three and four hundred five thousandths". |
| Expanded form | A number written as the sum of the values of its digits, like 3 + 0.4 + 0.005. |
| Equivalent decimals | Decimals that name the same amount, like 0.7, 0.70 and 0.700. |

## A new place: thousandths

In fourth grade you split a whole into 10 tenths and 100 hundredths. Now take just one of those hundredths and cut it into 10 equal parts. Each tiny part is **one thousandth**. It takes 1,000 of them to make a whole.

- 1/10 is written **0.1** (one tenth).
- 1/100 is written **0.01** (one hundredth).
- 1/1000 is written **0.001** (one thousandth).

Ten thousandths make one hundredth, the same way ten hundredths make one tenth.

**Try it.** Write 41/1000 as a decimal. Forty-one thousandths is 0 tenths, 4 hundredths and 1 thousandth: **0.041**.

## Reading and writing thousandths

To read a decimal, read the digits after the point as a whole number, then say the name of the **last** place. Say "and" for the decimal point.

- 0.538 is "five hundred thirty-eight thousandths".
- 6.072 is "six **and** seventy-two thousandths".

In **expanded form**, write the value of each digit and add them:

- 3.405 = 3 + 0.4 + 0.005. There are no hundredths, so nothing is added for that place.
- With fractions: 0.926 = 9 × 1/10 + 2 × 1/100 + 6 × 1/1000.

Zeros at the end after the decimal point do not change the value. 0.6, 0.60 and 0.600 are **equivalent**: 6 tenths = 60 hundredths = 600 thousandths.

## Thousandths on a number line

Look at the number line from 0.25 to 0.26. That stretch is one hundredth long. Split it into 10 equal parts, and each jump is one thousandth. Counting from 0.25, the jumps land on 0.251, 0.252, 0.253, and so on, up to 0.259 and then 0.26 (which is 0.260).

Count the **jumps**, not the tick marks. Seven jumps past 0.25 is 0.257.

## Ten times and one tenth

Write the number 7.777. Every digit is a 7, but each one is worth something different:

| Place | Ones | Tenths | Hundredths | Thousandths |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Value of the 7 | 7 | 0.7 | 0.07 | 0.007 |

Move one place to the **left**, and the 7 is worth **10 times** as much: 10 × 0.007 = 0.07. Move one place to the **right**, and it is worth **1/10** as much: 1/10 of 0.7 is 0.07.

This pattern is the same one you know from whole numbers, where 70 is 10 times 7. It keeps going to the right of the decimal point.

**Try it.** What is 1/10 of 0.5? One tenth of 5 tenths is 5 hundredths: **0.05**.

**Watch out.** A zero can change everything. 0.25 and 0.025 look alike, but 0.25 is 25 hundredths and 0.025 is only 25 thousandths, which is 1/10 as much. Always check which place each digit sits in. A place-value chart helps: write one digit in each column and the point between the ones and the tenths.

## For grown-ups at home

Decimals to thousandths show up in sports timing (a race won by 0.008 of a second) and in measuring (a 2.375-liter jug). Ask your child to read a decimal out loud using place names: "six and seventy-two thousandths", not "six point zero seven two". Then ask, "Which digit is worth the most? How many times as much is it worth as the digit to its right?" The answer should always be 10.

## Check yourself

1. Write 7/1000 as a decimal.
2. Write 5.209 in word form.
3. Write 8.063 in expanded form.
4. How many thousandths are in 0.03?
5. In 4.444, the 4 in the hundredths place is worth how many times the 4 in the thousandths place?

*Answers:* (1) 0.007. (2) Five and two hundred nine thousandths. (3) 8 + 0.06 + 0.003. (4) 30 thousandths. (5) 10 times (0.04 is 10 × 0.004).
