# Week 09 · Dividing a Unit Fraction by a Whole Number

**Standard:** 5.NF.B.7 Divide unit fractions by whole numbers and whole numbers by unit fractions
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Unit 3, Section B, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Share a **unit fraction** like 1/3 equally into a whole number of parts.
- Show the sharing with a **tape diagram** and a **number line**.
- See why dividing by 4 gives the same answer as taking 1/4 of the amount.
- **Check** a quotient with multiplication.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Unit fraction | A fraction with 1 as its numerator, like 1/3 or 1/8. |
| Divide | Share into equal parts, or find how many equal groups fit. |
| Quotient | The answer to a division problem. |
| Tape diagram | A bar drawn to show an amount and its parts. |
| Equal shares | Parts that are all the same size. |

## Sharing a fraction

Mateo has 1/3 of a loaf of banana bread. He shares it equally with 3 friends, so 4 people get some. How much of the whole loaf does each person get?

We write this as **1/3 ÷ 4**. It asks: if 1/3 is cut into 4 equal parts, how big is each part?

Draw a tape diagram:

1. Draw a bar for the whole loaf and cut it into 3 equal parts. Shade one part: that is 1/3.
2. Cut the shaded part into 4 equal pieces.
3. Ask: how many of those pieces would fill the **whole** bar? If every third were cut into 4 pieces, the bar would have 3 × 4 = **12** pieces.

So each piece is 1/12 of the loaf: **1/3 ÷ 4 = 1/12**.

**Try it.** What is 1/2 ÷ 5? *Cut the half into 5 pieces. Cutting both halves that way makes 10 pieces, so each is 1/10.*

## A quick way to see it

Each time, the whole was cut into more pieces:

| Division | Pieces in the whole | Quotient |
|---|---|---|
| 1/3 ÷ 4 | 3 × 4 = 12 | 1/12 |
| 1/2 ÷ 5 | 2 × 5 = 10 | 1/10 |
| 1/6 ÷ 2 | 6 × 2 = 12 | 1/12 |
| 1/5 ÷ 3 | 5 × 3 = 15 | 1/15 |

To divide a unit fraction by a whole number, **multiply the denominator by the whole number**. The numerator stays 1.

Notice that 1/3 ÷ 4 and 1/4 × 1/3 are both 1/12. Sharing something equally among 4 is the same as taking **1/4 of it**.

## On a number line

To find 1/2 ÷ 3, draw a number line from 0 to 1. Mark 1/2. Now split the space from 0 to 1/2 into 3 equal jumps.

How long is one jump? Keep making jumps of that size past 1/2: it takes **6** of them to reach 1. So each jump is **1/6**, and 1/2 ÷ 3 = 1/6.

**Try it.** On a number line, 1/4 is split into 2 equal jumps. How long is each jump? *8 of those jumps reach 1, so each is 1/8.*

## Check with multiplication

Division and multiplication undo each other. If 1/3 ÷ 4 = 1/12, then 4 shares of 1/12 should put the loaf back together:

**4 × 1/12 = 4/12**, and 4/12 is the same as 1/3. It checks.

Another one: 1/5 ÷ 2 = 1/10. Check: 2 × 1/10 = 2/10 = 1/5. ✓

## How big is the answer?

When you share a fraction among 2 or more people, each share is **smaller** than the fraction you started with. 1/4 ÷ 2 = 1/8, and 1/8 is less than 1/4. The more shares, the smaller each one: 1/2 ÷ 2 = 1/4, but 1/2 ÷ 10 = 1/20.

## A common mistake

Some students multiply the numerator instead: they say 1/3 ÷ 4 = 4/3. But 4/3 is more than a whole loaf! Sharing never makes a share bigger than what you started with. Another mistake is **adding** 3 + 4 to get 1/7. Picture the tape diagram: cutting each of 3 parts into 4 pieces makes 3 × 4 = 12 pieces, not 7.

Also be careful with the order. 1/3 ÷ 4 (share a third among 4) is very different from 4 ÷ 1/3 (how many thirds fit in 4). Next week is about the second kind.

## For grown-ups at home

Use real food: "We have half a pizza and 3 people. What part of the whole pizza does each person get?" Cut it and check by counting how many of those slices would fill a whole pizza. Folding paper strips works too: fold a strip into thirds, then fold one third into 4.

## Check yourself

1. What is 1/4 ÷ 3?
2. What is 1/2 ÷ 6?
3. Two hikers share 1/3 of a bag of raisins equally. What fraction of the bag does each get?
4. Write a multiplication that checks 1/6 ÷ 2 = 1/12.
5. Which is smaller, 1/5 ÷ 2 or 1/5 ÷ 4?

*Answers:* (1) 1/12. (2) 1/12. (3) 1/3 ÷ 2 = 1/6 of the bag. (4) 2 × 1/12 = 2/12 = 1/6. (5) 1/5 ÷ 4 = 1/20, which is smaller than 1/5 ÷ 2 = 1/10.
