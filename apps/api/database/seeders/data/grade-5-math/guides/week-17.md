# Week 17 · Adding and Subtracting Decimals

**Standards:** 5.NBT.B.7 Add, subtract, multiply and divide decimals to hundredths
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Unit 5, Section B, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Add and subtract decimals to hundredths by lining up **place values**.
- **Regroup** 10 hundredths as 1 tenth, and 10 tenths as 1 whole.
- **Decompose** a tenth or a whole when you need more to subtract from.
- Subtract by **counting up** on a number line, and check with an **estimate**.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Sum | The answer to an addition. |
| Difference | The answer to a subtraction. |
| Regroup | Trade 10 of one place for 1 of the next place to the left, like 10 hundredths for 1 tenth. |
| Decompose | Break 1 of a place into 10 of the next place to the right, like 1 tenth into 10 hundredths. |
| Estimate | A close answer found with rounded numbers. |

## Line up the places

When you add whole numbers, you add ones to ones and tens to tens. Decimals work the same way: add **tenths to tenths** and **hundredths to hundredths**. The easy way to do that is to line up the decimal points.

If one number has fewer places, write a zero to hold the empty place. A zero at the end after the decimal point does not change the value.

| | Ones | . | Tenths | Hundredths |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.4 written as 2.40 | 2 | . | 4 | 0 |
| plus 1.36 | 1 | . | 3 | 6 |
| Sum | 3 | . | 7 | 6 |

**Watch out.** Do not line up the last digits. In 3.6 + 2.75, putting the 6 over the 5 treats 3.6 like 0.36 and gives 3.11, which is too small. Line up the points: 3.60 + 2.75.

## Regrouping

Look at 0.46 + 0.38 on a hundredths grid. Shade 46 squares in blue and 38 more in green. Count: 84 squares are shaded, so the sum is **0.84**.

With the standard algorithm, the hundredths make 6 + 8 = 14. That is 1 tenth and 4 hundredths. Write 4 in the hundredths place and **regroup** the 1 tenth into the tenths column. Tenths: 4 + 3 + 1 = 8. The sum is 0.84.

Tenths regroup into ones in the same way. 3.60 + 2.75: tenths 6 + 7 = 13, which is 1 whole and 3 tenths. Ones: 3 + 2 + 1 = 6. The sum is **6.35**.

**Try it.** Nia buys a notebook for $2.85 and a pen for $1.49. Hundredths 5 + 9 = 14, tenths 8 + 4 + 1 = 13, ones 2 + 1 + 1 = 4. She spends **$4.34**.

## Subtracting

To find 0.75 − 0.48, start with the hundredths: 5 − 8 does not work. **Decompose** 1 tenth into 10 hundredths. Now there are 6 tenths and 15 hundredths. 15 − 8 = 7 and 6 − 4 = 2. The difference is **0.27**.

To subtract from a whole number, write zeros first. 5 − 2.37 becomes 5.00 − 2.37. Decompose 5 ones into 4 ones, 9 tenths and 10 hundredths. Then 10 − 7 = 3, 9 − 3 = 6 and 4 − 2 = 2. The answer is **2.63**.

**Watch out.** Taking the smaller digit from the bigger one in each place is a common mistake. 0.75 − 0.48 is not 0.33.

## Counting up on a number line

Subtraction also finds the distance between two numbers. For 3.2 − 1.45, start at 1.45 and jump to friendly numbers:

- 1.45 to 1.5 is **0.05**.
- 1.5 to 2 is **0.5**.
- 2 to 3.2 is **1.2**.

Add the jumps: 0.05 + 0.5 + 1.2 = **1.75**. Check by adding: 1.45 + 1.75 = 3.2.

## Estimate first

Round each number to the nearest whole number before you start. 4.87 + 2.19 is about 5 + 2 = 7. If you get 7.06, that is close to 7, so it makes sense. If you get 70.6 or 0.706, a decimal point is in the wrong place.

## For grown-ups at home

Money makes this real. Hand your child a receipt and ask for the total of two items, then the change from $10 or $20. Ask them to estimate first ("about $8, so about $2 change") and then find the exact answer. Counting up, the way a cashier gives change, is a strong strategy, not a shortcut to avoid.
