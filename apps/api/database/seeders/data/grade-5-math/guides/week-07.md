# Week 07 · Mixed Numbers and Real-World Multiplication

**Standard:** 5.NF.B.6 Solve real-world problems by multiplying fractions and mixed numbers
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Unit 2, Section C, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Multiply a whole number by a mixed number, like 3 × 2 1/4.
- Find the area of a rectangle with mixed-number sides by splitting it into smaller rectangles.
- Multiply mixed numbers by writing them as fractions first.
- Solve real-world problems about recipes, distances and gardens, and check that the answer makes sense.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Mixed number | A whole number and a fraction together, like 2 1/2. |
| Partial products | The smaller products you get when you split a factor into parts and multiply each part. |
| Area model | A rectangle split into parts to show a multiplication. |
| Estimate | A close answer found with friendly numbers, used to check the exact answer. |
| Improper fraction | A fraction whose numerator is at least as big as its denominator, like 9/4. |

## A whole number times a mixed number

A recipe uses 2 1/4 cups of flour. Ana makes 3 batches. How much flour does she need?

**Split the mixed number.** 2 1/4 is 2 and 1/4. Multiply each part by 3:

- 3 × 2 = 6
- 3 × 1/4 = 3/4

Add the partial products: 6 + 3/4 = **6 3/4 cups**.

**Or write a fraction first.** 2 1/4 = 9/4, because 2 wholes are 8 fourths and 1 more fourth makes 9. Then 3 × 9/4 = 27/4. Since 24/4 = 6, that is 6 3/4 cups again.

**Try it.** A ribbon for one bow is 1 2/3 feet long. How much ribbon do 5 bows need? *5 × 1 = 5 and 5 × 2/3 = 10/3 = 3 1/3. 5 + 3 1/3 = 8 1/3 feet.*

## Rectangles with mixed-number sides

A garden bed is 3 1/2 meters long and 1 1/2 meters wide. What is its area?

Draw an area model. Split the long side into 3 and 1/2. Split the short side into 1 and 1/2. That makes 4 smaller rectangles:

| Piece | Sides | Area |
|---|---|---|
| Big | 3 by 1 | 3 |
| Long thin | 3 by 1/2 | 3/2 = 1 1/2 |
| Short thin | 1/2 by 1 | 1/2 |
| Small corner | 1/2 by 1/2 | 1/4 |

Add the pieces: 3 + 1 1/2 + 1/2 + 1/4. The two halves make 1 whole, so the total is 3 + 1 + 1 + 1/4 = **5 1/4 square meters**.

Check with fractions: 3 1/2 = 7/2 and 1 1/2 = 3/2. Multiply the numerators and the denominators: 7/2 × 3/2 = 21/4. Since 20/4 = 5, that is 5 1/4. The two ways agree.

**Try it.** What is the area of a square 2 1/2 feet on each side? *5/2 × 5/2 = 25/4 = 6 1/4 square feet. (Pieces: 4 + 1 + 1 + 1/4.)*

## Real-world problems

**Making less.** A smoothie recipe uses 1 1/2 cups of yogurt. Kofi makes half the recipe. 1/2 × 1 1/2: half of 1 is 1/2, and half of 1/2 is 1/4. So he needs 1/2 + 1/4 = 3/4 cup.

**Going farther.** A walking loop is 1 1/4 kilometers. Sara walks it 4 times. 4 × 1 = 4 and 4 × 1/4 = 1, so she walks 5 kilometers.

**Part of a distance.** A bike trail is 4 1/2 miles long. Leo has ridden 2/3 of it. 2/3 × 9/2 = 18/6 = 3 miles.

## Does the answer make sense?

Before you finish, estimate with whole numbers. For 3 1/2 × 1 1/2, the answer should be more than 3 × 1 = 3 and less than 4 × 2 = 8. Our answer, 5 1/4, fits.

When you find a fraction of something, like 2/3 of a trail, the answer must be less than the whole trail. When you multiply by a whole number bigger than 1, the answer must be bigger than what you started with.

## A common mistake

Some students multiply only the whole numbers and only the fractions: for 2 1/2 × 3 1/2 they say 2 × 3 = 6 and 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4, so 6 1/4. That leaves out the two long thin pieces of the area model, 2 × 1/2 and 1/2 × 3. The right answer is 35/4 = 8 3/4.

## For grown-ups at home

Cooking is the perfect place for this week's math. Pick a recipe with mixed numbers, such as 1 3/4 cups of milk, and ask your child how much you would need for a double batch, or for half a batch. Ask them to estimate first ("about 3 1/2 cups?") and then work it out, by splitting the mixed number or by writing it as a fraction.

## Check yourself

1. What is 4 × 1 3/4?
2. Write 3 1/3 as a fraction.
3. What is the area of a rectangle 2 1/2 meters by 4 meters?
4. A recipe uses 3 1/2 cups of rice. How much is half the recipe?
5. Is 1 1/2 × 2 1/2 more or less than 2? How do you know without working it out?

*Answers:* (1) 4 × 1 = 4 and 4 × 3/4 = 3, so 7. (2) 10/3. (3) 2 × 4 = 8 and 1/2 × 4 = 2, so 10 square meters. (4) Half of 3 is 1 1/2 and half of 1/2 is 1/4, so 1 3/4 cups. (5) More, because both numbers are bigger than 1 × 2 = 2. (It is 3 3/4.)
