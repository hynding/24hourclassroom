# Week 23 · How Big Is the Product? Multiplying as Scaling

**Standard:** 5.NF.B.5 Understand multiplication as scaling
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Unit 6, Section C, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Think of multiplying as **scaling**: making a number bigger or smaller.
- Predict whether a product is greater than, less than or equal to a factor, without multiplying.
- Explain why multiplying by a fraction equal to 1, such as 4/4, makes an equivalent fraction.
- Order products by looking at their **scale factors**.
- Use scaling with recipes, bars and copies of pictures.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Scaling | Resizing a number or a picture by multiplying. |
| Scale factor | The number you multiply by to resize. |
| Product | The answer to a multiplication. |
| Equivalent fractions | Fractions that name the same amount, like 2/3 and 8/12. |

## Three cases

Start with a number, say 12, and multiply it by a scale factor.

| Scale factor | Example | The product is ... |
|---|---|---|
| More than 1 | 5/4 × 12 = 15 | more than 12 |
| Equal to 1 | 4/4 × 12 = 12 | the same as 12 |
| Less than 1 | 3/4 × 12 = 9 | less than 12 |

So you can say how big a product is just by comparing the scale factor with 1. 7/8 × 40 is less than 40, because 7/8 is less than 1. 1 1/2 × 40 is more than 40, because 1 1/2 is more than 1.

**Try it.** Without multiplying, is 0.9 × 250 more or less than 250? *Answer:* Less, because 0.9 is less than 1. Decimals scale the same way as fractions.

## Seeing it on a number line

Mark 0 and 12 on a number line. 3/4 × 12 lands three quarters of the way from 0 to 12, at 9. 5/4 × 12 goes one more quarter past 12, to 15. Factors less than 1 land between 0 and 12; factors more than 1 land past 12.

## Seeing it with bars

Draw a bar 8 cm long. A copy 3/4 times as long is shorter: 6 cm. A copy 5/4 times as long is longer: 10 cm. A copy 1 times as long matches the bar exactly.

## Which number is being scaled?

Either factor can be thought of as the scale factor. In 6 × 3/5, think "3/5 scaled by 6". Since 6 is more than 1, the product is more than 3/5. In 3/4 × 2/3, think "2/3 scaled by 3/4". Since 3/4 is less than 1, the product is less than 2/3.

When both factors are less than 1, the product is less than both of them: 1/2 × 1/3 = 1/6.

## Multiplying by 1 in disguise

4/4, 6/6 and 10/10 are all equal to 1. Multiplying by one of them does not change a number's size, which is why it makes an equivalent fraction:

2/3 × 4/4 = 8/12, and 8/12 is the same amount as 2/3.

That is the same move you used last week to find common denominators.

## Ordering products

**Try it.** Put 4/5 × 9, 9 and 6/5 × 9 in order from least to greatest. *Answer:* 4/5 is less than 1 and 6/5 is more than 1, so the order is 4/5 × 9, 9, 6/5 × 9.

## Recipes and pictures

A recipe that uses 4 cups of flour, made 1 1/2 times, needs 1 1/2 × 4 = 4 + 2 = 6 cups. A photo 15 cm wide, printed at 2/3 the size, is 10 cm wide. Before you work out the answer, say whether it should be bigger or smaller.

## Common mistakes

- **"Multiplying always makes bigger."** Only when the scale factor is more than 1. Half of 10 is 5.
- **Thinking every fraction is less than 1.** 5/4 and 9/5 are more than 1.
- **Forgetting that equal to 1 means no change.** 18 × 7/7 is exactly 18.
- **Not checking.** If 3/5 × 20 came out as 33, it must be wrong: the product has to be less than 20. It is 12.

## For grown-ups at home

Make a game of "bigger, smaller or the same?" Say a number and a scale factor, such as "3/4 times 100" or "1 1/2 times 100", and have your child answer before working it out. Scaling a recipe together (half a batch of pancakes, or one and a half batches) shows why the amounts shrink or grow.

## Check yourself

1. Is 5/6 × 48 more than, less than or equal to 48?
2. Is 2 1/4 × 3/5 more than, less than or equal to 3/5?
3. Is 9/9 × 7/8 more than, less than or equal to 7/8?
4. Put 1/2 × 30, 3/2 × 30 and 30 in order from least to greatest.
5. A recipe uses 6 eggs. How many eggs for 2/3 of the recipe?

*Answers:* (1) Less, because 5/6 is less than 1. (2) More, because 2 1/4 is more than 1. (3) Equal, because 9/9 = 1. (4) 1/2 × 30, 30, 3/2 × 30. (5) 2/3 × 6 = 4 eggs.
