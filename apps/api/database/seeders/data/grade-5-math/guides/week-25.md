# Week 25 · Graphing Points to Solve Problems

**Standard:** 5.G.A.2 Graph points in the first quadrant to solve problems
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Unit 7, Section A, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Use a map on a coordinate grid to find places and plan walks.
- Find the distance between two points that share a coordinate.
- Find a missing corner of a rectangle from its ordered pairs.
- Read what a point means on a graph of real data.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Ordered pair | Two numbers (x, y) that name a point: across first, then up. |
| Scale | What one unit on the grid stands for, like "1 unit = 1 block". |
| Distance along a grid line | How many units apart two points are when you travel straight across or straight up. |
| Axis title | The words on an axis that say what its numbers mean, like "Week" or "Height (cm)". |
| Data | Numbers collected about something real, like a plant's height each week. |

## Maps on a coordinate grid

Many maps put a grid over a town, a park or a school. Each place gets an ordered pair, so anyone can find it. A **scale** tells what one unit means. If 1 unit = 1 block, a point 4 units away is 4 blocks away.

**Try it.** On a map, the gym is at (3, 2) and the library is at (3, 9). Both have x-coordinate 3, so the library is straight above the gym. How far apart are they? Subtract the y-coordinates: 9 − 2 = 7 blocks.

## Distances along grid lines

When two points share a coordinate, they sit on the same grid line, and you can find the distance by subtracting.

- **Same y-coordinate:** the points are side by side on a horizontal line. Subtract the x-coordinates. From (2, 5) to (9, 5) is 9 − 2 = 7 units.
- **Same x-coordinate:** the points are one above the other. Subtract the y-coordinates. From (4, 1) to (4, 8) is 8 − 1 = 7 units.

If you have to turn a corner, add the two parts. From (1, 1) to (5, 4): 4 units across plus 3 units up is 7 units of walking.

**Watch out.** Count the jumps, not the grid lines. From 2 to 6 you touch 5 lines (2, 3, 4, 5, 6) but take only 4 jumps. Subtracting gives the jumps.

## Finding a missing corner

A rectangle's sides run straight across and straight up on the grid. So each corner shares its x-coordinate with one neighbor and its y-coordinate with another.

**Try it.** Three corners of a rectangle are (2, 1), (8, 1) and (8, 4). The fourth corner must be straight above (2, 1), so its x-coordinate is 2. It must be straight across from (8, 4), so its y-coordinate is 4. The fourth corner is (2, 4).

Now you can find side lengths too: 8 − 2 = 6 units across and 4 − 1 = 3 units up. The perimeter is 6 + 3 + 6 + 3 = 18 units.

## Graphs of real data

A coordinate grid can also show data. Suppose Marisol measures a sunflower every week:

| Week | Height (cm) |
|---|---|
| 1 | 3 |
| 2 | 5 |
| 3 | 8 |
| 4 | 8 |

Each row becomes an ordered pair: (1, 3), (2, 5), (3, 8), (4, 8). The axis titles tell you what the numbers mean: the x-coordinate is the week and the y-coordinate is the height.

- The point (3, 8) means "in week 3 the sunflower was 8 cm tall".
- From week 1 to week 3 it grew 8 − 3 = 5 cm.
- Weeks 3 and 4 have the same height, so it did not grow that week.

**Watch out.** Check the scale on each axis. If the y-axis counts by 2s, each grid line is worth 2, and a point halfway between 6 and 8 is at 7.

## For grown-ups at home

Draw a simple grid map of your neighborhood or a favorite park with your child, choosing where the origin goes and what one unit means. Ask questions like "How many blocks from the store to the bus stop?" and "If you walk 3 blocks up from the corner, what ordered pair are you at?" You can also graph something real together, such as the number of pages read each night or the temperature at breakfast, and talk about what each point means.

## Check yourself

1. How far apart are (3, 6) and (10, 6)?
2. How far apart are (5, 2) and (5, 11)?
3. Three corners of a rectangle are (1, 3), (7, 3) and (1, 8). Where is the fourth corner?
4. On a graph with x = day and y = dollars saved, what does the point (4, 12) mean?
5. A walk goes from (0, 0) to (6, 0) to (6, 5) along grid lines. How long is it?

*Answers:* (1) 10 − 3 = 7 units. (2) 11 − 2 = 9 units. (3) (7, 8). (4) On day 4, 12 dollars had been saved. (5) 6 + 5 = 11 units.
