# Week 21 · Adding and Subtracting Fractions with Unlike Denominators

**Standard:** 5.NF.A.1 Add and subtract fractions with unlike denominators
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Unit 6, Section B, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Explain why 1/3 + 1/4 cannot be added as it is written.
- Find a **common denominator** and rewrite fractions as **equivalent fractions**.
- Add and subtract fractions with **unlike denominators**.
- Write sums greater than 1 as mixed numbers.
- Add and subtract mixed numbers, trading a whole when you need to.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Unlike denominators | Denominators that are different, as in 1/3 and 1/4. |
| Common denominator | A denominator both fractions can be rewritten with. |
| Equivalent fractions | Fractions that name the same amount, like 1/4 and 3/12. |
| Mixed number | A whole number and a fraction together, like 1 5/12. |

## Why the parts must match

Last year you added fractions like 2/8 + 3/8 = 5/8. That works because eighths plus eighths are still eighths. But thirds and fourths are different-sized pieces. Adding 1/3 + 1/4 is like adding 1 apple and 1 orange and calling the answer "2 apples."

The fix is to cut both into the **same size pieces**. Draw a rectangle and cut it into 3 columns and 4 rows. Now it has 12 equal parts. One column (1/3) is 4 parts and one row (1/4) is 3 parts:

1/3 + 1/4 = 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12.

## Finding a common denominator

A common denominator is a number that both denominators divide into evenly.

- **Sure way:** multiply the denominators. For 2/5 and 1/3, use 5 × 3 = 15.
- **Smaller way:** list multiples of the bigger denominator until the smaller one fits. For 3/8 and 5/12: 12, 24. 24 is a multiple of 8, so use 24.

Then rewrite each fraction by multiplying its numerator and denominator by the same number. 3/8 = 9/24 because 3 × 3 = 9 and 8 × 3 = 24. The fraction looks different but its size does not change.

**Try it.** What is 3/8 + 5/12? *Answer:* 9/24 + 10/24 = 19/24.

## Subtracting

Subtracting works the same way. Change both fractions to the same denominator, then subtract the numerators.

5/6 − 1/4 = 10/12 − 3/12 = 7/12.

## On a number line

To add 1/2 + 2/3, mark a number line in sixths. 1/2 is 3/6 and 2/3 is 4/6. Start at 3/6 and jump 4 sixths. You land on 7/6, one tick past 1, which is 1 1/6.

## Mixed numbers

Add the whole numbers and the fractions separately, then put them together.

**Try it.** 2 1/2 + 1 2/3. *Answer:* Wholes: 2 + 1 = 3. Parts: 3/6 + 4/6 = 7/6 = 1 1/6. Total: 3 + 1 1/6 = 4 1/6.

When subtracting, the fraction you take away may be bigger than the one you have. Then **trade 1 whole** for a fraction equal to 1.

**Try it.** 4 1/5 − 1 1/2. *Answer:* In tenths: 4 2/10 − 1 5/10. 2/10 is less than 5/10, so trade 1 whole: 4 2/10 = 3 12/10. Now 3 12/10 − 1 5/10 = 2 7/10.

## Common mistakes

- **Adding the denominators.** 1/2 + 1/3 is not 2/5. A quick check: 2/5 is less than 1/2, but the sum must be more than 1/2.
- **Changing the denominator but not the numerator.** 5/6 is 10/12, not 5/12.
- **Forgetting the traded whole.** After 4 2/10 becomes 3 12/10, subtract from 3, not 4.
- **Leaving 7/6 inside a mixed number.** 3 7/6 should become 4 1/6.

## For grown-ups at home

Paper strips work well here. Fold one strip into thirds and another the same length into fourths, then fold both into twelfths to see why 1/3 = 4/12 and 1/4 = 3/12. In the kitchen, ask, "If we use 1/2 cup of milk and 1/3 cup of water, how much liquid is that?" Help your child say the common denominator out loud before adding.

## Check yourself

1. Rewrite 2/3 and 3/4 with a common denominator.
2. 2/3 + 3/4 = ____
3. 7/10 − 1/2 = ____
4. 1 1/4 + 2 1/3 = ____
5. 5 1/3 − 2 3/4 = ____

*Answers:* (1) 8/12 and 9/12. (2) 17/12 = 1 5/12. (3) 7/10 − 5/10 = 2/10, which is 1/5. (4) 3 + 3/12 + 4/12 = 3 7/12. (5) 5 4/12 − 2 9/12 = 4 16/12 − 2 9/12 = 2 7/12.
