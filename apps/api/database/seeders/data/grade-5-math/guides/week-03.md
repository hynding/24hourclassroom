# Week 03 · Volume of Composite Solids

**Standards:** 5.MD.C.5 Find volume with the formulas and by adding volumes
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Unit 1, Section C, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Find the volume of a **composite solid** by splitting it into rectangular prisms and adding.
- Split a solid in more than one way and check that the totals match.
- Find edge lengths that are not labeled.
- Solve real problems about planters, tanks, boxes and stages.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Composite solid | A solid figure made of two or more rectangular prisms put together. |
| Overlap | Space that two parts share. Overlapping parts count that space twice. |
| Additive | Volumes of parts that do not overlap add up to the volume of the whole. |
| Depth | How far a solid goes back from the front; another name for its width. |

## Volume adds up

Many real objects are not one box. An L-shaped bench, a two-level planter and a stage with a step are all **composite solids**. Their volume is still the space they take up, and space **adds up**: if two parts do not overlap, the volume of the whole is the sum of their volumes.

To find the volume of a composite solid:

1. **Split** it into rectangular prisms that do not overlap.
2. Find the **volume of each** prism with length × width × height.
3. **Add** the volumes.

## A worked example

A bookshelf is shaped like a backward L. The whole thing is 9 ft long and 2 ft deep. The tall part on the right is 3 ft long and 7 ft tall. The low part is 2 ft tall.

**Split straight down.**

- The tall part: 3 ft long, 2 ft deep, 7 ft tall. 3 × 2 × 7 = 42 cubic feet.
- The low part: 9 − 3 = **6 ft** long, 2 ft deep, 2 ft tall. 6 × 2 × 2 = 24 cubic feet.
- Total: 42 + 24 = **66 cubic feet**.

**Split straight across, to check.**

- The bottom slab: 9 ft long, 2 ft deep, 2 ft tall. 9 × 2 × 2 = 36 cubic feet.
- The block on top: 3 ft long, 2 ft deep, 7 − 2 = **5 ft** tall. 3 × 2 × 5 = 30 cubic feet.
- Total: 36 + 30 = **66 cubic feet**. The two ways agree.

Notice the two edges we had to work out: 9 − 3 = 6 and 7 − 2 = 5. Before multiplying, trace each part with your finger and name its length, width and height.

As an expression, the first way is (3 × 2 × 7) + (6 × 2 × 2). The parentheses keep each part together.

## Taking a piece away

Sometimes it is easier to start with a big box and subtract. The bookshelf fits in a full box 9 × 2 × 7 = 126 cubic feet. The empty space above the low part is 6 × 2 × 5 = 60 cubic feet. 126 − 60 = 66 cubic feet, the same answer again.

## Volume in real life

**Water in a tank.** A tank is 40 cm long, 30 cm wide and 35 cm tall. It is filled 20 cm deep. Use the height of the **water**: 40 × 30 × 20 = 24,000 cubic centimeters.

**Two boxes together.** A garden has two raised beds. One is 5 ft by 2 ft by 1 ft (10 cubic feet) and the other is 4 ft by 3 ft by 1 ft (12 cubic feet). Together they need 10 + 12 = 22 cubic feet of soil.

**A missing part.** Two boxes hold 90 cubic inches in all. One holds 54 cubic inches. The other holds 90 − 54 = 36 cubic inches. If its base is 3 in by 4 in (12 square inches), it is 36 ÷ 12 = 3 inches tall.

**Common mistake.** Letting the parts overlap. If you take the whole bottom slab **and** the whole tall part, the bottom of the tall part is counted twice. Each bit of space must belong to just one part. Another slip is multiplying the two volumes. Parts of 42 and 24 cubic feet make 42 + 24, not 42 × 24.

## For grown-ups at home

Stack two boxes of different sizes, such as a shoebox on a cereal box, and ask your child to find the space the stack takes up. Measure each box, find each volume and add. Then ask, "Would the answer change if we stacked them the other way?" (No: the same two volumes are added.) Building block towers in an L or step shape and counting the blocks two ways is a great check too.

## Check yourself

1. A solid is made of a box 4 × 3 × 2 and a box 2 × 3 × 5 that do not overlap. What is its volume?
2. A step-shaped solid is 8 m long in all. Its tall part is 3 m long. How long is its low part?
3. A block 5 in by 4 in by 3 in has a piece 2 in by 4 in by 1 in cut away. How much volume is left?
4. Two parts of a solid have volumes of 48 and 15 cubic units. What is the total?

*Answers:* (1) 24 + 30 = 54 cubic units. (2) 8 − 3 = 5 m. (3) 60 − 8 = 52 cubic inches. (4) 48 + 15 = 63 cubic units.
