# Week 10 · Dividing a Whole Number by a Unit Fraction

**Standard:** 5.NF.B.7 Divide unit fractions by whole numbers and whole numbers by unit fractions
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Unit 3, Section B, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Read a division like 3 ÷ 1/4 as "**how many fourths fit in 3?**"
- Show it with a **tape diagram** and with **jumps on a number line**.
- Find the quotient quickly and explain why it is **bigger** than the whole number.
- **Check** with multiplication, and tell 4 ÷ 1/3 apart from 1/3 ÷ 4.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Unit fraction | A fraction with 1 as its numerator, like 1/4. |
| Divisor | The number you divide by. In 3 ÷ 1/4, the divisor is 1/4. |
| Quotient | The answer to a division problem. |
| "How many fit?" | A way to read division: how many groups of this size are in that amount. |

## How many fit?

You already know that 12 ÷ 3 can mean "how many groups of 3 are in 12?" Division by a fraction can mean the same thing.

Coach Amara has 3 liters of water for the team. She pours it into cups that hold 1/4 liter each. How many cups can she fill?

We write **3 ÷ 1/4**. It asks: how many pieces of size 1/4 are in 3?

Draw a tape diagram. Draw 3 bars, one for each liter. Cut every bar into 4 equal parts, because each liter holds 4 fourths. Count all the parts: 4 + 4 + 4 = **12**. So **3 ÷ 1/4 = 12** cups.

**Try it.** How many 1/2-cup scoops of flour are in 5 cups? *Each cup holds 2 scoops, so 5 ÷ 1/2 = 10 scoops.*

## Jumps on a number line

To find 2 ÷ 1/3, draw a number line from 0 to 2 and split each whole into thirds. Start at 0 and jump 1/3 at a time. Count the jumps until you land on 2: 3 jumps reach 1, and 3 more reach 2. That is **6 jumps**, so 2 ÷ 1/3 = 6.

**Try it.** How many jumps of 1/5 get from 0 to 3? *5 jumps for every whole, so 3 × 5 = 15 jumps.*

## A quick way and a check

Look for the pattern:

| Division | Pieces in each whole | Quotient |
|---|---|---|
| 3 ÷ 1/4 | 4 | 3 × 4 = 12 |
| 2 ÷ 1/3 | 3 | 2 × 3 = 6 |
| 5 ÷ 1/2 | 2 | 5 × 2 = 10 |
| 4 ÷ 1/6 | 6 | 4 × 6 = 24 |

To divide a whole number by a unit fraction, **multiply the whole number by the denominator**.

To check, multiply the quotient by the size of each piece. 3 ÷ 1/4 = 12, so 12 × 1/4 should give back the 3 liters: 12 × 1/4 = 12/4 = **3**. ✓

## Why is the answer bigger?

When you divide 3 by 1/4, you are cutting 3 wholes into small pieces and counting them. Small pieces mean many pieces, so the quotient (12) is bigger than 3.

The smaller the unit fraction, the more pieces fit:

- 2 ÷ 1/2 = 4
- 2 ÷ 1/4 = 8
- 2 ÷ 1/8 = 16

## Two kinds of fraction division

Last week and this week used the same numbers in different orders. Compare:

| | 1/3 ÷ 4 | 4 ÷ 1/3 |
|---|---|---|
| It asks | Share 1/3 into 4 equal parts. How big is each part? | How many thirds fit in 4? |
| Story | 4 friends share 1/3 of a cake. | A 4-meter board is cut into 1/3-meter pieces. |
| Answer | 1/12 (a small fraction) | 12 (a whole number of pieces) |

Read the story carefully. Ask: "Am I sharing a fraction, or counting how many pieces fit?"

## A common mistake

Some students divide the whole number by the denominator: they say 3 ÷ 1/4 = 3/4. But 3/4 is less than 1 cup, and Coach Amara has 3 whole liters to pour! Ask yourself whether the answer makes sense: lots of small cups fit in 3 liters. Another mistake is swapping the numbers and solving 1/4 ÷ 3 instead.

## For grown-ups at home

Use measuring cups: "How many 1/3-cup scoops of rice make 2 cups?" Scoop and count together. In the car or on a walk, ask: "How many quarter-hours are in 3 hours?" Then ask your child to check the answer with multiplication.

## Check yourself

1. What is 4 ÷ 1/5?
2. What is 1 ÷ 1/8?
3. A 6-meter rope is cut into pieces 1/2 meter long. How many pieces?
4. Write a multiplication that checks 3 ÷ 1/6 = 18.
5. Which is greater, 2 ÷ 1/3 or 1/3 ÷ 2?

*Answers:* (1) 20. (2) 8. (3) 6 ÷ 1/2 = 12 pieces. (4) 18 × 1/6 = 18/6 = 3. (5) 2 ÷ 1/3 = 6 is greater; 1/3 ÷ 2 = 1/6.
