# Week 14 · Parentheses, Brackets and Multistep Problems

**Standards:** 5.OA.A.1 Use parentheses and brackets in expressions and evaluate them · 5.NBT.B.6 Divide up to four-digit numbers by two-digit numbers
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Unit 4, Section C, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Read and work out expressions with **parentheses** ( ) and **brackets** [ ].
- Write an expression for a story or a tape diagram.
- Plan and solve multistep problems with big numbers, including remainders.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Expression | Numbers and operation signs with no equal sign, like (9 + 6) × 4. |
| Value | The number an expression works out to. |
| Parentheses | The symbols ( ). Work out what is inside them first. |
| Brackets | The symbols [ ]. They group like parentheses and often hold parentheses inside. |

## Parentheses show what comes first

Compare two expressions with the same numbers:

- (9 + 6) × 4: add first, 15 × 4 = **60**.
- 9 + 6 × 4: there are no parentheses, so multiply first, 9 + 24 = **33**.

When an expression has no grouping symbols, multiply and divide before you add and subtract. Parentheses let you change that order.

Moving parentheses can change the value. (20 − 8) − 2 = 10, but 20 − (8 − 2) = 14. When you only add, or only multiply, it does not matter: (4 + 5) + 6 and 4 + (5 + 6) are both 15.

## Brackets: work from the inside out

Brackets hold a group that has another group inside it. Start with the innermost group.

**2 × [15 − (3 + 4)]**

1. Parentheses: 3 + 4 = 7.
2. Brackets: 15 − 7 = 8.
3. Last step: 2 × 8 = **16**.

**Try it.** [(7 + 5) × 4] − 18. 7 + 5 = 12, 12 × 4 = 48, 48 − 18 = **30**.

## From words and pictures to expressions

Words like "then" tell you the order. "Add 9 and 6, then multiply by 4" is (9 + 6) × 4. "Subtract 5 from 32, then divide by 3" is (32 − 5) ÷ 3, which is 9. Watch the order in "subtract 5 from 32": it starts with 32.

A tape diagram of 3 equal parts, each split into 12 and 8, shows 3 × (12 + 8). Its value is 3 × 20 = 60. It also shows 3 × 12 + 3 × 8 = 36 + 24 = 60, so the two expressions are equal.

## Multistep problems

Use a plan:

1. Read the question. What do you need to find?
2. Decide the steps and write one expression with parentheses.
3. Work it out, inside groups first.
4. Check: does the answer fit the story?

**Example.** 187 students and 15 adults go on a trip. Each bus has 48 seats. How many buses are needed?

Expression: (187 + 15) ÷ 48. 187 + 15 = 202, and 202 ÷ 48 = 4 R 10. Four buses leave 10 people without a seat, so the school needs **5 buses**.

How many seats are empty on those 5 buses? 5 × 48 − (187 + 15) = 240 − 202 = **38**. The parentheses matter: 5 × 48 − 187 + 15 would add the adults back in.

**Try it.** A bakery makes 1,440 muffins, packs 12 in a box and puts 15 boxes on a tray. (1,440 ÷ 12) ÷ 15 = 120 ÷ 15 = **8 trays**.

## Common mistakes

- **Working left to right and skipping the groups.** In 2 × [15 − (3 + 4)], start with 3 + 4, not 2 × 15.
- **Multiplying only part of a group.** 2 × [15 − 7] is 2 × 8, not 2 × 15 − 7.
- **Forgetting parentheses when you write.** "Add, then multiply" needs ( ) around the adding.
- **Stopping after one step.** In a multistep problem, check that you answered the question that was asked.

## For grown-ups at home

Parentheses and brackets are how mathematicians write down the order of their thinking. Next year this grows into the order of operations and algebra. At home, ask your child to turn a real plan into one expression: "We have 3 packs of 8 juice boxes and 2 extra. How many?" is 3 × 8 + 2, while "3 packs, each with 8 juice boxes and 2 snacks" is 3 × (8 + 2).

## Check yourself

1. Find the value of 48 ÷ (6 + 2) + 5.
2. Find the value of 3 × [20 − (4 × 2)].
3. Write an expression: add 18 and 15, then multiply by 24.
4. Mr. Reyes has 5 boxes of 36 crayons. He keeps 20 and shares the rest equally among 4 tables. How many crayons does each table get?

*Answers:* (1) 48 ÷ 8 + 5 = 6 + 5 = 11. (2) 4 × 2 = 8, 20 − 8 = 12, 3 × 12 = 36. (3) (18 + 15) × 24, which is 33 × 24 = 792. (4) [(5 × 36) − 20] ÷ 4 = (180 − 20) ÷ 4 = 160 ÷ 4 = 40 crayons.
