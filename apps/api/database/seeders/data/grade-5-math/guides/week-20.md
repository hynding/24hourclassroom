# Week 20 · Converting Measurements and Powers of 10

**Standards:** 5.MD.A.1 Convert measurements within one system · 5.NBT.A.2 Explain patterns when multiplying or dividing by powers of 10
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Unit 6, Section A, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Write powers of 10 with an **exponent**, such as 10³ = 1,000.
- Multiply and divide by 10, 10², 10³ and 10⁴ by moving digits.
- **Convert** a measurement to a smaller unit by multiplying, and to a larger unit by dividing.
- Use metric units, which grow by powers of 10, and customary units, which do not.
- Give conversion answers as decimals or fractions, such as 2.5 m or 1 1/2 ft.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Power of 10 | A number made by multiplying 10s together: 10, 100, 1,000, ... |
| Exponent | The small raised number that tells how many 10s are multiplied. In 10³ it is 3. |
| Convert | Write the same amount in a different unit. |
| Metric system | Units such as meters, grams and liters, which change by powers of 10. |
| Customary system | Units such as feet, pounds and gallons, used in the United States. |

## Powers of 10 with exponents

10² means 10 × 10 = 100. 10³ means 10 × 10 × 10 = 1,000. 10⁴ is 10,000. The exponent matches the number of zeros after the 1.

When you **multiply** by a power of 10, each digit moves left by as many places as the exponent, so it is worth more. When you **divide**, each digit moves right.

- 3.6 × 10³ = 3,600 (the 3 moves from the ones place to the thousands place)
- 475 ÷ 10² = 4.75 (the 4 moves from the hundreds place to the ones place)

**Try it.** What is 0.82 × 10²? *Answer:* 82. Each digit moves 2 places left.

## Metric units and powers of 10

| Larger unit | Smaller unit | As a power of 10 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 kilometer (km) | 1,000 meters (m) | 10³ m |
| 1 meter (m) | 100 centimeters (cm) | 10² cm |
| 1 centimeter (cm) | 10 millimeters (mm) | 10 mm |
| 1 kilogram (kg) | 1,000 grams (g) | 10³ g |
| 1 liter (L) | 1,000 milliliters (mL) | 10³ mL |

Because metric units grow by powers of 10, metric conversions are just digit moves. 4.2 km = 4.2 × 10³ = 4,200 m.

## Two directions

Think about counting the same hallway in big steps and in small steps. Small steps take more of them; big steps take fewer.

- **To a smaller unit, multiply.** 6 m = 6 × 100 = 600 cm.
- **To a larger unit, divide.** 600 cm = 600 ÷ 100 = 6 m.

Dividing often gives a decimal: 2,750 g = 2,750 ÷ 1,000 = 2.75 kg, and 80 cm = 0.8 m.

## Customary units

| Larger unit | Smaller unit |
|---|---|
| 1 mile (mi) | 5,280 feet (ft) |
| 1 yard (yd) | 3 feet (ft) |
| 1 foot (ft) | 12 inches (in) |
| 1 ton | 2,000 pounds (lb) |
| 1 pound (lb) | 16 ounces (oz) |
| 1 gallon (gal) | 4 quarts (qt) |
| 1 quart (qt) | 2 pints (pt) |
| 1 pint (pt) | 2 cups (c) |
| 1 day | 24 hours (hr) |
| 1 hour (hr) | 60 minutes (min) |

These do not use powers of 10, so dividing to a larger unit often leaves a remainder. Turn the remainder into a fraction of the larger unit.

**Try it.** How many feet is 18 inches? *Answer:* 18 ÷ 12 = 1 R 6. The 6 inches are 6/12 = 1/2 foot, so 18 inches = 1 1/2 feet.

**Try it.** A cooler holds 10 quarts. How many gallons? *Answer:* 10 ÷ 4 = 2 R 2. The 2 quarts are 2/4 = 1/2 gallon, so 2 1/2 gallons.

## Tables and number lines

A conversion table can run both ways. If 1 m is 100 cm, then 2.5 m is 250 cm, and 80 cm is 0.8 m. A double number line with gallons on top (0, 1, 2, 3) and quarts below (0, 4, 8, 12) shows that 10 quarts sits halfway between 2 and 3 gallons.

## Common mistakes

- **Going the wrong way.** If you change to a larger unit, the number must get smaller. 300 cm is 3 m, not 30,000 m.
- **Using the wrong fact.** A kilogram is 1,000 grams, not 100. A foot is 12 inches, not 10.
- **Writing a remainder as a decimal.** 18 ÷ 12 = 1 R 6 is not 1.6 feet. Write the remainder over the fact: 6/12 = 1/2, so 1 1/2 feet.
- **Miscounting places.** 10³ moves digits 3 places, not 2.

## For grown-ups at home

Ask your child to read units on food labels and convert them: "This juice is 1.89 L. Is that more or less than 2,000 mL?" or "The recipe needs 3 cups of milk. How many pints is that?" Talk about which way the number should move before working it out. Saying "bigger unit, smaller number" out loud catches most mistakes.

## Check yourself

1. 10³ = ____
2. 5.4 × 10² = ____
3. 3,500 mL = ____ L
4. 30 inches = ____ feet (as a mixed number)
5. How many cups are in 3 pints?

*Answers:* (1) 1,000. (2) 540. (3) 3.5 L. (4) 30 ÷ 12 = 2 R 6, so 2 6/12 = 2 1/2 feet. (5) 3 × 2 = 6 cups.
