# Week 30 · Fractions and Decimals Side by Side

**Standards:** 5.NF.A.1 Add and subtract fractions with unlike denominators · 5.NBT.B.7 Add, subtract, multiply and divide decimals to hundredths
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Unit 8, Section C, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Add and subtract fractions and mixed numbers with **unlike denominators** by finding a **common denominator**.
- Add and subtract decimals by lining up the places.
- Multiply and divide decimals, and use a hundredths grid to see why the answers make sense.
- Switch between fractions and decimals when a problem has both.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Unlike denominators | Denominators that are different, like 3 and 4. |
| Common denominator | A denominator two fractions can share. 12 works for thirds and fourths. |
| Equivalent fractions | Fractions that name the same amount, like 3/4 and 9/12. |
| Mixed number | A whole number and a fraction together, like 3 1/2. |
| Hundredths | 1 of 100 equal parts. 0.24 is 24 hundredths. |
| Estimate | A close answer from rounded numbers, used to check. |

## Fractions with unlike denominators

You can only add pieces of the same size. Thirds and fourths are different sizes, so first rename both as pieces that fit them both. Twelfths work, because 3 × 4 = 12.

**2/3 + 1/4:** 2/3 = 8/12 and 1/4 = 3/12. So 8/12 + 3/12 = 11/12. On a number line split into twelfths, a jump of 8 and a jump of 3 land on 11/12.

Subtracting works the same way. 2/3 − 1/4 = 8/12 − 3/12 = 5/12.

**Mixed numbers.** For 3 1/2 − 1 2/3, use sixths: 3 3/6 − 1 4/6. You cannot take 4/6 from 3/6, so trade one whole for 6/6: 3 3/6 = 2 9/6. Now 2 9/6 − 1 4/6 = 1 5/6.

**Estimate to check.** 1/3 + 3/7 must be less than 1, because each fraction is less than 1/2.

## Adding and subtracting decimals

Line up the places so tenths sit under tenths and hundredths under hundredths. Writing extra zeros helps.

- 4.75 + 2.6 = 4.75 + 2.60 = 7.35.
- 6 − 2.45 = 6.00 − 2.45. Regroup 6.00 as 5 ones, 9 tenths and 10 hundredths. Then 10 − 5 = 5 hundredths, 9 − 4 = 5 tenths and 5 − 2 = 3 ones: 3.55.

## Multiplying decimals

On a hundredths grid, the whole square is 1 by 1. To show 0.6 × 0.4, shade a rectangle 6 tenths wide and 4 tenths tall. It covers 6 × 4 = 24 of the 100 small squares, so 0.6 × 0.4 = 0.24. Tenths times tenths make hundredths.

When one factor is a whole number, split the decimal into parts. 1.25 × 6 = 1 × 6 + 0.25 × 6 = 6 + 1.5 = 7.5.

**Try it.** 3.4 × 2.5. Multiply 34 × 25 = 850. Tenths times tenths make hundredths, so the product is 850 hundredths, which is 8.5. Check: 3 × 2.5 = 7.5, and 3.4 is a little more than 3, so 8.5 makes sense.

## Dividing decimals

Think about what the decimal is made of.

- 4.5 ÷ 9: 4.5 is 45 tenths. 45 tenths ÷ 9 = 5 tenths = 0.5.
- 9.6 ÷ 0.4: How many 4 tenths are in 96 tenths? 96 ÷ 4 = 24.

Check by multiplying back: 9 × 0.5 = 4.5, and 24 × 0.4 = 9.6.

## When a problem has both

Leo walks 1 3/4 miles and then 2/3 mile. In twelfths, that is 1 9/12 + 8/12 = 1 17/12 = 2 5/12 miles. His sister walks 2.75 miles. Since 0.75 = 3/4 = 9/12, she walks 2 9/12 miles, which is 4/12 = 1/3 mile farther. Change everything to fractions, or everything to decimals, before you compare.

**A common mistake.** 1/2 + 1/3 is not 2/5. Adding the numerators and the denominators gives an answer smaller than 1/2, which cannot be right. Rename as sixths: 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6. With decimals, a common slip is 0.3 × 0.2 = 0.6. Three tenths of two tenths is much smaller than either one: 0.06.

## For grown-ups at home

Cooking and shopping are full of this week's math. Ask, "We need 3/4 cup of milk and 1/3 cup of water. Is that more or less than a cup?" At the store, round prices to estimate a total before the register does, then compare. Let your child explain why an answer is reasonable, not just what it is.
