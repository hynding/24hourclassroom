# Week 13 · Dividing by Two-Digit Numbers

**Standard:** 5.NBT.B.6 Divide up to four-digit numbers by two-digit numbers
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Unit 4, Section B, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Divide three- and four-digit numbers by a two-digit number with **partial quotients**.
- Show a division with an area diagram.
- Estimate a quotient before you divide.
- Decide what a remainder means in a story, and check by multiplying.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Dividend | The number being divided. In 1,512 ÷ 24, it is 1,512. |
| Divisor | The number you divide by. In 1,512 ÷ 24, it is 24. |
| Quotient | The answer to a division. |
| Partial quotient | The number of groups taken away in one step. |
| Remainder | What is left over when the groups cannot use up the dividend. |

## Make a list of friendly multiples

Dividing by 24 asks "how many 24s fit?" Start with a short list of easy multiples:

- 24 × 10 = 240
- 24 × 20 = 480
- 24 × 50 = 1,200
- 24 × 100 = 2,400

Times 10 and times 100 are quick. Doubling and halving give more: 24 × 5 is half of 240, which is 120.

## Partial quotients

Take away a friendly multiple of the divisor, write how many groups it was, and keep going until what is left is smaller than the divisor.

**1,512 ÷ 24**

- Take away 1,200 (50 groups). 1,512 − 1,200 = 312 left.
- Take away 240 (10 groups). 312 − 240 = 72 left.
- Take away 72 (3 groups). Nothing is left.

Add the groups: 50 + 10 + 3 = **63**. Check: 24 × 63 = 1,512.

**Try it.** 4,176 ÷ 48. Take away 2,400 (50 groups), leaving 1,776. Take away 1,440 (30 groups), leaving 336. Take away 336 (7 groups). The quotient is 50 + 30 + 7 = **87**.

You can take away smaller chunks if you like. It takes more steps, but the answer is the same, as long as you add every group at the end.

## The area diagram

A division can be drawn as a rectangle. The area is the dividend and one side is the divisor. Split the area into parts you know, and write each part's missing side on top.

For 1,512 ÷ 24, the side is 24 and the parts are 1,200, 240 and 72. Their tops are 50, 10 and 3, so the other side is 63.

## Estimate first

Round to numbers that divide easily. 2,754 ÷ 51 is close to 2,500 ÷ 50 = 50 or 3,000 ÷ 50 = 60, so the quotient is between 50 and 60. (It is 54.)

Dividing tens by tens is like dividing the digits: 3,600 ÷ 40 is 360 tens ÷ 4 tens = **90**.

## Remainders

935 ÷ 22: 22 × 40 = 880, leaving 55. 22 × 2 = 44, leaving 11. The quotient is 42 and 11 is left: **42 R 11**. A remainder must be less than the divisor; if it is not, another group fits.

Check: 22 × 42 = 924, and 924 + 11 = 935.

In a story, the question tells you what to do with a remainder:

| Question | 412 ÷ 48 = 8 R 28 means |
|---|---|
| How many buses so every student rides? | 9 buses: the 28 left need one more. |
| How many buses are completely full? | 8 buses. |
| How many students ride in the last bus? | 28 students. |

## Common mistakes

- **Writing the amount instead of the groups.** Taking away 1,440 from 4,176 is 30 groups of 48. Write 30, not 1,440.
- **A remainder that is too big.** 935 ÷ 22 is not 41 R 33, because 33 holds another 22.
- **Forgetting a partial quotient.** Add every number in the groups column.
- **Answering with "R".** "8 R 28 buses" is not an answer. Decide what the 28 means.

## For grown-ups at home

Your child is not yet expected to use the long division you may remember; that comes in Grade 6. Partial quotients let them use any multiples they know, so there are many right paths. Ask "How many groups was that?" and "How can you check?" Multiplying back is the best habit to build.

## Check yourself

1. 3,600 ÷ 40 = ?
2. Use partial quotients to find 1,368 ÷ 38.
3. 1,000 ÷ 24 = ? Give the remainder.
4. 650 juice boxes go in packs of 24. How many packs are full?

*Answers:* (1) 90. (2) 38 × 30 = 1,140, leaving 228; 38 × 6 = 228; the quotient is 36. (3) 41 R 16, because 24 × 41 = 984 and 1,000 − 984 = 16. (4) 27 full packs, because 650 ÷ 24 = 27 R 2.
