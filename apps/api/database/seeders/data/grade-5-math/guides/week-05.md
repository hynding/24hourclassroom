# Week 05 · Fractions of Whole Numbers

**Standard:** 5.NF.B.4 Multiply a fraction or whole number by a fraction
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Unit 2, Section B, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Find a fraction of a whole number, such as 1/4 of 12 or 3/4 of 12.
- Write "a fraction of a number" as a multiplication: 3/4 of 12 is 3/4 × 12.
- Use tape diagrams to see the equal parts.
- Find a fraction of a number even when the answer is not a whole number, like 2/3 × 5.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Fraction of a number | Some of the equal parts of that number. 1/4 of 12 is one of 4 equal parts of 12. |
| Tape diagram | A long bar cut into equal parts to show a number and its parts. |
| Numerator | The top number of a fraction: how many of the equal parts you take. |
| Denominator | The bottom number of a fraction: how many equal parts the number is split into. |
| Product | The answer to a multiplication. |

## One part first

A class has 12 paint brushes and gives 1/4 of them to the art table. How many brushes is that?

Split the 12 brushes into 4 equal groups. Each group has 12 ÷ 4 = 3 brushes. One group is 1/4 of the brushes, so **1/4 of 12 is 3**.

In math, the word "of" here means multiply. We write **1/4 × 12 = 3**.

So finding a unit fraction of a number is the same as dividing: 1/4 × 12 = 12 ÷ 4.

**Try it.** What is 1/5 of 30? *30 ÷ 5 = 6, so 1/5 × 30 = 6.*

## More than one part

Now the art table gets 3/4 of the 12 brushes. That is 3 of the 4 equal groups.

Draw a tape diagram. Make a bar for 12 and cut it into 4 equal parts. Each part is 3. Shade 3 parts: 3 + 3 + 3 = 9. So **3/4 × 12 = 9**.

The steps were: divide by the denominator to find one part, then multiply by the numerator to take that many parts.

- 3/4 × 12: 12 ÷ 4 = 3, and 3 × 3 = 9.
- 2/3 × 18: 18 ÷ 3 = 6, and 2 × 6 = 12.
- 5/6 × 30: 30 ÷ 6 = 5, and 5 × 5 = 25.

You can also do the steps in the other order: multiply first, then divide. 3/4 × 12 = (3 × 12) ÷ 4 = 36 ÷ 4 = 9. Both orders give the same answer.

**Try it.** What is 3/8 of 24? *24 ÷ 8 = 3, and 3 × 3 = 9.*

## When the answer is not a whole number

What is 1/3 of 5? Five does not split into 3 equal whole-number groups. But last week you learned that 5 ÷ 3 = 5/3. So **1/3 × 5 = 5/3**.

Then 2/3 of 5 is twice as much: 2 × 5/3 = **10/3**. Using the other order gives the same thing: (2 × 5) ÷ 3 = 10 ÷ 3 = 10/3.

This gives one rule that always works: **to find a/b × n, multiply n by the numerator a, then divide by the denominator b.** The answer is (a × n)/b.

- 2/5 × 3 = 6/5
- 3/4 × 7 = 21/4, which is between 5 and 6, since 20/4 = 5 and 24/4 = 6.

**Try it.** What is 3/8 × 5, and between which two whole numbers is it? *15/8. 8/8 = 1 and 16/8 = 2, so it is between 1 and 2.*

## Story problems

When a story asks for a fraction of an amount, multiply.

A trail is 7 kilometers long, and Jamal has walked 2/3 of it. He has walked 2/3 × 7 = 14/3 kilometers. Since 12/3 = 4, that is 4 2/3 kilometers.

A bag has 20 marbles and 3/5 of them are blue. 20 ÷ 5 = 4, and 3 × 4 = 12 blue marbles.

Order does not change a product. 2/3 × 6 and 6 × 2/3 are both 12/3, which is 4.

## A common mistake

Some students multiply the whole number into both the top and the bottom: they say 2/3 × 12 = 24/36. But 24/36 is less than 1, and 2/3 of 12 things is clearly more than 1 thing. Only the numerator is multiplied by 12: 2/3 × 12 = 24/3 = 8.

Another mistake is to stop after dividing. 12 ÷ 3 = 4 is only 1/3 of 12. For 2/3 of 12 you need 2 of those parts: 8.

## For grown-ups at home

Fractions of a group show up all the time: "We have 16 grapes and you may eat 3/4 of them." Ask your child how many that is and how they know. Encourage a quick sketch: a bar cut into 4 equal parts with 4 grapes in each part. Then try a case that does not come out even, like 1/3 of 2 sandwiches, and talk about why the answer is 2/3 of a sandwich.

## Check yourself

1. What is 1/6 × 42?
2. What is 4/5 of 35?
3. Write 2/7 × 3 as a fraction.
4. Between which two whole numbers is 3/4 × 9?
5. A class of 28 students voted, and 3/7 of them chose soccer. How many chose soccer?

*Answers:* (1) 42 ÷ 6 = 7. (2) 35 ÷ 5 = 7, and 4 × 7 = 28. (3) 6/7. (4) 27/4. Since 24/4 = 6 and 28/4 = 7, it is between 6 and 7. (5) 28 ÷ 7 = 4, and 3 × 4 = 12 students.
