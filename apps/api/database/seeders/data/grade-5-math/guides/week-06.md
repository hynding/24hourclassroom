# Week 06 · Area of Rectangles With Fraction Side Lengths

**Standard:** 5.NF.B.4 Multiply a fraction or whole number by a fraction, including area with fractional sides
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Unit 2, Section C, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Find the area of a rectangle whose sides are fractions of a unit, by tiling it with small equal pieces.
- See why 1/2 × 1/5 = 1/10 using a unit square.
- Multiply two fractions to find an area: multiply the numerators and multiply the denominators.
- Find the area of a rectangle with one whole-number side and one fraction side.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Unit square | A square with sides 1 unit long. Its area is 1 square unit. |
| Area | How many square units cover a flat shape with no gaps or overlaps. |
| Side length | How long one side of a shape is. It can be a whole number or a fraction. |
| Tile | Cover a shape with equal pieces that do not overlap. |
| Area model | A rectangle drawn to show a multiplication. The side lengths are the factors and the area is the product. |

## Cutting up a unit square

Start with a square that is 1 meter on each side. Its area is 1 square meter.

Cut it into 5 equal columns and 2 equal rows. Now the square is tiled by 5 × 2 = 10 equal pieces. Each piece is 1/10 of the square, so **each piece has an area of 1/10 square meter**.

How long are the sides of one piece? It is 1/5 meter wide (one of 5 columns) and 1/2 meter tall (one of 2 rows). So a rectangle 1/5 meter by 1/2 meter has an area of 1/10 square meter:

**1/5 × 1/2 = 1/10**

The denominator of the answer counts how many pieces fill the whole unit square. That is why we multiply the denominators.

**Try it.** A unit square is cut into 3 columns and 4 rows. What is the area of one piece? *There are 3 × 4 = 12 equal pieces, so each one is 1/12 square unit. 1/3 × 1/4 = 1/12.*

## More than one piece

Now cut a unit square into 4 columns and 3 rows, so there are 12 pieces of 1/12 each. Shade a rectangle that is 3 columns wide and 2 rows tall. Its sides are 3/4 unit and 2/3 unit.

Count the shaded pieces: 3 in each row and 2 rows, so 3 × 2 = 6 pieces. Each piece is 1/12 square unit. The shaded area is **6/12 square unit**.

So **2/3 × 3/4 = 6/12**. The numerators tell how many pieces are shaded (2 × 3 = 6). The denominators tell how many pieces fill the unit square (3 × 4 = 12).

This gives a rule for the area of any rectangle with fraction sides: **multiply the numerators, and multiply the denominators.**

- 3/5 × 2/3 = 6/15
- 1/2 × 3/4 = 3/8
- 4/5 × 1/2 = 4/10

**Try it.** What is the area of a rectangle 2/5 foot by 2/3 foot? *2 × 2 = 4 and 5 × 3 = 15, so 4/15 square foot.*

## A whole number and a fraction

A rectangle can have one whole-number side. Picture 2 unit squares side by side, each cut into 4 equal rows. Shade the bottom 3 rows all the way across. The shaded rectangle is 2 units long and 3/4 unit wide.

In each unit square 3 pieces are shaded, and each piece is 1/4 square unit. That is 2 × 3 = 6 pieces of 1/4, so the area is **6/4 square units**. That is more than 1 square unit, because the strip runs across 2 whole squares.

**2 × 3/4 = 6/4**. This matches what you learned in Grade 4 about a whole number times a fraction.

## When is the area less than 1?

If both sides are less than 1 unit, the rectangle fits inside a unit square, so its area is less than 1 square unit. If one side is a whole number like 2, the area can be more than 1, as with 6/4 above.

## A common mistake

Some students add instead of multiplying: they say 1/2 × 1/3 = 2/5. But a rectangle 1/2 unit by 1/3 unit is small. It is one of 6 equal pieces of a unit square, so its area is 1/6.

Another mistake is to multiply the numerators but add the denominators. For 2/3 × 3/4, the unit square has 3 × 4 = 12 pieces, not 3 + 4 = 7.

## For grown-ups at home

Use a sheet of paper as a "unit square." Fold it into thirds one way and into fourths the other way, then open it and count the 12 boxes. Ask your child to color a rectangle 2 boxes tall and 3 boxes wide and to name its side lengths (2/3 and 3/4) and its area (6/12). Ask why the area is less than the whole sheet.

## Check yourself

1. A unit square is cut into 2 columns and 6 rows. What is the area of one piece?
2. What is 1/4 × 1/5?
3. What is the area of a rectangle 3/4 meter by 1/2 meter?
4. What is the area of a rectangle 4 units by 2/3 unit?
5. Is the area of a rectangle 5/6 foot by 7/8 foot more or less than 1 square foot? Why?

*Answers:* (1) 1/12 square unit. (2) 1/20. (3) 3/8 square meter. (4) 8/3 square units. (5) Less. Both sides are less than 1 foot, so the rectangle fits inside a 1-foot square. Its area is 35/48 square foot.
