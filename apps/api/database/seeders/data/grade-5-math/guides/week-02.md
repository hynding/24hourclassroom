# Week 02 · Volume Formulas and Expressions

**Standards:** 5.MD.C.5 Find volume with the formulas and by adding volumes · 5.OA.A.2 Write and read simple expressions without evaluating them
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Unit 1, Section B, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Find the volume of a rectangular prism with **V = l × w × h** and with **V = B × h**.
- Explain why the edge lengths can be multiplied in any order or grouping.
- Find a missing edge length when you know the volume.
- Write and read **expressions** with **parentheses** without working them out.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Edge length | How long one edge of a prism is, in units. |
| Base | The face a prism stands on. |
| Base area (B) | The area of the base: length × width. |
| Height (h) | How tall the prism is: the number of layers. |
| Expression | Numbers and operation signs with no equal sign, such as (3 + 5) × 2. |
| Parentheses | The marks ( ) that group part of an expression to be done first. |

## From counting layers to a formula

Last week we counted cubes by layers: cubes in one layer × number of layers. Look at what those numbers are.

- The cubes in one layer make rows: **length × width**.
- The number of layers is the **height**.

So for any rectangular prism:

**Volume = length × width × height**, or **V = l × w × h**

This works for real boxes too, where we cannot see any cubes. A cereal box 20 cm long, 6 cm wide and 25 cm tall has a volume of 20 × 6 × 25. First 20 × 6 = 120, then 120 × 25 = 3,000 cubic centimeters.

## Base area times height

The length × width part is the **area of the base**, written **B**. That gives a second formula:

**V = B × h**

It is handy when you are told the base area. A shoebox with a base of 70 square inches and a height of 5 inches has a volume of 70 × 5 = 350 cubic inches. Each layer holds 70 inch cubes, and there are 5 layers.

Any face can be the base. Stand a box on its end and the numbers trade places, but the volume stays the same.

## Grouping the factors

There are three edge lengths to multiply, so we have choices. For a prism 5 by 3 by 4:

- (5 × 3) × 4 = 15 × 4 = 60
- 5 × (3 × 4) = 5 × 12 = 60

Both give 60 cubic units. This is the **associative property of multiplication**: changing the grouping does not change the product. In a picture, (5 × 3) × 4 counts flat layers of 15, and 5 × (3 × 4) counts 5 upright slices of 12. Pick the grouping that is easiest: 25 × 7 × 4 is easier as 25 × 4 = 100, then 100 × 7 = 700.

## Finding a missing edge

If you know the volume and two edges, ask, "Base area times what makes the volume?"

**Try it.** A box has a volume of 84 cubic feet. Its base is 7 ft by 3 ft. The base area is 7 × 3 = 21 square feet. 21 × 4 = 84, so the box is **4 ft** tall.

## Writing and reading expressions

An **expression** uses numbers and operations to stand for a value. **Parentheses** show which part to do first.

| Words | Expression |
|---|---|
| Add 9 and 6, then multiply by 2 | (9 + 6) × 2 |
| Multiply 9 by 6, then add 2 | (9 × 6) + 2 |
| Three times the sum of 40 and 15 | 3 × (40 + 15) |
| Base of 4 by 5, then 6 layers | (4 × 5) × 6 |

You can compare expressions **without** working them out. 6 × (1,248 + 397) is 6 times as large as 1,248 + 397, because the whole sum is multiplied by 6. And (12 + 8) × 5 is larger than 12 + 8 × 5, because in the first the whole 20 is multiplied by 5.

**Common mistake.** Adding the edge lengths. A box 4 by 3 by 2 does not have a volume of 4 + 3 + 2 = 9. Volume multiplies: 4 × 3 × 2 = 24 cubic units. Also watch the units. Volume is in **cubic** units, not square units.

## For grown-ups at home

Find a box at home, such as a cereal box, and measure its length, width and height with a ruler, rounding to whole inches or centimeters. Multiply to find the volume together, then try grouping the numbers a different way and check that you get the same answer. For expressions, read a short phrase aloud ("add 5 and 3, then double it") and let your child write it with parentheses.

## Check yourself

1. Find the volume of a box 9 m long, 2 m wide and 5 m tall.
2. A prism has a base area of 32 square centimeters and a height of 3 centimeters. What is its volume?
3. A box has a volume of 60 cubic inches and a base 5 inches by 4 inches. How tall is it?
4. Write "subtract 4 from 10, then multiply by 7" as an expression.

*Answers:* (1) 9 × 2 × 5 = 18 × 5 = 90 cubic meters. (2) 32 × 3 = 96 cubic centimeters. (3) The base is 20 square inches and 20 × 3 = 60, so 3 inches. (4) (10 − 4) × 7.
