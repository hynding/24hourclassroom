# Week 29 · Putting Volume to Work

**Standards:** 5.MD.C.5 Find volume with the formulas and by adding volumes
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Unit 8, Section B, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Use the two **volume** formulas, V = l × w × h and V = B × h, to solve problems.
- Find a missing edge when you know the volume.
- See that boxes with different shapes can have the same volume.
- Find the volume of a solid made of two rectangular prisms by splitting it and adding.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Volume | The amount of space a solid takes up, counted in unit cubes. |
| Unit cube | A cube with edges 1 unit long. It has a volume of 1 cubic unit. |
| Rectangular prism | A box shape with six rectangle faces. |
| Base | The face the prism sits on. B stands for the area of the base. |
| Height | How tall the prism is: the number of layers of cubes. |
| Cubic units | Units for volume, such as cubic centimeters (cm³) or cubic feet (ft³). |

## Two formulas, one idea

Picture a box filled with cubes in layers. The bottom layer covers the base. If the base is 8 centimeters by 5 centimeters, one layer holds 8 × 5 = 40 cubes. A box 6 centimeters tall has 6 layers, so it holds 40 × 6 = 240 cubes. Its volume is 240 cubic centimeters.

That gives two ways to write the same thing:

- **V = l × w × h**: length times width times height. 8 × 5 × 6 = 240.
- **V = B × h**: the area of the base times the height. 40 × 6 = 240.

You can multiply the three edges in any order. 8 × 5 × 6, 6 × 8 × 5 and 5 × 6 × 8 are all 240, so pick the order that is easiest. For 25 × 7 × 4, do 25 × 4 = 100 first, then 100 × 7 = 700.

**Try it.** A fish tank is 50 cm long, 25 cm wide and 40 cm tall. The base is 50 × 25 = 1,250 square centimeters, and 1,250 × 40 = 50,000 cubic centimeters.

## Working backwards: a missing edge

Sometimes you know the volume and need an edge. A box holds 360 cubic inches. Its base is 9 inches by 8 inches. Each layer holds 9 × 8 = 72 cubes, so the number of layers is 360 ÷ 72 = 5. The box is 5 inches tall.

The steps are: multiply the two edges you know, then divide the volume by that product.

## Same volume, different boxes

A box 3 cm by 4 cm by 10 cm and a box 6 cm by 5 cm by 4 cm look very different. One is long and thin, the other short and wide. But 3 × 4 × 10 = 120 and 6 × 5 × 4 = 120, so they hold the same amount.

To list boxes with a volume of 36 cubic units, look for three whole numbers that multiply to 36: 1 by 4 by 9, 2 by 3 by 6, 3 by 3 by 4, 2 by 2 by 9 and more. Designers use this when they need a box that fits a shelf but still holds enough.

## Solids made of two prisms

Many real objects, like a set of steps or an L-shaped planter, are two boxes joined together. To find the volume:

1. Split the solid into two rectangular prisms that do not overlap.
2. Find any edge that is not labeled by using the edges that are.
3. Find each volume.
4. Add.

**Example.** A low block is 10 feet long, 4 feet deep and 3 feet tall. A taller block, 4 feet long and 4 feet deep, stands on its left end and rises 5 feet above it. Low block: 10 × 4 × 3 = 120 cubic feet. Top block: 4 × 4 × 5 = 80 cubic feet. Total: 120 + 80 = 200 cubic feet.

There is often more than one way to split. You could cut the solid into a left part 4 feet long and 8 feet tall (4 × 4 × 8 = 128) and a right part 6 feet long and 3 feet tall (6 × 4 × 3 = 72). The total is still 128 + 72 = 200.

**A common mistake.** Do not let the two parts overlap. If you take the whole bottom block (120) and then the whole left tower 8 feet tall (128), the corner where they meet is counted twice and you get 248, which is too much. Another slip is using a whole length where only part of it belongs. If a solid is 11 m long and the tall part is 4 m long, the low part is 11 − 4 = 7 m long, not 11 m.

## For grown-ups at home

Look for boxes at home: cereal boxes, shoe boxes, a drawer or a fridge. Measure the edges in inches or centimeters together, estimate the volume, then multiply. Ask, "Could we make a box with the same volume that is taller and thinner?" Building with sugar cubes or small blocks makes layers easy to see.

## Check yourself

1. Find the volume of a box 6 in by 4 in by 11 in.
2. A box has a base area of 35 square feet and a height of 4 feet. What is its volume?
3. A box holds 630 cubic inches. Its base is 10 inches by 9 inches. How tall is it?
4. A step shape has a tall part 2 m long, 4 m deep and 5 m tall, and a low part 5 m long, 4 m deep and 2 m tall. What is its volume?
5. Name two different boxes with a volume of 24 cubic units.

*Answers:* (1) 6 × 4 × 11 = 264 cubic inches. (2) 35 × 4 = 140 cubic feet. (3) 10 × 9 = 90, and 630 ÷ 90 = 7 inches. (4) 2 × 4 × 5 = 40 and 5 × 4 × 2 = 40, so 80 cubic meters. (5) For example 2 by 3 by 4 and 1 by 4 by 6.
