# Week 16 · Comparing and Rounding Decimals

**Standards:** 5.NBT.A.4 Round decimals to any place · 5.NBT.A.3 Read, write and compare decimals to thousandths
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Unit 5, Section A, in our own words

## What you will learn

- **Compare** and **order** decimals to thousandths, place by place.
- Explain why a decimal with more digits is not always greater.
- **Round** a decimal to the nearest whole number, tenth or hundredth.
- Use a number line to show which number a decimal is closer to.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Compare | Decide which of two numbers is greater, or if they are equal. |
| Greater than / less than | Words for comparing. 0.7 is greater than 0.65; 0.65 is less than 0.7. |
| Equivalent decimals | Decimals that name the same amount, like 0.4, 0.40 and 0.400. |
| Round | Find the closest number that stops at a chosen place. |
| Halfway number | The number exactly in the middle of two others, like 4.65 between 4.6 and 4.7. |

## Comparing place by place

To compare two decimals, line up their decimal points and look from the **left**. The first place where the digits are different decides.

Compare 0.462 and 0.48:

1. Ones: both 0. Same.
2. Tenths: both 4. Same.
3. Hundredths: 6 and 8. 8 is more, so **0.48 is greater**.

You can also write zeros so both numbers have the same number of places. 0.48 = 0.480. Now compare 462 thousandths and 480 thousandths: 480 is more.

**Try it.** Which is greater, 3.05 or 3.005? Write 3.05 as 3.050. 50 thousandths is more than 5 thousandths, so 3.05 is greater.

## Ordering decimals

To put several decimals in order, write them all with the same number of places. Order 0.35, 0.305, 0.4 and 0.053 from least to greatest:

| Decimal | In thousandths |
|---|---|
| 0.35 | 0.350 |
| 0.305 | 0.305 |
| 0.4 | 0.400 |
| 0.053 | 0.053 |

Now order 53, 305, 350 and 400: the list is **0.053, 0.305, 0.35, 0.4**.

In a race, the **least** time is the fastest. A runner with 12.084 seconds beats a runner with 12.4 seconds, because 12.4 = 12.400.

## Rounding with a number line

To **round** 4.63 to the nearest tenth, find the two tenths it sits between: 4.6 and 4.7. Draw a number line from 4.6 to 4.7 split into hundredths. The middle tick is the halfway number, **4.65**.

4.63 is to the left of 4.65, so it is closer to 4.6. Rounded to the nearest tenth, **4.63 is about 4.6**.

A number exactly at the halfway mark rounds **up**. 6.25 rounds to 6.3.

## Rounding with the digit to the right

You do not always need to draw. Look at the digit **one place to the right** of the place you are rounding to:

- 0, 1, 2, 3 or 4: round down (keep the digit).
- 5, 6, 7, 8 or 9: round up (add one to the digit).

| Round | Look at | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 15.372 to the nearest hundredth | the 2 (thousandths) | 15.37 |
| 7.48 to the nearest whole number | the 4 (tenths) | 7 |
| 2.639 to the nearest tenth | the 3 (hundredths) | 2.6 |

**Watch out.** Look at only **one** digit. 0.849 rounds to 0.8 for the nearest tenth, because the hundredths digit is 4. Do not round the 9 first and then round again.

**Watch out, too.** Sometimes rounding up carries into the next place. 9.96 to the nearest tenth: 9.9 becomes 10 tenths, which is a whole, so the answer is **10.0**, not 9.10.

## For grown-ups at home

Prices and sports results are good practice. At the store, ask "About how many dollars is $12.49? And $12.50?" (12 and 13). When comparing, ask your child to say each number in thousandths: "Is 0.7 the same as 700 thousandths? Then which is more, 0.7 or 0.695?" Watch for the habit of picking the number with more digits.

## Check yourself

1. Which is greater, 0.59 or 0.6?
2. Order from least to greatest: 1.2, 1.02, 1.202, 1.022.
3. Round 6.847 to the nearest hundredth.
4. Round 3.95 to the nearest tenth.
5. Name a number with thousandths that rounds to 4.5 when rounded to the nearest tenth.

*Answers:* (1) 0.6, because 0.6 = 0.60 and 60 hundredths is more than 59. (2) 1.02, 1.022, 1.2, 1.202. (3) 6.85. (4) 4.0. (5) Many answers, any number from 4.45 up to but not including 4.55, such as 4.462 or 4.517.
