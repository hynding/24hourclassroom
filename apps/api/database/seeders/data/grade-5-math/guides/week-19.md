# Week 19 · Dividing Decimals

**Standards:** 5.NBT.B.7 Add, subtract, multiply and divide decimals to hundredths
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Unit 5, Section D, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Divide a decimal by a whole number by **sharing** tenths and hundredths.
- Divide by a decimal by asking **"how many fit?"**
- Explain why dividing by a number less than 1 gives an answer **greater** than the number you started with.
- **Check** a quotient by multiplying, and estimate first.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Dividend | The number being divided. In 4.8 ÷ 4, the dividend is 4.8. |
| Divisor | The number you divide by. In 4.8 ÷ 4, the divisor is 4. |
| Quotient | The answer to a division. In 4.8 ÷ 4 = 1.2, the quotient is 1.2. |
| Tape diagram | A bar split into parts to show how an amount is shared. |
| Equal groups | Groups that all have the same amount. |

## Sharing a decimal

A ribbon 4.8 meters long is cut into 4 equal pieces. Draw a tape diagram: one bar labeled 4.8, split into 4 equal parts.

Share one place at a time:

- 4 ones ÷ 4 = 1 one.
- 8 tenths ÷ 4 = 2 tenths.

Each piece is **1.2 meters**. Check: 4 × 1.2 = 4.8.

When the ones do not share evenly, think of the whole number in tenths or hundredths:

- 2.4 ÷ 3: 2.4 is **24 tenths**. 24 tenths ÷ 3 = 8 tenths, so the quotient is **0.8**.
- 1.56 ÷ 4: 1.56 is **156 hundredths**. 156 ÷ 4 = 39, so the quotient is **0.39**.

**Try it.** Four friends share a $9.60 bill. $8 ÷ 4 = $2 and $1.60 ÷ 4 = $0.40, so each pays **$2.40**.

## How many fit?

Division also asks how many groups of one number fit into another. 3 ÷ 0.1 asks, "How many tenths are in 3?" Each whole has 10 tenths, so 3 wholes have **30 tenths**. 3 ÷ 0.1 = 30.

A number line shows this kind of division well. For 2 ÷ 0.4, jump by 0.4 from 0: 0.4, 0.8, 1.2, 1.6, 2. That is **5 jumps**, so 2 ÷ 0.4 = 5. Count the jumps, not the tick marks (there are 6 tick marks, counting 0).

When both numbers are tenths, you can think in tenths. 1.2 ÷ 0.3 is 12 tenths ÷ 3 tenths, which is the same as 12 ÷ 3 = **4**. In the same way, 0.36 ÷ 0.04 is 36 hundredths ÷ 4 hundredths = **9**.

## Dividing by less than 1

Look at these:

| Problem | Quotient |
|---|---|
| 6 ÷ 2 | 3 |
| 6 ÷ 1 | 6 |
| 6 ÷ 0.5 | 12 |
| 6 ÷ 0.1 | 60 |

As the divisor gets smaller, more groups fit, so the quotient gets bigger. **Dividing by a number less than 1 gives a quotient greater than the dividend.**

**Watch out.** 4 ÷ 0.5 is not 2. It asks how many halves fit in 4. There are 2 halves in each whole, so there are **8** halves in 4.

## Check and estimate

Multiplication undoes division. To check 7.2 ÷ 9 = 0.8, multiply: 9 × 0.8 = 7.2. It matches.

Estimate before you divide. 7.8 ÷ 4 is about 8 ÷ 4 = 2, so an answer of 19.5 or 0.195 has its decimal point in the wrong place. The exact quotient is 1.95.

## For grown-ups at home

Cooking and sharing are great for this. "We have 2 liters of juice and cups that hold 0.25 liter. How many cups?" (8.) "Three of us split $7.50. How much each?" ($2.50.) Ask your child to say whether the answer should be bigger or smaller than the starting number before they work it out, and to check by multiplying.

## Check yourself

1. 6.3 ÷ 3
2. 0.84 ÷ 4
3. 5 ÷ 0.1
4. 2.4 ÷ 0.6
5. Without dividing, which is greater: 8 ÷ 0.4 or 8 ÷ 4? Why?

*Answers:* (1) 2.1. (2) 0.21. (3) 50. (4) 4. (5) 8 ÷ 0.4 is greater (it is 20, and 8 ÷ 4 is 2), because more groups of a small number fit into 8.
