# Unit 3 · Multiplying and Dividing Fractions · Summary

**Weeks:** 8 Fractions of Fractions and Scaling · 9 Dividing a Unit Fraction by a Whole Number · 10 Dividing a Whole Number by a Unit Fraction · 11 Problem Solving With Fractions and Line Plots
**Standards:** 5.NF.B.5 · 5.NF.B.4 · 5.NF.B.7 · 5.NF.B.6 · 5.MD.B.2 · **Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Unit 3, in our own words

## The big ideas

1. **"Of" means multiply.** 2/3 of 3/4 is 2/3 × 3/4. Taking a part of a part gives a smaller part.
2. **An area model shows a product of fractions.** In a unit square cut into 4 columns and 3 rows, a rectangle 3/4 wide and 2/3 tall covers 3 × 2 = 6 of the 4 × 3 = 12 small parts: 3/4 × 2/3 = 6/12.
3. **Multiply numerators, multiply denominators.** 2/5 × 3/4 = 6/20.
4. **Multiplication is scaling.** A factor less than 1 shrinks a number, a factor equal to 1 keeps it the same, and a factor greater than 1 stretches it. You can compare without multiplying.
5. **Sharing a unit fraction makes smaller pieces.** 1/3 ÷ 4 = 1/12: cutting each third into 4 parts cuts the whole into 12.
6. **Dividing a whole number by a unit fraction counts pieces.** 3 ÷ 1/4 = 12, because 12 fourths fit in 3 wholes. The answer is bigger than 3.
7. **Multiplication checks division.** 4 × 1/12 = 1/3 and 12 × 1/4 = 3.
8. **Line plots hold fraction data.** Count the Xs, multiply a value by its number of Xs to find a total, and divide a total by the number of Xs to share it equally.

## Strategies with an example

| Strategy | When to use it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Area model in a unit square | Seeing a product of two fractions | 2/3 × 4/5: 8 of 15 parts are shaded, 8/15 |
| Multiply across | Any fraction × fraction | 3/8 × 2/5 = 6/40 |
| Compare the factor with 1 | Bigger or smaller without multiplying | 7/8 × 20 is less than 20 |
| Tape diagram, cut the shaded part | Unit fraction ÷ whole number | 1/2 ÷ 3: half cut into 3, each 1/6 |
| Multiply the denominator | Unit fraction ÷ whole number | 1/6 ÷ 3 = 1/18 |
| Count jumps on a number line | Whole number ÷ unit fraction | 2 ÷ 1/3: 6 jumps of 1/3 reach 2 |
| Wholes times pieces per whole | Whole number ÷ unit fraction | 4 ÷ 1/5 = 4 × 5 = 20 |
| Split the mixed number | Mixed number × whole number | 3 × 2 1/2 = 6 + 1 1/2 = 7 1/2 |
| Write the mixed number as a fraction | Mixed number × fraction | 2/3 × 1 1/2 = 2/3 × 3/2 = 1 |
| Total, then divide | Sharing line plot data equally | 6 liters in 12 bottles: 1/2 liter each |

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Factor | A number being multiplied. |
| Product | The answer to a multiplication. |
| Scaling | Multiplying to make a number bigger or smaller. |
| Unit fraction | A fraction with 1 as its numerator, like 1/5. |
| Quotient | The answer to a division. |
| Divisor | The number you divide by. |
| Line plot | A number line with an X for each measurement. |

## Watch out for

- **Adding instead of multiplying.** 2/5 × 1/3 is 2/15, not 3/8.
- **Multiplying the numerators but adding the denominators.** 3/4 × 2/3 is 6/12, not 6/7.
- **"Multiplying always makes it bigger."** Only when the factor is greater than 1. 3/4 × 8 = 6.
- **Mixing up the two kinds of division.** 1/3 ÷ 4 = 1/12 (sharing), but 4 ÷ 1/3 = 12 (how many fit).
- **Dividing the wrong way.** 3 ÷ 1/4 is not 3/4. Lots of small pieces fit in 3 wholes.
- **Multiplying only the whole part of a mixed number.** 3 × 2 1/2 is 7 1/2, not 6 1/2.
- **Counting tick marks instead of Xs** on a line plot. Each X is one measurement.

## Practice set

1. What is 1/2 of 4/5?
2. What is 2/3 × 5/6?
3. Is 9/8 × 40 more or less than 40? How do you know?
4. What is 1/3 ÷ 5?
5. What is 6 ÷ 1/4?
6. Two runners share 1/3 liter of sports drink equally. How much does each get?
7. How many 1/2-meter pieces can be cut from 7 meters of string?
8. A trail is 1 3/4 miles. Kim hikes it 4 times. How far does she hike?
9. A line plot has 5 Xs at 3/8 inch. What is their total length?
10. Eight bottles hold 2 liters of water in all. If the water were shared equally, how much would each bottle hold?

*Answers:* (1) 1/2 × 4/5 = 4/10. (2) 10/18. (3) More, because 9/8 is greater than 1 (it is 45). (4) 1/15. (5) 24. (6) 1/3 ÷ 2 = 1/6 liter. (7) 7 ÷ 1/2 = 14 pieces. (8) 4 + 3 = 7 miles. (9) 15/8 = 1 7/8 inches. (10) 2 ÷ 8 = 2/8 liter.
