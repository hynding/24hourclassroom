## The big ideas

1. **Place value keeps going to the right.** A hundredth split into 10 equal parts makes thousandths. Each place is worth 10 times the place to its right and 1/10 of the place to its left, so the 7s in 7.777 are worth 7, 0.7, 0.07 and 0.007.
2. **Compare from the left.** Line up the decimal points, or write zeros so both numbers have the same number of places, then compare place by place. More digits does not mean a greater number.
3. **Round to the nearest friendly number.** Look at the digit one place to the right of the place you want. 0 to 4 rounds down, 5 to 9 rounds up, and a halfway number rounds up.
4. **Add and subtract like places.** Tenths go with tenths and hundredths with hundredths. Regroup 10 of a place into 1 of the next place to the left, and decompose 1 into 10 when you need more to subtract from.
5. **Powers of 10 move the digits.** Multiplying by 10, 100 or 1,000 moves each digit 1, 2 or 3 places to the left; dividing moves them to the right.
6. **Multiplying and dividing decimals are about the size of the pieces.** A tenth of a tenth is a hundredth. Dividing by a number less than 1 gives a greater answer, because more small groups fit.
7. **Estimate, then check.** Round first to know about how big the answer should be. Check sums with subtraction, differences with addition, and quotients with multiplication.

## Strategies with an example

| Strategy | Example |
|---|---|
| Place-value chart | 4.063 = 4 ones, 0 tenths, 6 hundredths, 3 thousandths |
| Expanded form | 3.405 = 3 + 0.4 + 0.005 |
| Thousandths on a number line | From 0.25 to 0.26 in 10 jumps: the seventh jump is 0.257 |
| Comparing with zeros | 0.7 = 0.700, and 700 thousandths is more than 695, so 0.7 is greater than 0.695 |
| Rounding on a number line | 4.63 is left of the halfway mark 4.65, so it rounds to 4.6 |
| Adding with regrouping | 3.60 + 2.75 = 6.35 (13 tenths become 1 one and 3 tenths) |
| Counting up to subtract | 3.2 − 1.45: jumps of 0.05, 0.5 and 1.2 make 1.75 |
| Moving digits | 4.25 × 100 = 425; 36.8 ÷ 10 = 3.68 |
| Tenths times tenths on a grid | 0.3 × 0.4 covers 12 of 100 squares: 0.12 |
| Area model | 2.4 × 1.3 = 2 + 0.4 + 0.6 + 0.12 = 3.12 |
| Sharing in smaller units | 1.56 ÷ 4 = 156 hundredths ÷ 4 = 39 hundredths = 0.39 |
| How many fit? | 2 ÷ 0.4 = 5, because 5 jumps of 0.4 reach 2 |

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Thousandths place | The third place after the decimal point; 0.001 is one thousandth. |
| Expanded form | A number written as the sum of its digits' values, like 6 + 0.07 + 0.002. |
| Equivalent decimals | Decimals that name the same amount, like 0.4 and 0.400. |
| Round | Find the closest number that stops at a chosen place. |
| Regroup / decompose | Trade 10 of a place for 1 of the next place left / 1 of a place for 10 of the next place right. |
| Power of 10 | 10, 100, 1,000, and so on. |
| Product / quotient | The answer to a multiplication / a division. |

## Watch out for

- **Zeros that hold places.** 0.25 and 0.025 are different numbers: 25 hundredths and 25 thousandths.
- **More digits is not bigger.** 0.6 is greater than 0.599.
- **Rounding twice.** 0.849 rounds to 0.8 for the nearest tenth. Look at one digit only.
- **Rounding up into the next place.** 9.96 to the nearest tenth is 10.0, not 9.10.
- **Lining up the last digits.** In 3.6 + 2.75, line up the decimal points, not the 6 and the 5.
- **"Just add a zero."** 2.5 × 10 is 25, not 2.50.
- **Tenths times tenths.** 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25, not 2.5.
- **Dividing by less than 1.** 4 ÷ 0.5 = 8, not 2.
