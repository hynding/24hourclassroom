# Unit 6 · More Decimal and Fraction Operations · Unit summary

**Weeks:** 20–23 · **Standards:** 5.MD.A.1, 5.NBT.A.2, 5.NF.A.1, 5.NF.A.2, 5.NF.B.5
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Unit 6, in our own words

## The big ideas

1. **Exponents count the 10s.** 10³ = 10 × 10 × 10 = 1,000. Multiplying by 10³ moves every digit 3 places to the left; dividing by 10³ moves every digit 3 places to the right.
2. **Converting goes both ways.** To change to a smaller unit, multiply: there will be more of them. To change to a larger unit, divide: there will be fewer. Metric units change by powers of 10, so 2,750 g = 2.75 kg. Customary units do not, so a remainder becomes a fraction: 10 qt = 2 2/4 = 2 1/2 gal.
3. **Fractions must share a denominator to be added or subtracted.** Rewrite them as equivalent fractions with a common denominator: 1/3 + 1/4 = 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12.
4. **Estimate fraction stories with benchmarks.** Compare each fraction with 0, 1/2 and 1. An answer far from the estimate needs another look.
5. **Multiplying is scaling.** A scale factor more than 1 makes a product bigger, less than 1 makes it smaller, and equal to 1 keeps it the same.

## Strategies and examples

| Strategy | When it helps | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Move digits by the exponent | Multiplying or dividing by a power of 10 | 3.6 × 10³ = 3,600; 475 ÷ 10² = 4.75 |
| Multiply by the fact | Changing to a smaller unit | 4 gal = 4 × 4 = 16 qt |
| Divide by the fact | Changing to a larger unit | 3,500 mL = 3,500 ÷ 1,000 = 3.5 L |
| Remainder over the fact | Customary conversions with a remainder | 40 oz = 2 R 8 = 2 8/16 = 2 1/2 lb |
| Area diagram cut both ways | Seeing a common denominator | Thirds by fourths makes twelfths |
| Multiply the denominators | Finding a common denominator fast | 2/5 and 1/3: use 15 |
| Trade a whole | Subtracting mixed numbers | 4 2/10 − 1 5/10 = 3 12/10 − 1 5/10 = 2 7/10 |
| Benchmarks | Estimating and checking | 7/8 + 4/9 is about 1 + 1/2 |
| Compare the scale factor with 1 | Predicting the size of a product | 7/8 × 40 is less than 40 |

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Exponent | The raised number that tells how many 10s are multiplied, as in 10³. |
| Convert | Write the same amount in a different unit. |
| Common denominator | A denominator two fractions can both be rewritten with. |
| Equivalent fractions | Fractions that name the same amount, like 3/4 and 9/12. |
| Benchmark | A friendly number for estimating, such as 0, 1/2 or 1. |
| Scaling | Resizing by multiplying. |
| Scale factor | The number you multiply by when you scale. |

## Watch out for

- **Converting the wrong way.** A larger unit means a smaller number: 300 cm = 3 m.
- **Adding denominators.** 1/2 + 1/3 is 5/6, not 2/5.
- **Changing only the denominator.** 5/6 is 10/12, not 5/12.
- **Forgetting the traded whole** when subtracting mixed numbers.
- **"Multiplying always makes bigger."** 3/4 × 12 = 9, which is less than 12.

## Practice set

1. 2.08 × 10² = ____
2. 4 1/2 lb = ____ oz
3. 2,600 m = ____ km
4. 3/5 + 1/4 = ____
5. 2 1/6 − 1 1/2 = ____
6. Estimate 11/12 + 5/9.
7. A ribbon is 5/6 yd long. Kim cuts off 1/4 yd. How much is left?
8. Without multiplying, is 4/3 × 27 more or less than 27?
9. Put 0.5 × 16, 16 and 1.5 × 16 in order from least to greatest.

*Answers:* (1) 208. (2) 4 × 16 + 8 = 72 oz. (3) 2.6 km. (4) 12/20 + 5/20 = 17/20. (5) 2 1/6 − 1 3/6 = 1 7/6 − 1 3/6 = 4/6, which is 2/3. (6) About 1 + 1/2 = 1 1/2. (7) 10/12 − 3/12 = 7/12 yd. (8) More, because 4/3 is more than 1 (it is 36). (9) 0.5 × 16, 16, 1.5 × 16 (8, 16, 24).

## For grown-ups at home

This unit pulls together decimals, fractions and measurement. Two questions help in every problem: "Should the answer be bigger or smaller?" (for conversions and scaling) and "About how much should it be?" (for fraction stories). Asking these before your child calculates builds the number sense that catches mistakes. Measuring cups, food labels and road signs give plenty of real practice.
