# Grade 5 Math · Year Review Guide

**Built from:** the eight units of this course (IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Units 1–8, in our own words) · **Use with:** the eight unit summaries and the End-of-Year Check (week 33)

Fifth grade covered a lot of ground: volume, fractions of every kind, big multiplication and division, decimals to thousandths, measurement, the coordinate grid and families of shapes. This guide walks through each unit, explains how the End-of-Year Check is set up, and gives you a two-week plan to get ready. Read it with a grown-up if you can.

## How the End-of-Year Check works

The check has 40 questions and takes about 80 minutes. The questions go in course order: Unit 1 first, Unit 8 last. Bigger units have more questions.

| Unit | Questions | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Finding Volume | 4 | Counting unit cubes, the volume formula, an expression for two boxes |
| 2 · Fractions as Quotients and Fraction Multiplication | 5 | Sharing as division, a fraction of a number, area with fraction sides, mixed numbers |
| 3 · Multiplying and Dividing Fractions | 5 | A line plot in fourths, dividing with unit fractions, products bigger or smaller |
| 4 · Wrapping Up Multiplication and Division with Multi-digit Numbers | 5 | The standard algorithm, two-digit divisors, brackets, a multistep problem |
| 5 · Place Value Patterns and Decimal Operations | 6 | Thousandths on a number line, rounding, place value, decimal operations, powers of 10 |
| 6 · More Decimal and Fraction Operations | 6 | Converting units, exponents, unlike denominators, estimating, scaling |
| 7 · Shapes on the Coordinate Plane | 5 | Reading points, distances on a grid, the shape hierarchy, two patterns |
| 8 · Putting It All Together | 4 | Volume of two boxes, money with decimals, mixed numbers, moving on a grid |

**Kinds of questions.** Most questions are multiple choice or ask for a number. There are also three true/false questions, one fill-in-a-word question, one select-all question (choose every right answer) and two short answers. Six questions have a picture: a prism built from cubes, a fraction area model, a line plot, a thousandths number line, a town map and a diagram of quadrilaterals. Three pairs of questions share one picture, so look at it carefully the first time.

**Points.** Most questions are worth 1 point. The select-all question is worth 2 points, and you get part of the points for part of the right answers. Each short answer is worth 3 points, one for each part. The check is out of 45 points. Your teacher checks the short answers, so show your work in words, numbers or a drawing.

**Typing answers.** For a number answer, type only the number. Type big numbers without a comma: 13512, not 13,512. When a question asks for a decimal, type it with a decimal point, such as 2.65. Fraction answers that are not whole numbers are always multiple choice.

## What each unit asks you to do

**Unit 1 · Finding Volume.** Volume is the number of unit cubes that fill a solid. A box 4 cubes long, 3 wide and 2 tall has 4 × 3 = 12 cubes in each layer and 2 layers, so 24 cubes, including the ones you cannot see. The formulas are V = l × w × h and V = B × h. For a solid made of two boxes, find each volume and add.

**Unit 2 · Fractions as Quotients and Fraction Multiplication.** A fraction is a division: 2 sandwiches shared by 3 friends is 2 ÷ 3 = 2/3 of a sandwich each. To find 2/5 of 30, find 1/5 of 30 (which is 6) and take 2 of them: 12. The area of a rectangle with fraction sides is their product: 1/2 × 3/5 = 3/10. Split mixed numbers into wholes and parts: 4 × 3 1/2 = 12 + 2 = 14.

**Unit 3 · Multiplying and Dividing Fractions.** Multiplying by a number less than 1 makes a product smaller; multiplying by a number more than 1 makes it bigger. 1/3 ÷ 2 splits a third into 2 parts: 1/6. 3 ÷ 1/4 asks how many fourths are in 3: 12. Read line plots marked in halves, fourths or eighths, and use them to add and multiply.

**Unit 4 · Wrapping Up Multiplication and Division with Multi-digit Numbers.** Use the standard algorithm: one row for the ones digit, one row for the tens digit with a 0 in the ones place, then add. Divide by two-digit numbers with partial quotients, and check by multiplying back. In 4 × [20 − (3 + 5)], work inside the parentheses, then the brackets, then multiply: 4 × 12 = 48.

**Unit 5 · Place Value Patterns and Decimal Operations.** Each place is worth 10 times the place to its right and 1/10 of the place to its left. Thousandths fill the space between hundredths: 5.618 is between 5.61 and 5.62. Round by looking at the next digit. Line up places to add and subtract, and remember that tenths times tenths make hundredths. Multiplying by 100 moves each digit two places left: 0.36 × 100 = 36.

**Unit 6 · More Decimal and Fraction Operations.** Convert units within one system: 1 kilogram = 1,000 grams, 1 pound = 16 ounces, 1 meter = 100 centimeters, 1 foot = 12 inches. Bigger unit to smaller unit means multiply; smaller to bigger means divide. 10³ means 10 × 10 × 10 = 1,000, and the 3 is the exponent. Add and subtract fractions with unlike denominators by renaming: 2/3 + 1/5 = 10/15 + 3/15 = 13/15. Estimate with 0, 1/2 and 1.

**Unit 7 · Shapes on the Coordinate Plane.** An ordered pair says across first, then up. Points with the same y-coordinate sit on a horizontal line, so subtract the x-coordinates to find the distance. A square is a rectangle and a rhombus, because it has every property of both. Two number patterns can be graphed as ordered pairs; in (2, 10), (4, 20), (6, 30) each second number is 5 times the first.

**Unit 8 · Putting It All Together.** Use everything at once: volume of two boxes, decimals in money problems, mixed numbers with unlike denominators, and moving points on a grid. Read each problem twice, make a plan and check with an estimate.

## A two-week practice plan

Each day takes about 25 to 30 minutes. Read that unit's summary first, then do its practice set, then look back at quiz questions you missed during the year.

| Day | Focus | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Unit 1 | Find the volume of 4 boxes at home by measuring them. Build or draw one solid made of two boxes and find its volume. |
| 2 | Unit 2 | Solve 3 sharing problems as fractions. Find 2/3 of 18, 3/5 of 40 and 5/6 of 24. |
| 3 | Unit 2 and Unit 3, multiplying | Draw area models for 2/3 × 1/2 and 3/4 × 4/5. Sort 6 products into "bigger than" and "smaller than" the first factor. |
| 4 | Unit 3, dividing and line plots | Solve 2 problems like 1/3 ÷ 2 and 2 like 3 ÷ 1/4, with a drawing for each. Make a line plot of 8 lengths in fourths of an inch. |
| 5 | Unit 4 | Do 3 multiplications with the standard algorithm and 3 divisions by two-digit numbers. Estimate each first. |
| 6 | Unit 5 | Place 3 numbers on a thousandths number line. Round each to the nearest tenth and hundredth. |
| 7 | Rest, or redo your hardest day | Look back at the mistakes from days 1 to 6. |
| 8 | Unit 5, operations | Add, subtract, multiply and divide 2 pairs of decimals each. Check each answer with an estimate. |
| 9 | Unit 6, measurement | Make a table for meters to centimeters and kilograms to grams. Write 10², 10³ and 10⁴ as numbers. |
| 10 | Unit 6, fractions | Add and subtract 4 pairs of fractions with unlike denominators, 2 of them mixed numbers. |
| 11 | Unit 7 | Draw a coordinate grid and plot a map of 5 places. Find 3 distances. Draw the quadrilateral family diagram from memory. |
| 12 | Unit 8 | Solve 3 problems that mix volume, decimals and fractions. Write an expression for each before you work it out. |
| 13 | Mixed review | Take turns with a grown-up making up one question from each unit. |
| 14 | End-of-Year Check | Take the check in one sitting or in two halves. Then go over every question you missed. |

## Tips for the day of the check

- **Read the question twice.** Underline what it asks you to find and what units it wants.
- **Count every cube.** In a prism picture, find the number in one layer, then multiply by the number of layers.
- **Look at the scale** on a number line or line plot before you read it. Count the spaces between labels.
- **Rename before you add.** Fractions need a common denominator; decimals need lined-up places.
- **Across, then up.** Say it every time you read or plot a point.
- **Estimate.** If your answer is far from your estimate, look again.
- **Label your answers** in the short answers: 2,100 sticks, 7/12 mile.
- **Do not leave a question blank.** If you are stuck, make your best try and come back later.

## For grown-ups at home

Short, friendly practice every day helps more than one long session. Ask your child to explain how they got an answer, even when it is right, and praise the strategy, not just the answer. Everyday life is full of fifth-grade math: recipes to double or halve, prices and change, the volume of a moving box, distances on a map and measurements in two units. If a topic is still hard, that unit's weekly guides and flashcards are a good place to start again.
