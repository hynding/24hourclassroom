# Unit 4 · Wrapping Up Multiplication and Division with Multi-digit Numbers · Unit summary

**Weeks:** 12–14 · **Standards:** 5.NBT.B.5, 5.NBT.B.6, 5.OA.A.1
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 5, Unit 4, in our own words

## The big ideas

1. **The standard algorithm is partial products, written short.** Multiply one place at a time from the ones, and regroup into the next place: 473 × 6 = 2,838.
2. **A two-digit factor makes two rows.** 56 × 23 is 56 × 3 = 168 plus 56 × 20 = 1,120. The second row ends in 0 because it multiplies by tens.
3. **Division finds how many groups fit.** 1,512 ÷ 24 asks "24 times what is 1,512?" Take away friendly multiples of 24 (**partial quotients**) and add the groups: 50 + 10 + 3 = 63.
4. **A remainder is less than the divisor.** 935 ÷ 22 = 42 R 11. In a story, decide whether to drop it, round up or give it as the answer.
5. **Parentheses and brackets show what comes first.** Work from the inside out: 2 × [15 − (3 + 4)] = 2 × 8 = 16.
6. **One expression can hold a whole plan.** (187 + 15) ÷ 48 finds the buses for 187 students and 15 adults.
7. **Estimate to check.** 487 × 62 is about 500 × 60 = 30,000; 2,754 ÷ 51 is about 2,500 ÷ 50 = 50.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Standard algorithm | A short written method that multiplies one place at a time. |
| Partial product | One part of a product, such as 56 × 20 in 56 × 23. |
| Regroup | Trade 10 of one place for 1 of the next place. |
| Dividend | The number being divided. |
| Divisor | The number you divide by. |
| Quotient | The answer to a division. |
| Partial quotient | The groups taken away in one step of a division. |
| Remainder | What is left after making equal groups. |
| Expression | Numbers and operation signs with no equal sign. |
| Parentheses, brackets | ( ) and [ ]: symbols that group part of an expression. |

## Watch out for

- **The missing 0.** The second row of 312 × 24 is 6,240, not 624.
- **A forgotten regrouped digit.** Multiply, then add the small digit.
- **Writing the amount instead of the groups.** Taking away 1,440 is 30 groups of 48.
- **A remainder that is too big.** 41 R 33 for 935 ÷ 22 means another group fits.
- **Skipping the grouping.** In 3 × [20 − (4 × 2)], start with 4 × 2.
- **Answering the wrong question.** "How many buses?" and "how many full buses?" can have different answers.

## Practice set

1. 2,615 × 6 = ?
2. 427 × 35 = ?
3. 2,940 ÷ 35 = ?
4. 1,000 ÷ 32 = ? Give the remainder.
5. 3 × [20 − (4 × 2)] = ?
6. Write an expression for "add 18 and 15, then multiply by 24", and find its value.
7. 650 juice boxes go in packs of 24. How many packs are needed to hold every box?
8. Estimate 692 × 48, then find the exact product.

*Answers:* (1) 15,690. (2) 2,135 + 12,810 = 14,945. (3) 80 + 4 = 84. (4) 31 R 8, because 32 × 31 = 992. (5) 36. (6) (18 + 15) × 24 = 33 × 24 = 792. (7) 650 ÷ 24 = 27 R 2, so 28 packs; the last pack holds only 2. (8) About 700 × 50 = 35,000; exactly 33,216.

## For grown-ups at home

This unit finishes the work with whole numbers that started in Grade 3. Your child now multiplies with the standard algorithm, divides by two-digit numbers in chunks they choose, and writes whole plans as one expression with parentheses. Keep asking "About how much should it be?", "How can you check?" and "What does the remainder mean here?"
