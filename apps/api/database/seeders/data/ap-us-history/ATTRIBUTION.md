# Attribution

Every study guide, unit summary, flashcard deck and test item in this
directory is **original writing** for this course, organised around the
College Board *AP U.S. History Course and Exam Description* (topic codes and
titles; learning objectives paraphrased, not copied). The exam format and the
May 2027 changes come from AP Central (the AP U.S. History exam page and the
AP History Exam Updates page, accessed 2026-10-13). AP® is a trademark of
the College Board, which does not endorse this material.

Nothing here is adapted from a textbook. OpenStax *U.S. History* is linked
only as free further reading: its current licence is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and its
terms bar ingestion by language models, so its text was not used. Nor was
text taken from The American Yawp, Khan Academy, commercial review books or
College Board released exam questions.

**Quotations** are limited to the public domain: U.S. federal government
works (statutes, constitutional text, treaties, court opinions, congressional
documents, presidential messages and speeches made in office, federal
reports) and works published before 1931. Each was checked word for word
against the host named in the tables below (the National Archives, Founders
Online, the Library of Congress, the Avalon Project at Yale Law School, the
American Presidency Project, the Miller Center, Justia, Cornell's Legal
Information Institute, Project Gutenberg and similar). Where a text could not
be verified it is paraphrased without quotation marks. Copyrighted
20th-century private writing (for example Martin Luther King Jr., Betty
Friedan, Rachel Carson, Malcolm X) is described, never quoted.

**Interpretations** used as secondary-source stimuli are written for this
course and labelled so; none puts words in a real historian's mouth.
**Images, maps and cartoons** are described in words, not reproduced.
**Data tables** are rounded figures attributed to their federal source
(U.S. Census Bureau, *Historical Statistics of the United States*, the
Bureau of Labor Statistics and others); several were rounded from the
authors' knowledge rather than re-fetched and are listed for spot-checking
in the pull request that added the course.

Items that rest on no specific source carry `source: "general knowledge"` in
their YAML so a reviewer can find them.
