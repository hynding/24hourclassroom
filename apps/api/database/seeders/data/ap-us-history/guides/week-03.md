## What you need to be able to do

- **2.1** Explain the context in which several European empires competed for North America after 1607: what was new (lasting English, French and Dutch colonies) and what continued from Period 1 (Spanish power, disease, the hunt for wealth and converts).
- **2.2** Explain how and why the goals of Spanish, French, Dutch and British colonization differed, and how those goals shaped each empire's relations with American Indians, its labor systems and its settlement patterns.

In plain language: for any colony, be able to say what its founders wanted, how many people came and who they were, and what that meant for the American Indians already living there. Then be able to compare two colonies and explain *why* they differed.

## The context: an old empire and three new rivals (2.1)

By 1607 Spain had been an American power for a century. Its conquests of the Aztec and Inca empires (Period 1) had produced the silver that made it the richest state in Europe, and its colonial system rested on Native labor through encomienda and on enslaved Africans. Spain's success provoked its rivals. England, France and the Netherlands wanted colonies for national rivalry, profit and religion; Protestant England and the Netherlands saw Catholic Spain as their great enemy.

North America had changed too: smallpox, measles and influenza had run ahead of European settlers, and many Native nations had already lost a large share of their people. Yet Native nations still controlled almost the whole continent. Every European colony in Period 2 began as a small outpost that depended on Native trade, food or tolerance.

The most important thing to grasp for 2.1 is that the European empires *wanted different things*, and those wants predicted almost everything else about their colonies.

## Spain on the northern frontier

Spain's North American frontier was not like Mexico or Peru. There was no gold in Florida or New Mexico, few settlers wanted to go there, and the crown valued it chiefly as a buffer protecting the silver routes. Spain held it with three institutions:

- **Missions.** Franciscan friars founded missions among the Pueblos of New Mexico and the peoples of Florida, aiming to convert American Indians and to reshape their lives around the church, Spanish crops and Spanish labor routines.
- **Presidios.** Small garrisons protected missions and roads.
- **Labor demands.** Spanish colonists in New Mexico claimed Native labor and tribute through encomienda and repartimiento, a direct continuation of Period 1 practice.

Juan de Oñate's expedition of 1598 brought Spanish rule to New Mexico, and Santa Fe became the capital around 1610. Spanish settlers were always a small minority among the Pueblos. That imbalance mattered later: when the Pueblos rose in 1680 (week 5), the Spanish could not hold on.

## France: furs, faith and alliance

Samuel de Champlain founded Quebec in 1608, a year after Jamestown. New France existed for the fur trade, and beaver pelts could come only from Native hunters and traders. So the French needed partners, not land. They allied with the Huron (Wendat), Algonquin and other nations, and they fought alongside them against the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois). French **coureurs de bois** lived in Native villages, learned Native languages and often married Native women; their children, the **métis**, are evidence of how far the French were woven into Native societies. **Jesuit** missionaries also lived among Native peoples and tried to convert them without the forced labor typical of Spanish missions.

New France grew slowly. The crown restricted settlement to Catholics, the climate was hard and few French people wanted to emigrate. Even by the 1750s, New France's European population was a small fraction of the British colonies'. That fact would decide the imperial contest of the 1750s (Unit 3).

## The Dutch: a trading company's colony

After Henry Hudson's voyage of 1609, the Dutch West India Company built Fort Orange (Albany) and New Amsterdam (Manhattan) in the 1620s. Like the French, the Dutch wanted furs, which they bought from the Haudenosaunee and Mahican with guns and cloth, but they had little missionary interest.

New Netherland stayed small because the Dutch Republic was prosperous and few Dutch people wanted to leave; **patroonships** (big land grants worked by tenants) mostly failed to attract settlers. What the colony did have was variety. New Amsterdam held Dutch, Walloons, Germans, Scandinavians, English, free and enslaved Africans and, from 1654, a small Jewish community, tolerated chiefly because trade mattered more than uniformity. In 1664 an English fleet took the colony without a fight, and it became New York. The Dutch legacy of diversity and commerce shaped the Middle Colonies (week 4).

## England: land for settlers

England's colonies were different because England sent people, many of them. Population growth, enclosure of common land, rising rents and religious conflict pushed English people out; joint-stock companies and, later, proprietors pulled them over by promising land.

**Jamestown (1607).** The Virginia Company's colonists, mostly young men, arrived in Powhatan territory hoping for gold or a passage to Asia. Disease, brackish water, poor planning and conflict nearly destroyed the colony; in the starving time of 1609–1610 most colonists died. The Powhatan at first traded corn to the English and could have let them starve. **Tobacco** saved Virginia: John Rolfe's crop sold in England from about 1612, and planters spread up the rivers in search of land and laborers. That spread led to the Powhatan attacks of 1622 and 1644 and to the destruction of Powhatan power.

**Plymouth (1620).** Separatist Pilgrims, travelling with other passengers, landed at Cape Cod, outside the Virginia Company's grant. Before going ashore, the men signed the Mayflower Compact, creating a government by mutual agreement.

**Massachusetts Bay (1630).** Puritans under John Winthrop came to build a model Christian commonwealth (in Winthrop's image, paraphrased here, a city on a hill that the whole world would watch). They came in families during the Great Migration of the 1630s, about twenty thousand people in a decade.

All the English colonies shared large numbers, a hunger for land and little interest in incorporating American Indians, whom they usually pushed off land they wanted.

## Comparing the empires

| | Spain | France | Netherlands | England |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Main goal | Converts, labor, defence of silver empire | Furs, converts | Trade (furs) | Land for settlers; cash crops |
| Colonists | Few; soldiers, friars, some settlers | Few; mostly men | Few; diverse | Many; families (NE), servants (Chesapeake) |
| Relations with American Indians | Missions, labor demands, coercion | Trade, alliance, intermarriage | Trade | Displacement and wars over land |
| Government | Royal officials | Royal officials | Company officials | Charters, assemblies, compacts |

The pattern to remember: **goals → numbers → relations**. An empire that wanted furs needed Native partners and few settlers; an empire that wanted land sent many settlers and pushed Native peoples aside.

## Primary sources to read closely

**1. The First Charter of Virginia (1606)**

> "We, greatly commending, and graciously accepting of, their Desires for the Furtherance of so noble a Work, which may, by the Providence of Almighty God, hereafter tend to the Glory of his Divine Majesty, in propagating of Christian Religion to such People, as yet live in Darkness and miserable Ignorance of the true Knowledge and Worship of God, and may in time bring the Infidels and Savages, living in those parts, to human Civility, and to a settled and quiet Government ..."

*HIPP.* **Historical situation:** England was a late entrant to American colonization, watching Spain's wealth. **Intended audience:** investors, English subjects and rival monarchs. **Purpose:** to license a profit-seeking company while giving it religious and national legitimacy. **Point of view:** the crown's, which assumed European superiority. *Why it matters:* it shows that English colonizers, like the Spanish, mixed religious language with economic aims; in practice Virginia became a tobacco colony and conversion barely happened.

**2. The Mayflower Compact (1620)**

> "Having undertaken for the Glory of God, and Advancement of the Christian Faith, and the Honour of our King and Country, a Voyage to plant the first Colony in the northern Parts of Virginia; Do by these Presents, solemnly and mutually, in the Presence of God and one another, covenant and combine ourselves together into a civil Body Politick, for our better Ordering and Preservation, and Furtherance of the Ends aforesaid ..."

*HIPP.* **Historical situation:** the Mayflower had landed outside the Virginia Company's grant, and some passengers who were not Separatists talked of going their own way. **Audience:** the passengers themselves. **Purpose:** to create a legitimate government where none existed. **Point of view:** that of Separatists who saw their community as bound by covenant with God and one another. *Why it matters:* an early example of government by consent, a theme that runs through 2.3 and into the Revolution.

## Historical thinking: worked examples

**Worked example 1: a contextualization paragraph.** Prompt: *Evaluate the extent of difference between French and English colonization in North America from 1607 to 1754.* Contextualization must describe the broader situation, not just mention a date.

> "By the early 1600s, Spain's conquests in Mexico and Peru had made it the richest power in Europe, and Protestant England and the Netherlands, along with Catholic France, were determined to share in American wealth. At the same time, epidemic disease carried by the Columbian Exchange had already devastated many American Indian nations, though they still controlled most of the continent. Into this world the French and English brought colonies with very different aims."

Why it works: it explains why new empires were colonizing and what conditions they met, then leads into the thesis.

**Worked example 2: a comparison chain.** Build comparison as a chain from goal to consequence.

- France wanted furs → needed Native hunters → few colonists, mostly men → coureurs de bois and intermarriage → alliances with the Huron and Algonquin → drawn into wars against the Haudenosaunee.
- England wanted land → large migration, many in families → farms that displaced Native towns → little intermarriage → recurrent wars over land (Powhatan, Pequot).

Then state the comparison in one sentence: *Because France wanted furs and England wanted land, French colonists depended on American Indian partners while English colonists displaced them.*

**Worked example 3: complicate your own thesis.** For the complexity point, show a similarity under the difference: both empires used religious justifications, and early Jamestown depended on Powhatan corn as Quebec depended on Huron furs. The difference widened as English numbers grew.

## Common misconceptions

- **"The Pilgrims and the Puritans were the same."** The Pilgrims were Separatists who had left the Church of England; the Puritans wanted to reform it from within. Plymouth (1620) and Massachusetts Bay (1630) were different colonies until they merged in 1691.
- **"Jamestown was founded for religious freedom."** It was a business venture. Religious language appears in its charter, but profit drove it.
- **"The French were kind to American Indians."** French colonists were fewer and needed Native partners, so relations were more cooperative, but they also brought disease, alcohol, war and missionary pressure.
- **"American Indians were passive."** Native nations chose trading partners and allies and decided the fate of early colonies.
