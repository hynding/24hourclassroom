# Unit 8 · Period 8: 1945–1980 · The night-before sheet

**Exam weight:** 10–17% of the AP exam. **CED topics:** 8.1 Contextualizing Period 8 · 8.2 The Cold War from 1945 to 1980 · 8.3 The Red Scare · 8.4 Economy after 1945 · 8.5 Culture after 1945 · 8.6 Early Steps in the Civil Rights Movement · 8.7 America as a World Power · 8.8 The Vietnam War · 8.9 The Great Society · 8.10 The African American Civil Rights Movement (1960s) · 8.11 The Civil Rights Movement Expands · 8.12 Youth Culture of the 1960s · 8.13 The Environment and Natural Resources from 1968 to 1980 · 8.14 Society in Transition · 8.15 Continuity and Change in Period 8.

## The ideas that generate every question

1. **Containment organised foreign policy from Truman to Carter.** The goal never changed; the means did: economic aid (1947), alliances (1949), militarisation (NSC-68, Korea), nuclear deterrence and covert action (Eisenhower), flexible response (Kennedy), limited war (Vietnam), détente (Nixon), human rights (Carter).
2. **The Cold War shaped life at home.** It produced the Red Scare, paid for the Sun Belt, built highways and schools, framed religion and gave civil rights an international audience.
3. **Prosperity was broad but unequal.** The GI Bill, suburbs and union contracts built a mass middle class; redlining, regional poverty and discrimination kept many out.
4. **Rights expanded through courts, protest and federal law.** Brown, Montgomery, Birmingham, Selma, the Civil Rights and Voting Rights Acts; then women, Latinos, American Indians and gay Americans adapted the model.
5. **The liberal consensus peaked and cracked.** The Great Society was its high point; Vietnam, urban riots, the counterculture, Watergate and stagflation broke it, from the left and the right.
6. **By 1980 confidence had given way to limits.** Defeat in Vietnam, a resigned president, oil shocks and inflation produced distrust of government and the New Right.

## Key chains

**Causation.** Soviet control of Eastern Europe → Truman Doctrine and Marshall Plan → Berlin and NATO → 1949 shocks → NSC-68 → Korea → permanent military-industrial complex.

**Causation.** Televised violence (Birmingham 1963, Selma 1965) → presidential action → Civil Rights Act and Voting Rights Act → Black registration surges → Southern whites shift toward the Republican Party.

**Causation.** Tet → credibility gap → Johnson withdraws → Nixon and Vietnamization → Paris Accords → War Powers Resolution.

**Comparison.** First Red Scare (immigrants, short) vs Second (officials, institutionalised). New Deal (depression, recovery) vs Great Society (abundance, inclusion). Massive retaliation vs flexible response. Integrationist civil rights vs Black Power.

**Continuity and change (8.15).** Continuity: containment; a large federal government; racial inequality in wealth and housing. Change: legal segregation ended; women's legal equality advanced; growth gave way to stagflation; trust in government collapsed; the New Deal coalition split.

## Thesis-ready arguments

- "Containment remained the goal of American foreign policy from 1947 to 1980, but its means shifted from economic aid to military commitment and then to negotiation, as the costs of Korea and Vietnam exposed the limits of American power."
- "Postwar prosperity built a mass middle class, but because federal housing and veterans' policies were administered in discriminatory ways, its benefits flowed disproportionately to white suburban families."
- "The federal government advanced African American civil rights substantially between 1945 and 1968, but it usually acted only after grassroots protest forced it to, and it did little about Northern economic inequality."
- "The Great Society extended the New Deal's logic from security for workers to opportunity for the excluded, but its dependence on growth made it vulnerable when the boom ended."
- "The rights movements of the 1960s and 1970s borrowed the civil rights movement's tactics and won legal gains, but their varied goals and the rise of the New Right limited what they achieved."

## The unit exam

Forty-four questions: six source-based multiple-choice sets (Executive Order 9835, Kennedy's inaugural, the domino press conference, the Civil Rights Act of 1964, Nixon's 1970 State of the Union, and a poverty-rate table), standalone items across every topic, about a fifth reviewing earlier periods, one short-answer question on Vietnamization, and a seven-point DBQ on how far the federal government advanced African American civil rights from 1945 to 1968, using seven public-domain documents. Before you start the DBQ, date every document and ask who wrote it, for whom and why; at least two of your sourcing explanations must connect to your argument.
