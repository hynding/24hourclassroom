# Unit 1 · Period 1: 1491–1607 · The night-before sheet

**Exam weight:** about 4–6% of the AP exam · **CED topics:** 1.1 Contextualizing Period 1 · 1.2 Native American Societies Before European Contact · 1.3 European Exploration in the Americas · 1.4 Columbian Exchange, Spanish Exploration, and Conquest · 1.5 Labor, Slavery, and Caste in the Spanish Colonial System · 1.6 Cultural Interactions Between Europeans, Native Americans, and Africans · 1.7 Causation in Period 1
**Themes:** GEO, MIG, SOC, WOR, WXT, ARC · **Reasoning process:** Causation
**Built from:** Harriot, *A Briefe and True Report* (1588); Columbus's letter on the first voyage (1493); the Treaty of Tordesillas (1494); Las Casas, *A Brief Account of the Destruction of the Indies* (1552)

## The ideas that generate every question

1. **Diversity, shaped by environment.** North America in 1491 held millions of people in hundreds of societies. Maize spreading north from Mexico supported settled, complex societies in the Southwest and the Mississippi Valley; the dry Great Basin and Plains favoured mobility; rich coasts let Northwest and California peoples settle without farming.
2. **Motives plus means.** Europeans sailed for wealth, Christianity and advantage over rivals (God, gold and glory), and could do so because of caravels, the compass and astrolabe, and stronger monarchies.
3. **Biology as history.** The Columbian Exchange moved crops, livestock and germs both ways. Epidemics in populations with no immunity killed most Native people in many regions and made conquest possible.
4. **Conquest needed allies.** A few thousand Spaniards won empires because of disease, Native allies and divided enemies, as well as steel and horses.
5. **Labor systems and hierarchy.** Spain built its colonies on coerced Native labor (encomienda, mita), then enslaved Africans, ranked by the casta system.
6. **Encounters were two-sided and contested.** Native peoples and Africans resisted, allied and adapted; Europeans argued among themselves (Las Casas against Sepúlveda) about whether conquest was just.

## Native societies before 1492 (1.1, 1.2)

| Region | Economy | Settlement and society | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Southwest | irrigated maize, beans, squash, cotton | multistorey adobe and stone towns | Ancestral Puebloans (Chaco, c. 850–1150); Hopi, Zuni |
| Mississippi Valley, Southeast | maize-based farming | mound centres, ranked chiefdoms, long-distance trade | Cahokia (c. 1050–1350) |
| Great Basin | gathering seeds and roots, hunting | small mobile bands | |
| Great Plains | bison hunting on foot; some valley farming | mobile; earth lodges in some valleys | no horses before the Spanish |
| Northeast, Atlantic seaboard | Three Sisters plus hunting and fishing | villages moved every few years | Haudenosaunee longhouses, matrilineal clans, Five Nations league |
| Pacific Northwest | salmon, shellfish, forest resources | permanent plank-house villages, no farming | Chinook; potlatch |
| California | acorns, fishing, hunting | dense, settled, many languages | |

Common threads: land held in common for use, not sold; complementary gender roles with real power for women in farming societies; landscapes actively shaped by burning and irrigation.

**Columbian Exchange.** To the Americas: horses, cattle, pigs, sheep, wheat, sugarcane, rice, smallpox, measles, influenza (later malaria and yellow fever). From the Americas: maize, potatoes, tomatoes, beans, squash, cacao, tobacco, silver.

## Labor, slavery and caste (1.5)

| Institution | What it was | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Encomienda | colonist granted Native labor and tribute, owed protection and Christian instruction | widespread abuse |
| New Laws (1542) | banned Native enslavement, limited encomiendas | resisted; partly relaxed 1545 |
| Repartimiento | rotating labor draft under officials | coercion continued |
| Mita | Inca labor draft adapted for Potosí | high mortality |
| African slavery | from the early 1500s, for Caribbean and Brazilian sugar and mines | start of the transatlantic trade to the Americas |
| Casta system | peninsulares > criollos > mestizos, mulattoes and others > Native people, enslaved Africans | ranked mixed ancestry |

## Chains to have ready

- **Causation:** Ottoman control of trade + Portuguese navigation + Spanish unity → voyages → Columbian Exchange → epidemics → conquest → labor demand → encomienda → Native collapse + New Laws → African slavery → silver and sugar wealth → European rivals seek colonies after 1600.
- **Comparison:** Pueblo vs Plains (same dry West; irrigated farming vs mobile hunting). Mississippian chiefdoms vs Haudenosaunee councils (ranked vs balanced by clan mothers). Spain vs Portugal (conquest and settlement vs trading posts, until Brazil).
- **Continuity and change:** Native agency continues across the period; Iberian habits (Reconquista warfare, Atlantic island plantations) continue across the ocean; what changes is the scale of death, the labor system and the global economy.

## Thesis-ready arguments

- "Disease and Native alliances, more than European technology, explain Spanish success in conquering the Aztec and Inca empires."
- "The Columbian Exchange transformed the Americas far more than Europe before 1607: epidemics and livestock shattered Native societies, while in Europe silver changed economies but social structures largely continued."
- "Environment set the basic possibilities for Native societies before 1492, but the spread of maize and political choices produced different societies even in similar environments."
- "Spain's colonial labor system shifted from coerced Native labor toward African slavery because of Native population collapse, Crown restrictions and the demands of sugar."

## Sourcing reminders

- **Columbus (1493):** a report and a sales pitch to his sponsors; gentle, unarmed people and promises of gold and slaves.
- **Las Casas (1542/1552):** a reformer's purpose; vivid and with exaggerated numbers; later reused as anti-Spanish propaganda.
- **Harriot (1588):** a careful eyewitness, but a promoter writing for English investors and seeing through English and Christian eyes.
- **Tordesillas (1494):** two Catholic crowns dividing lands they had barely seen; no Native voice.

## The unit exam

Forty-five items, about 75 minutes, every topic from 1.1 to 1.7. Five source-based multiple-choice sets (Columbus's promises to the monarchs, Harriot on Algonquian religion, an interpretation of the Columbian Exchange, a map of Spanish expeditions and two positions at Valladolid), stand-alone multiple choice, select-all, true/false and fill-in-the-blank items, one SAQ on John White's painting of Secotan, and one LEQ on the extent of the Columbian Exchange's effects. For the SAQ, answer each part separately with one specific example. For the LEQ, take a position on "extent", contextualize before the thesis, and compare the Americas with Europe.
