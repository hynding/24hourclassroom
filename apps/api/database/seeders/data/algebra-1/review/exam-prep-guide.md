# Algebra 1 · Final Exam Prep Guide

**Built from:** the seven units of this course (IM Algebra 1, Units 1–7, in our own words) · **Use with:** the seven unit summaries ("night-before sheets") and the practice final (week 34)

## The format of the practice final

The practice final is a full-year paper of 70 questions, about 2 hours 30 minutes, in course order: Unit 1 first, Unit 7 last. Each unit's share follows its weight in the course map.

| Unit | Weight | Questions | What it covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · One-variable Statistics | 10–12% | 7 | Data displays, shape, centre and spread, standard deviation, outliers, comparing distributions |
| 2 · Linear Equations, Inequalities, and Systems | 22–25% | 16 | Writing and solving equations, equivalent equations, graphs, systems by substitution and elimination, inequalities in one and two variables, systems of inequalities |
| 3 · Two-variable Statistics | 8–10% | 6 | Two-way tables, relative frequencies, associations, lines of fit, residuals, correlation, causation |
| 4 · Functions | 12–15% | 10 | Function notation, features of graphs, average rate of change, domain and range, piecewise and absolute value functions, inverses |
| 5 · Introduction to Exponential Functions | 15–18% | 11 | Growth and decay factors, a · bˣ, negative exponents, graphs, percent change, compound interest, comparing with linear growth |
| 6 · Introduction to Quadratic Functions | 12–15% | 9 | Quadratic change, patterns, projectile and revenue models, standard, factored and vertex forms, graphs |
| 7 · Quadratic Equations | 15–18% | 11 | Zero product property, factoring, completing the square, the quadratic formula, the discriminant, vertex form, modelling |

**Item types.** Multiple choice (1 point), select-all (2 points, partial credit), true/false (1), fill-in-the-blank (1), numeric (1), three short answers (3 points each, parts (a), (b), (c)) and two long answers (8 points each, four numbered parts of 2 points). Several items share a stimulus: a table, a described graph, a scenario or a function. The paper is out of 100 points.

**Timing.** About two minutes per one-point item, four for a select-all, six for a short answer and twelve for a long answer leaves a few minutes to check. Do the free-response items in the order given; a long answer is worth eight multiple-choice items, so do not leave it for the last five minutes.

**Grading.** Multiple choice, select-all, true/false and numeric items are graded at once; fill-in-the-blank items are matched automatically; the short answers and long answers are graded by your teacher against the scoring notes attached to each.

## What each unit contributes

**Unit 1.** You will read a dot plot, histogram or box plot described in words, name the shape (symmetric, skewed left or right, uniform, bimodal), choose mean or median and standard deviation or IQR to match the shape, compute a five-number summary, test for outliers with 1.5 × IQR, and compare two distributions in sentences that use centre, spread and shape.

**Unit 2.** The largest share. Write an equation or inequality from a sentence or a situation; solve it and explain each step as a move that keeps the solutions; rewrite an equation to solve for a chosen variable; connect a linear equation to its graph (slope, intercepts, standard form); solve a system by substitution or elimination and interpret the solution, including systems with no solution or infinitely many; graph a linear inequality in two variables and a system of them, and name a point in the solution region.

**Unit 3.** Read and build two-way tables and relative frequency tables (by row, by column, by total), decide whether two categorical variables are associated, fit a line to data, compute and interpret a residual, interpret a correlation coefficient's sign and size, and explain why correlation does not establish causation.

**Unit 4.** Evaluate and interpret f(x) notation in context, read a graph's intercepts, maximum and minimum, increasing and decreasing intervals, compute average rate of change, state domain and range from a situation or a graph, evaluate piecewise and absolute value functions, and find and interpret an inverse.

**Unit 5.** Classify a table as linear or exponential, write a · bˣ from a description or two points, convert percent change to a factor, evaluate with negative exponents, read a and b from a graph, compute compound interest with different compounding intervals, rewrite 1.005^(12t) as 1.0617ᵗ, and explain why exponential growth eventually beats linear growth.

**Unit 6.** Recognise quadratic change from second differences, write an expression from a pattern, build 16t² and h₀ + vt − 16t² models and revenue models, expand and factor, read intercepts and the vertex from the three forms, describe how a, b, c, h and k move a parabola, and read a model's graph.

**Unit 7.** Solve by taking roots, by the zero product property after factoring, by completing the square, and by the quadratic formula; count solutions with the discriminant; classify them as rational or irrational; rewrite a function in vertex form to find a maximum or minimum; and solve a modelling problem from start to finish, including where a line meets a parabola.

## A two-week review plan

Each day is 45 to 60 minutes. Start with the unit summary, then redo the drills at its end, then rework the quiz items you got wrong during the year.

| Day | Focus | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Unit 1 | Summary; a five-number summary and outlier check by hand; standard deviation of a small set; one comparison paragraph. |
| 2 | Unit 2, equations | Summary sections on writing and solving; rewrite three formulas for a different variable; explain each step of a solution. |
| 3 | Unit 2, systems | One system by substitution, one by elimination, one with no solution; interpret each in a context. |
| 4 | Unit 2, inequalities | Solve and graph one-variable inequalities; graph two two-variable inequalities and shade the system's region; pick and check a point. |
| 5 | Unit 3 | Build a relative frequency table both ways; compute two residuals; write one sentence on correlation versus causation. |
| 6 | Unit 4 | Function notation in context; average rate of change on two intervals; domain and range of two situations; one absolute value and one piecewise evaluation; one inverse. |
| 7 | Rest, or redo the weakest day | Review the mistakes you made on days 1–6. |
| 8 | Unit 5, functions | Classify tables; write a · bˣ from two points; negative exponents; percent change factors; a decay model. |
| 9 | Unit 5, interest and comparison | Compound interest at two compounding intervals; rewrite with a different step; a linear-versus-exponential comparison with rates of change. |
| 10 | Unit 6, forms | Second differences; a pattern expression; expand and factor; read intercepts and the vertex from each form; sketch one parabola per form. |
| 11 | Unit 6, models | One projectile and one revenue model: zeros, vertex, domain, meaning; the effects of a, b, c, h, k. |
| 12 | Unit 7, solving | Ten equations: three by roots, three by factoring, two by completing the square, two by the formula; check each. |
| 13 | Unit 7, discriminant and modelling | Classify five equations by discriminant; vertex form of three functions; one full modelling problem; one line-meets-parabola. |
| 14 | Practice final | Sit the practice final in one block. Mark it, then spend the time left on the units where you lost the most points. |

## The formulas and procedures to know cold

| Topic | Know |
|---|---|
| Centre and spread | mean, median, IQR = Q3 − Q1, standard deviation (the typical distance from the mean); outlier if below Q1 − 1.5·IQR or above Q3 + 1.5·IQR |
| Slope and lines | m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁); y = mx + b; Ax + By = C; intercepts by setting the other variable to 0 |
| Systems | substitution: replace a variable; elimination: add multiples to cancel a variable; same line means infinitely many solutions, parallel lines mean none |
| Inequalities | flip the sign when multiplying or dividing by a negative; boundary solid for ≤ and ≥, dashed for < and >; test a point to shade |
| Two-way tables | row, column and total relative frequencies; an association shows as different conditional percentages |
| Lines of fit | residual = actual − predicted; r between −1 and 1, sign gives direction, size gives strength |
| Functions | f(a) = b means input a gives output b; average rate of change (f(b) − f(a))/(b − a); domain and range from the situation; f⁻¹ swaps input and output |
| Absolute value | |x − a| is the distance from a; graph a V with vertex at (a, 0) |
| Exponential | f(x) = a · bˣ, a the value at 0, b the factor; b = 1 + r for growth, 1 − r for decay; b⁻ⁿ = 1/bⁿ; factor over width w is bʷ |
| Interest | P(1 + r)ⁿ per period; P(1 + R/k)^(kt) for k periods a year; simple interest is linear |
| Quadratic forms | standard ax² + bx + c (y-intercept c); factored a(x − p)(x − q) (zeros p, q); vertex a(x − h)² + k (vertex (h, k)) |
| Quadratic graphs | axis halfway between zeros; a sets direction and width; c and k shift vertically; b and h shift the vertex sideways |
| Models | free fall 16t²; launch h₀ + vt − 16t²; revenue = price × quantity |
| Solving | isolate a square and take ± roots; zero product property (needs 0 on one side); factor by product and sum; complete the square by adding (b/2)²; x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac))/(2a) |
| Discriminant | b² − 4ac: negative none, zero one, perfect square rational, otherwise irrational |

## How to show work so a grader can follow it

The short and long answers are graded against scoring notes that award a point for each part. The graders look for specific things; make them easy to find.

1. **Label the parts.** Write (a), (b), (c) or (1), (2), (3), (4) and answer each in order. A part answered inside another part is easy to miss.
2. **Write the equation before the answer.** "16t² = 144, so t = 3" earns the point; "3" alone may not, and cannot earn partial credit if it is wrong.
3. **Keep units and context.** "The ball lands after 3 seconds" rather than "3". For a model, say what a zero, a vertex or an intercept means.
4. **Reject the extra solution out loud.** "w = −6 is rejected because a width cannot be negative." The rejection is usually worth a point by itself.
5. **Give exact forms and decimals when asked.** 2 ± √7 is the exact answer; 4.65 and −0.65 are approximations. If the question says "to two decimal places", give two.
6. **Check once.** Substitute a solution back into the original equation and write the check. Checks catch sign errors and earn credit in scoring notes that ask for them.
7. **For comparisons and explanations, use the key words.** "Constant ratios, so exponential"; "second differences constant, so quadratic"; "the discriminant is negative, so no real solutions". A grader is looking for the reason, not just the conclusion.
8. **Describe graphs in words.** State the intercepts, the vertex or the slope, the direction, and one or two more points. A sketch without labels earns little.

## The night before

Reread the seven unit summaries, not the week guides. Redo the ten quick drills at the end of each summary and check the answers. Sleep. On the day, read each stimulus once carefully before its set of questions, answer every multiple-choice item (there is no penalty for a wrong answer), and keep fifteen minutes at the end to reread the long answers.
