## The ideas that generate every question

1. **A third kind of change.** Linear tables have constant first differences, exponential tables constant ratios, quadratic tables constant **second** differences. The coefficient of n² is half the second difference.
2. **Patterns give expressions.** An n-by-n block plus two columns of n is n² + 2n; a hollow frame is 4n − 4 (linear). Check every expression against the figures.
3. **Exponential beats quadratic in the end.** n² is ahead of 2ⁿ at n = 3, equal at 2 and 4, behind from 5 on. Each step adds a linearly growing amount to a quadratic but multiplies an exponential.
4. **Three situations.** Free fall: d = 16t² feet, height h₀ − 16t². Launch: h₀ + vt − 16t². Revenue: price × quantity, with quantity falling as price rises. Zeros, vertex and domain all mean something.
5. **Three forms, one function.** Standard ax² + bx + c shows the y-intercept c. Factored a(x − p)(x − q) shows the x-intercepts p and q. Vertex a(x − h)² + k shows the vertex (h, k). All share a. Move between them by expanding.
6. **The vertex is halfway between the zeros.** Average the x-intercepts, substitute, and you have the vertex; the axis of symmetry is the vertical line through it, and points equally far from it have equal outputs.
7. **What a, b, c, h, k do.** a: sign gives the direction (up if positive), size gives the width (bigger is narrower). c: slides vertically, is the y-intercept. b: moves the vertex sideways (x² + bx has zeros 0 and −b, vertex at −b/2). h: horizontal shift, with the backwards sign. k: vertical shift, the maximum or minimum value.
8. **Read the graph of a model.** y-intercept: the start. Vertex: the peak or the best price. Positive x-intercept: the landing or the price at which sales stop. Domain: only the part of the parabola the situation uses.

## Key formulas and procedures

| Task | Procedure |
|---|---|
| Classify a table | first differences constant: linear; ratios constant: exponential; second differences constant: quadratic |
| Expression from a quadratic table | coefficient of n² = half the second difference; subtract that part and fit the linear remainder |
| Dropped object | d = 16t² ft; height h₀ − 16t²; lands when 16t² = h₀ |
| Launched object | h(t) = h₀ + vt − 16t²; peak halfway between equal heights |
| Revenue | r(p) = p · (quantity sold at p); zeros where p = 0 or quantity = 0; best price halfway between |
| Expand (x + m)(x + n) | x² + (m + n)x + mn; area diagram with four pieces |
| Factor x² + bx + c | two numbers with product c and sum b |
| Intercepts | factored form → x-intercepts; standard form → y-intercept |
| Vertex from factored form | x = (p + q)/2, then substitute |
| Vertex from vertex form | (h, k); minimum if a > 0, maximum if a < 0 |
| y-intercept from vertex form | a · h² + k |
| x-intercepts from vertex form | set to 0, isolate the square, x − h = ±√(−k/a) |
| Shift a parabola | right h: (x − h)²; up k: + k |
| Vertex form to standard form | expand the square, multiply by a, add k |

## Worked examples

**Classify and build.** 3, 8, 15, 24: differences 5, 7, 9; second differences 2; n² plus 2, 4, 6 = 2n: n² + 2n; step 10 is 120. 2, 8, 18, 32: second differences 4, so 2n².

**Free fall.** From 576 ft: height 576 − 16t²; 320 ft at t = 4; lands at t = 6. A rock falls 16, 48, 80 ft in successive seconds.

**Launch.** h(t) = 6 + 64t − 16t²: starts at 6 ft, 64 ft/s; heights 6, 54, 70, 54, 6 at t = 0 to 4; peak 70 ft at t = 2; average rate 48 ft/s then 16 ft/s over the first two seconds.

**Revenue.** p(200 − 10p): zeros 0 and 20, best price $10, revenue $1,000, domain 0 ≤ p ≤ 20. 120p − 20p²: best price $3, $180.

**Forms.** (x − 2)(x + 4) = x² + 2x − 8: intercepts 2, −4; y-intercept −8; vertex (−1, −9). x² − 6x + 5 = (x − 1)(x − 5) = (x − 3)² − 4: intercepts 1, 5; vertex (3, −4). 2(x − 1)(x + 5) = 2x² + 8x − 10.

**Graphs.** −(x − 1)(x − 5): opens down, intercepts 1, 5, vertex (3, 4), y-intercept −5. 2x² − 8: vertex (0, −8), intercepts ±2, narrower. x² + 6x: zeros 0, −6, vertex (−3, −9). x² + 4x and x² − 4x: mirror images.

**Vertex form.** (x + 2)² − 5: vertex (−2, −5). −(x − 2)² + 9: max 9 at x = 2, y-intercept 5, x-intercepts −1 and 5. 2(x + 1)² − 8: min −8, y-intercept −6, x-intercepts 1 and −3. 3(x − 7)² − 11: minimum −11.

**Shifts.** x² right 3, down 4: (x − 3)² − 4. (x + 3)² is x² moved **left** 3. Vertex (0.5, 11.25) through (0, 10): a = −5.

**Model graph.** Fountain −0.5(x − 4)² + 8: nozzle at (0, 0), peak (4, 8), lands at x = 8, domain 0 to 8. Diver −5(t − 0.5)² + 11.25 = −5t² + 5t + 10: platform 10 m, peak 11.25 m, enters at t = 2.

## Common errors

- Calling a table quadratic from first differences alone; check the second differences.
- Using the second difference itself as the coefficient of n².
- Writing h₀ − 16t (linear) for a dropped object, or leaving out vt for a thrown one.
- Revenue written as the quantity 200 − 10p instead of p(200 − 10p).
- (x + 3)(x + 5) = x² + 15, forgetting the middle terms; (x + 4)² = x² + 16.
- Reading the x-intercept of (x + 4) as 4; the vertex of (x + 2)² − 5 as (2, −5).
- Averaging the numbers inside the factors instead of the intercepts.
- Taking the y-intercept of vertex form as k.
- Deciding the direction from k or c instead of a.
- Thinking c moves the graph sideways, or that (x + 3)² moves it right.
- Giving a model the whole parabola as its domain.

## Quick drills (answers at the end)

1. Classify 4, 7, 12, 19, 28 and find its expression.
2. An object is dropped from 256 ft. When does it land?
3. h(t) = 8 + 32t − 16t². Starting height, launch speed, h(1), maximum height.
4. Tickets at p dollars sell 90 − 15p. Best price and revenue.
5. Expand (x − 4)(x + 7). Factor x² + 9x + 20.
6. Vertex and intercepts of y = (x + 3)(x − 5).
7. Describe y = −3x² + 1 relative to y = x².
8. Vertex of y = x² − 12x.
9. Vertex, y-intercept and x-intercepts of y = (x − 4)² − 9.
10. Equation of y = x² moved left 5 and up 2.

*Answers:* (1) Second differences 2: n² + 3 (check: 1 + 3 = 4, 4 + 3 = 7). (2) 16t² = 256, t = 4 s. (3) 8 ft; 32 ft/s; h(1) = 24; peak at t = 1 (h(0.5) = 20 = h(1.5)), 24 ft. (4) Zeros 0 and 6; p = 3; 3 · 45 = $135. (5) x² + 3x − 28; (x + 4)(x + 5). (6) Intercepts −3, 5; y-intercept −15; vertex (1, −16). (7) Flipped downward, narrower, up 1; vertex (0, 1). (8) Zeros 0, 12; vertex (6, −36). (9) (4, −9); 16 − 9 = 7; (x − 4)² = 9, x = 7 or 1. (10) y = (x + 5)² + 2.

## What to review from earlier units

- Average rate of change (unit 4) measures how a thrown ball slows; it is the slope between two points on the parabola.
- Features of graphs (unit 4): intercepts, maximum and minimum, increasing and decreasing intervals, all read on parabolas now.
- Domain and range (unit 4) restrict every model to the part of the parabola that makes sense.
- Exponential functions (unit 5): constant ratios versus constant second differences, and why 2ⁿ eventually passes n².
- Equivalent expressions and the distributive property (unit 2) are the engine behind expanding and factoring.
