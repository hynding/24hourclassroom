## What was adapted and what is original

**Adapted from IM** (structure, not text):

- The sequence of seven units and the unit titles.
- The lesson titles, which serve as this course's topic titles, and the lesson
  numbers, which serve as its topic codes (`"2.14"` is Unit 2, Lesson 14).
- The learning goals of each lesson, paraphrased in each week guide's "What
  you need to be able to do" list.
- The eight Standards for Mathematical Practice (MP1–MP8), which IM tags its
  lessons with and which this course names on each guide. The standards
  themselves are from the Common Core State Standards for Mathematics
  (© 2010 National Governors Association Center for Best Practices and Council
  of Chief State School Officers), reproduced here only by their short titles.
- The broad kinds of situation each unit models (data distributions, pizza
  parties and ticket sales, cooling and battery power, bacteria and bank
  accounts, falling and thrown objects, revenue and pricing), chosen so the
  course matches IM's story arc.

**Original to this course** (written for it, not copied or lightly edited):

- Every explanation, definition and table in the 32 week guides, the seven
  unit summaries and the final-exam prep guide.
- Every worked example and every "Try it" exercise, with their numbers,
  contexts and answers.
- Every flashcard (fronts and backs) in the 32 decks.
- Every quiz item, unit-exam item and practice-final item: prompts, stimuli,
  options, answers, rationales and scoring notes. No IM activity, practice
  problem, cool-down or assessment item was reproduced, and the numbers in
  this course's problems were chosen independently.

No text, figure or data set was copied from any other textbook. OpenStax
*Elementary Algebra*, *Intermediate Algebra* and *College Algebra* were not
used: their licence is now CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 with a clause barring ingestion
by language models. Nothing was taken from Khan Academy, Desmos, commercial
review books or released state or college-entrance exam questions.
Wikipedia and Euclid's *Elements* were not used. Items that rest on no
particular IM lesson carry the nearest lesson in their `source` field so a
reviewer can see what they are meant to assess.

Mathematics is written in plain Unicode (x², √7, ≤, π) rather than LaTeX or
HTML; graphs and figures are described in words. The standard deviation
divides by n unless an item says otherwise, following IM's convention.

for it; each unit's summary and exam are built on all of that unit's lessons;
the prep guide and practice final draw on all seven units. Every URL was
