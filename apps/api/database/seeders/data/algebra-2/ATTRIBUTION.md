## What was adapted and what is original

- **Adapted:** the order of units and lessons, what each lesson sets out to
  teach, and the broad modelling contexts the IM course is known for (box
  volumes, Ferris wheels, population growth, sampling simulations). Where a
  guide paraphrases an IM lesson's goal it does so in its own words; no IM
  task statements, problem sets or lesson narrative are reproduced, and no
  quoted phrase exceeds a few words.
- **Original:** every study guide, every worked example and "Try it" set,
  every flashcard, every quiz, unit-exam and practice-final item with its
  rationales and scoring notes, every unit summary and the exam-prep guide.
  Numeric data in stimuli (tables of sample statistics, daylight hours, tide
  times, account balances) was invented for this course and computed to be
  internally consistent; it is not real-world data.
- Items that rest on no specific lesson carry `source: "general knowledge"`
  in their YAML so a reviewer can find them. Every other item names the IM
  lesson it follows (`source: "IM Algebra 2, Unit N, Lesson L"`).

No material was taken from OpenStax (whose current licence forbids it), Khan
Academy, commercial textbooks or any other site. Standard definitions
(normal distribution, complex number, radian) are common mathematical
knowledge and were not copied from any reference; no Wikipedia text and no
Euclid were used.

Standard normal areas quoted inside statistics items are rounded table
values supplied in the item itself, so the student never needs an outside
table.
