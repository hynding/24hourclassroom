# Algebra 2 · Final Exam Prep Guide

**Format:** the practice final (week 34) is 70 questions in about 2 hours 15 minutes, spanning all seven units in course order and weighted as the table below shows · **Built from:** the seven unit summaries and the 32 week guides of this course, in our own words

## The format

| Part | Items | Points | How it is graded |
|---|---|---|---|
| Multiple choice | about 45 | 1 each | at once |
| Select-all | 2 | 2 each, partial credit | at once |
| True/false | 4 | 1 each | at once |
| Fill-in-the-blank | 3 | 1 each | matched automatically |
| Numeric | 11 | 1 each | at once, within a stated tolerance |
| Short answer | 3 | 3 each, parts (a) (b) (c) | by your teacher against the scoring notes |
| Extended response | 2 | 6 each | by your teacher against the scoring notes |

Seven shared-context sets of three questions open each unit's block: a sequence, an open box, two complex numbers, a growing population, a function given by points, a Ferris wheel and a normal distribution of battery lifetimes. Read the stimulus once, carefully, and reuse it for all three.

A scientific calculator is allowed and needed (logarithms, e, trigonometric values in radians, square roots). No formula sheet: the formulas below are the ones to know cold. Where a question needs a normal-table value it is given in the question.

## The formulas and facts to know cold

**Sequences.** Arithmetic: a(n) = a(1) + d(n − 1); sum of n terms = n · (first + last)/2. Geometric: a(n) = a(1) · r^(n−1); sum of n terms = a(1)(rⁿ − 1)/(r − 1).

**Polynomials.** Degree and leading coefficient fix end behavior (even degree: both ends the same way; odd: opposite; negative leading coefficient flips). A factor (x − a) means a zero at a; odd multiplicity crosses, even touches. Remainder theorem: dividing by (x − a) leaves p(a). Identities: (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b², a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b), a³ ± b³ = (a ± b)(a² ∓ ab + b²).

**Rational functions.** Vertical asymptote where the denominator is 0 (and the numerator is not); zero where the numerator is 0; horizontal asymptote y = 0 if the denominator's degree is larger, y = (ratio of leading coefficients) if equal, none if the numerator's degree is larger. Solve by multiplying out denominators and checking every candidate.

**Exponents and radicals.** a^(m/n) = (ⁿ√a)ᵐ; a^(−k) = 1/aᵏ; √(x²) = |x|. Squaring both sides can add solutions: check.

**Complex numbers.** i² = −1; √(−k) = i√k; add and multiply like binomials, replace i²; (a + bi)(a − bi) = a² + b². Quadratic formula x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac))/(2a); negative discriminant means two non-real conjugate solutions.

**Exponentials and logarithms.** f(t) = a · bᵗ, growth factor b = 1 + rate; half-life and doubling by rational exponents. log_b(c) = x means bˣ = c; log is base 10, ln is base e ≈ 2.718; solve bˣ = c with x = log(c)/log(b). A(t) = P · e^(rt) for continuous growth. y = log_b(x) has asymptote x = 0 and passes through (1, 0).

**Transformations.** f(x) + k up, f(x − h) right, −f(x) across the x-axis, f(−x) across the y-axis, k · f(x) vertical stretch, f(kx) horizontal compression by 1/k. Even: f(−x) = f(x); odd: f(−x) = −f(x).

**Trigonometry.** Point at angle θ on radius r: (r cos θ, r sin θ). Radians = degrees · π/180. Exact values at π/6, π/4, π/3 and the axes; reference angles and quadrant signs. cos²θ + sin²θ = 1. tan θ = sin θ / cos θ, period π, undefined at odd multiples of π/2. Period 2π for sine and cosine; cos even, sin odd. y = a sin(b(θ − h)) + k: amplitude |a|, period 2π/b, shift h, midline k. Wheel with period T: b = 2π/T; +cos from the top, −cos from the bottom, +sin from level and rising.

**Statistics.** Mean, median, standard deviation; skew named for the tail; 68–95–99.7; z = (x − μ)/σ; area right = 1 − left; between = difference of lefts; cutoff x = μ + zσ. Margin of error = 2 × (standard deviation of sample statistics); four times the sample halves it. Experiment: z = difference ÷ regrouping standard deviation, both tails, compare with a cutoff set beforehand.

## How to show work on the free-response items

The three short-answer items (3 points, one per part) and the two extended responses (6 points) are graded against scoring notes that award points for specific things. To collect them:

1. **Label every part.** Write (a), (b), (c) or (1) to (4) and answer each in order. An unlabelled answer to part (c) buried in part (a) can be missed.
2. **State the equation before solving it.** "18 − 16 cos(πt/24) = 26, so cos(πt/24) = −1/2" earns the method point even if the arithmetic slips.
3. **Give exact forms and decimals when asked.** "x = log₃(50) ≈ 3.56" covers both.
4. **Check candidates.** For radical and rational equations, write "check: …" and reject extraneous values explicitly; the rejection is often a point.
5. **Say which sign and why.** In trigonometry, name the quadrant; in statistics, say "both tails".
6. **Interpret in context.** A margin of error, an asymptote, a midline: one sentence on what it means in the situation.
7. **Use careful statistical language.** "Not enough evidence", not "no effect"; "likely", not "certainly".
8. **Do not leave a part blank.** A partial setup can earn the point; a blank cannot.

## Two-week review plan (weeks 33 and 34)

About 60 minutes a day. Each day pairs a unit summary (the night-before sheet at the end of each unit) with that unit's exam and the week quizzes you scored lowest on.

| Day | Content | Practice |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Unit 1 summary | Re-take the Unit 1 exam; write the explicit and recursive rules for three sequences from memory |
| 2 | Unit 2 summary, part 1: polynomials (weeks 4–7) | Sketch three polynomials from factored form; divide two cubics; remainder theorem drill |
| 3 | Unit 2 summary, part 2: rational functions and identities (weeks 8–10) | Re-take the Unit 2 exam; solve five rational equations with checks |
| 4 | Unit 3 summary | Re-take the Unit 3 exam; ten rational-exponent evaluations and five complex products by hand |
| 5 | Unit 4 summary | Re-take the Unit 4 exam; solve eight exponential equations, half with logarithms, half by matching bases |
| 6 | Unit 5 summary | Track one point through all six transformations; write five equations from descriptions |
| 7 | Rest, or re-take any week quiz you scored under 70% on | — |
| 8 | Unit 6 summary, part 1: the circle (weeks 24–26) | Fill in the whole unit circle from memory; evaluate ten values at angles beyond 2π or negative |
| 9 | Unit 6 summary, part 2: transformations and models (weeks 27–28) | Re-take the Unit 6 exam; build two time-based wheel models and one tide model |
| 10 | Unit 7 summary | Re-take the Unit 7 exam; three z-score problems, two margin-of-error problems, one experiment analysis |
| 11 | **Practice exam, items 1–50** (about 80 minutes) | Mark the auto-graded items; list every miss by unit and topic code |
| 12 | **Practice exam, items 51–70** (about 55 minutes) | Grade your free-response answers against the scoring notes, point by point |
| 13 | Re-study the five topic codes you missed most | Re-take those weeks' quizzes; redo the practice items you missed without looking |
| 14 | This guide's formula section and each unit's common-errors list | Stop early. Check your calculator's mode (radians), sleep. |

## The night before and on the day

Re-read the formula section and the common-errors lists, not new content. On the day: pace the auto-graded items at about a minute each and move on from anything that stalls; do the three short answers before the two extended responses; leave fifteen minutes to check radical and rational equations for extraneous solutions, calculator mode on every trigonometric value, and both tails on every significance proportion. Every scoring-note point is independent: a correct setup still scores when the final number is wrong.
