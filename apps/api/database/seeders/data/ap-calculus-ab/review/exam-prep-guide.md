# Exam Prep Guide · AP Calculus AB

**Use it:** in week 33, before the practice final in week 34
**Covers:** all eight units, every one of which the AP Calculus AB exam tests
**Framework:** AP Calculus AB and BC Course and Exam Description; exam format from AP Central, May 2027 exam

This guide explains the May 2027 AP Calculus AB exam and its calculator rules, shows how the practice final rehearses it, explains how free responses are scored, and collects, unit by unit, the facts and justification sentences to know without looking anything up. It ends with a two-week review plan. If you are taking calculus as a regular course, the whole practice final is your final exam.

## The AP exam in May 2027

The exam is on **Monday, May 10, 2027**, in the first session of the day. It is a hybrid digital exam: multiple-choice answers are entered in the Bluebook app, and free-response answers are **handwritten** in a paper booklet.

| Section | Part | Questions | Time | Calculator | Share of score |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| I: Multiple choice | A | 29 | 62 minutes | not allowed | 50% for all of Section I |
| I: Multiple choice | B | 13 | 38 minutes | graphing calculator required | (Part A and Part B together) |
| II: Free response | A | 2 (questions 1 and 2) | 30 minutes | graphing calculator required | 50% for all of Section II |
| II: Free response | B | 4 (questions 3 to 6) | 60 minutes | not allowed | (Part A and Part B together) |

Section I is 42 questions in 1 hour 40 minutes; Section II is 6 questions in 1 hour 30 minutes, 15 minutes and 9 points each. Each section is half the score. There is no penalty for a wrong multiple-choice answer, so never leave one blank.

### Calculator rules

On the calculator parts you are expected to use a graphing calculator, and the exam is written assuming it can do four things. Practise each until it takes seconds:

1. **Graph a function** in any viewing window, so you can see where it is positive, where two graphs cross, and how many solutions there are.
2. **Solve an equation numerically**: find a zero, or the intersection of two graphs, to at least three decimal places.
3. **Calculate the numerical derivative of a function at a point**, such as f′(1.5) for f(x) = e^(sin x) + x².
4. **Calculate the value of a definite integral numerically**, such as ∫ from 1 to 3 of √(t³ + 1) dt.

Three habits:

- **Radian mode, always.** Every trigonometric model in the course assumes radians. Several of the practice final's distractors are the exact results a calculator gives in degree mode, so if your answer matches one, check the mode.
- **Three decimal places.** A decimal answer must be correct to three places after the decimal point, rounded or truncated. Store intermediate values in the calculator rather than copying rounded numbers forward; rounding 5.289 to 5.3 halfway through a free-response question can push the final answer outside the accepted range.
- **Write mathematics, not keystrokes.** On a free response, "∫ from 0 to 10 of R(t) dt ≈ 221.490" earns the setup point; "fnInt(Y1, X, 0, 10)" does not. Show the integral or the derivative you asked the calculator for, with its limits, then the value.

Check AP Central's calculator policy before May for the approved models and how calculators work in the digital sections.

## How the practice final maps onto it

The practice final has 70 questions. **Questions 1–48 are a full mock AP exam** in the order and timing above:

- Questions 1–29: Section I, Part A. No calculator. All eight units, roughly in proportion to their AP weightings.
- Questions 30–42: Section I, Part B. Graphing calculator required; each prompt starts "(Calculator.)". At least two need a numerical derivative or a numerical integral, and the explanations give the calculator output.
- Questions 43–44: Section II, Part A, with a calculator. Question 43 is a rate-in/rate-out context (rainwater entering a basin while a pump removes it); question 44 is a table of values (a cyclist's velocity), with a difference quotient, a trapezoidal sum, a Mean Value Theorem argument and an average value.
- Questions 45–48: Section II, Part B, no calculator. Particle motion; the analysis of a function from the graph of its derivative; a differential equation with slope-field work, a tangent-line approximation and separation of variables; and an area-and-volume question.

Each free-response question is scored out of 9 with scoring notes in the style of the AP rubrics. Sit questions 1–48 in one block with the AP timings (3 hours 10 minutes, plus a 10-minute break after question 42). The College Board does not publish score cut points in advance, so don't convert your total into a 1–5 score; note the topic of every lost point and review those.

**Questions 49–70** are not AP format: select-all, true/false, fill-in-the-blank, numeric and short-answer items across all eight units, opening with four questions on one table of f, f′, g and g′. Do them on another day, in about 45 minutes.

## The free-response questions and how they are scored

Six questions, 9 points each. The contexts change every year; the shapes are steady:

- **Rates in and out** (usually calculator). Something enters and leaves at given rates. You integrate a rate to get an amount, compare rates to decide whether the amount is increasing, and find a maximum or minimum amount with a candidates test.
- **Table of values** (often calculator). You estimate a derivative with a difference quotient, approximate an integral with a Riemann or trapezoidal sum (watch for unequal widths), interpret with units, and use the Mean Value Theorem or the Intermediate Value Theorem on the data.
- **Particle motion.** Direction from the sign of velocity, speeding up or slowing down from the signs of velocity and acceleration together, position from an initial value plus an integral, and total distance from the integral of speed.
- **Graph of f′ or of a rate.** You are given a graph and asked about an accumulation function g(x) = ∫ from a to x of f(t) dt: values from signed areas, extrema and concavity from the graph, and limits that may need L'Hospital's Rule.
- **Differential equations.** Slopes from the equation, a slope-field description, a tangent-line approximation with a concavity check, and a particular solution by separation of variables.
- **Area and volume.** Area between curves, a solid with known cross sections, a solid of revolution about a line that is not an axis, and sometimes an equation for a line that splits the region.

**How the 9 points work.** Each point is attached to one specific thing, and the scoring notes in this course name it, so read them after every attempt:

- **Setup points** go to the correct integral or derivative expression, with its limits and its constant (for example, the π and the limits in a volume integral). A correct number with no setup can lose points.
- **Answer points** go to the value, to three decimal places on calculator questions, or exact on no-calculator questions (leave 64/15 and 29/3 as fractions; simplifying is not required).
- **Justification points** go to a reason that uses the right evidence, stated in a complete sentence. "f has a relative maximum at x = 2 because f′ changes from positive to negative there" earns it; "because f′(2) = 0" does not.
- **Units and meaning points** go to an interpretation that names the quantity, the time and the units: "at t = 4 hours the inflow rate is decreasing at 3.121 cubic metres per hour per hour".
- **Consistency.** A wrong answer carried correctly into a later part can still earn the later points, so always finish a question even if an earlier part went wrong. Read every part before you start: later parts often reuse earlier results.

## Know these cold

### Unit 1

- lim (x→a) f(x) = L exists when both one-sided limits exist and equal L. A limit does not care about f(a).
- For 0/0 forms: factor and cancel, multiply by a conjugate, or use a trigonometric limit such as lim (x→0) sin x / x = 1.
- Squeeze Theorem: if g(x) ≤ f(x) ≤ h(x) near a and g and h both tend to L, then f tends to L too.
- **Continuity at x = a** needs three checks: f(a) is defined, lim (x→a) f(x) exists, and they are equal. A removable discontinuity is a hole; a jump has unequal one-sided limits; an infinite discontinuity sits at a vertical asymptote.
- Limits at infinity of rational functions compare the degrees of numerator and denominator.
- **IVT sentence:** "f is continuous on [a, b] and k is between f(a) and f(b), so by the Intermediate Value Theorem there is a c in (a, b) with f(c) = k."

### Unit 2

- f′(a) = lim (h→0) (f(a + h) − f(a))/h, also lim (x→a) (f(x) − f(a))/(x − a). Recognise these limits as derivatives.
- From a table, estimate f′(c) with the difference quotient over the two closest data points around c.
- Differentiable implies continuous; continuous does not imply differentiable (corners, cusps, vertical tangents).
- Rules: power, constant multiple, sum, product (uv)′ = u′v + uv′, quotient (u/v)′ = (u′v − uv′)/v².
- d/dx sin x = cos x; d/dx cos x = −sin x; d/dx tan x = sec² x; d/dx sec x = sec x tan x; d/dx eˣ = eˣ; d/dx ln x = 1/x.

### Unit 3

- Chain rule: d/dx f(g(x)) = f′(g(x)) · g′(x). Most lost points in Unit 3 are a missing inner derivative.
- Implicit differentiation: differentiate both sides with respect to x, attach dy/dx to every y term, then solve for dy/dx. Horizontal tangents where the numerator of dy/dx is 0, vertical where the denominator is 0 (check the point is on the curve).
- Inverse functions: if g = f⁻¹ and f(a) = b, then g′(b) = 1/f′(a). Find a first.
- d/dx arcsin x = 1/√(1 − x²); d/dx arctan x = 1/(1 + x²).

### Unit 4

- A derivative's units are output units per input unit. **Interpretation sentence:** "At t = 5 minutes, the amount of water is decreasing at 3 gallons per minute."
- Motion: v = x′, a = v′ = x″. **Speeding up** when v and a have the same sign; **slowing down** when they have opposite signs. Moving right (or up) when v > 0.
- Related rates: write an equation relating the quantities, differentiate with respect to t, and substitute values only after differentiating.
- Local linearity: L(x) = f(a) + f′(a)(x − a). The tangent line overestimates where the graph is concave down and underestimates where it is concave up.
- L'Hospital's Rule for 0/0 or ∞/∞: lim f/g = lim f′/g′. State the indeterminate form each time you use it.

### Unit 5

- **MVT sentence:** "f is continuous on [a, b] and differentiable on (a, b), so there is a c in (a, b) with f′(c) = (f(b) − f(a))/(b − a)." From a table, say that f is differentiable (hence continuous) because the problem says so.
- **EVT:** a function continuous on a closed interval has an absolute maximum and minimum there.
- Critical points are where f′ = 0 or f′ does not exist. They are candidates, not answers.
- **First derivative test sentence:** "f has a relative minimum at x = 4 because f′ changes from negative to positive there."
- **Candidates test:** for an absolute extremum on [a, b], evaluate f at the endpoints and every critical point; the largest value is the maximum. Say so in words.
- Concavity: concave up where f″ > 0 (equivalently, f′ increasing). **Point of inflection sentence:** "the graph of f has a point of inflection at x = 3 because f″ changes sign there" (or because f′ changes from increasing to decreasing, or from decreasing to increasing, there).
- Second derivative test: f′(c) = 0 and f″(c) < 0 gives a relative maximum; f″(c) > 0 a relative minimum.
- Optimization: write the quantity as a function of one variable, state the domain, find critical points, and justify the maximum or minimum (first derivative test on the domain or a candidates test).

### Unit 6

- A Riemann sum ∑ f(xₖ)Δx approximates ∫ from a to b of f(x) dx. Left and right sums use endpoints; the trapezoidal sum averages them. For an increasing function a left sum underestimates; for a concave-up function the trapezoidal sum overestimates.
- Convert a limit of a sum to an integral by matching Δx = (b − a)/n and xₖ = a + kΔx.
- **FTC, part 1:** d/dx ∫ from a to x of f(t) dt = f(x); with an upper limit u(x), multiply by u′(x).
- **FTC, part 2:** ∫ from a to b of f(x) dx = F(b) − F(a), where F′ = f.
- Accumulation: g(x) = g(a) + ∫ from a to x of g′(t) dt. Values come from signed areas: area below the axis counts negatively, and reversing the limits changes the sign.
- Properties: ∫ from a to b of f = ∫ from a to c of f + ∫ from c to b of f; constants factor out; integrals of sums split; but there is no product or quotient rule for integrals.
- Antiderivatives: ∫ xⁿ dx = xⁿ⁺¹/(n + 1) + C (n ≠ −1); ∫ 1/x dx = ln|x| + C; ∫ eˣ dx = eˣ + C; ∫ cos x dx = sin x + C; ∫ sin x dx = −cos x + C; ∫ sec² x dx = tan x + C; ∫ 1/(1 + x²) dx = arctan x + C.
- Substitution: choose u, find du, and for a definite integral change the limits to u-values (or substitute back before evaluating). Long division and completing the square turn awkward quotients into these forms.

### Unit 7

- Verify a solution by substituting it into the equation.
- Slope fields: compute dy/dx at each point. Segments are horizontal where dy/dx = 0; if dy/dx depends only on x, every column is parallel; if only on y, every row is.
- **Separation of variables** in order: separate, antidifferentiate both sides, add the constant **immediately**, use the initial condition to find it, then solve for y. A missing constant or an unseparated equation costs most of the points.
- dy/dt = ky has solutions y = y₀e^(kt): growth if k > 0, decay if k < 0.

### Unit 8

- Average value of f on [a, b]: (1/(b − a)) ∫ from a to b of f(x) dx. Do not confuse it with the average rate of change.
- Position from velocity: x(b) = x(a) + ∫ from a to b of v(t) dt. Displacement is ∫ v dt; **total distance is ∫ |v(t)| dt**.
- Amount from rate: A(b) = A(a) + ∫ from a to b of A′(t) dt, where A′ = rate in − rate out.
- Area between curves: ∫ (top − bottom) dx, or ∫ (right − left) dy. Find all intersections first; if the curves cross inside the interval, split it.
- Known cross sections: V = ∫ A(x) dx. Square of side s: s²; semicircle of diameter s: (π/8)s²; isosceles right triangle with leg s: s²/2; equilateral triangle of side s: (√3/4)s².
- Discs: V = π ∫ R² dx. Washers: V = π ∫ (R² − r²) dx, with both radii measured from the axis of revolution. About y = k, a radius is |k − y|.

## A two-week review plan

**Week 33 (this week): rebuild the toolkit.**

- Day 1: Units 1–3 summary sheets, then fifteen minutes of mixed derivative drills.
- Day 2: Unit 4 and Unit 5 summary sheets. Write out every justification sentence above from memory, then redo the Unit 5 exam's free-response questions without looking at your first attempt.
- Day 3: Unit 6 summary sheet. Drill antiderivatives and substitution for 30 minutes, then do one graph-of-f′ accumulation question.
- Day 4: Units 7 and 8 summary sheets. One separation-of-variables problem from start to finish, one area-and-volume problem with a non-axis line of revolution.
- Day 5: Calculator day. The four required capabilities above until they are automatic, then the calculator questions from the unit exams. Confirm radian mode.

**Week 34: the practice final.**

- Day 1: Sit questions 1–48 under AP timing, in one sitting, with the break after question 42.
- Day 2: Score it with the explanations and the 9-point scoring notes. For every lost point, write one line: what the question tested and what you will do differently. Count your losses by unit.
- Day 3: Re-teach yourself the two topics that cost you the most points, using their weekly guides and flashcards.
- Day 4: Questions 49–70, timed at 45 minutes.
- Day 5: Redo every free-response question you scored under 6 out of 9, writing full justifications, then stop. Rest matters more than one more set.

Until the exam, alternate a timed multiple-choice block with one free-response question every other day.

## Test-day reminders

- Bring a charged, approved graphing calculator and pencils and a pen for the booklet.
- In Section I, mark any question that takes longer than three minutes and come back to it. Answer everything.
- In Section II, read every part of a question before writing, show each setup, and give exact answers on the no-calculator part.
