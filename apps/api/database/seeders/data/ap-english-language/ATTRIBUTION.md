# Attribution

Every text quoted in this directory, in study guides, unit summaries,
flashcards and test items, is in the public domain in the United States:
either first published in 1930 or earlier (translations likewise), or a work
of the U.S. federal government (17 U.S.C. 105), such as a president's
inaugural address. Each passage was copied from the edition listed below,
usually the Project Gutenberg text, and checked mechanically, word for word,
against that saved text. Excerpts are kept short. Where an edition's spelling
or punctuation differs from the familiar modern text, the quotation follows
the edition cited; Gutenberg's underscore italics are shown as italics.
Copyrighted twentieth-century writing (for example Martin Luther King Jr.'s
speeches and letters) is described in the guides where useful, never quoted.

All commentary, worked examples, flashcards, questions, answer rationales,
model answers and scoring guides were written for this course, and so were
every numbered revision draft and every synthesis source: these are labelled
`written for this course` in each item's `source` and describe no real person
or publication. Historical context given in the guides without quotation
marks is the course's own summary.

The course is organised around the College Board AP English Language and
Composition Course and Exam Description (fall 2024): its 22 skills (1.A ...
8.C, listed in `course.yaml`), the skill focus it gives each of nine units
(the unit titles are this course's own, since the CED leaves them to the
teacher), and the exam format and 6-point essay rubric it describes. No
College Board questions are reproduced.

`Database\Seeders\ApLiterature\Lint` enforces the public-domain cutoff on
every week's `texts` and every item's `source`, with the U.S. government work
exception switched on in `course.yaml`.
