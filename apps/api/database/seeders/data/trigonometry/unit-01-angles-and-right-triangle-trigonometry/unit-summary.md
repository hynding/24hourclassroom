## The ideas that generate every question

1. **An angle is an amount of turning.** In standard position it starts on the positive x-axis; counterclockwise is positive. Angles that differ by whole turns (360° or 2π) are coterminal and end on the same ray. 1° = 60′, 1′ = 60″.
2. **Similar right triangles have equal ratios of sides.** Once one acute angle is fixed, the shape is fixed (AA), so sin, cos and tan depend only on the angle, never on the size of the triangle. The 45-45-90 triangle has sides 1 : 1 : √2; the 30-60-90 triangle has sides 1 : √3 : 2.
3. **Six ratios, three of them reciprocals.** SOH-CAH-TOA, then csc = 1/sin, sec = 1/cos, cot = 1/tan. Knowing any one ratio of an acute angle gives a triangle, and the Pythagorean theorem gives the third side.
4. **Complementary angles swap names.** sin θ = cos(90° − θ), tan θ = cot(90° − θ), sec θ = csc(90° − θ): the side opposite one acute angle is adjacent to the other.
5. **Solving a triangle means choosing the ratio that links what you know to what you want.** For a side, write a ratio with the unknown in it; for an angle, use an inverse key (sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹) to find "the angle whose ratio is…". In context, draw the right triangle first: elevation and depression are measured from the horizontal, bearings from north.
6. **Radians measure an angle by arc ÷ radius.** θ = s/r, a full turn is 2π, and 180° = π. Every formula about circles (s = rθ, A = ½r²θ, v = rω) assumes radians.
7. **Any angle has trigonometric values.** With (x, y) on the terminal side and r = √(x² + y²): sin θ = y/r, cos θ = x/r, tan θ = y/x. Signs come from the quadrant (ASTC); a value is undefined where its denominator is 0.
8. **Reference angles reduce every angle to an acute one.** The value at θ equals ± the value at its reference angle θ′ (the acute angle to the x-axis); the quadrant chooses the sign.
9. **On the unit circle the point at angle θ is (cos θ, sin θ).** Arc length equals the radian angle, values repeat every full turn, cosine is even and sine is odd.
10. **Identities come from x² + y² = r².** sin²θ + cos²θ = 1, and dividing it by cos²θ or sin²θ gives the other two. An identity gives the size of a value; the quadrant gives its sign.

## Classic question types and how to attack them

**"Find all six ratios" (from a triangle, a point or one value).** Sketch the triangle or point, find the missing side with x² + y² = r², then write the six ratios. For any angle, attach signs by quadrant; r stays positive.

**"Solve the right triangle."** List the knowns, pick the ratio that contains exactly one unknown, and solve. The two acute angles add to 90°. Check: the hypotenuse is the longest side, and the larger angle faces the larger leg.

**"How tall / how far" (elevation, depression, two sightings).** Draw the horizontal line first. An angle of depression from above equals the angle of elevation from below. With two sightings, never use the difference of the angles; subtract distances.

**"Convert and use the circle formulas."** Convert degrees to radians before s = rθ or A = ½r²θ. For rpm, multiply by 2π; for speed, use the radius, not the diameter; match the time units.

**"Find the exact value of a trig function at a large or negative angle."** Remove whole turns, find the quadrant and reference angle, take the special value, and attach the sign. Use even/odd symmetry for negative angles if you like: sin(−5π/6) = −sin(5π/6).

**"Given one value and a quadrant, find the others."** Use the triangle-and-point method or an identity: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 when you know sine or cosine; 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ when you know tangent or secant. Square roots give ±; the quadrant decides.

**"Simplify."** Rewrite in sines and cosines, combine fractions, look for sin²θ + cos²θ.

## Common errors

- Calculator in radian mode on a degree problem (or the reverse).
- Using SOH-CAH-TOA with the sides named from the wrong angle.
- Using the difference of two angles of elevation instead of the difference of two distances.
- Measuring a bearing from east, or counterclockwise.
- Degrees in s = rθ or A = ½r²θ; forgetting the ½ in sector area; forgetting 2π in rpm conversions.
- Measuring a reference angle to the y-axis.
- Calling an undefined value 0 (sec 90°, tan 270°).
- Swapping the coordinates at π/6 and π/3.
- Dropping the ± after a square root, or choosing the sign without checking the quadrant.
- Writing sin θ + cos θ = 1; the squares matter.

## Calculator or not?

Exact-value questions (special angles, unit-circle points, quadrantal values, identities) are designed to be done by hand, and an exact answer is expected: √3/2, not 0.866. Questions that use measurements (a 52° angle, a 45 m lighthouse, a 2.5-radian angle in degrees) need a calculator: check the mode first, keep full precision until the last step, and round only as the question asks.

## Self-test

1. Find an angle between 0° and 360° coterminal with −500°, and write 72.35° in degrees and minutes.
2. A 30-60-90 triangle has hypotenuse 14. Find both legs.
3. sin θ = 8/17 for an acute angle θ. Find cot θ and sec θ.
4. Solve sin(3x)° = cos(x + 10)° for acute angles.
5. From 50 m away, the angle of elevation to the top of a hypothetical mast is 38°. How tall is the mast, to the nearest hundredth of a metre?
6. Convert 165° to radians and 7π/12 to degrees.
7. A wheel of radius 0.4 m turns at 300 rpm. Find its linear speed in metres per second.
8. The terminal side of θ passes through (−9, 40). Find sin θ, cos θ and the reference angle to the nearest hundredth of a degree.
9. Find sin(−11π/6), cos(14π/3) and tan(5π/4) exactly.
10. tan θ = −5/12 with θ in quadrant II. Use an identity to find sec θ, then find sin θ.

**Answers.**
1. −500° + 720° = 220°; 0.35 · 60 = 21, so 72° 21′.
2. Short leg 7, long leg 7√3.
3. Adjacent side 15: cot θ = 15/8, sec θ = 17/15.
4. 3x + x + 10 = 90, so x = 20.
5. 50 tan 38° ≈ 39.06 m.
6. 11π/12; 105°.
7. ω = 600π rad/min = 10π rad/s; v = 0.4 · 10π = 4π ≈ 12.57 m/s.
8. r = 41: sin θ = 40/41, cos θ = −9/41; reference angle tan⁻¹(40/9) ≈ 77.32°.
9. −11π/6 is coterminal with π/6: 1/2. 14π/3 − 4π = 2π/3: −1/2. tan(5π/4) = 1.
10. sec²θ = 1 + 25/144 = 169/144; secant is negative in II, so sec θ = −13/12, cos θ = −12/13, and sin θ = tan θ · cos θ = 5/13.
