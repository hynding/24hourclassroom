# Attribution

Every study guide, unit summary, flashcard deck, quiz, unit exam, the exam-prep
guide and the practice final in this directory is **original writing** for this
course. The course is organised around the trigonometry standards of the
Common Core State Standards for Mathematics: Similarity, Right Triangles and
Trigonometry (HSG-SRT.C–D), Trigonometric Functions (HSF-TF), Complex Numbers
in polar form (HSN-CN.A–B) and Vector and Matrix Quantities (HSN-VM.A–B). The
standards are cited by code; their text is not reproduced. Topics that go
beyond Common Core but belong in a full trigonometry course or a precalculus
requirement (the dot product, De Moivre's theorem and complex roots, polar
equations of conics, Euler's formula) are marked "(extension)" in each item's
`source`. The five units, their topic codes and titles were designed for this
course.

## What the foundation weeks follow

Weeks 1–10 (angles and right triangles, radians, the unit circle, graphs of the
trigonometric functions and sinusoidal models) follow the approach of the
trigonometry lessons in **IM 9–12 Math** by Illustrative Mathematics: the
Geometry course's right-triangle trigonometry unit and Algebra 2 Unit 6,
"Trigonometric Functions" (https://im.kendallhunt.com/HS/teachers/3/6/index.html).
© 2019 Illustrative Mathematics, licensed under the Creative Commons
Attribution 4.0 license (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The
sequence of ideas (similar triangles before ratios, the unit circle as the
bridge from triangles to functions, radians as arc length, sinusoids built from
circular motion) follows IM; the explanations, examples, flashcards and
questions were written for this course and are not copied from IM. The
Illustrative Mathematics name and logo are not covered by its CC BY licence and
are used here only to credit the source. Those weeks' guides say so in their
footer.

## What was checked and not used

No complete trigonometry textbook was found under a licence this course can
build on, so nothing else is adapted. These books were read only to check scope
and topic order (the 2026-11-05 research note is in the project files):

- Katherine Yoshiwara, *Trigonometry* (American Institute of Mathematics
  approved textbook): GNU Free Documentation License. A derivative would have
  to stay under the GFDL, which conflicts with this course's CC BY 4.0.
- Michael Corral, *Trigonometry*: GNU Free Documentation License 1.3, same
  problem.
- Matt Boelkins, *Active Prelude to Calculus*: CC BY-SA 4.0 (ShareAlike).
- David Lippman and Melonie Rasmussen, *Precalculus: An Investigation of
  Functions*: CC BY-SA (ShareAlike).
- Mike Weimerskirch, *Trigonometry* (University of Minnesota, 2022): CC BY-NC
  4.0 (NonCommercial).
- Ted Sundstrom and Steven Schlicker, *Trigonometry* (Grand Valley State
  University): listed as CC BY-NC-SA.
- OpenStax *Precalculus 2e* and *Algebra and Trigonometry 2e*: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0,
  and their terms bar ingestion by language models.
- CK-12 *Trigonometry*: CC BY-NC. Carl Stitz and Jeff Zeager, *Precalculus*:
  CC BY-NC-SA.

The classic heights-and-distances and navigation problem types (a tower
sighted from two points, a ship's course on bearings, the width of a river) are
centuries old and appear in public-domain texts such as Hugh Blackburn,
*Elements of Plane Trigonometry* (1871, Project Gutenberg #32973); every
instance here uses its own numbers and wording.

Nor was text taken from Khan Academy, commercial textbooks or study sites.
Definitions, theorems, identities and formulas (the Pythagorean and
sum-and-difference identities, the laws of sines and cosines, Heron's formula,
the dot product, De Moivre's theorem, Euler's formula) are standard
mathematics and were not copied from any reference.

**Data.** Every number in an applied context (towers, ramps, ships, aircraft
and wind, Ferris wheels, tides, daylight, springs, forces and cables,
projectiles) was invented for this course and computed to fit the item. None of
it is real-world measurement data, and items that might be mistaken for real
data say "hypothetical".

**Figures** are described in words or given as tables of values; the app renders
no images or LaTeX, so mathematics is written in plain Unicode.
