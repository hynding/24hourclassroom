# Week 2 · The Six Trigonometric Ratios

**Topics:** 1.3 The Six Trigonometric Ratios · 1.4 Cofunctions and Complementary Angles
**Standards:** HSG-SRT.C.6, HSG-SRT.C.7, HSG-SRT.C.8
**Spiral:** Week 1 showed, through AA similarity, that a ratio of two sides of a right triangle depends only on the acute angle. This week names those ratios. The 45°-45°-90° and 30°-60°-90° triangles from week 1 give their exact values, the Pythagorean theorem finds a missing side, and week 1's fact that the two acute angles of a right triangle are complementary becomes the cofunction identities.

## What you need to be able to do

- **1.3** Label the opposite side, adjacent side and hypotenuse relative to a chosen acute angle, and write sin, cos and tan of that angle (SOH-CAH-TOA).
- **1.3** Define csc, sec and cot as the reciprocals of sin, cos and tan, and write all six ratios from a labelled triangle.
- **1.3** Given one ratio of an acute angle, sketch a triangle, find the third side with the Pythagorean theorem, and write the other five ratios exactly (rationalising denominators).
- **1.3** Give exact values of all six ratios at 30°, 45° and 60°, and use them to evaluate expressions.
- **1.4** Explain why sin θ = cos(90° − θ), and use the three cofunction pairs to rewrite ratios and solve simple equations.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Opposite, adjacent | For an acute angle θ of a right triangle, the leg across from θ and the leg that forms θ along with the hypotenuse. |
| Sine, cosine, tangent | sin θ = opp/hyp, cos θ = adj/hyp, tan θ = opp/adj. |
| Cosecant, secant, cotangent | csc θ = hyp/opp = 1/sin θ, sec θ = hyp/adj = 1/cos θ, cot θ = adj/opp = 1/tan θ. |
| Reciprocal ratios | Pairs whose product is 1: sin and csc, cos and sec, tan and cot. |
| Cofunctions | Pairs whose names differ by "co-": sine/cosine, tangent/cotangent, secant/cosecant. |
| Rationalise | Rewrite a fraction so no radical is in the denominator: 1/√3 = √3/3. |

## Naming the ratios (1.3)

Pick one acute angle θ of a right triangle. The **hypotenuse** is across from the right angle and does not depend on θ. The **opposite** side is across from θ, and the **adjacent** side is the leg touching θ. Choose the other acute angle and "opposite" and "adjacent" swap.

The three basic ratios are remembered as **SOH-CAH-TOA**:

- sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse
- cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse
- tan θ = opposite/adjacent

Their reciprocals have their own names:

- csc θ = hypotenuse/opposite = 1/sin θ
- sec θ = hypotenuse/adjacent = 1/cos θ
- cot θ = adjacent/opposite = 1/tan θ

Notice the pairing: **s**ine with **c**osecant and **c**osine with **s**ecant, each reciprocal starting with the other letter. Because the hypotenuse is the longest side, sine and cosine of an acute angle are between 0 and 1, and secant and cosecant are greater than 1. Tangent and cotangent can be any positive number. Also tan θ = sin θ/cos θ: divide opp/hyp by adj/hyp and the hypotenuse cancels.

**Worked example 1 (all six from a triangle).** Right triangle PQR has its right angle at R, PR = 12, QR = 5 and PQ = 13. Write all six ratios of angle P.

From P: the opposite side is QR = 5, the adjacent side is PR = 12, the hypotenuse is 13. So sin P = 5/13, cos P = 12/13, tan P = 5/12, csc P = 13/5, sec P = 13/12, cot P = 12/5.

For angle Q the legs swap: sin Q = 12/13 and cos Q = 5/13. That swap is the start of the cofunction story below.

## Finding the other five from one ratio (1.3)

One ratio fixes the angle, so it fixes all the others. Draw any right triangle with that ratio, find the third side with the Pythagorean theorem, and read off the rest.

**Worked example 2.** θ is acute and sin θ = 3/7. Find the other five ratios exactly.

Draw opposite 3 and hypotenuse 7. The adjacent side is √(7² − 3²) = √40 = 2√10. Then

- cos θ = 2√10/7
- tan θ = 3/(2√10) = 3√10/20
- csc θ = 7/3
- sec θ = 7/(2√10) = 7√10/20
- cot θ = 2√10/3

Rationalising is a convention, not a different answer: 3/(2√10) and 3√10/20 are both about 0.474.

**Worked example 3.** θ is acute and tan θ = 8/15. Find sin θ and sec θ.

Opposite 8, adjacent 15, hypotenuse √(64 + 225) = √289 = 17. So sin θ = 8/17 and sec θ = 17/15.

## Exact values at 30°, 45° and 60° (1.3)

Use the triangles from week 1: legs 1, 1, hypotenuse √2; and short leg 1, long leg √3, hypotenuse 2.

| θ | sin θ | cos θ | tan θ | csc θ | sec θ | cot θ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30° | 1/2 | √3/2 | √3/3 | 2 | 2√3/3 | √3 |
| 45° | √2/2 | √2/2 | 1 | √2 | √2 | 1 |
| 60° | √3/2 | 1/2 | √3 | 2√3/3 | 2 | √3/3 |

You do not need to memorise the whole table. Memorise the triangles, and remember that sine grows from 1/2 to √2/2 to √3/2 as the angle grows from 30° to 60°, while cosine runs the same list backwards.

**Worked example 4 (evaluating).** Find the exact value of sin 30° · cos 60° + tan² 45° and of sec² 30°.

sin 30° · cos 60° = (1/2)(1/2) = 1/4, and tan² 45° means (tan 45°)² = 1. The sum is 5/4.

sec 30° = 2/√3, so sec² 30° = 4/3.

## Cofunctions and complementary angles (1.4)

In a right triangle with acute angles θ and 90° − θ, the side opposite θ is the side adjacent to 90° − θ. So the ratio that is "opposite over hypotenuse" for one angle is "adjacent over hypotenuse" for the other:

- sin θ = cos(90° − θ) and cos θ = sin(90° − θ)
- tan θ = cot(90° − θ) and cot θ = tan(90° − θ)
- sec θ = csc(90° − θ) and csc θ = sec(90° − θ)

That is where the "co" in cosine comes from: the **co**sine of θ is the sine of the **co**mplement of θ. The table above shows it: sin 30° = cos 60° = 1/2, and tan 30° = cot 60° = √3/3.

**Worked example 5 (cofunction equation).** Find the acute angle x for which cos(2x + 10°) = sin(x + 20°).

A sine and a cosine of acute angles are equal when the angles are complementary, so (2x + 10°) + (x + 20°) = 90°. Then 3x = 60° and x = 20°. Check: cos 50° = sin 40°, and 50° + 40° = 90°.

**Try it.**
1. A right triangle has legs 7 and 24. Find the hypotenuse, then sin, sec and cot of the angle opposite the leg of 7.
2. θ is acute and cos θ = 2/3. Find tan θ and csc θ exactly.
3. Write tan 72° as a cofunction of a complementary angle, and find x if sec x = csc 35°.

*Answers:* 1. 25; sin = 7/25, sec = 25/24, cot = 24/7. 2. The opposite side is √5: tan θ = √5/2, csc θ = 3/√5 = 3√5/5. 3. cot 18°; x = 55°.

## Common errors

- **Labelling from the wrong angle.** "Opposite" and "adjacent" depend on which acute angle you are working from; the hypotenuse does not.
- **Mixing up the reciprocal pairs.** sec θ = 1/cos θ, not 1/sin θ. Cosecant goes with sine.
- **Reading sin⁻¹ as a reciprocal.** The reciprocal of sine is csc; sin⁻¹ (week 3) means "the angle whose sine is".
- **Forgetting Pythagoras gives the third side.** From sin θ = 3/7 the adjacent side is √40, not 4.
- **Squaring the angle.** tan² 45° means (tan 45°)², not tan(45²°).
- **Cofunctions on the same angle.** sin θ = cos(90° − θ), not cos θ; sin 30° ≠ cos 30°.

## Where this goes next

Week 3 turns the ratios into tools: given an angle and a side, solve for every part of a right triangle with a calculator, and given two sides, find an angle with sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹ or tan⁻¹. Week 5 extends all six ratios beyond acute angles using a point on the terminal side, so the definitions here become y/r, x/r and y/x. Week 6 finds sin² θ + cos² θ = 1 hiding in the Pythagorean theorem you used in every example this week.
