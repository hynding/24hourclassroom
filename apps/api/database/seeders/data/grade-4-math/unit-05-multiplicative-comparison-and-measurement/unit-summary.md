# Unit 5 · Multiplicative Comparison and Measurement · Unit summary

**Weeks:** 14–16 · **Standards:** 4.OA.A.1, 4.OA.A.2, 4.MD.A.1, 4.MD.A.2
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 5, in our own words

## The big ideas

1. **Multiplication can compare.** "18 is 3 times as many as 6" means 18 = 3 × 6. Every multiplication equation can be read as two comparison sentences: 18 is 3 times as many as 6, and 18 is 6 times as many as 3.
2. **"Times as many" is not "more".** If Ari has 5 cards and Jo has 3 times as many, Jo has 15 cards. That is 10 more, not 3 more. "How many more?" is answered by subtracting.
3. **Comparison stories have three numbers.** The smaller amount, the number of times and the larger amount. If the larger amount is unknown, multiply. If it is given, divide or find the missing factor.
4. **Bigger units hold many smaller units.** A meter is 100 times as long as a centimeter. To change to a smaller unit, multiply by the conversion fact.
5. **Measurement stories need one unit.** Convert first, then add, subtract, multiply or divide. Count on with jumps for elapsed time, and work in cents for money.

## Strategies and examples

| Strategy | When it helps | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Read the equation two ways | Writing comparison sentences | 42 = 6 × 7: 42 is 6 times as many as 7, and 7 times as many as 6 |
| Tape diagram | Any comparison story | Small bar of 8; big bar of 4 boxes of 8 makes 32 |
| Multiply | Larger amount unknown | A dog weighs 6 times as much as a 4 kg cat: 6 × 4 = 24 kg |
| Divide | Smaller amount or number of times unknown | 40 is 5 times as many as ?: 40 ÷ 5 = 8 |
| Multiply by the fact | Changing to a smaller unit | 7 m = 7 × 100 = 700 cm |
| Conversion table | Several conversions at once | 1 yd = 3 ft, 2 yd = 6 ft, 3 yd = 9 ft |
| Convert, then add the rest | Mixed measurements | 4 lb 3 oz = 64 + 3 = 67 oz |
| Open number line | Elapsed time | 8:50 to 10:20 is 10 min + 1 hr + 20 min = 1 hr 30 min |
| Work in cents | Money and change | Pay $5.00 for $3.70: 500 − 370 = 130¢ = $1.30 |

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Multiplicative comparison | Comparing by "times as many": 20 is 4 times as many as 5. |
| Additive comparison | Comparing by "how many more" or "how many fewer": 20 is 15 more than 5. |
| Tape diagram | Bars made of equal boxes that show how amounts are related. |
| Unit | A fixed amount we measure with, such as a gram or a minute. |
| Convert | Write the same amount in a different unit. |
| Conversion table | A table that lists matching amounts in two units. |
| Elapsed time | The time that passes from the start of an event to its end. |

## Watch out for

- **Adding instead of multiplying.** "4 times as many as 9" is 36, not 13.
- **Multiplying when the larger amount is given.** If a rope is 30 m and 6 times as long as a string, the string is 30 ÷ 6 = 5 m.
- **Using the wrong fact.** A meter is 100 cm; a kilometer is 1,000 m; a foot is 12 inches, not 10.
- **Mixing units.** 2 km + 500 m is 2,500 m, not 502 m.
- **Treating an hour as 100 minutes.** 3/4 of an hour is 45 minutes, and 10:20 − 8:50 is 1 hr 30 min.

## Practice set

1. Write two comparison sentences for 36 = 4 × 9.
2. A goat eats 7 kg of hay a day. A horse eats 3 times as much. How much does the horse eat?
3. Mia has 56 stickers. That is 8 times as many as Leo. How many stickers does Leo have? How many more does Mia have?
4. 9 m = ____ cm
5. 4 kg = ____ g
6. 3 yd = ____ ft = ____ in
7. A show starts at 4:25 and ends at 5:40. How long is it?
8. A pitcher holds 2 L. Four glasses of 300 mL are poured from it. How much is left?
9. Muffins cost 85¢ each. How much do 3 muffins cost? How much change from $5.00?

*Answers:* (1) 36 is 4 times as many as 9; 36 is 9 times as many as 4. (2) 3 × 7 = 21 kg. (3) 56 ÷ 8 = 7 stickers; 56 − 7 = 49 more. (4) 900 cm. (5) 4,000 g. (6) 9 ft = 108 in. (7) 35 min + 40 min = 75 min, which is 1 hr 15 min. (8) 2,000 − 4 × 300 = 2,000 − 1,200 = 800 mL. (9) 3 × 85 = 255¢ ($2.55); 500 − 255 = 245¢ ($2.45) change.

## For grown-ups at home

This unit connects multiplication to the measuring children do every day. Two habits help most. First, before solving a comparison story, ask, "Which amount is bigger?" so your child decides whether to multiply or divide. Second, in any measuring story, ask, "Are the units the same?" Kitchen labels, clocks and coins make good practice: "This carton is 1 L. How many 250 mL cups does it fill?"
