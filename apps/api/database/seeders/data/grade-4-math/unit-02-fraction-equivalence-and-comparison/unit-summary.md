# Unit 2 · Fraction Equivalence and Comparison · Summary

**Weeks:** 3 The Size and Location of Fractions · 4 Equivalent Fractions · 5 Comparing Fractions
**Standards:** 4.NF.A.2 · 4.NF.A.1 · **Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 2, in our own words

## The big ideas

1. **More parts means smaller parts.** When the same whole is cut into more equal parts, each part is smaller. 1/12 is less than 1/6, even though 12 is the bigger number.
2. **Fractions live on the number line.** Split the space from 0 to 1 into equal parts and count from 0. Fractions keep going past 1: 7/4 is three fourths past 1.
3. **Benchmarks help.** Deciding whether a fraction is near 0, near 1/2 or near 1 tells you a lot. A fraction is more than 1/2 when its numerator is more than half of its denominator.
4. **Equivalent fractions name the same amount.** Cutting every part into the same number of smaller parts changes the numbers but not the amount: 2/5 = 4/10.
5. **The multiplying rule.** Multiply (or divide) the numerator and the denominator by the same number to get an equivalent fraction. Adding the same number does not work.
6. **Comparing needs same-size wholes.** Then compare by same denominator, same numerator, a benchmark, or a common denominator.

## Strategies with an example

| Strategy | When to use it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Line up fraction strips | Seeing which part is bigger | A 1/3 piece is longer than a 1/4 piece |
| Count spaces from 0 | Placing a fraction on a number line | Line in eighths: 5 spaces from 0 is 5/8 |
| Half of the denominator | Comparing with 1/2 | 5/8: half of 8 is 4, so more than 1/2 |
| Cut every part into smaller parts | Making an equivalent fraction with a picture | 2 of 3 columns, cut in half: 4/6 |
| Multiply top and bottom by the same number | Making an equivalent fraction with numbers | 3/4 = (3 × 3)/(4 × 3) = 9/12 |
| Same denominator: compare numerators | 3/10 and 7/10 | 7/10 is greater |
| Same numerator: bigger parts win | 2/3 and 2/8 | 2/3 is greater |
| Compare the missing pieces | Both fractions near 1 | 9/10 is missing less than 7/8, so 9/10 is greater |
| Common denominator | Anything else | 2/3 = 8/12 and 3/4 = 9/12, so 3/4 is greater |

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Numerator | The top number: how many equal parts we count. |
| Denominator | The bottom number: how many equal parts make one whole. |
| Fraction strip | A strip one whole long, cut into equal parts. |
| Benchmark | A friendly number to compare with, such as 0, 1/2 or 1. |
| Equivalent fractions | Different fractions that name the same amount. |
| Common denominator | A denominator two fractions can both be written with. |

## Watch out for

- **Judging by big numbers.** 4/10 has bigger numbers than 1/2 but is less than 1/2.
- **Counting tick marks instead of spaces.** A line from 0 to 1 with 7 tick marks is in sixths.
- **Counting from 1 instead of 0.** 3 spaces before 1 on a line in tenths is 7/10.
- **Adding to make equivalent fractions.** 1/3 and 2/4 are not equal. Multiply both numbers; never add.
- **Changing only one number.** 2/5 is not equal to 2/10. If the denominator doubles, the numerator must double too.
- **"One part short" means equal.** 3/4 and 7/8 are each one part short of 1, but the missing fourth is bigger, so 7/8 is greater.
- **Different-size wholes.** Half of a large pizza is more than half of a small one.

## Practice set

1. Put in order from least to greatest: 1/4, 1/10, 1/2.
2. Is 7/12 more or less than 1/2?
3. Where is 5/3 on a number line?
4. 3/5 = ?/10
5. 10/12 = ?/6
6. Write 4/10 as hundredths.
7. Compare 3/8 and 3/6.
8. Compare 5/6 and 3/4.

*Answers:* (1) 1/10, 1/4, 1/2. (2) More: half of 12 is 6, and 7 is more. (3) Two thirds past 1, between 1 and 2. (4) 6. (5) 5. (6) 40/100. (7) 3/6 is greater, because sixths are bigger parts than eighths. (8) In twelfths, 10/12 and 9/12, so 5/6 is greater.
