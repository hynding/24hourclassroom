# Unit 8 · Properties of Two-dimensional Shapes · Unit summary

**Weeks:** 26 to 28
**Standards:** 4.G.A.2 · 4.G.A.3 · 4.MD.A.3
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 8, in our own words

## The big ideas

1. **Shapes are sorted by sides and angles.** Count the sides, compare their lengths, look for parallel and perpendicular sides, and check each angle: right, acute or obtuse. Size, color and which way a shape is turned never change its name.
2. **Triangles have two kinds of names.** By angles: a right triangle has one right angle, an obtuse triangle has one obtuse angle, and an acute triangle has three acute angles. By sides: equilateral (3 equal sides), isosceles (at least 2 equal sides) and scalene (no equal sides). A triangle never has two right angles or two obtuse angles.
3. **Quadrilaterals form a family.** A parallelogram has 2 pairs of parallel sides. A rectangle has 4 right angles, and a rhombus has 4 equal sides; both are parallelograms. A square has 4 equal sides and 4 right angles, so it is a rectangle, a rhombus and a parallelogram. The names only work one way: not every rectangle is a square.
4. **A line of symmetry folds a shape onto itself.** The two halves must match exactly, corner on corner. Equal area is not enough: a long rectangle's corner-to-corner line is not a line of symmetry.
5. **Area and perimeter formulas run both ways.** A = l × w and P = 2 × l + 2 × w. Knowing the area or perimeter and one side, you can find the other side.

## Strategies at a glance

| Strategy | When to use it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Check the biggest angle | Naming a triangle by angles | Angles 25°, 45°, 110°: obtuse triangle |
| Measure every side | Naming a triangle by sides | Sides 5, 5, 3 cm: isosceles |
| Check for parallel sides | Is it a parallelogram? | Top and bottom level, both slants lean the same way: yes |
| Name every name that fits | Shape families | 4 equal sides and 4 right angles: square, rectangle, rhombus, parallelogram |
| Fold test | Is a line a line of symmetry? | Long rectangle, corner to corner: corners stick out, so no |
| Count squares from the line | Finishing a symmetric shape | A square 2 from the line copies to 2 on the other side |
| A = l × w | Area of a rectangle | 14 m by 5 m: 70 square meters |
| P = 2 × l + 2 × w | Perimeter of a rectangle | 14 m by 5 m: 28 + 10 = 38 m |
| Divide the area | Missing side from area | Area 84, side 7: 84 ÷ 7 = 12 |
| Halve, then subtract | Missing side from perimeter | Perimeter 34, length 11: 17 − 11 = 6 |
| Split into rectangles | Area of an L-shaped plan | 8 × 3 + 4 × 5 = 24 + 20 = 44 |

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Right triangle | A triangle with one right angle. |
| Acute triangle | A triangle with three acute angles. |
| Obtuse triangle | A triangle with one obtuse angle. |
| Isosceles triangle | A triangle with at least 2 sides the same length. |
| Parallel sides | Sides that point the same way and never meet. |
| Perpendicular sides | Sides that meet at a right angle. |
| Parallelogram | A quadrilateral with 2 pairs of parallel sides. |
| Line of symmetry | A fold line that makes two halves match exactly. |
| Area | The number of square units that cover a shape. |
| Perimeter | The total length around a shape. |

## Lines of symmetry to know

A square has 4, a rectangle that is not a square has 2, a rhombus that is not a square has 2 (corner to corner), and a parallelogram that is not a rectangle or a rhombus has none. An equilateral triangle has 3, an isosceles triangle that is not equilateral has 1, and a scalene triangle has none. A circle has more than you can count.

## Watch out for

- Calling a triangle acute because two of its angles are acute. Every triangle has at least two; look at the biggest.
- Thinking a turned square is a different shape. A square on its corner is still a square.
- Forgetting that a square is also a rectangle and a rhombus, or that every rectangle and rhombus is a parallelogram.
- Drawing a rectangle's diagonal as a line of symmetry. It works only for a square.
- Subtracting just one side when finding a missing side from the perimeter. Halve first.
- Multiplying the outside lengths of an L-shape. That counts the empty corner.
- Writing area in plain units or perimeter in square units.

## Quick practice (answers at the end)

1. A triangle has angles of 90°, 60° and 30°. What kind of triangle is it?
2. True or false: every rhombus is a parallelogram.
3. How many lines of symmetry does a rhombus that is not a square have?
4. A rectangle has an area of 54 square m and a width of 6 m. How long is it?
5. A rectangle has a perimeter of 50 cm and a length of 16 cm. What is its width?
6. A square has a perimeter of 44 ft. What is its area?

*Answers:* (1) A right triangle. (2) True: its opposite sides are parallel. (3) 2, the two corner-to-corner lines. (4) 54 ÷ 6 = 9 m. (5) 50 ÷ 2 = 25, 25 − 16 = 9 cm. (6) Each side is 11 ft, so the area is 121 square feet.
