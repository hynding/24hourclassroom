# Week 31 · Solving Problems With Everything We Know

**Standards:** 4.OA.A.3 Solve multistep word problems with whole numbers, including remainders · 4.MD.A.2 Solve word problems about distance, time, volume, mass and money
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 9, Section C, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Follow a plan to solve word problems that take more than one step.
- Write an equation with a letter for the unknown number.
- Decide what a remainder means in a story.
- Solve problems about length, liquid volume, mass, money and time, changing units when you need to.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Multistep problem | A word problem that needs two or more calculations. |
| Unknown | The number you are trying to find. A letter can stand for it. |
| Equation | A number sentence with an equal sign, like 4 × 36 − 58 = s. |
| Remainder | What is left over after making as many full groups as you can. |
| Estimate | A close answer found with rounded numbers. |
| Elapsed time | How much time passes from a start time to an end time. |

## A plan for any word problem

1. **Read** the problem twice. Say what it asks in your own words.
2. **Draw** a picture, a tape diagram or a number line.
3. **Write** an equation with a letter for the unknown.
4. **Solve** one step at a time.
5. **Check** with an estimate. Does the answer make sense?

**Try it.** Kenji buys 4 packs with 36 stickers each and gives away 58. Equation: 4 × 36 − 58 = s. Step 1: 4 × 36 = 144. Step 2: 144 − 58 = 86, so s = 86. Estimate: 4 × 40 = 160 and 160 − 60 = 100. 86 is close to 100, so it is reasonable.

## What does the remainder mean?

The same division can give three different answers, depending on the question. Here is 50 ÷ 6 = 8 R 2 in three stories.

| Story | What to do | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 50 students ride in vans of 6. How many vans? | Round up: the 2 extra students need a van. | 9 vans |
| 50 muffins go in boxes of 6. How many full boxes? | Drop the remainder: 2 muffins cannot fill a box. | 8 boxes |
| 50 cards are dealt to 6 players. How many are left over? | The remainder is the answer. | 2 cards |

Always reread the question after you divide.

## Measurement problems

**Change units first.** You cannot add 2 meters and 40 centimeters until both are in centimeters: 200 + 40 = 240 cm. The big units you need most:

| Bigger unit | Smaller units |
|---|---|
| 1 kilometer | 1,000 meters |
| 1 kilogram | 1,000 grams |
| 1 liter | 1,000 milliliters |
| 1 pound | 16 ounces |
| 1 yard | 3 feet, or 36 inches |
| 1 hour | 60 minutes |

**Try it.** A garden club has 3 liters of water, and each plant gets 250 milliliters. 3 liters = 3,000 mL. Four plants use 1,000 mL, so 3 liters water 3 × 4 = 12 plants.

**Money.** Line up the dollars and cents. To find change, count up. Rosa spends $2.75 + $1.60 = $4.35 and pays $5.00. From $4.35, 5 cents gets to $4.40 and 60 cents gets to $5.00, so her change is $0.65.

**Time.** Jump to the next hour, then by hours, then the extra minutes. From 2:45 p.m. to 4:10 p.m.: 15 minutes to 3:00, 1 hour to 4:00, 10 minutes to 4:10. That is 1 hour 25 minutes.

**Fractions in measurement.** 2 3/4 cups of flour and 1 3/4 cups more make 3 6/4 cups, which is 4 2/4 cups.

**A common mistake.** Subtracting times like ordinary numbers: 4:10 − 2:45 as 410 − 245 = 165 gives "1 hour 65 minutes". An hour has 60 minutes, not 100, so use jumps on a number line instead. Another mistake is stopping after the first step. In the sticker problem, 144 is only how many stickers Kenji bought, not how many he has now.

## For grown-ups at home

Real life is full of multistep problems. At the store, ask "If we buy 3 of these at $1.25, how much change from $5?" In the car, ask "We left at 9:40 and the trip is 1 hour 35 minutes. When do we arrive?" Let your child explain what each number in their answer means, especially a remainder. Estimating out loud before solving is a great habit to build.

## Check yourself

1. 62 students are put in teams of 5. How many full teams are there, and how many students are not on a full team?
2. A puppy weighs 3 kilograms. How many grams is that?
3. A movie starts at 6:50 p.m. and lasts 1 hour 45 minutes. When does it end?
4. Lena buys 2 notebooks at $1.85 each. How much does she spend?
5. Write an equation with a letter: 6 bags of 24 apples, then 15 apples are eaten.

*Answers:* (1) 62 ÷ 5 = 12 R 2: 12 full teams and 2 students left. (2) 3,000 grams. (3) 8:35 p.m. (10 minutes to 7:00, 1 hour to 8:00, 35 more minutes). (4) $3.70. (5) 6 × 24 − 15 = a; 144 − 15 = 129 apples.
