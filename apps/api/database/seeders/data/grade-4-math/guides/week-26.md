# Week 26 · Sorting Triangles and Quadrilaterals by Sides and Angles

**Standard:** 4.G.A.2 Sort shapes by their sides and angles, including right triangles
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 8, Section A, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Sort triangles by their angles: **right**, **acute** and **obtuse** triangles.
- Sort triangles by their sides: **equilateral**, **isosceles** and **scalene** triangles.
- Find **parallel** and **perpendicular** sides in a shape.
- Explain how parallelograms, rectangles, rhombuses and squares are related.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Right triangle | A triangle with one right angle (90°). |
| Acute triangle | A triangle whose three angles are all less than 90°. |
| Obtuse triangle | A triangle with one angle more than 90°. |
| Parallel sides | Sides that point the same way and would never meet, even if you made them longer. |
| Perpendicular sides | Sides that meet at a right angle. |
| Parallelogram | A quadrilateral with 2 pairs of parallel sides. |

## Sorting triangles by their angles

Every triangle has 3 angles. Look at the biggest one.

- If it is exactly a right angle, the triangle is a **right triangle**.
- If it is wider than a right angle, the triangle is an **obtuse triangle**.
- If even the biggest angle is smaller than a right angle, all three are acute, and the triangle is an **acute triangle**.

To check an angle, hold the corner of an index card in it. A right angle fits exactly, an acute angle is narrower and an obtuse angle opens wider.

A triangle can never have two right angles, or two obtuse angles. Try to draw one: the two sides going up from the ends of the bottom would stand straight up or lean apart, and they would never meet. So the other two angles of a right triangle or an obtuse triangle are always acute.

**Try it.** A triangle has angles of 30°, 40° and 110°. What kind is it? 110° is more than 90°, so it is an obtuse triangle.

## Sorting triangles by their sides

You can also measure the sides.

- **Equilateral:** all 3 sides are the same length.
- **Isosceles:** at least 2 sides are the same length.
- **Scalene:** no two sides are the same length.

"At least 2" matters. An equilateral triangle has 3 equal sides, so it also has 2 equal sides, and it is isosceles too.

The two ways of sorting work together. Cut a square from one corner to the opposite corner. Each half has a right angle and two equal sides, so it is a right triangle **and** an isosceles triangle.

## Parallel and perpendicular sides

Last unit you met parallel and perpendicular lines. Now look for them in shapes.

- Two sides are **parallel** if they point the same way and stay the same distance apart, like the rails of a ladder.
- Two sides are **perpendicular** if they meet at a right angle, like the edges at a corner of this page.

On grid paper, two sides that both run left to right are parallel, and a side that runs left to right meets a side that runs up and down at a right angle.

**Try it.** How many pairs of parallel sides does a rectangle have? Top and bottom, and left and right: 2 pairs.

## The quadrilateral family

All of these shapes have 4 sides, so they are all quadrilaterals.

- A **parallelogram** has 2 pairs of parallel sides. Its opposite sides are also the same length.
- A **rectangle** has 4 right angles. Its opposite sides are parallel, so every rectangle is a parallelogram.
- A **rhombus** has 4 sides of the same length. Its opposite sides are parallel, so every rhombus is a parallelogram.
- A **square** has 4 equal sides and 4 right angles. So it is a rectangle, a rhombus and a parallelogram all at once.

The names work only one way. A long, thin rectangle is not a square, and a parallelogram that leans over is not a rectangle. A quadrilateral with no parallel sides is still a quadrilateral, just not a parallelogram.

**Try it.** Name every name that fits a square with 6 cm sides. Square, rectangle, rhombus, parallelogram and quadrilateral.

**Watch out.** Turning a shape does not change its name. A square turned so it stands on one corner is still a square, even if it looks like a diamond. Another mistake is calling a triangle "acute" because two of its angles are acute. Every triangle has at least two acute angles, so look at the biggest angle.

## For grown-ups at home

Hunt for shapes together: a sandwich cut corner to corner, window panes, road signs, tiles. For each one, ask: "Are any sides parallel? Are any perpendicular? Is the biggest angle right, acute or obtuse?" An index card is a handy right-angle tester. Encourage your child to give every name that fits, and to back each one up with a reason, such as "It is a rhombus because all four sides are the same length."

## Check yourself

1. A triangle has angles of 90°, 45° and 45°. Is it right, acute or obtuse?
2. Can an equilateral triangle be called isosceles?
3. How many pairs of parallel sides does a parallelogram have?
4. A quadrilateral has 4 equal sides and no right angles. What is its most exact name?
5. True or false: every rectangle is a parallelogram.

*Answers:* (1) Right, because one angle is exactly 90°. (2) Yes: it has at least 2 equal sides. (3) 2 pairs. (4) Rhombus. (5) True: its opposite sides are parallel.
