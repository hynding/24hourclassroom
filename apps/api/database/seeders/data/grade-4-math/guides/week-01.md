# Week 01 · Factor Pairs and Multiples

**Standard:** 4.OA.B.4 Find factor pairs and multiples, and tell prime from composite numbers
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 1, Section A, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Use rectangles made of square tiles to find **factor pairs** of a number.
- Find **all** the factors of a number with a system that misses nothing.
- Name **multiples** of a number by skip-counting.
- See how factors and multiples come from the same multiplication fact.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Factor | A whole number that multiplies with another whole number to make a given number. |
| Factor pair | Two whole numbers that multiply to make the number, like 4 and 6 for 24. |
| Multiple | A number you say when you count by a number. 18 is a multiple of 3 and of 6. |
| Area | How many square units cover a flat shape. |
| Common multiple | A number that is a multiple of two numbers at once, like 12 for 3 and 4. |

## Rectangles and factor pairs

Imagine you have 18 square tiles and you want to lay all of them out as a rectangle, with no gaps and no tiles left over.

You could make 1 row of 18 tiles. You could make 2 rows of 9 tiles. You could make 3 rows of 6 tiles. Each time, the number of rows times the number of tiles in a row is 18:

- 1 × 18 = 18
- 2 × 9 = 18
- 3 × 6 = 18

The side lengths of each rectangle are a **factor pair** of 18. The rectangle's area is 18 square units, and its side lengths are factors of 18.

Can you make 4 rows? 4 rows of 4 is 16 and 4 rows of 5 is 20. Neither is 18, so 4 is **not** a factor of 18.

A rectangle that is 3 by 6 and one that is 6 by 3 are the same rectangle turned on its side. They show the same factor pair, so list it only once.

**Try it.** Which rectangles can you make with 15 tiles? 1 by 15 and 3 by 5. So the factors of 15 are 1, 3, 5 and 15.

## Finding every factor pair

It is easy to miss a pair if you guess. Use a system instead:

1. Start with 1. One always works: 1 × 28 = 28.
2. Try 2, then 3, then 4, and so on, in order.
3. For each number that works, write its partner.
4. Stop when the number you are trying reaches a partner you already wrote.

Here is 28:

| Try | Does it work? | Pair |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Yes | 1 and 28 |
| 2 | Yes | 2 and 14 |
| 3 | No: 3 × 9 = 27 | — |
| 4 | Yes | 4 and 7 |
| 5 | No: 5 × 5 = 25, 5 × 6 = 30 | — |
| 6 | No: 6 × 4 = 24, 6 × 5 = 30 | — |
| 7 | Already have 7 (with 4) | Stop |

The factors of 28 are 1, 2, 4, 7, 14 and 28.

Some quick checks help. An even number always has 2 as a factor. A number that ends in 0 or 5 has 5 as a factor. A number that ends in 0 has 2, 5 and 10 as factors.

## Multiples

The **multiples** of a number are what you get when you multiply it by 1, 2, 3, 4 and so on. They are the numbers you say when you skip-count.

Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42, 48, 54, 60, ...

A number line shows this well. Start at 0 and make jumps of 6. Every place you land is a multiple of 6. After 7 jumps you land on 7 × 6 = 42.

The list of multiples never ends. The list of factors always ends, because a factor cannot be bigger than the number.

## Factors and multiples go together

One multiplication fact tells you both. Look at 5 × 8 = 40:

- 5 and 8 are **factors** of 40.
- 40 is a **multiple** of 5, and 40 is a **multiple** of 8.

A number that is on two counting lists is a **common multiple**. Count by 4s: 4, 8, **12**, 16, 20, **24**. Count by 6s: 6, **12**, 18, **24**. So 12 and 24 are common multiples of 4 and 6.

**Common mistake.** Do not add when you should multiply. 5 + 7 = 12, but 5 and 7 are not a factor pair of 12, because 5 × 7 = 35. Also, a number that ends in 7, like 27, is not always a multiple of 7. Check by multiplying.

## For grown-ups at home

This unit builds on the times tables from third grade. Ask your child to find every way to arrange 24 snack crackers in equal rows, or every way to split a class of 30 into equal teams. Ask "How do you know you found them all?" A good answer is "I tried each number in order and the pairs started to repeat."

## Check yourself

1. List all the factor pairs of 32.
2. Is 6 a factor of 44? How do you know?
3. Write the first five multiples of 9.
4. Name a common multiple of 3 and 5.

*Answers:* (1) 1 and 32, 2 and 16, 4 and 8. (2) No: 6 × 7 = 42 and 6 × 8 = 48, so 44 is skipped. (3) 9, 18, 27, 36, 45. (4) 15 (also 30, 45, ...).
