# Week 16 · Measurement Word Problems: Distance, Time, Liquid, Mass and Money

**Standard:** 4.MD.A.2 Solve word problems about distance, time, volume, mass and money
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 5, Section C, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Solve word problems about **distance**, **liquid volume**, **mass**, **time** and **money**.
- Put two measurements in the **same unit** before you add or subtract them.
- Find **elapsed time** with jumps on an open number line.
- Use fractions of an hour, such as 1/4 hour and 3/4 hour.
- Count money, work in cents, and make change.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Distance | How far it is from one place to another. |
| Elapsed time | The time that passes from the start of an event to its end. |
| Liquid volume | How much liquid there is or how much a container holds. |
| Mass | How heavy something is, in grams or kilograms. |
| Change | The money you get back when you pay more than something costs. |
| Cent (¢) | One hundredth of a dollar. $1 = 100¢. |

## Step 1: same unit first

Many measurement stories use two units. Change them to the smaller unit before you add, subtract, multiply or divide.

**Try it.** Omar biked 2 km to the park and then 800 m around the pond. How far did he bike in all? *Answer:* 2 km = 2,000 m, and 2,000 + 800 = 2,800 m.

**Try it.** A bag holds 4 kg of rice. The cook uses 1,500 g. How much is left? *Answer:* 4 kg = 4,000 g, and 4,000 − 1,500 = 2,500 g.

Sharing works the same way. Pour 1 L of milk equally into 4 cups: 1 L = 1,000 mL, and 1,000 ÷ 4 = 250 mL in each cup.

## Elapsed time on a number line

Time does not count by 100s, so subtracting clock times like ordinary numbers gives wrong answers. Count on with jumps instead.

Swim lessons run from 3:35 to 5:20. Draw an open number line and jump:

| Jump | From | To | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3:35 | 4:00 | 25 min |
| 2 | 4:00 | 5:00 | 1 hr |
| 3 | 5:00 | 5:20 | 20 min |

Add the jumps: 25 + 60 + 20 = 105 minutes. Since 105 = 60 + 45, that is **1 hour 45 minutes**.

To find an end time, jump forward from the start. A train leaves at 1:40 and rides for 2 hours 30 minutes. Jump 2 hours to 3:40, then 20 minutes to 4:00, then 10 more to 4:10.

## Fractions of an hour

An hour is 60 minutes, so a fraction of an hour is a fraction of 60.

- 1/2 hour = 60 ÷ 2 = 30 minutes
- 1/4 hour = 60 ÷ 4 = 15 minutes
- 3/4 hour = 3 × 15 = 45 minutes

## Money: work in cents

A dollar is 100 cents. A quarter is 25¢, a dime is 10¢ and a nickel is 5¢. Writing money in cents turns a money story into a whole-number story.

**Try it.** Zoe has one $1 bill, 5 quarters, 2 dimes and 3 nickels. How much is that? *Answer:* 100 + 125 + 20 + 15 = 260¢, which is $2.60.

**Making change.** A book costs $2.45 and Tom pays $5.00. In cents, 500 − 245 = 255, so his change is 255¢, or $2.55. You can also count up: 245 to 250 is 5, 250 to 300 is 50, and 300 to 500 is 200. 5 + 50 + 200 = 255.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing units.** 2 km + 800 m is not 802 of anything. Convert first.
- **Treating an hour like 100 minutes.** 5:20 − 3:35 is not 1 hr 85 min. Count on with jumps.
- **Leaving out coins.** Count bills, then quarters, then dimes, then nickels, and write a running total.
- **Forgetting the unit.** "2,800" is not an answer. "2,800 m" is.

## For grown-ups at home

Real life is full of these problems. Ask how long until dinner, how many minutes a show lasts, or how much change you will get at a shop. Let your child hold the coins and count them out loud. When cooking, ask how many milliliters are in a 1 L carton or how many grams of flour are left in a 2 kg bag after using some.

## Check yourself

1. A movie starts at 6:45 and ends at 8:10. How long is it?
2. Leo's dog walk is 1 km. He has walked 650 m. How many more meters?
3. A 2 L jug of juice is shared equally by 5 friends. How many milliliters does each get?
4. Stickers cost 45¢ each. How much do 3 stickers cost? How much change from $2.00?

*Answers:* (1) 15 min + 1 hr + 10 min = 85 minutes, which is 1 hour 25 minutes. (2) 1,000 − 650 = 350 m. (3) 2,000 ÷ 5 = 400 mL. (4) 3 × 45 = 135¢ ($1.35); 200 − 135 = 65¢ change.
