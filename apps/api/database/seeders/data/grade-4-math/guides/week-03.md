# Week 03 · The Size and Location of Fractions

**Standard:** 4.NF.A.2 Compare fractions with different numerators and denominators
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 2, Section A, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Use **fraction strips** to see how big a fraction is.
- Explain why more parts in a whole means smaller parts.
- Place fractions on a **number line**, including fractions greater than 1.
- Decide whether a fraction is close to **0**, close to **1/2** or close to **1**.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Numerator | The top number: how many equal parts we are counting. |
| Denominator | The bottom number: how many equal parts make one whole. |
| Unit fraction | A fraction with 1 on top, like 1/6. It is one equal part. |
| Fraction strip | A strip one whole long, cut into equal parts. |
| Benchmark | A friendly number to compare with, such as 0, 1/2 or 1. |

## How big is a fraction?

In fourth grade we work with halves, thirds, fourths, fifths, sixths, eighths, tenths, twelfths and hundredths.

Take some paper strips that are all the same length. Leave one whole. Cut one into 2 equal pieces, one into 4, one into 8 and one into 12. Line them up under each other.

You will notice something important: **the more pieces a whole is cut into, the smaller each piece is.** One eighth is shorter than one fourth. One twelfth is shorter than one eighth. So 1/12 is less than 1/8, even though 12 is a bigger number than 8.

Think of sharing a sandwich. If 2 friends share it, each gets a big piece. If 8 friends share the same sandwich, each piece is small.

**Try it.** Put 1/5, 1/2 and 1/10 in order from smallest to greatest. Tenths are the smallest pieces, then fifths, then halves: 1/10, 1/5, 1/2.

## Fractions on a number line

A number line works like a fraction strip stretched into a line.

To place 3/4:

1. Split the space from 0 to 1 into 4 equal parts. You need 3 tick marks between 0 and 1.
2. Start at 0 and count 3 parts.
3. Mark the point. That is 3/4.

Count the **spaces**, not the tick marks. A line from 0 to 1 split into 6 parts has 7 tick marks (counting 0 and 1), but the denominator is 6.

Fractions do not stop at 1. Keep counting the same size parts past 1:

- 4/4 is at 1.
- 5/4 is one fourth past 1.
- 8/4 is at 2.

When the numerator is bigger than the denominator, the fraction is more than 1 whole. 9/5 is 5/5 plus 4/5, so it sits just before 2.

## Benchmarks: 0, 1/2 and 1

Often we do not need an exact answer. We only need to know if a fraction is "a little", "about half" or "almost all". These friendly numbers are called **benchmarks**.

**Close to 0.** The numerator is tiny compared with the denominator: 1/10, 1/12, 2/100.

**Close to 1.** Only a small piece is missing: 7/8 (one eighth missing), 11/12, 9/10.

**More or less than 1/2?** Find half of the denominator and compare it with the numerator.

| Fraction | Half of the denominator | Compare | So the fraction is |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5/8 | 4 | 5 is more than 4 | more than 1/2 |
| 2/6 | 3 | 2 is less than 3 | less than 1/2 |
| 5/10 | 5 | 5 is the same as 5 | equal to 1/2 |
| 2/5 | 2 1/2 | 2 is less than 2 1/2 | less than 1/2 |

**Try it.** A cat sleeps 5/12 of a day. Is that more or less than half a day? Half of 12 is 6, and 5 is less than 6, so it is a little less than half a day.

## Watch out

- **Bigger numbers do not mean a bigger fraction.** 4/10 has bigger numbers than 1/2, but 4/10 is less than 1/2.
- **Count from 0.** On a number line, a point 3 parts before 1 on a line in tenths is 7/10, not 3/10.
- **Same size wholes.** Half of a small pizza is not the same amount as half of a large one. Comparing fractions only makes sense when the wholes are the same size.

## For grown-ups at home

This unit is all about thinking of fractions as numbers with a size and a place on the number line, not just "shaded parts". When you cut food, ask, "If we cut this into more pieces, are the pieces bigger or smaller?" When you see a fraction in a recipe, ask whether it is closer to 0, to a half or to a whole.

## Check yourself

1. Which is greater: 1/6 or 1/10?
2. A number line from 0 to 1 is in eighths. A point is 2 parts before 1. What fraction is it?
3. Is 7/12 more or less than 1/2?
4. Where is 6/5 on a number line?

*Answers:* (1) 1/6, because sixths are bigger pieces than tenths. (2) 6/8. (3) More: half of 12 is 6, and 7 is more than 6. (4) One fifth past 1, between 1 and 2.
