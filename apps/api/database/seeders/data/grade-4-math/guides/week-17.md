# Week 17 · Number and Shape Patterns That Follow a Rule

**Standard:** 4.OA.C.5 Make and describe number and shape patterns that follow a rule
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 6, Section A, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Make a number pattern when you are given a **rule**.
- Find the rule of a pattern by looking at every pair of neighbouring terms.
- Describe a growing shape pattern and predict a later step.
- Use a repeating pattern to find a shape far down the line.
- Notice **features** of a pattern that the rule does not say out loud.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Pattern | Numbers or shapes that follow a rule. |
| Rule | The instruction that tells how to get from one term to the next, such as "add 4". |
| Term | Each number or shape in a pattern. In 3, 7, 11, the 2nd term is 7. |
| Feature | Something true about the terms that the rule does not say, like "every term is odd". |
| Repeating pattern | A pattern where the same group of shapes or numbers comes again and again. |

## Making a pattern from a rule

A rule has two parts: where to **start** and what to **do** each time.

**Try it.** Start at 7 and add 6. The terms are 7, 13, 19, 25, 31, 37 ...

Rules can multiply or subtract too.

- Start at 1 and multiply by 3: 1, 3, 9, 27, 81.
- Start at 90 and subtract 7: 90, 83, 76, 69, 62.

To find a later term without writing them all, count the jumps. From the 1st term to the 5th term there are 4 jumps, not 5. So the 5th term of "start at 7, add 6" is 7 + 4 × 6 = 7 + 24 = 31.

## Finding the rule

Look at each pair of neighbours. What happened to get from one to the next?

**Try it.** 6, 11, 16, 21. 11 − 6 = 5, 16 − 11 = 5 and 21 − 16 = 5. The rule is "start at 6 and add 5".

Always test every pair, not just the first two. In 4, 12, 36, 108, the first jump is 8 more, but 12 + 8 is 20, not 36. Each term is 3 times the one before it, so the rule is "multiply by 3".

## Growing shape patterns

Amara builds plus signs out of square tiles. Step 1 has a middle tile and four arms of 1 tile: 5 tiles. Step 2 has arms of 2 tiles: 9 tiles. Step 3 has arms of 3 tiles: 13 tiles.

Say what **stays the same** and what **changes**. The middle tile stays. Each of the 4 arms gets 1 more tile, so each step adds 4 tiles. The rule is "start at 5 and add 4".

Shortcut: Step 10 has 4 arms of 10 tiles plus the middle, so 4 × 10 + 1 = 41 tiles.

## Repeating patterns

The shapes go circle, triangle, square, circle, triangle, square ... The group that repeats has 3 shapes. Every 3rd shape (3, 6, 9, 12 ...) ends a group, so it is a square. Those positions are the **multiples of 3**.

**Try it.** Which shape is in position 14? 12 is a multiple of 3, so position 12 is a square. Then 13 is a circle and 14 is a triangle.

## Features: odd and even

Some features come from odd and even numbers.

| Start | Add | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Odd | Even | Every term is odd | 3, 7, 11, 15 |
| Even | Even | Every term is even | 2, 8, 14, 20 |
| Odd or even | Odd | The terms take turns | 4, 7, 10, 13 |

So the plus-sign pattern can never have 30 tiles: it starts at 5 (odd) and adds 4 (even), so every step is odd.

## A common mistake

Counting the jumps wrong. For "start at 7, add 6", the 5th term is **not** 7 + 5 × 6. The 1st term is 7 with no jumps yet, so the 5th term needs only 4 jumps. Write the first few terms to check.

## For grown-ups at home

Patterns are everywhere: house numbers on one side of a street go up by 2. Ask your child to make up a rule, write five terms, and then say one thing that is always true about the terms. "Why will it never land on 30?" builds the habit of explaining, not just calculating.

## Check yourself

1. Write the first five terms: start at 4, add 9.
2. What is the rule? 100, 92, 84, 76.
3. A pattern repeats red, blue, blue, green. What colour is the 12th one?
4. Start at 5 and add 10. Will any term be odd? Why?

*Answers:* (1) 4, 13, 22, 31, 40. (2) Start at 100 and subtract 8. (3) Green: the group has 4 colours, and 12 is a multiple of 4, so it is the last colour. (4) Every term is odd: 5, 15, 25 ... An odd number plus an even number (10) is odd.
