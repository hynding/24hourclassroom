# Week 09 · Tenths and Hundredths: Equivalent Fractions and Sums

**Standard:** 4.NF.C.5 Add tenths and hundredths written as fractions
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 3, Section C, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Show **tenths** and **hundredths** on a grid of 100 squares.
- Write any number of tenths as hundredths: 1/10 = 10/100.
- Add tenths and hundredths by writing both in hundredths first.
- See dimes and pennies as tenths and hundredths of a dollar.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Tenth | One of 10 equal parts of a whole, written 1/10. |
| Hundredth | One of 100 equal parts of a whole, written 1/100. |
| Hundredths grid | A square split into 10 columns and 100 small squares. The whole square is 1. |
| Equivalent fractions | Fractions that name the same amount, like 3/10 and 30/100. |

## Tenths and hundredths on a grid

Picture a big square that stands for 1 whole. Split it into 10 equal columns. Each column is **one tenth** of the square.

Now split every column into 10 small squares. The whole square has 10 × 10 = 100 small squares, so each small square is **one hundredth**.

One column holds 10 small squares. That means one tenth and ten hundredths cover the same amount:

**1/10 = 10/100**

The same is true for any number of columns. Two columns are 20 small squares, so 2/10 = 20/100. Seven columns are 70 small squares, so 7/10 = 70/100.

You can also multiply: to turn tenths into hundredths, multiply the numerator and the denominator by 10. For 8/10: 8 × 10 = 80 and 10 × 10 = 100, so 8/10 = 80/100.

**Try it.** Write 9/10 in hundredths. *90/100.*

## Reading a grid

Suppose a grid has 2 full columns shaded and 8 more small squares in the next column. That is 20 + 8 = 28 small squares, so **28/100** of the grid is shaded.

We can also say it in two parts: 2 tenths and 8 hundredths. So 2/10 + 8/100 = 28/100.

**Try it.** A grid has 5 full columns and 1 extra small square shaded. What fraction is shaded? *51/100.*

## Adding tenths and hundredths

To add fractions, the parts need to be the same size. Tenths and hundredths are different sizes, so first write the tenths as hundredths. Then add.

4/10 + 15/100
= 40/100 + 15/100
= **55/100**

The order does not matter:

7/100 + 9/10
= 7/100 + 90/100
= **97/100**

Sometimes the answer is a whole: 6/10 + 40/100 = 60/100 + 40/100 = 100/100 = 1.

**Try it.** What is 3/10 + 26/100? *30/100 + 26/100 = 56/100.*

## Money: dimes and pennies

A dollar is a good whole to think with. Ten dimes make a dollar, so a dime is **1/10** of a dollar. One hundred pennies make a dollar, so a penny is **1/100** of a dollar.

2 dimes and 5 pennies are 2/10 + 5/100 of a dollar. That is 20/100 + 5/100 = 25/100 of a dollar, which is 25 cents.

**Try it.** What fraction of a dollar are 8 dimes and 4 pennies? *80/100 + 4/100 = 84/100.*

## A common mistake

Some students add the tops and the bottoms: 4/10 + 15/100 = 19/110. That cannot be right. 4/10 alone is almost half the grid, and 19/110 is much less than that! Always change tenths into hundredths first, so the parts are the same size.

Another slip is to treat tenths like hundredths: reading 4/10 + 15/100 as 4 + 15 = 19 hundredths. Four tenths are 40 hundredths, not 4.

## For grown-ups at home

Coins make this week concrete. Lay out some dimes and pennies and ask, "What fraction of a dollar is this?" Have your child say it in tenths and hundredths ("3 tenths and 6 hundredths") and then all in hundredths ("36 hundredths"). Next week the class will write these amounts as decimals, like 0.36, so this is a helpful bridge.

## Check yourself

1. Write 5/10 as hundredths.
2. Write 60/100 as tenths.
3. What is 1/10 + 9/100?
4. What is 32/100 + 5/10?
5. What fraction of a dollar are 6 dimes and 2 pennies?

*Answers:* (1) 50/100. (2) 6/10. (3) 10/100 + 9/100 = 19/100. (4) 32/100 + 50/100 = 82/100. (5) 60/100 + 2/100 = 62/100.
