# Week 23 · Angles as Turns: Degrees and Kinds of Angles

**Standard:** 4.MD.C.5 Understand angles and how they are measured in degrees
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 7, Section B, in our own words

## What you will learn

- See an angle as a **turn** of one ray around the vertex.
- Know that a full turn is **360 degrees** (360°) and a one-degree angle is 1/360 of a turn.
- Find the size of quarter, half and three-quarter turns, and of angles on a clock face.
- Sort angles as **acute**, **right**, **obtuse** or **straight** by their measure.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Degree (°) | The unit for measuring angles. A full turn is 360°. |
| Full turn | Spinning all the way around and back to the start: 360°. |
| Acute angle | An angle more than 0° and less than 90°. |
| Right angle | An angle of exactly 90°, a quarter turn. |
| Obtuse angle | An angle more than 90° and less than 180°. |
| Straight angle | An angle of exactly 180°, a half turn. Its rays make a straight line. |

## An angle is a turn

Hold one pencil still on your desk. Lay a second pencil on top of it, with the ends touching, and spin the top pencil around the touching ends. The space you turn through is an **angle**. A small turn makes a small angle. A big turn makes a big angle.

That means the size of an angle has nothing to do with how long the rays are. Draw the same turn with long rays and with short rays, and the angles are the same size.

## Measuring turns in degrees

To measure turns, people long ago split a full circle into **360** tiny, equal turns. Each tiny turn is a **one-degree angle**, written 1°. It is 1/360 of a full turn.

An angle that turns through 1 degree 50 times measures 50°. An angle that turns through n one-degree angles measures n degrees.

Some turns are worth knowing by heart:

| Turn | Fraction of a full turn | Degrees |
|---|---|---|
| Quarter turn | 1/4 | 360 ÷ 4 = 90° |
| Half turn | 1/2 | 360 ÷ 2 = 180° |
| Three-quarter turn | 3/4 | 3 × 90 = 270° |
| Full turn | 1 | 360° |

**Try it.** A carousel turns 1/10 of the way around. How many degrees? 360 ÷ 10 = 36°.

## Angles on a clock

A clock face is a circle split into 12 equal parts. Each step from one number to the next is 360 ÷ 12 = **30°**.

To find the angle the minute hand turns, count the steps and multiply by 30. From 12 to 2 is 2 steps: 2 × 30 = 60°. From 12 to 6 is 6 steps: 180°, a half turn.

At an exact hour, the hour hand points right at a number too. At 4:00 the hands are 4 steps apart, so the smaller angle between them is 4 × 30 = **120°**. (The other way around the clock is 8 steps, 240°.)

## Kinds of angles

Now that angles have numbers, sorting them is easy:

- **Acute:** less than 90°, like 35° or 89°.
- **Right:** exactly 90°, a square corner.
- **Obtuse:** more than 90° but less than 180°, like 91° or 150°.
- **Straight:** exactly 180°. The two rays point in opposite directions and make a line.

**Try it.** Which kind is a 100° angle? It is more than 90° and less than 180°, so it is obtuse.

**Common mistake.** Thinking a wide-looking drawing must be a big angle because the rays are long. Look at the turn between the rays, not at their length. Another mix-up is counting the 12 parts of a clock as 12 degrees, or as 10 degrees each. Each part is 30°.

## For grown-ups at home

Angles become numbers this week. You can practice with turns: stand up together and do a quarter turn, a half turn and a full turn, saying 90, 180 and 360. On an analog clock, ask how many degrees the minute hand turns in 15 minutes (90°) or 20 minutes (120°). If your child is unsure, go back to the idea that a full turn is 360 equal tiny turns, and work out fractions of 360 together.

## Check yourself

1. How many degrees are in a half turn?
2. What fraction of a full turn is 90°?
3. The minute hand moves from 12 to 5. How many degrees does it turn?
4. Is a 175° angle acute, right, obtuse or straight?
5. An angle turns through 72 one-degree angles. What is its measure?

*Answers:* (1) 180°. (2) 1/4. (3) 5 × 30 = 150°. (4) Obtuse: it is more than 90° and less than 180°. (5) 72°.
