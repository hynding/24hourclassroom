# Week 11 · Place Value to a Million

**Standards:** 4.NBT.A.1 Know that a digit is worth ten times what it is worth one place to its right · 4.NBT.A.2 Read, write and compare multi-digit whole numbers
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 4, Section B, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Name every place from the ones to the hundred thousands, and know what **one million** is.
- Use the **ten times rule**: a digit is worth 10 times as much as the same digit one place to its right.
- Find the value of any digit in a big number.
- Read and write numbers in **standard form**, **word form** and **expanded form**.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Digit | One of the symbols 0 to 9 that we use to write numbers. |
| Place value | The value a digit has because of where it sits in a number. |
| Standard form | A number written with digits and commas, like 407,250. |
| Word form | A number written in words, like four hundred seven thousand, two hundred fifty. |
| Expanded form | A number written as a sum of the values of its digits: 400,000 + 7,000 + 200 + 50. |
| Million | 1,000,000, which is ten hundred thousands. |

## The places keep growing by ten

You already know that 10 ones make a ten, 10 tens make a hundred and 10 hundreds make a thousand. The pattern never stops:

- 10 thousands make **ten thousand** (10,000).
- 10 ten thousands make **one hundred thousand** (100,000).
- 10 hundred thousands make **one million** (1,000,000).

A **place-value chart** shows the places in order. Here is 472,305:

| Hundred thousands | Ten thousands | Thousands | Hundreds | Tens | Ones |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 7 | 2 | 3 | 0 | 5 |

The comma goes after the thousands digit. It splits the number into "472 thousand" and "305", which is how we read it.

## The ten times rule

Look at the 5s in 5,555. From right to left they are worth 5, 50, 500 and 5,000. Each 5 is worth **10 times** as much as the 5 just to its right.

So 800 is 10 times as much as 80, and 8,000 is 10 times as much as 800. Two places apart means 10 times and then 10 times again, which is **100 times**: 80,000 is 100 times as much as 800.

The rule works backwards too. 6,000 ÷ 10 = 600, and 6,000 ÷ 100 = 60.

**Try it.** How many tens are in 4,000? 4,000 is 40 hundreds, and each hundred is 10 tens. 40 × 10 = 400 tens. Check: 400 × 10 = 4,000.

## Three ways to write a number

Take the number 306,048.

- **Standard form:** 306,048.
- **Word form:** three hundred six thousand, forty-eight. Read the part before the comma, say "thousand", then read the part after it.
- **Expanded form:** 300,000 + 6,000 + 40 + 8.

Notice the zeros. 306,048 has 0 ten thousands and 0 hundreds. In expanded form you can skip a zero, but in standard form the zero must stay to hold its place. Without the zeros, the digits 3, 6, 4 and 8 would make 3,648, a much smaller number.

**Try it.** Write fifty thousand, seven hundred nine in standard form. Fifty thousand is 50,000 and seven hundred nine is 709, so the number is 50,709.

## The value of a digit

To find what a digit is worth, find its place and write zeros for every place to its right.

- In 846,215, the 6 is in the thousands place, so it is worth **6,000**.
- In 846,215, the 4 is in the ten thousands place, so it is worth **40,000**.

**Watch out.** A common slip is to count places from the left instead of the right. Always start at the ones place on the right and move left: ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, ten thousands, hundred thousands. Another slip is reading 208,040 as "two hundred eighty thousand". The 0 in the ten thousands place means there are no ten thousands, so it is two hundred **eight** thousand, forty.

## For grown-ups at home

Big numbers are everywhere: stadium crowds, city populations, the distance to the moon (about 384,000 kilometers). Read them aloud together, pausing at the comma. A quick game: write a number such as 703,520 and ask "What is the 7 worth? And the 5?" Then ask your child to make the number 10 times as big, and notice how every digit slides one place to the left.

## Check yourself

1. What is the value of the 9 in 591,402?
2. Write 400,000 + 30,000 + 600 + 2 in standard form.
3. Write 120,015 in word form.
4. 70,000 is how many times as much as 700?
5. How many hundreds are in 9,000?

*Answers:* (1) 90,000. (2) 430,602. (3) One hundred twenty thousand, fifteen. (4) 100 times. (5) 90 hundreds.
