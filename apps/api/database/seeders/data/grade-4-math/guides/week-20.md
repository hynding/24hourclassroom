# Week 20 · Dividing by a One-Digit Number

**Standard:** 4.NBT.B.6 Divide up to four digits by one digit, with remainders
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 6, Section C, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Divide tens, hundreds and thousands using facts you know.
- Divide in friendly chunks with **partial quotients**.
- Show a division with an area diagram.
- Write a **remainder** and check every answer by multiplying.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Dividend | The number being divided. In 852 ÷ 4, it is 852. |
| Divisor | The number you divide by. In 852 ÷ 4, it is 4. |
| Quotient | The answer to a division. 852 ÷ 4 = 213, so the quotient is 213. |
| Partial quotient | The number of groups in one friendly chunk, like 200 groups of 4 in 800. |
| Remainder | What is left over when the total cannot be split exactly. Written with R. |

## Dividing tens, hundreds and thousands

Use a fact and keep track of the place value.

- 240 ÷ 6: 240 is 24 tens. 24 tens ÷ 6 = 4 tens = **40**.
- 3,500 ÷ 7: 3,500 is 35 hundreds. 35 hundreds ÷ 7 = 5 hundreds = **500**.

## Partial quotients

Division asks "how many groups of the divisor fit in the dividend?" You do not have to find it all at once. Take away a friendly chunk, write down how many groups it was, and keep going.

**Try it.** 576 ÷ 3.

| Take away | Groups of 3 | Left |
|---|---|---|
| 300 | 100 | 276 |
| 270 | 90 | 6 |
| 6 | 2 | 0 |

Add the groups: 100 + 90 + 2 = **192**. So 576 ÷ 3 = 192.

Good chunks are 10, 100 or 1,000 groups of the divisor, or a few of those. For dividing by 6: 60, 120, 300 and 600 are all friendly. Use the biggest chunk that fits, but any chunk that fits still works.

## Division area diagrams

852 ÷ 4 can be drawn as a rectangle with an area of 852 and one side of 4. The other side is the quotient. Split the area into parts you can divide:

- 800 ÷ 4 = 200
- 40 ÷ 4 = 10
- 12 ÷ 4 = 3

The tops add up to 200 + 10 + 3 = **213**.

**Try it.** 1,345 ÷ 5. 1,000 ÷ 5 = 200, 300 ÷ 5 = 60 and 45 ÷ 5 = 9. The quotient is 269.

Watch for a missing place. 3,248 ÷ 8: 3,200 ÷ 8 = 400 and 48 ÷ 8 = 6, so the answer is 406. There are no tens, so a 0 holds the tens place.

## Remainders

Ines shares 38 stickers equally into 5 bags. 5 × 7 = 35, so each bag gets 7 and 3 are left. We write **38 ÷ 5 = 7 R 3**. You can also say it in words: "7 in each bag, with 3 left over."

The remainder must be **smaller than the divisor**. If you get 59 ÷ 4 = 13 R 7, you are not finished: 7 is enough for one more group of 4. The answer is 14 R 3.

## Check by multiplying

Multiply the quotient by the divisor, then add any remainder. You should get the dividend.

- 852 ÷ 4 = 213: 4 × 213 = 852. ✓
- 157 ÷ 6 = 26 R 1: 6 × 26 = 156, and 156 + 1 = 157. ✓

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting a partial quotient.** Add **all** the groups you took away, not just the last one.
- **A remainder that is too big.** Keep dividing until what is left is smaller than the divisor.
- **Subtracting the divisor once.** 852 − 4 is not 852 ÷ 4. Division counts how many groups of 4 fit.

## For grown-ups at home

Partial quotients let children divide with numbers they trust. One child may take away 100 groups at a time while another takes away 10; both are right, and the first is just quicker. The long-division method you may know comes in later grades and is a shorter way of writing the same steps. At home, ask "How could you check that?" after every division until multiplying back becomes a habit.

## Check yourself

1. 4,200 ÷ 6 = ?
2. Use partial quotients: 434 ÷ 7.
3. What is 50 ÷ 4? Write the remainder.
4. Check: is 812 ÷ 4 = 23 right?

*Answers:* (1) 700. (2) 420 ÷ 7 = 60 and 14 ÷ 7 = 2, so 62. (3) 12 R 2, because 4 × 12 = 48. (4) No. 4 × 23 = 92, not 812. The right answer is 203: 800 ÷ 4 = 200 and 12 ÷ 4 = 3.
