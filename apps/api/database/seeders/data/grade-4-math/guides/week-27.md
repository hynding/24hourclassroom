# Week 27 · Lines of Symmetry

**Standard:** 4.G.A.3 Find lines of symmetry
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 8, Section A, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Say what a **line of symmetry** is.
- Test a line with the **fold test** and the **mirror test**.
- Find every line of symmetry in squares, rectangles, rhombuses, parallelograms and triangles.
- Finish a symmetric shape on a grid when only half of it is drawn.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Line of symmetry | A line that cuts a shape into two halves that match exactly when folded. |
| Symmetric | Having at least one line of symmetry. |
| Fold test | Folding a shape along a line to see if the halves land on each other. |
| Mirror image | A copy flipped over a line, like your reflection in a mirror. |
| Regular polygon | A polygon whose sides are all equal and whose angles are all equal. |

## What a line of symmetry is

Cut a paper heart out of a folded sheet and open it up. The crease runs down the middle, and the left half is a flipped copy of the right half. That crease is a **line of symmetry**.

A line is a line of symmetry only if the two halves match **exactly** when you fold along it: every edge on top of an edge, every corner on top of a corner. In pictures, lines of symmetry are often drawn **dashed** so they do not look like sides.

There are two ways to test a line.

- **Fold test.** Trace the shape, cut it out and fold on the line. Do the halves land on each other?
- **Mirror test.** Pick a corner on one side. Go straight across the line to the same distance on the other side. Is there a matching corner there? Check every corner.

**Try it.** A figure 8 is drawn as two equal circles, one on top of the other. Does it have a line of symmetry? Yes, two: a line straight up and down through its middle, and a line straight across where the circles touch.

## Counting lines of symmetry

Some shapes have many lines of symmetry, some have one and some have none. Here are the ones to know.

| Shape | Lines of symmetry | Where they go |
|---|---|---|
| Square | 4 | Up and down, across, and two corner to corner |
| Rectangle that is not a square | 2 | Through the middles of opposite sides |
| Rhombus that is not a square | 2 | Corner to opposite corner |
| Parallelogram that is not a rectangle or a rhombus | 0 | None |
| Equilateral triangle | 3 | Each corner to the middle of the opposite side |
| Isosceles triangle that is not equilateral | 1 | From the corner between the equal sides |
| Scalene triangle | 0 | None |
| Regular hexagon | 6 | 3 corner to corner, 3 side to side |
| Circle | Too many to count | Every line through the center |

**A common mistake: the rectangle's diagonal.** Draw a rectangle 8 cm long and 3 cm tall and a line from one corner to the opposite corner. The two triangles have the same size, but fold on that line and two corners stick out. Same area is not enough. The halves must land exactly on each other.

**Another mistake:** thinking a line of symmetry must go straight up and down. The corner-to-corner lines of a square are slanted, and they still work.

## Finishing a symmetric shape on a grid

Sometimes you see only half of a shape and the dashed line of symmetry. To draw the other half, work square by square:

1. Pick a shaded square and count how many squares it is from the line.
2. In the same row, count the same number of squares on the other side and shade that square.
3. Repeat for every square, then check: the new half should look like the first half flipped over the line.

**Try it.** The left half of a shape has rows of 1, 3 and 2 squares, each touching the dashed line. How many squares will the whole shape cover? Each row doubles: 2 + 6 + 4 = 12 squares.

Once the shape is finished, you can find its area by counting squares and its perimeter by counting the unit edges around the outside.

## For grown-ups at home

Look for symmetry around you: leaves, butterflies, faces, doors, plates, road signs and capital letters. Ask, "Where would you fold it so the halves match? Is there another fold?" Folding paper and cutting shapes is the best practice: fold once and cut to make a shape with one line of symmetry, or fold twice for two. Encourage your child to check each line with a fold rather than guess from the look of it.

## Check yourself

1. How many lines of symmetry does a rectangle 9 cm long and 4 cm wide have?
2. True or false: every line of symmetry goes straight up and down.
3. How many lines of symmetry does an equilateral triangle have?
4. The left half of a shape covers 7 grid squares. How many squares does the whole symmetric shape cover?
5. Does a triangle with sides of 2 cm, 5 cm and 6 cm have a line of symmetry?

*Answers:* (1) 2. (2) False: lines of symmetry can go across or on a slant. (3) 3. (4) 14. (5) No: all three sides are different lengths.
