# Week 08 · Mixed Numbers and Line Plots With Fractions

**Standards:** 4.NF.B.3 Add and subtract fractions and mixed numbers with like denominators · 4.MD.B.4 Make and use line plots with fractions
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 3, Section B, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Write an amount greater than 1 as a **mixed number** (like 1 3/4) or as a fraction (like 7/4).
- Add and subtract mixed numbers with the same denominator.
- Trade 1 whole for fraction parts when you need more parts to subtract.
- Make and read a **line plot** of measurements in halves, fourths and eighths.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Mixed number | A whole number and a fraction together, like 2 3/5. It means 2 + 3/5. |
| Fraction greater than 1 | A fraction whose numerator is bigger than its denominator, like 13/5. |
| Trade (regroup) | Swap 1 whole for the same amount in parts, like 1 = 4/4. |
| Line plot | A number line with an X above it for each measurement. |
| Data | The numbers we collect, such as the lengths we measured. |

## Two names for the same amount

On a number line from 0 to 2 in fourths, find the point 3 fourths past 1. We can name it two ways:

- As a **mixed number**: 1 whole and 3 fourths, written **1 3/4**.
- As a fraction: count every fourth from 0. One whole is 4 fourths, plus 3 more, so **7/4**.

To change a mixed number into a fraction, change the wholes into parts and add the extra parts. For 3 2/5: three wholes are 3 × 5 = 15 fifths, and 15/5 + 2/5 = **17/5**.

To go the other way, make as many wholes as you can. For 13/8: 8 eighths make 1 whole and 5 eighths are left, so 13/8 = **1 5/8**.

**Try it.** Write 2 5/6 as a fraction. *2 wholes are 12 sixths, so 12/6 + 5/6 = 17/6.*

## Adding mixed numbers

One way is to add the wholes, then add the fractions.

2 1/8 + 3 4/8: the wholes make 5 and the eighths make 5/8, so the sum is **5 5/8**.

Sometimes the fraction parts make another whole. 1 5/6 + 2 4/6: the wholes make 3 and the sixths make 9/6. But 9/6 = 6/6 + 3/6 = 1 3/6. So the sum is 3 + 1 3/6 = **4 3/6**.

Another way is to change both numbers into fractions first: 11/6 + 16/6 = 27/6, which is also 4 3/6.

**Try it.** What is 3 2/5 + 2 4/5? *The wholes make 5, and 6/5 = 1 1/5, so 6 1/5.*

## Subtracting mixed numbers

When the first fraction part is big enough, subtract wholes from wholes and parts from parts:
5 7/8 − 2 3/8 = **3 4/8**.

But look at 3 1/4 − 1 3/4. You cannot take 3 fourths from 1 fourth. So **trade** 1 of the 3 wholes for 4 fourths: 3 1/4 becomes 2 5/4. Now subtract: 2 5/4 − 1 3/4 = **1 2/4**.

You can always check by changing to fractions: 13/4 − 7/4 = 6/4 = 1 2/4.

**Try it.** What is 4 2/6 − 1 5/6? *Trade a whole: 3 8/6 − 1 5/6 = 2 3/6.*

## Line plots with fractions

Mr. Haddad's class measured 10 crayons to the nearest quarter inch. The lengths were 2 1/2, 2 3/4, 3, 2 3/4, 3 1/4, 3, 2 3/4, 3, 3 1/4 and 3.

To make a line plot, draw a number line from 2 1/2 to 3 1/4 with marks every 1/4 inch. Then put one X above the mark for each crayon. The plot has 1 X at 2 1/2, 3 Xs at 2 3/4, 4 Xs at 3 and 2 Xs at 3 1/4.

Now we can answer questions:

- **How many crayons?** Count all the Xs: 1 + 3 + 4 + 2 = 10.
- **Most common length?** The tallest stack, 3 inches.
- **Longest minus shortest?** 3 1/4 − 2 1/2. Write 2 1/2 as 2 2/4, then 3 1/4 − 2 2/4 = 3/4 inch.
- **Total length of the 2 longest crayons?** 3 1/4 + 3 1/4 = 6 2/4 inches.

## A common mistake

When a mixed number becomes a fraction, some students just add the two numbers (2 3/5 becomes 5/5) or write them side by side (23/5). Remember that the whole number counts **wholes**. Each whole holds as many parts as the denominator says, so 2 3/5 = 10/5 + 3/5 = 13/5.

## For grown-ups at home

Measuring together is the best practice. Use a ruler marked in quarter inches to measure spoons, pencils or toy cars, write down the lengths, and help your child build a small line plot on paper. Then ask: "How much longer is the longest one than the shortest one?"

## Check yourself

1. Write 11/3 as a mixed number.
2. Write 4 1/2 as a fraction.
3. What is 1 3/8 + 2 6/8?
4. What is 5 − 2 1/4?
5. A line plot has Xs at 1 1/4, 1 2/4, 1 2/4 and 2 inches. How much longer is the longest than the shortest?

*Answers:* (1) 3 2/3. (2) 9/2. (3) 3 9/8 = 4 1/8. (4) Trade a whole: 4 4/4 − 2 1/4 = 2 3/4. (5) 2 − 1 1/4 = 3/4 inch.
