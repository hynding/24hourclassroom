# Week 05 · Comparing Fractions

**Standard:** 4.NF.A.2 Compare fractions with different numerators and denominators
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 2, Section C, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Compare fractions that have the **same denominator** or the **same numerator**.
- Compare fractions with the benchmarks **1/2** and **1**.
- Compare any two fractions by writing them with a **common denominator**.
- Put several fractions in order and explain your thinking.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Compare | Decide which of two amounts is greater, or whether they are equal. |
| Benchmark | A friendly number to compare with, like 0, 1/2 or 1. |
| Common denominator | A denominator that both fractions can be written with. |
| Is greater than / is less than | Words for comparing. "3/4 is greater than 1/2." |

## Way 1: Same denominator

When the denominators match, the parts are the same size. Just compare how many parts there are.

5/12 and 7/12: both are twelfths, and 7 is more than 5, so **7/12 is greater than 5/12**.

## Way 2: Same numerator

When the numerators match, you have the same number of parts. Now the size of the parts decides. Remember: the bigger the denominator, the smaller each part.

3/5 and 3/10: three fifths and three tenths. Fifths are bigger than tenths, so **3/5 is greater than 3/10**.

## Way 3: Use a benchmark

**Compare with 1/2.** If one fraction is more than half and the other is less, you are done.

4/6 and 3/8: half of 6 is 3, and 4 is more, so 4/6 is more than 1/2. Half of 8 is 4, and 3 is less, so 3/8 is less than 1/2. So **4/6 is greater than 3/8**.

**Compare with 1.** When both fractions are close to 1, look at the missing piece.

5/6 and 9/10: 5/6 is missing 1/6, and 9/10 is missing 1/10. Tenths are smaller than sixths, so 9/10 has the smaller gap. It is closer to 1, so **9/10 is greater than 5/6**.

## Way 4: Make a common denominator

When nothing else works, rewrite one or both fractions as equivalent fractions with the same denominator. Then use Way 1.

**Try it.** Compare 3/4 and 5/6.

- 4 and 6 are both factors of 12, so use twelfths.
- 3/4 = 9/12 (multiply both numbers by 3).
- 5/6 = 10/12 (multiply both numbers by 2).
- 10/12 is greater than 9/12, so **5/6 is greater than 3/4**.

Sometimes only one fraction needs changing. To compare 2/5 and 3/10, write 2/5 = 4/10. Then 4/10 is greater than 3/10, so 2/5 is greater.

## Choosing a way

| If you see | Try | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Same bottom number | Compare the numerators | 2/8 and 5/8 |
| Same top number | Bigger parts win | 4/6 and 4/12 |
| One above half, one below | Benchmark 1/2 | 7/10 and 2/6 |
| Both close to 1 | Compare the missing pieces | 7/8 and 11/12 |
| Anything else | Common denominator | 2/3 and 3/4 |

## Putting fractions in order

To order 1/10, 2/3 and 5/12 from least to greatest, sort them first. 1/10 is close to 0. 5/12 is just under 1/2. 2/3 is more than 1/2. So the order is **1/10, 5/12, 2/3**.

## Watch out

- **Do not judge by the denominator alone.** 1/12 is less than 1/3, even though 12 is bigger.
- **"One piece short" is not the same amount.** 3/4 and 7/8 are each one piece short of 1, but a fourth is a bigger missing piece than an eighth, so 7/8 is greater.
- **Keep the wholes the same size.** Comparing 1/2 of a large pizza with 3/4 of a small one does not tell you which fraction is greater.

## For grown-ups at home

Comparing fractions is about reasoning, not just a rule. Ask your child to explain *why* one fraction is greater, and which of the four ways they used. Try a quick game: each person names a fraction with a denominator of 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10 or 12, and the one who can explain which is greater wins the round.

## Check yourself

1. Compare 4/8 and 4/5.
2. Compare 5/12 and 7/8.
3. Compare 2/3 and 7/12.
4. Put in order from least to greatest: 3/4, 1/6, 1/2.

*Answers:* (1) 4/5 is greater: fifths are bigger parts than eighths. (2) 7/8 is greater: it is more than 1/2, and 5/12 is less than 1/2. (3) 2/3 = 8/12, so 2/3 is greater than 7/12. (4) 1/6, 1/2, 3/4.
