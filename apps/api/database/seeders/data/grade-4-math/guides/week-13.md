# Week 13 · Adding and Subtracting With the Standard Algorithm

**Standard:** 4.NBT.B.4 Add and subtract multi-digit whole numbers with the standard algorithm
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 4, Section D, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Add numbers up to a million with the **standard algorithm**, regrouping when a place has 10 or more.
- Subtract numbers up to a million, **decomposing** when a place does not have enough.
- Subtract across zeros, like 7,000 − 2,465.
- Check your answers with the opposite operation and with an estimate.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Standard algorithm | A step-by-step way to add or subtract in columns, one place at a time, starting with the ones. |
| Regroup | Trade 10 of one place for 1 of the next place to the left. |
| Decompose | Break 1 of a place into 10 of the next place to the right, like 1 hundred into 10 tens. |
| Sum | The answer to an addition problem. |
| Difference | The answer to a subtraction problem. |

## Adding with the standard algorithm

Write the numbers in columns so the places line up from the **right**: ones under ones, tens under tens. Then add one place at a time, starting with the ones.

Add 4,687 + 2,546:

- **Ones:** 7 + 6 = 13. That is 1 ten and 3 ones. Write 3 and put a small 1 above the tens.
- **Tens:** 8 + 4 + 1 = 13 tens. Write 3 and put a 1 above the hundreds.
- **Hundreds:** 6 + 5 + 1 = 12 hundreds. Write 2 and put a 1 above the thousands.
- **Thousands:** 4 + 2 + 1 = 7.

The sum is **7,233**. The little 1s are the regrouped tens, hundreds and thousands. If you forget them, you get 6,123, which is more than 1,000 too small.

The same steps work for bigger numbers. 38,605 + 24,798 = 63,403: the ones make 13, the tens make 10, the hundreds make 14 and the thousands make 13, so every place regroups.

**Try it.** Add 25,317 + 6,485. Ones 12 (write 2, regroup 1), tens 1 + 8 + 1 = 10 (write 0, regroup 1), hundreds 3 + 4 + 1 = 8, thousands 5 + 6 = 11 (write 1, regroup 1), ten thousands 2 + 1 = 3. The sum is 31,802.

## Subtracting with the standard algorithm

Line up the places again and start with the ones. Always take the **bottom** digit from the **top** digit. If the top digit is too small, decompose 1 from the place to its left.

Subtract 6,213 − 2,874:

- **Ones:** 3 − 4 will not work. Decompose 1 ten: now there are 13 ones and 0 tens. 13 − 4 = 9.
- **Tens:** 0 − 7 will not work. Decompose 1 hundred: 10 tens and 1 hundred. 10 − 7 = 3.
- **Hundreds:** 1 − 8 will not work. Decompose 1 thousand: 11 hundreds and 5 thousands. 11 − 8 = 3.
- **Thousands:** 5 − 2 = 3.

The difference is **3,339**.

## Subtracting across zeros

7,000 − 2,465 has no ones, tens or hundreds to decompose. Trade from the thousands all at once: 7,000 is the same as 6 thousands, 9 hundreds, 9 tens and 10 ones. Now every place has enough:

10 − 5 = 5, 9 − 6 = 3, 9 − 4 = 5, 6 − 2 = 4. The answer is **4,535**.

**Watch out.** A very common mistake is to take the smaller digit from the bigger one in each place, no matter which is on top. For 52 − 37 that gives 25, but the right answer is 15. The top number is the one you start with, so you must decompose when its digit is too small.

## Check your work

- **Check subtraction with addition.** Add your answer to the number you took away. 3,339 + 2,874 = 6,213, so 6,213 − 2,874 = 3,339 is right.
- **Check with an estimate.** Round, then add or subtract. 38,605 + 24,798 is about 40,000 + 20,000 = 60,000, and 63,403 is close.

## Story problems

A **tape diagram** shows how the numbers fit together. A town wants 25,000 trees and has planted 16,348. Draw a tape for 25,000 split into 16,348 and an unknown part t. Then t = 25,000 − 16,348 = 8,652.

For a problem with two steps, do one step at a time. A library had 12,450 books, gave away 1,875 and bought 2,300. First 12,450 − 1,875 = 10,575. Then 10,575 + 2,300 = 12,875 books.

## For grown-ups at home

Your child may have learned other ways to add and subtract, such as adding by place or counting up on a number line. The standard algorithm is the same idea written in a short form: each small 1 is a regrouped ten, hundred or thousand. Ask your child to explain what a small 1 stands for. Before working out the exact answer, ask for an estimate, and afterwards ask "Does that make sense?"
