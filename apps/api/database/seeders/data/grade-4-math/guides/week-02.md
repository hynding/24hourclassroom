# Week 02 · Prime and Composite Numbers, Area and Perimeter

**Standards:** 4.OA.B.4 Find factor pairs and multiples, and tell prime from composite numbers · 4.MD.A.3 Use the area and perimeter formulas for rectangles
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 1, Section B, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Sort whole numbers into **prime** and **composite** by their factor pairs.
- Explain why 1 is neither prime nor composite.
- Find the **area** and **perimeter** of a rectangle.
- Use area or perimeter to find a missing side length.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Prime number | A whole number greater than 1 with exactly one factor pair: 1 and itself. |
| Composite number | A whole number greater than 1 with more than one factor pair. |
| Area | The number of square units that cover a shape. |
| Perimeter | The distance all the way around a shape. |
| Length and width | The two side lengths of a rectangle. |

## Prime or composite?

Last week we built rectangles out of square tiles. Some numbers of tiles make lots of rectangles. 12 tiles make 1 by 12, 2 by 6 and 3 by 4. Other numbers make only one.

Try 7 tiles. You can make 1 row of 7. You cannot make 2 equal rows, or 3, or any other number of rows. The only factor pair of 7 is 1 and 7.

- A number like 7, with **exactly one** factor pair, is **prime**.
- A number like 12, with **more than one** factor pair, is **composite**.

The prime numbers up to 20 are 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17 and 19.

What about 1? Its only factor is 1. It does not have two different factors, so it is **neither** prime nor composite.

**Try it.** Is 35 prime or composite? It ends in 5, so 5 is a factor: 5 × 7 = 35. It has the pairs 1 × 35 and 5 × 7, so it is composite.

## Testing a number

To test a number, try small factors in order: 2, 3, 5, 7. If one of them works, the number is composite.

Be careful with numbers that "look" prime. 51 is odd and does not end in 5, but 3 × 17 = 51, so it is composite. 91 is 7 × 13, so it is composite too. And 2 is prime even though it is even: it is the only even prime.

## Area of a rectangle

Area counts the square units that cover a shape. In a rectangle the squares sit in rows, so we multiply:

**Area = length × width**

A playground that is 9 meters long and 6 meters wide has an area of 9 × 6 = 54 square meters. Area is always in **square** units.

## Perimeter of a rectangle

Perimeter is the distance around, like a fence or a picture frame. Add all four sides:

**Perimeter = length + width + length + width**, or **2 × length + 2 × width**

The same playground has a perimeter of 9 + 6 + 9 + 6 = 30 meters. Perimeter is in plain length units, not square units.

Rectangles with the same area can have different perimeters. With 16 square tiles, a 2 by 8 rectangle has a perimeter of 20 units, but a 4 by 4 square has a perimeter of only 16 units.

## Finding a missing side

**From the area.** A rectangle has an area of 48 square inches and a width of 6 inches. Ask: 6 times what is 48? 6 × 8 = 48, so the length is 8 inches.

**From the perimeter.** A rectangle has a perimeter of 30 feet and a length of 10 feet. Two lengths use 20 feet. That leaves 30 − 20 = 10 feet for the two widths, so each width is 5 feet. Check: 10 + 5 + 10 + 5 = 30.

**Common mistake.** Mixing up area and perimeter. If the question is about covering (carpet, tiles, paint), it is area: multiply. If it is about going around (fence, ribbon, border), it is perimeter: add the four sides. Also remember a rectangle has **four** sides, not two: 9 + 5 is only half of the perimeter of a 9 by 5 rectangle.

## For grown-ups at home

Primes and composites build number sense that will help with fractions later this year. Pick a number on a house or a bus and ask, "Prime or composite? How do you know?" For area and perimeter, measure a table or a rug together in feet and find both. Ask which one you would need to buy a tablecloth and which for a ribbon border.

## Check yourself

1. Is 41 prime or composite?
2. Is 49 prime or composite?
3. Find the area and perimeter of a rectangle 7 cm long and 3 cm wide.
4. A rectangle has an area of 40 square feet and one side of 5 feet. How long is the other side?

*Answers:* (1) Prime: only 1 × 41. (2) Composite: 7 × 7 = 49. (3) Area 21 square cm; perimeter 7 + 3 + 7 + 3 = 20 cm. (4) 8 feet, because 5 × 8 = 40.
