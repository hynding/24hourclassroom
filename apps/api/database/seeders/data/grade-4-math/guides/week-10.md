# Week 10 · Decimals: Tenths and Hundredths

**Standards:** 4.NF.C.6 Write tenths and hundredths as decimals · 4.NF.C.7 Compare decimals to hundredths
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 4, Section A, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Write fractions with a denominator of 10 or 100 as **decimals**, and decimals as fractions.
- Name the **tenths place** and the **hundredths place**.
- Show decimals on a hundredths grid and on a number line.
- **Compare** two decimals and explain which one is greater.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Decimal | A number written with a decimal point, like 0.4 or 2.75. |
| Decimal point | The dot that separates the whole numbers from the parts less than one. |
| Tenths place | The first place after the decimal point. In 0.7, the 7 means 7 tenths. |
| Hundredths place | The second place after the decimal point. In 0.36, the 6 means 6 hundredths. |
| Equivalent decimals | Decimals that name the same amount, like 0.5 and 0.50. |

## From fractions to decimals

Last week you worked with fractions like 6/10 and 25/100. A **decimal** is another way to write the very same numbers. The places to the right of the decimal point keep going the way place value always goes: each place is one tenth the size of the place to its left.

- 6/10 is written **0.6** (six tenths).
- 25/100 is written **0.25** (twenty-five hundredths: 2 tenths and 5 hundredths).
- 4/100 is written **0.04**. There are no tenths, so a 0 holds the tenths place.

A whole number can go in front. The mixed number 3 7/10 is **3.7**, and 1 45/100 is **1.45**. Money works this way too: $2.35 is 2 whole dollars and 35 hundredths of a dollar, which is 35 cents.

**Try it.** Write 8/100 as a decimal. There are 0 tenths and 8 hundredths, so it is 0.08, not 0.8.

## Pictures of decimals

A **hundredths grid** is a big square split into 100 small squares. The whole grid is 1.

- One small square is 1/100, or 0.01.
- One full column of 10 squares is 1/10, or 0.1.

To show 0.47, shade 4 full columns (4 tenths) and 7 more squares (7 hundredths). That is 47 small squares out of 100.

A **number line** works too. Split the space from 0 to 1 into 10 equal parts. Each jump is one tenth. Count the jumps from 0, not the tick marks: the fourth tick after 0 is 0.4. A number like 0.65 sits halfway between 0.6 and 0.7, because 65 hundredths is halfway between 60 and 70 hundredths.

## Tenths and hundredths together

Shade 5 columns of a grid. That is 0.5. Now count the small squares: there are 50, so it is also 0.50. **0.5 and 0.50 are equal.** A zero at the end of a decimal does not change its value.

This trick helps when you compare. Any number of tenths can be written as hundredths: 0.3 = 0.30 and 0.9 = 0.90.

## Comparing decimals

To compare two decimals, make sure they are parts of the **same whole**. Half of a large pizza is more than half of a small one. Then compare place by place, from left to right:

1. Compare the whole numbers. 1.2 is greater than 0.95, because it has a whole one.
2. If the wholes match, compare the tenths. 0.62 is greater than 0.58, because 6 tenths is more than 5 tenths.
3. If the tenths match too, compare the hundredths. 0.47 is greater than 0.43.

Another way: write both numbers in hundredths. Compare 0.4 and 0.35. 0.4 = 0.40, which is 40 hundredths, and 40 is more than 35. So 0.4 is greater than 0.35.

**Watch out.** A decimal with more digits is not always bigger. Many students think 0.35 is greater than 0.4 "because 35 is more than 4". But the 4 in 0.4 is 4 tenths, and the 3 in 0.35 is only 3 tenths. On a grid, 0.4 shades 40 squares and 0.35 shades only 35.

**Try it.** Which is greater, 0.7 or 0.69? 0.7 = 0.70. 70 hundredths is more than 69 hundredths, so 0.7 is greater.

## For grown-ups at home

Decimals build on the fractions with denominators of 10 and 100 from last week, so let your child say a decimal as a fraction out loud: "zero point three" is "three tenths". Money is the best everyday model. Ask "Which costs more, $0.80 or $0.75?" and then drop the dollar sign: 0.8 or 0.75. When your child compares, ask which place they looked at first and why.

## Check yourself

1. Write 37/100 as a decimal.
2. Write 0.6 as a fraction with a denominator of 100.
3. Write 5 2/10 as a decimal.
4. Which is greater, 0.09 or 0.1?
5. Put in order from least to greatest: 0.5, 0.45, 0.54.

*Answers:* (1) 0.37. (2) 60/100. (3) 5.2. (4) 0.1, because 0.1 is 10 hundredths and 0.09 is only 9 hundredths. (5) 0.45, 0.5, 0.54.
