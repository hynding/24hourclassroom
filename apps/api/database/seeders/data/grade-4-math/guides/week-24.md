# Week 24 · Measuring and Drawing Angles With a Protractor

**Standard:** 4.MD.C.6 Measure and draw angles with a protractor
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 7, Section B, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Name the parts of a **protractor**: the center mark, the baseline and the two rows of numbers.
- Measure an angle to the nearest degree, choosing the right row of numbers.
- Measure an angle when neither ray is on 0.
- Draw an angle of a given size, and check your work with a square corner.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Protractor | A tool for measuring and drawing angles in degrees. |
| Center mark | The dot, hole or cross that goes on the vertex. |
| Baseline | The straight line through the center mark, from 0 on one side to 180 on the other. |
| Scale | A row of numbers on the protractor. Most protractors have two. |
| Benchmark angle | An angle you know well, like 90° or 180°, used to check an answer. |

## Getting to know the protractor

A protractor is half of a circle, so it shows half of a full turn: 0° to 180°. Small marks show every degree. Longer marks show every 5 and every 10 degrees.

Look closely and you will see **two rows of numbers**. On one row, 0 is on the right and the numbers count up toward the left. On the other row, 0 is on the left and the numbers count up toward the right. The two rows let you measure an angle that opens either way.

Every mark has one number from each row, and the two always add to 180, like 50 and 130, or 20 and 160.

## Measuring an angle

1. Put the **center mark** exactly on the **vertex**.
2. Turn the protractor so one ray lies along the **baseline** and passes through a **0**.
3. Find the row of numbers that starts at **that** 0. Follow it up and around until you reach the other ray. Read the number there.

**Try it.** An angle's first ray points right, through the 0 on the inner row. The other ray crosses 50 on the inner row and 130 on the outer row. The inner row starts at 0 on the first ray, so the angle measures 50°.

**Check with a benchmark.** Before you write a number, ask: is this angle acute or obtuse? The angle above is narrower than a square corner, so it must be less than 90°. 50 fits; 130 does not.

If a ray is too short to reach the numbers, extend it with a ruler. That does not change the angle.

## When neither ray is on 0

Sometimes neither ray is on 0. Read **both** rays on the **same** row, then subtract.

**Try it.** On the inner row, one ray crosses 20 and the other crosses 95. The angle is 95 − 20 = 75°. On the outer row they cross 160 and 85, and 160 − 85 = 75° too. Same row, same answer.

## Drawing an angle

To draw a 70° angle:

1. Draw a ray with a ruler. Its endpoint will be the vertex.
2. Put the center mark on the endpoint. Line the ray up with the baseline so it passes through a 0.
3. On the row that starts at **that** 0, make a small dot at 70.
4. Lift the protractor and draw a ray from the vertex through the dot. Label the angle 70°.

Then check: 70° is less than 90°, so your angle should be narrower than a square corner.

**Common mistake.** Reading or drawing on the wrong row. If you start at the 0 on the right but use the row that starts on the left, a 70° angle turns into a 110° angle. The benchmark check catches this every time.

## For grown-ups at home

If you have a protractor at home, let your child measure angles you draw, then swap roles. The two rows of numbers are the hard part. A helpful question is "Which 0 is your first ray on? Follow the numbers that start there." Also ask "Should this be more or less than 90?" before they read the number. No protractor? Printable ones are easy to find, or fold a sheet of paper to make 90° and 45° and use those to estimate.

## Check yourself

1. Where does the center mark of a protractor go?
2. A ray crosses 30 on one row. What does the other row show at that ray?
3. An angle is obtuse. A student reads 40° and 140°. Which is the measure?
4. On the same row, the rays of an angle cross 15 and 105. What is the angle?
5. To draw a 125° angle, will the new ray lean toward the 0 you started from or away from it?

*Answers:* (1) On the vertex of the angle. (2) 150, because the two readings add to 180. (3) 140°, because obtuse angles are more than 90°. (4) 105 − 15 = 90°, a right angle. (5) Away from it: 125° is wider than a square corner, so the ray leans past straight up.
