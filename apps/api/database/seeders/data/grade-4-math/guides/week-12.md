# Week 12 · Comparing, Ordering and Rounding Big Numbers

**Standards:** 4.NBT.A.3 Round multi-digit whole numbers to any place · 4.NBT.A.2 Read, write and compare multi-digit whole numbers
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 4, Section C, in our own words

## What you will learn

- **Compare** two numbers up to a million and say which is greater.
- **Order** a list of big numbers from least to greatest or greatest to least.
- **Round** a number to the nearest ten, hundred, thousand, ten thousand or hundred thousand.
- Use rounded numbers to **estimate** and to check an answer.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Compare | Decide which of two numbers is greater, or if they are equal. |
| Order | Put numbers in a line from least to greatest, or the other way. |
| Round | Change a number to a nearby friendly number with zeros at the end. |
| Halfway point | The number exactly in the middle of two friendly numbers, like 45,000 between 40,000 and 50,000. |
| Estimate | A close answer found with rounded numbers. |

## Comparing big numbers

**Step 1: count the digits.** A six-digit number is always greater than a five-digit number. 120,050 is greater than 98,700, even though 9 is bigger than 1.

**Step 2: if the digit counts match, start at the left.** Compare the digits in the biggest place. If they are the same, move one place to the right. The first place where the digits differ decides.

Compare 86,402 and 86,240:

- Ten thousands: 8 and 8. Same.
- Thousands: 6 and 6. Same.
- Hundreds: 4 and 2. Different! 4 hundreds is more than 2 hundreds.

So **86,402 is greater than 86,240**. Notice that both numbers use the same digits. What matters is where each digit sits.

To **order** numbers, compare them in pairs, or find the least one first, then the next, and so on. From least to greatest: 9,875; 86,240; 86,402; 120,050.

**Try it.** Which is greater, 305,190 or 350,019? Both have 3 hundred thousands. The ten thousands are 0 and 5, so 350,019 is greater.

## Rounding on a number line

Rounding tells you which friendly number a number is **closest** to. A number line helps you see it.

Round 47,600 to the nearest thousand. It is between 47,000 and 48,000. Draw a line from 47,000 to 48,000 and mark the halfway point, 47,500. 47,600 is past 47,500, so it is closer to 48,000. It rounds to **48,000**.

A number exactly at the halfway point is not closer to either end. The rule is to **round up**: 45,000 rounds to 50,000 for the nearest ten thousand.

## Rounding with the digit to the right

You do not always need to draw a line. Follow these steps:

1. Find the place you are rounding to.
2. Look at the digit **one place to its right**.
3. If that digit is 0 to 4, round down: keep the digit in your place. If it is 5 to 9, round up: add one to it.
4. Change every digit after your place to 0.

Round 362,519 to the nearest ten thousand. The ten thousands digit is 6. The digit to its right is 2, so round down: **360,000**.

Round 8,451 to the nearest hundred. The hundreds digit is 4. The digit to its right is 5, so round up: **8,500**.

**Watch out.** Round only once, from the number you started with. 14,480 rounds to 14,000 for the nearest thousand, because the hundreds digit is 4. If you first round to 14,500 and then round again, you get 15,000, which is wrong. Also watch for a 9 that rounds up: 59,800 to the nearest thousand is 60,000, not 50,000.

## Estimating

An estimate is a quick, close answer. Round each number, then add or subtract.

- 4,826 + 3,189 is about 5,000 + 3,000 = **8,000**. The exact sum is 8,015.
- 61,920 − 28,350 is about 60,000 − 30,000 = **30,000**. The exact difference is 33,570.

Estimates are good for checking. If someone says 4,826 + 3,189 = 7,005, the estimate of 8,000 tells you to look again.

## For grown-ups at home

News stories often use rounded numbers: "About 70,000 fans came to the game." Ask your child what the exact number might have been, and which place was rounded. When comparing, encourage your child to say why: "Both have 86 thousands, but this one has more hundreds." The reason matters more than the answer.

## Check yourself

1. Which is greater, 74,099 or 74,190?
2. Order from least to greatest: 52,300; 5,230; 523,000.
3. Round 238,615 to the nearest thousand.
4. Round 238,615 to the nearest hundred thousand.
5. Estimate 7,180 + 2,940 by rounding each number to the nearest thousand.

*Answers:* (1) 74,190 (1 hundred is more than 0 hundreds). (2) 5,230; 52,300; 523,000. (3) 239,000. (4) 200,000. (5) 7,000 + 3,000 = 10,000.
