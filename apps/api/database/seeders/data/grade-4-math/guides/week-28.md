# Week 28 · Reasoning About Shapes, Area and Perimeter

**Standards:** 4.MD.A.3 Use the area and perimeter formulas for rectangles · 4.G.A.2 Sort shapes by their sides and angles, including right triangles
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 8, Section B, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Use the **area** and **perimeter** formulas for rectangles, including with big numbers.
- Find a **missing side** when you know the area or the perimeter.
- Find hidden sides and the area of a plan made of rectangles.
- Use clues about sides and angles to name a shape, and give its **most exact name**.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Area | How many square units cover a shape. |
| Perimeter | The total length of all the sides of a shape. |
| Formula | A rule written with letters that works every time. |
| Length and width | The two side lengths of a rectangle. |
| Most exact name | The shape name that tells the most about the shape. |

## Two formulas for rectangles

For a rectangle with length **l** and width **w**:

- **Area:** A = l × w. A rug 9 ft by 6 ft covers 9 × 6 = 54 square feet.
- **Perimeter:** P = 2 × l + 2 × w. The same rug has a perimeter of 18 + 12 = 30 ft. You can also add one length and one width and double it: (9 + 6) × 2 = 30.

The formulas work for big rectangles too. A parking lot 60 m by 25 m covers 60 × 25 = 1,500 square meters. Its perimeter is 120 + 50 = 170 m.

Keep the units straight. Perimeter is a length, so it is in feet or meters. Area covers a surface, so it is in **square** feet or **square** meters.

## Finding a missing side

A formula can also run backward.

- **From the area.** A rectangle has an area of 72 square cm and a width of 6 cm. Ask: 6 times what is 72? 72 ÷ 6 = 12, so the length is 12 cm.
- **From the perimeter.** A rectangle has a perimeter of 40 m and a length of 14 m. Half of 40 is 20, which is one length plus one width. 20 − 14 = 6, so the width is 6 m. Check: 14 + 6 + 14 + 6 = 40.

**Watch out.** A common mistake with perimeter is subtracting only one side: 40 − 14 = 26 is what is left for the other three sides, not the width. Halve first, then subtract.

**Try it.** A square has a perimeter of 28 in. What is its area? Each side is 28 ÷ 4 = 7 in, so the area is 7 × 7 = 49 square inches.

## Plans made of rectangles

Many rooms and gardens are shaped like an L. If every corner is a square corner, you can find hidden sides by balancing opposite sides: the short pieces on one side add up to the long side across from them.

**Try it.** A garden is an L. The bottom is 10 m, the left side is 8 m, the top is 4 m and the short right side is 3 m. The hidden level step is 10 − 4 = 6 m, and the hidden upright side is 8 − 3 = 5 m.

- **Perimeter:** 10 + 3 + 6 + 5 + 4 + 8 = 36 m.
- **Area:** split it into a 10 by 3 rectangle (30 square meters) and a 4 by 5 rectangle above it (20 square meters). 30 + 20 = 50 square meters.

Do not multiply 10 × 8 = 80: that counts the empty corner as garden.

## Same area, different perimeter

Rectangles can share an area and still have different perimeters. A 1 by 16 rectangle, a 2 by 8 rectangle and a 4 by 4 square all cover 16 square units. Their perimeters are 34, 20 and 16 units. So before you build, ask which one the job needs: carpet, paint and tiles need **area**; fences, ribbon and borders need **perimeter**.

## Reasoning from clues

Last week and the week before, you sorted shapes by sides and angles. Put those facts to work with clues.

**Try it.** "I am a parallelogram. One of my angles is a right angle. All my sides are the same length." In a parallelogram, one square corner makes every corner square, so the shape has 4 right angles and is a rectangle. All sides equal too means it is a square.

Every clue removes some shapes. The name that fits every clue and tells the most is the **most exact name**. A square is also a rectangle, a rhombus, a parallelogram and a quadrilateral, but "square" says the most.

## For grown-ups at home

Measure a real rectangle together, such as a table, a rug or a garden bed, then find its area and perimeter. Ask the question backward: "If the area is 24 square feet and one side is 4 feet, how long is the other?" Play "guess my shape" with clues about parallel sides, right angles and equal sides. Ask your child to explain why each clue rules shapes in or out.

## Check yourself

1. What is the area of a rectangle 15 m by 4 m?
2. A rectangle has an area of 63 square cm and one side of 7 cm. How long is the other side?
3. A rectangle has a perimeter of 26 ft and a length of 8 ft. What is its width?
4. Is a parallelogram with one right angle always a rectangle?
5. Which needs area: tiling a floor or putting trim around a door?

*Answers:* (1) 60 square meters. (2) 9 cm. (3) 26 ÷ 2 = 13, 13 − 8 = 5 ft. (4) Yes: one square corner makes all 4 corners square. (5) Tiling a floor.
