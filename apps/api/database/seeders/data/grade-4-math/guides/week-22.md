# Week 22 · Points, Lines, Rays and Angles

**Standard:** 4.G.A.1 Draw and name points, lines, segments, rays, angles, and perpendicular and parallel lines
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 7, Section A, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Tell apart a **point**, a **line**, a **line segment** and a **ray**, and name each one with letters.
- Know what an **angle** and its **vertex** are, and name an angle with three letters.
- Compare an angle with a square corner to call it **right**, **acute** or **obtuse**.
- Spot **parallel**, **perpendicular** and **intersecting** lines in drawings and in the world around you.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Point | An exact spot, drawn as a dot and named with a capital letter. |
| Line | A straight path that goes on forever in both directions. |
| Line segment | A part of a line with two endpoints. |
| Ray | A part of a line with one endpoint that goes on forever in one direction. |
| Angle | Two rays that share an endpoint. |
| Vertex | The shared endpoint of the two rays of an angle. |
| Parallel lines | Lines that never cross and stay the same distance apart. |
| Perpendicular lines | Lines that cross and make right angles. |

## The building blocks

Geometry starts with a **point**, an exact spot, drawn as a dot and named with a capital letter.

A **line** is perfectly straight and never ends. We draw an arrow on each end to show it never stops. Two points name it: line PQ, or line QP.

A **line segment** is a piece of a line. It stops at two **endpoints**, so you can measure how long it is. The edge of your desk is a segment. Name it by its endpoints: segment JK or segment KJ.

A **ray** starts at one endpoint and goes on forever in one direction, like the beam of a flashlight. Draw a dot at the start and an arrow at the other end. When you name a ray, the endpoint always comes first. A ray that starts at M and goes through N is **ray MN**. Ray NM would start at N and point the other way, so it is a different ray.

**Try it.** A figure has a dot at C, passes through D and has an arrow past D. It has one endpoint, so it is a ray. Its name is ray CD.

## Angles

An **angle** is made of two rays that start at the same point. That shared point is the **vertex**.

To name an angle, use three letters: a point on one ray, then the vertex, then a point on the other ray. The vertex always goes in the **middle**. If the rays start at B and pass through A and C, the angle is angle ABC or angle CBA. Angle BAC would be a different angle, with its vertex at A.

Next week you will measure angles in degrees. For now, compare an angle with a **square corner**, like the corner of a sheet of paper:

- A **right angle** fits the square corner exactly. Drawings often mark it with a small square.
- An **acute angle** is narrower than a square corner.
- An **obtuse angle** is wider than a square corner, but not opened out into a straight line.

**Try it.** Hold an index card against the corner of a book and the tip of a pizza slice. The book's corner is a right angle; the pizza tip is acute.

## Lines that meet and lines that never meet

Two lines that cross are **intersecting lines**. They cross at exactly one point.

If two lines cross and make square corners, they are **perpendicular**. The edges of a window frame that meet at a corner are perpendicular, and so are the bar and stem of the letter T.

Two lines that never cross, however far they go, are **parallel**. They stay the same distance apart, like the rails of a train track or the lines on notebook paper. On a map, two streets that run side by side and never meet are parallel.

If the space between two lines gets smaller in one direction, they will meet somewhere, so they are not parallel.

**Common mistake.** Thinking that any two lines that meet are perpendicular. Lines that cross on a slant intersect, but they are only perpendicular if the angles are right angles.

## For grown-ups at home

This week is about the words of geometry, so ask your child to use them out loud. Go on a "line hunt": parallel lines (fence boards), perpendicular lines (tiles, door frames), segments (a table edge) and angles (scissors opened partway). Naming a ray endpoint-first and an angle vertex-in-the-middle matters all unit.

## Check yourself

1. How many endpoints does a line segment have? A ray? A line?
2. A ray starts at point R and passes through point S. Name it.
3. In angle XYZ, which point is the vertex?
4. Two roads cross and make four square corners. What word describes them?
5. Are the top and bottom edges of a door parallel or perpendicular?

*Answers:* (1) A segment has 2, a ray has 1 and a line has 0. (2) Ray RS. (3) Y, the middle letter. (4) Perpendicular. (5) Parallel: they never meet and stay the same distance apart.
