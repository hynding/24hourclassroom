# Week 25 · Adding Angles and Finding Unknown Angles

**Standard:** 4.MD.C.7 Add angle measures and find unknown angles
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 7, Section C, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Add the parts of an angle to find the whole.
- Find a missing part of a right angle, a straight angle or a full turn.
- Write an equation with a letter for the unknown angle, and solve it.
- Solve word problems about turns and angles.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Adjacent angles | Angles side by side that share a vertex and a ray and do not overlap. |
| Unknown angle | An angle whose measure we need to find, often shown with a letter or "?". |
| Equation | A number sentence with an equal sign, like 35 + x = 90. |
| Right angle | 90°. |
| Straight angle | 180°. |
| Full turn | 360°, all the way around a point. |

## Angles add up

Put a 30° angle next to a 45° angle so they share a ray and do not overlap. Together they make one bigger angle. Its measure is the **sum** of the parts: 30 + 45 = **75°**.

This works for any number of parts, as long as they do not overlap. If they overlapped, the shared part would be counted twice.

You can see this on a protractor. Three rays that cross 0, 40 and 100 on the same row make two parts: 40 − 0 = 40° and 100 − 40 = 60°. The whole angle is 100°, and 40 + 60 = 100.

## Finding a missing part

If you know the whole and one part, **subtract** to find the other part. The trick is knowing the whole. Three wholes come up again and again:

| The whole is... | It measures | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A right angle (square corner) | 90° | Parts 35° and ?: 90 − 35 = 55° |
| A straight angle (a straight line) | 180° | Parts 128° and ?: 180 − 128 = 52° |
| All the way around a point | 360° | Parts 90°, 120°, 100° and ?: 360 − 310 = 50° |

**Try it.** A straight line is split into three angles: 65°, x and 40°. The known parts add to 65 + 40 = 105. Then 180 − 105 = 75, so x = 75°. Check: 65 + 75 + 40 = 180.

## Writing an equation

A letter can stand for the angle you do not know. Write what you know about the parts and the whole:

- A right angle split into 27° and x: **27 + x = 90**, so x = 63°.
- A straight angle split into 112° and x: **112 + x = 180**, so x = 68°.
- Three equal angles that make a straight angle: **a + a + a = 180**, or 3 × a = 180, so a = 60°.

Always check by adding the parts back together. They should make the whole.

## Turns and tiles

Angle problems also hide in turns and patterns.

**Turns.** A skater spins 90°, then 45°, then 90° more in the same direction. Turns in the same direction add: 90 + 45 + 90 = 225°.

**Tiles.** Shapes that fit around a point with no gaps fill 360°. Four square tiles fit, because 4 × 90 = 360. Six triangle pattern blocks fit, so each corner is 360 ÷ 6 = 60°. Three hexagons fit, so each hexagon corner is 360 ÷ 3 = 120°.

**Common mistake.** Using the wrong whole. Before you subtract, look at the picture and ask: is the whole a square corner (90), a straight line (180) or all the way around (360)? Subtracting from 180 when the whole is a right angle is the most common slip.

## For grown-ups at home

This week ties together addition, subtraction and the angle facts from the last two weeks. Ask your child, "What is the whole?" before they start a problem, and "Do the parts add back to it?" when they finish. Pattern blocks or paper shapes are great for this: fit them around a point and work out each angle from 360°.

## Check yourself

1. A 25° angle and a 50° angle sit side by side with no overlap. What is the whole angle?
2. A right angle is split into two parts. One part is 72°. What is the other?
3. Write an equation for a straight angle split into 145° and x. Solve it.
4. Five equal angles fill the space all the way around a point. How big is each one?
5. Angles of 100°, 95° and x fill a full turn. Find x.

*Answers:* (1) 75°. (2) 90 − 72 = 18°. (3) 145 + x = 180, so x = 35°. (4) 360 ÷ 5 = 72°. (5) 100 + 95 = 195, and 360 − 195 = 165°.
