# Week 19 · Multiplying Two-Digit Numbers by Two-Digit Numbers

**Standard:** 4.NBT.B.5 Multiply up to four digits by one digit, and two digits by two digits
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 6, Section B, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Multiply tens by tens, like 40 × 30.
- Split **both** factors into tens and ones and draw a four-part area diagram.
- Use a **partial-product table** to keep the four parts in order.
- Estimate to check a two-digit product.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Two-digit number | A number from 10 to 99, made of tens and ones. |
| Area diagram | A rectangle whose sides are the factors and whose area is the product. |
| Partial product | The product of one part of each factor, such as 20 × 5. |
| Partial-product table | A grid with the parts of one factor across the top and the other down the side. |

## Tens times tens

20 × 30 is 2 tens times 3 tens. Multiply the digits, 2 × 3 = 6. Then 10 × 10 = 100, so the answer is 6 hundreds = **600**.

- 40 × 50: 4 × 5 = 20, so 20 hundreds = **2,000**.
- 60 × 70: 6 × 7 = 42, so 42 hundreds = **4,200**.

## Four parts in an area diagram

Look at 23 × 15. Split 23 into 20 and 3 across the top. Split 15 into 10 and 5 down the side. Now the rectangle has **four** parts.

- 20 × 10 = 200
- 3 × 10 = 30
- 20 × 5 = 100
- 3 × 5 = 15

Add them: 200 + 30 + 100 + 15 = **345**. So 23 × 15 = 345.

You can also add one column at a time. The left column is 20 × 15 = 300 and the right column is 3 × 15 = 45. 300 + 45 = 345, the same answer.

## The partial-product table

A table is an area diagram with the drawing taken out. Here is 34 × 26.

| × | 30 | 4 |
|---|---|---|
| 20 | 600 | 80 |
| 6 | 180 | 24 |

Each box is its column number times its row number. Add all four boxes: 600 + 80 + 180 + 24 = **884**.

**Try it.** 28 × 35. 20 × 30 = 600, 20 × 5 = 100, 8 × 30 = 240, 8 × 5 = 40. The total is 980.

## Friendly shortcuts

Sometimes splitting only one factor is quicker. 11 × 37 = 10 × 37 + 1 × 37 = 370 + 37 = 407. And four 25s make 100, so 25 × 40 = 1,000.

## Estimate to check

Round both factors to the nearest ten. 61 × 39 is about 60 × 40 = 2,400. The exact product, 2,379, is close, so it makes sense.

A two-digit number times a two-digit number is always at least 100 (10 × 10) and less than 10,000 (100 × 100).

## The big mistake to avoid

"Tens with tens, ones with ones" leaves out half the work. 23 × 15 is **not** 20 × 10 + 3 × 5 = 215. That uses only two of the four parts. The 3 must also be multiplied by the 10, and the 20 by the 5. Drawing the four boxes every time stops this mistake.

## For grown-ups at home

This is the biggest multiplication your child does this year. The area diagram may look slow, but it shows exactly where every part of the answer comes from, which is what makes the shorter method in Grade 5 make sense. If your child gets a wrong answer, ask them to draw the four boxes and look for the missing one. Real-life practice: rows of seats at a game, eggs in cartons, or pages read in a few weeks.

## Check yourself

1. 30 × 80 = ?
2. Find 12 × 14 with four partial products.
3. Fill in a table for 45 × 22 and find the product.
4. Ben says 32 × 24 = 7,680. Use an estimate to decide whether that is reasonable.

*Answers:* (1) 2,400. (2) 100 + 20 + 40 + 8 = 168. (3) 800, 100, 80 and 10; the product is 990. (4) Not reasonable. 30 × 20 = 600, so the product should be in the hundreds, not the thousands. The exact product is 600 + 40 + 120 + 8 = 768.
