# Week 21 · Multistep Problems, Remainders and Estimates

**Standard:** 4.OA.A.3 Solve multistep word problems with whole numbers, including remainders
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 6, Section D, in our own words

## What you will learn

- Plan and solve word problems that take two or more steps.
- Write an equation with a **letter** for the unknown number.
- Decide what a **remainder** means in a story.
- Use an **estimate** to see whether an answer makes sense.

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Multistep problem | A word problem where the answer to one step is used in the next. |
| Unknown | The number you are looking for. A letter such as n can stand for it. |
| Remainder | What is left over after making equal groups. |
| Estimate | A number close to the exact answer, found by rounding. |
| Reasonable | Makes sense: close to the estimate and fits the story. |

## Four steps for any word problem

1. **Read.** Say in your own words what the question asks.
2. **Plan.** Which steps? Which operations? A tape diagram can help.
3. **Solve.** Do one step at a time and label each result.
4. **Check.** Estimate. Does the answer fit the story?

**Try it.** A farm stand has 6 crates with 48 apples in each. They sell 135 apples. How many are left?

- Step 1: apples at the start: 6 × 48 = 288.
- Step 2: apples left: 288 − 135 = **153**.
- Check: 6 × 50 = 300, and 300 − 140 = 160. 153 is close.

## Letters for unknowns

An equation can hold the whole plan. Tomás has 3 packs of 24 cards and gives away 15. If n is the number he has left:

n = 3 × 24 − 15

Multiply first: 3 × 24 = 72. Then 72 − 15 = 57, so n = 57.

A tape diagram shows the same thing. A class wants 650 cans. They collect 125 cans on each of 4 days. Draw a tape for 650 with four parts of 125 and one unknown part c. Then 4 × 125 + c = 650, so 500 + c = 650 and c = **150**.

## What does the remainder mean?

When a story ends with a division, the remainder can mean different things. Read the question again before you answer.

| The question asks | What to do | Example: 52 bars, bags of 6 (8 R 4) |
|---|---|---|
| How many full groups? | Drop the remainder | 8 full bags |
| How many groups so all fit? | Round up | 9 bags to hold every bar |
| How many are left? | The remainder is the answer | 4 bars left |
| Can the leftover be cut? | Share it as a fraction | (Pizzas, yes; bars in bags, no) |

**Try it.** 127 students and 12 adults go on a trip in vans with 8 seats each. 127 + 12 = 139 people. 139 ÷ 8 = 17 R 3. Seventeen vans leave 3 people standing, so they need **18 vans**.

## Is the answer reasonable?

Round to friendly numbers before or after you solve.

- Lucia read 289 pages in March and 312 in April. About 300 + 300 = 600 pages. The exact total, 601, matches.
- Ari says 38 × 21 = 7,980. Estimate 40 × 20 = 800. His answer is about ten times too big. (It is 798.)

## Common mistakes

- **Stopping after step 1.** Finding 139 people is not the answer to "how many vans?"
- **Ignoring the story when there is a remainder.** You cannot rent 17 vans and 3/8 of a van.
- **Mixing up the order.** In 3 × 24 − 15, multiply before you subtract.

## For grown-ups at home

Real life is full of multistep problems. Planning a party is a good one: "We have 30 guests, juice boxes come in packs of 8. How many packs?" Let your child decide what the remainder means. It also helps to ask for an estimate before anyone calculates. A child who expects "about 4 packs" will notice if a calculator slip gives 40.

## Check yourself

1. A teacher buys 4 boxes of 36 pencils and gives 50 to another class. How many does she keep?
2. 75 beads are shared equally by 8 kids. How many does each kid get? How many are left?
3. Write an equation with a letter: 5 bags of 12 oranges, then 9 more oranges. How many oranges?
4. Ella says 6 × 198 = 1,788. Estimate to check.

*Answers:* (1) 4 × 36 = 144, and 144 − 50 = 94 pencils. (2) 75 ÷ 8 = 9 R 3: 9 beads each, 3 left. (3) n = 5 × 12 + 9 = 60 + 9 = 69. (4) 6 × 200 = 1,200, so 1,788 is too big. The exact product is 1,188.
