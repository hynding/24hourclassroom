# Unit 3 · Extending Operations to Fractions · Summary

**Weeks:** 6 Equal Groups of Fractions: Multiplying a Fraction by a Whole Number · 7 Adding and Subtracting Fractions With the Same Denominator · 8 Mixed Numbers and Line Plots With Fractions · 9 Tenths and Hundredths: Equivalent Fractions and Sums
**Standards:** 4.NF.B.4 · 4.NF.B.3 · 4.MD.B.4 · 4.NF.C.5 · **Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 3, in our own words

## The big ideas

1. **Every fraction is built from unit fractions.** 5/6 is 5 copies of 1/6, so 5/6 = 5 × 1/6.
2. **Equal groups of a fraction are multiplication.** 4 groups of 2/5 make 8 fifths: 4 × 2/5 = 8/5. The number of parts is multiplied; the size of the parts (the denominator) stays the same.
3. **Same denominator, same size parts.** To add or subtract fractions with like denominators, add or subtract the numerators and keep the denominator: 3/8 + 4/8 = 7/8.
4. **Fractions can be decomposed.** 5/8 = 1/8 + 4/8 = 2/8 + 3/8. Breaking fractions apart makes adding and subtracting easier.
5. **Amounts greater than 1 have two names.** 2 3/4 is a mixed number, and 11/4 is the same amount written as a fraction.
6. **Line plots show measurements.** Each X is one measurement on a number line marked in halves, fourths or eighths. We add and subtract the values to answer questions.
7. **Tenths and hundredths fit together.** 1/10 = 10/100, so 3/10 + 6/100 = 30/100 + 6/100 = 36/100.

## Strategies with an example

| Strategy | When to use it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Write as unit fractions | Seeing a fraction as a multiple | 7/4 = 7 × 1/4 |
| Multiply the numerator only | Whole number × fraction | 3 × 5/6 = 15/6 |
| Find the wholes on either side | Which two whole numbers is it between? | 15/6: 12/6 = 2 and 18/6 = 3, so between 2 and 3 |
| Count parts of the same size | Adding or subtracting like denominators | 9/12 − 4/12 = 5/12 |
| Write 1 in parts | Subtracting from 1 whole | 1 − 2/5 = 5/5 − 2/5 = 3/5 |
| Add wholes, then parts | Adding mixed numbers | 1 2/6 + 2 5/6 = 3 7/6 = 4 1/6 |
| Trade a whole for parts | Subtracting when the fraction part is too small | 4 1/8 − 1 6/8 = 3 9/8 − 1 6/8 = 2 3/8 |
| Change everything to fractions | Any mixed number problem | 2 1/4 + 1 2/4 = 9/4 + 6/4 = 15/4 |
| Count Xs, then add or subtract values | Answering line plot questions | Longest 2 1/2 in, shortest 1 3/4 in: 3/4 in apart |
| Tenths to hundredths first | Adding tenths and hundredths | 5/10 + 12/100 = 50/100 + 12/100 = 62/100 |

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Unit fraction | A fraction with 1 on top, like 1/8. |
| Multiple of a fraction | A whole number of copies of it. 6/5 is a multiple of 1/5. |
| Like denominators | The same denominator, so the parts are the same size. |
| Decompose | Break a number into parts that add up to it. |
| Mixed number | A whole number and a fraction, like 3 1/2. |
| Line plot | A number line with an X for each measurement. |
| Tenth, hundredth | One of 10 equal parts (1/10), one of 100 equal parts (1/100). |

## Watch out for

- **Multiplying the denominator.** 3 × 2/5 is 6/5, not 6/15. The parts stay fifths.
- **Adding instead of multiplying.** 4 × 3/8 is 12/8, not 7/8.
- **Adding the denominators.** 2/6 + 3/6 = 5/6, not 5/12.
- **Forgetting the whole when subtracting from 1.** Write 1 as 8/8 or 10/10 first.
- **Subtracting the small fraction from the big one.** In 5 1/4 − 2 3/4 you cannot take 1/4 from 3/4 instead. Trade a whole.
- **Writing the digits side by side.** 2 3/5 is 13/5, not 23/5 or 5/5.
- **Counting tick marks instead of spaces** on a line plot or number line.
- **Treating tenths as hundredths.** 4/10 + 3/100 is 43/100, not 7/100 and not 7/110.

## Practice set

1. Write 9/10 as a whole number times a unit fraction.
2. What is 6 × 2/8? Between which two whole numbers is it?
3. What is 7/12 + 3/12?
4. What is 1 − 5/8?
5. Write 4/6 as a sum in two different ways.
6. Write 3 3/4 as a fraction.
7. What is 2 5/8 + 3 4/8?
8. What is 6 1/3 − 2 2/3?
9. A line plot has Xs at 1/2, 1/2, 3/4, 1 and 1 1/4 inches. How many measurements are there, and how far apart are the longest and the shortest?
10. What is 7/10 + 21/100?

*Answers:* (1) 9 × 1/10. (2) 12/8, between 1 and 2. (3) 10/12. (4) 3/8. (5) For example, 1/6 + 3/6 and 2/6 + 2/6. (6) 15/4. (7) 5 9/8 = 6 1/8. (8) Trade a whole: 5 4/3 − 2 2/3 = 3 2/3. (9) 5 measurements; 1 1/4 − 1/2 = 3/4 inch. (10) 70/100 + 21/100 = 91/100.
