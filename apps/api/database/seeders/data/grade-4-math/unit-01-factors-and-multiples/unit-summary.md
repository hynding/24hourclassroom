# Unit 1 · Factors and Multiples · Summary

**Weeks:** 1 Factor Pairs and Multiples · 2 Prime and Composite Numbers, Area and Perimeter
**Standards:** 4.OA.B.4 · 4.MD.A.3 · **Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 1, in our own words

## The big ideas

1. **Rectangles show factor pairs.** Lay out square tiles as a rectangle with no gaps. The number of rows and the number in each row are a **factor pair** of the number of tiles. 20 tiles can be 1 by 20, 2 by 10 or 4 by 5.
2. **A factor multiplies to make the number.** 6 is a factor of 42 because 6 × 7 = 42. Every number has 1 and itself as factors.
3. **Multiples are skip-counting numbers.** The multiples of 8 are 8, 16, 24, 32 and so on. The list never ends.
4. **One fact, two ideas.** 4 × 9 = 36 tells us 4 and 9 are factors of 36, and 36 is a multiple of 4 and of 9.
5. **Prime or composite.** A **prime** number (greater than 1) has exactly one factor pair: 1 and itself. A **composite** number has more than one. The number 1 is neither.
6. **Area and perimeter of rectangles.** Area = length × width, in square units. Perimeter = the distance around = length + width + length + width, in plain units. The side lengths of a rectangle with whole-number sides are a factor pair of its area.

## Strategies with an example

| Strategy | When to use it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Build or draw rectangles | Finding factor pairs | 18 tiles: 1 by 18, 2 by 9, 3 by 6 |
| Try 1, 2, 3, ... in order | Making sure you have every factor | 28: 1 and 28, 2 and 14, 4 and 7; stop at 7 |
| Quick checks for 2, 5 and 10 | Spotting factors fast | 70 ends in 0, so 2, 5 and 10 are factors |
| Skip-count or jump on a number line | Listing multiples | Jumps of 7: 7, 14, 21, 28 |
| Look for a number on both lists | Finding a common multiple | 4s and 6s both reach 12 |
| Test small factors 2, 3, 5, 7 | Deciding prime or composite | 51 = 3 × 17, so composite |
| Multiply the sides | Area of a rectangle | 8 m by 5 m: 40 square meters |
| Add all four sides | Perimeter of a rectangle | 8 + 5 + 8 + 5 = 26 meters |
| Ask "what times the side makes the area?" | Missing side from the area | Area 63, side 7: 7 × 9 = 63, so 9 |
| Take away two sides, then halve | Missing side from the perimeter | Perimeter 22, length 7: 22 − 14 = 8, 8 ÷ 2 = 4 |

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Factor | A whole number that multiplies with another whole number to make a given number. |
| Factor pair | Two whole numbers whose product is the number. |
| Multiple | A product of the number and a whole number; a skip-counting number. |
| Common multiple | A number that is a multiple of two numbers at once. |
| Prime number | A whole number greater than 1 with exactly one factor pair. |
| Composite number | A whole number greater than 1 with more than one factor pair. |
| Area | The number of square units that cover a shape. |
| Perimeter | The distance all the way around a shape. |

## Watch out for

- **Adding instead of multiplying.** 3 + 9 = 12, but 3 and 9 are not a factor pair of 12. Check: 3 × 9 = 27.
- **Mixing up factor and multiple.** 4 is a factor of 12; 12 is a multiple of 4. A factor is never bigger than the number.
- **Missing a factor pair.** Guessing skips pairs. Try every number in order until the pairs repeat.
- **Thinking all odd numbers are prime.** 9, 15, 21, 27 and 51 are odd and composite.
- **Calling 1 prime.** It has only one factor, so it is neither prime nor composite.
- **Using two sides for perimeter.** A rectangle has four sides. 6 + 4 is only half the perimeter of a 6 by 4 rectangle.
- **Wrong units.** Area is in square units; perimeter is not.

## Practice set

1. List all the factor pairs of 30.
2. Is 8 a factor of 52?
3. Write the multiples of 7 from 7 to 49.
4. Name a common multiple of 4 and 10.
5. Is 47 prime or composite? Is 57?
6. Find the area and perimeter of a rectangle 9 feet by 4 feet.
7. A rectangle has an area of 48 square centimeters and a width of 6 centimeters. How long is it?
8. A rectangle has a perimeter of 34 meters and a length of 10 meters. How wide is it?

*Answers:* (1) 1 and 30, 2 and 15, 3 and 10, 5 and 6. (2) No: 8 × 6 = 48 and 8 × 7 = 56. (3) 7, 14, 21, 28, 35, 42, 49. (4) 20 (or 40, 60, ...). (5) 47 is prime; 57 is composite (3 × 19). (6) Area 36 square feet; perimeter 26 feet. (7) 8 centimeters. (8) 7 meters, because 34 − 20 = 14 and 14 ÷ 2 = 7.
