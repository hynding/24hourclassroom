# Grade 4 Math · Year Review Guide

**Built from:** the nine units of this course (IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Units 1–9, in our own words) · **Use with:** the nine unit summaries and the End-of-Year Check (week 33)

You learned a lot of math in fourth grade: factors, fractions, decimals, numbers up to a million, measurement, big multiplication and division, angles and shapes. This guide walks through each unit, tells you how the End-of-Year Check is set up, and gives you a two-week plan to get ready. Read it with a grown-up if you can.

## How the End-of-Year Check works

The check has 39 questions and takes about 80 minutes. The questions go in course order: Unit 1 first, Unit 9 last. Bigger units have more questions.

| Unit | Questions | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Factors and Multiples | 3 | Factor pairs, prime and composite numbers, area of a rectangle |
| 2 · Fraction Equivalence and Comparison | 4 | Equivalent fractions, comparing with 1/2 and with the same numerator or denominator |
| 3 · Extending Operations to Fractions | 5 | Line plots in eighths, adding mixed numbers, a whole number times a fraction, tenths plus hundredths |
| 4 · From Hundredths to Hundred-thousands | 5 | Decimals on a hundredths grid, comparing decimals, place value, rounding, subtracting big numbers |
| 5 · Multiplicative Comparison and Measurement | 4 | Feet and inches, "times as many", making change |
| 6 · Multiplying and Dividing Multi-digit Numbers | 6 | Number patterns, multiplying, dividing with remainders, a multistep problem |
| 7 · Angles and Angle Measurement | 5 | Reading a protractor, unknown angles, kinds of angles, perpendicular lines |
| 8 · Properties of Two-dimensional Shapes | 4 | Lines of symmetry, right triangles, quadrilaterals, perimeter |
| 9 · Putting It All Together | 3 | Subtracting mixed numbers, a two-step problem, multiplying and dividing |

**Kinds of questions.** Most questions are multiple choice or ask for a number. There are also true/false questions, one fill-in-a-word question, one select-all question (choose every right answer) and two short answers. Six questions have a picture: fraction strips, a line plot, a hundredths grid, a table, a protractor and shapes with dashed lines. Three pairs of questions share one picture, so read the picture carefully the first time.

**Points.** Most questions are worth 1 point. The select-all question is worth 2 points, and you get part of the points for part of the right answers. Each short answer is worth 3 points, one for each part. The check is out of 44 points. Your teacher checks the short answers, so show your work in words, numbers or a drawing.

**Typing answers.** For a number answer, type only the number, such as 856. For money in dollars, type a decimal, such as 2.75.

## What each unit asks you to do

**Unit 1 · Factors and Multiples.** Find every factor pair of a number by trying 1, 2, 3 and so on until the pairs repeat: 24 has 1 × 24, 2 × 12, 3 × 8 and 4 × 6. A prime number has exactly two factors (like 7 or 13); a composite number has more (like 51 = 3 × 17). Area of a rectangle is length × width; perimeter is the distance around.

**Unit 2 · Fraction Equivalence and Comparison.** Multiply the numerator and denominator by the same number to make an equivalent fraction: 3/4 = 9/12. Compare fractions with the same denominator by counting pieces, with the same numerator by piece size, or by asking "more or less than 1/2?"

**Unit 3 · Extending Operations to Fractions.** Add and subtract fractions with the same denominator by counting pieces: 3/8 + 7/8 = 10/8 = 1 2/8. A whole number times a fraction is equal groups: 5 × 3/4 = 15/4 = 3 3/4. Read line plots marked in fourths or eighths. Change tenths to hundredths before adding: 3/10 + 46/100 = 30/100 + 46/100 = 76/100.

**Unit 4 · From Hundredths to Hundred-thousands.** 63 hundredths is 0.63. Compare decimals by place: 0.5 = 0.50, which is more than 0.45. Each place is worth ten times the place to its right, so in 472,816 the 7 is worth 70,000. Round by looking at the digit to the right of the place you round to. Add and subtract with the standard algorithm, regrouping across zeros carefully.

**Unit 5 · Multiplicative Comparison and Measurement.** "6 times as tall as 9 feet" means 6 × 9 = 54 feet, not 9 + 6. Know your conversions: 1 foot = 12 inches, 1 yard = 3 feet, 1 pound = 16 ounces, 1 kilogram = 1,000 grams, 1 liter = 1,000 milliliters, 1 hour = 60 minutes. Find change by counting up.

**Unit 6 · Multiplying and Dividing Multi-digit Numbers.** Describe patterns: adding 4 to an odd number always gives an odd number. Multiply with area models or partial products: 52 × 38 = 1,500 + 400 + 60 + 16 = 1,976. Divide with partial quotients and write remainders: 38 ÷ 4 = 9 R 2. In a story, decide whether to round up, drop the remainder, or answer with it.

**Unit 7 · Angles and Angle Measurement.** Put the protractor's center on the vertex and one ray on 0, then read where the other ray crosses. Acute is less than 90°, right is 90°, obtuse is between 90° and 180°, and straight is 180°. Angles that make a straight line add to 180°. Perpendicular lines cross at right angles; parallel lines never meet.

**Unit 8 · Properties of Two-dimensional Shapes.** Sort shapes by their sides (parallel, perpendicular, equal) and angles (right, acute, obtuse). A rectangle always has four right angles; a rhombus always has four equal sides. A line of symmetry folds a shape into two matching halves. Use perimeter and area to find a missing side.

**Unit 9 · Putting It All Together.** Trade a whole when you subtract mixed numbers, multiply and divide fluently, and solve multistep problems with an equation and an estimate.

## A two-week practice plan

Each day takes about 25 to 30 minutes. Read that unit's summary first, then do its practice set, then look back at quiz questions you missed during the year.

| Day | Focus | Do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Unit 1 | List the factor pairs of 36 and 48. Sort 10 numbers into prime and composite. |
| 2 | Unit 2 | Make 3 equivalent fractions for 2/3. Compare 4 pairs of fractions and say which way you used. |
| 3 | Unit 3, adding and subtracting | Add and subtract 4 pairs of mixed numbers. Circle each time you traded a whole. |
| 4 | Unit 3, multiplying and line plots | Solve 3 whole number × fraction problems. Make a line plot of 8 pencil lengths to the nearest eighth inch. |
| 5 | Unit 4 | Shade 3 decimals on hundredths grids. Round 4 big numbers to two different places. |
| 6 | Unit 5 | Make a feet-and-inches table to 6 feet. Solve 2 "times as many" problems and 2 change problems. |
| 7 | Rest, or redo your hardest day | Look back at the mistakes from days 1 to 6. |
| 8 | Unit 6, multiplying | Do 4 multiplications with area models, 2 of them two-digit × two-digit. Estimate each first. |
| 9 | Unit 6, dividing | Do 4 divisions with partial quotients. Check each by multiplying and adding the remainder. |
| 10 | Unit 7 | Draw 4 angles and measure them. Find 2 unknown angles that make a straight line. |
| 11 | Unit 8 | Draw a right triangle, a rhombus and a trapezoid. Find the lines of symmetry in 4 letters of the alphabet. |
| 12 | Unit 9 | Solve 3 two-step word problems with an equation and an estimate. Decide what each remainder means. |
| 13 | Mixed review | Take turns with a grown-up making up one question from each unit. |
| 14 | End-of-Year Check | Take the check in one sitting or in two halves. Then go over every question you missed. |

## Tips for the day of the check

- **Read the question twice.** Underline what it asks you to find.
- **Look at the scale** on a line plot or protractor before you read it. Count spaces, not tick marks.
- **Draw a picture** when a problem feels hard: an area model, a tape diagram or a number line.
- **Keep the denominator** when you add or subtract fractions with the same denominator.
- **Check with the opposite operation.** Check division with multiplication and subtraction with addition.
- **Estimate.** If your answer is far from your estimate, look again.
- **Label your answers** in the short answers: 189 dollars, 5 vans, 48 plants.
- **Do not leave a question blank.** If you are stuck, make your best try and come back to it later.

## For grown-ups at home

Short, friendly practice every day helps more than one long session. Ask your child to explain how they got an answer, even when it is right, and praise the strategy, not just the answer. Everyday life is full of fourth-grade math: recipes with fractions, prices with decimals, road signs in miles, clocks and timetables, and angles in furniture and buildings. If a topic is still hard, that unit's weekly guides and flashcards are a good place to start again.
