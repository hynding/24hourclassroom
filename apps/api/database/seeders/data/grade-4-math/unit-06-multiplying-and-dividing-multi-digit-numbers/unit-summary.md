# Unit 6 · Multiplying and Dividing Multi-digit Numbers · Unit summary

**Weeks:** 17–21 · **Standards:** 4.OA.C.5, 4.NBT.B.5, 4.NBT.B.6, 4.OA.A.3
**Built from:** IM K–5 Math Grade 4, Unit 6, in our own words

## The big ideas

1. **Patterns follow rules.** A rule says where to start and what to do each time ("start at 5, add 4"). Test a rule on every pair of terms. Patterns also have features the rule does not say, such as "every term is odd".
2. **Big multiplications are made of small ones.** Split a factor by place value, multiply each part, and add the **partial products**: 4 × 326 = 1,200 + 80 + 24 = 1,304.
3. **Two-digit times two-digit has four parts.** 23 × 15 = 200 + 30 + 100 + 15 = 345. An area diagram or a table shows all four.
4. **Division finds a missing factor.** 852 ÷ 4 asks "4 times what is 852?" Take away friendly chunks (**partial quotients**) and add the groups: 200 + 10 + 3 = 213.
5. **Some divisions have a remainder.** 38 ÷ 5 = 7 R 3. The remainder is always smaller than the divisor.
6. **In a story, think about the remainder.** Drop it, round up, or give it as the answer, depending on the question.
7. **Estimate to check.** 61 × 39 is about 60 × 40 = 2,400. An answer far from the estimate needs another look.

## Strategies and examples

| Strategy | When it helps | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Count the jumps | Finding a later term in a pattern | Start at 7, add 6: the 5th term is 7 + 4 × 6 = 31 |
| Multiples in a repeating pattern | Finding a far-off shape | Group of 3 shapes: positions 3, 6, 9 ... are the last shape |
| Area diagram | Seeing every part of a product | 4 × 326: parts 1,200, 80 and 24 |
| Partial products | Multiplying without a drawing | 6 × 1,234 = 6,000 + 1,200 + 180 + 24 = 7,404 |
| Partial-product table | Two-digit by two-digit | 34 × 26: 600 + 80 + 180 + 24 = 884 |
| Partial quotients | Dividing in friendly chunks | 576 ÷ 3: 100 + 90 + 2 = 192 |
| Multiply back | Checking a division | 6 × 26 + 1 = 157, so 157 ÷ 6 = 26 R 1 |
| Tape diagram and a letter | Planning a multistep problem | 4 × 125 + c = 650, so c = 150 |

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Rule | How to get from one term of a pattern to the next. |
| Term | Each number or shape in a pattern. |
| Feature | Something true about a pattern that the rule does not say. |
| Partial product | The product of one part of a factor, such as 4 × 20 in 4 × 326. |
| Area diagram | A rectangle whose sides are factors and whose area is the product. |
| Quotient | The answer to a division. |
| Partial quotient | The groups in one chunk, such as 200 groups of 4 in 800. |
| Remainder | What is left after making equal groups. |
| Estimate | A close answer found with rounded numbers. |

## Watch out for

- **Counting jumps wrong.** The 5th term is 4 jumps after the 1st term, not 5.
- **Tens with tens, ones with ones.** 23 × 15 is not 200 + 15. All four parts count.
- **Losing place value.** 4 × 300 is 1,200, not 12 or 120.
- **Leaving out a partial quotient.** Add every chunk's groups at the end.
- **A remainder bigger than the divisor.** 59 ÷ 4 is 14 R 3, not 13 R 7.
- **Answering the wrong question.** "How many vans?" is not the same as "how many people?"

## Practice set

1. Start at 3 and multiply by 2. Write the first five terms.
2. Repeating pattern: star, moon, moon. What is the 15th shape?
3. 9 × 2,016 = ?
4. 52 × 17 = ?
5. 1,284 ÷ 6 = ?
6. 95 ÷ 7 = ? Write the remainder.
7. 100 students go to a museum. Each guide leads 8 students. How many guides are needed?
8. Estimate 49 × 31, then find the exact product.

*Answers:* (1) 3, 6, 12, 24, 48. (2) Moon: 15 is a multiple of 3, so it is the last shape of a group. (3) 18,000 + 0 + 90 + 54 = 18,144. (4) 500 + 20 + 350 + 14 = 884. (5) 1,200 ÷ 6 = 200, 84 ÷ 6 = 14, so 214. (6) 13 R 4, because 7 × 13 = 91. (7) 100 ÷ 8 = 12 R 4, so 13 guides, because the last 4 students need a guide too. (8) About 50 × 30 = 1,500; exactly 1,519.

## For grown-ups at home

This unit is the heart of Grade 4 arithmetic. Your child now multiplies and divides numbers in the thousands by splitting them into parts they understand. Every line of the written work shows a place value. Keep asking "About how much should it be?" and "How can you check?"
