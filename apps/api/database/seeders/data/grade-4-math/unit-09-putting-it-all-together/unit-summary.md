## The big ideas

1. **Same denominator, same size pieces.** To add or subtract fractions like 3/8 and 4/8, count the pieces and keep the denominator: 7/8.
2. **A whole is a full set of pieces.** 1 = 4/4 = 6/6 = 8/8. Trade a whole for pieces when you subtract mixed numbers: 4 1/8 = 3 9/8.
3. **Compare with a plan.** Same denominator: more pieces wins. Same numerator: bigger pieces win. Otherwise use 1/2, the missing piece, or a common denominator.
4. **Place value powers multiplication.** Split numbers by place and multiply each part. Two-digit times two-digit makes four partial products.
5. **Division takes out groups.** Take out big, easy chunks of the divisor, add the chunks, and keep any remainder smaller than the divisor.
6. **Read the story twice.** Multistep problems need a plan, an equation with a letter, units that match, and a remainder that fits the question.

## Strategies at a glance

| Strategy | When to use it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Count like pieces | Adding fractions with the same denominator | 5/12 + 4/12 = 9/12 |
| Add wholes, then fractions | Adding mixed numbers | 1 5/6 + 2 4/6 = 3 9/6 = 4 3/6 |
| Trade a whole | Subtracting when the top fraction is too small | 3 1/4 − 1 3/4 = 2 5/4 − 1 3/4 = 1 2/4 |
| Compare with 1/2 | Fractions on different sides of a half | 3/8 is less than 1/2; 4/6 is more |
| Common denominator | Fifths and tenths, thirds and sixths | 3/5 = 6/10, which is less than 7/10 |
| Expanded form | One digit times a big number | 5 × 1,328 = 5,000 + 1,500 + 100 + 40 = 6,640 |
| Area model | Two-digit times two-digit | 38 × 27 = 600 + 160 + 210 + 56 = 1,026 |
| Partial quotients | Dividing by one digit | 945 ÷ 7 = 100 + 30 + 5 = 135 |
| Use the remainder | Division in a story | 75 students, vans of 8: 9 R 3, so 10 vans |
| Change units first | Mixed units | 3 ft 4 in = 36 + 4 = 40 inches |
| Count up | Change and elapsed time | 2:45 to 4:10 = 15 min + 1 hr + 10 min |

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Mixed number | A whole number and a fraction together, like 2 3/4. |
| Decompose | Break a number into parts that add up to it. |
| Common denominator | The same denominator for two fractions. |
| Partial product | One part of a product, added with the others at the end. |
| Partial quotient | One chunk of a quotient, added with the others at the end. |
| Remainder | What is left over when no more full groups fit. |
| Estimate | A close answer from rounded numbers, used to check. |
| Elapsed time | How much time passes between a start and an end. |

## Watch out for

- **Adding the denominators.** 1/4 + 3/4 is 4/4, not 4/8. The pieces stay fourths.
- **Subtracting the wrong way.** In 3 1/4 − 1 3/4 you cannot take 1/4 from 3/4 instead. Trade a whole.
- **Bigger denominator, bigger fraction?** No: 1/8 is smaller than 1/6, because the whole is cut into more pieces.
- **Missing partial products.** 38 × 27 is not 30 × 20 + 8 × 7. Draw all four boxes.
- **Losing a zero.** 1,624 ÷ 8 is 203, not 23. 60 × 40 is 2,400, not 240.
- **A remainder too big.** 75 ÷ 4 = 17 R 7 is not finished: 7 is more than 4.
- **Ignoring the question.** 9 R 3 vans means 10 vans, but 9 R 3 boxes means 9 full boxes.
- **Time is not base ten.** 4:10 − 2:45 is not 1 hour 65 minutes. An hour has 60 minutes.

## Practice set

1. 7/10 − 3/10
2. 2 5/8 + 1 6/8
3. 5 1/3 − 2 2/3
4. Which is greater, 5/12 or 2/3? Explain.
5. 6 × 2,043
6. 54 × 32
7. 1,935 ÷ 5
8. 100 people go on a boat trip. Each boat holds 9 people. How many boats are needed?
9. Jade buys 3 juice boxes at $0.85 each and pays with $5.00. How much change does she get?

*Answers:* (1) 4/10. (2) 3 11/8 = 4 3/8. (3) Trade a whole: 4 4/3 − 2 2/3 = 2 2/3. (4) 2/3, because 2/3 = 8/12, which is more than 5/12 (or: 5/12 is less than 1/2 and 2/3 is more). (5) 12,000 + 240 + 18 = 12,258. (6) 1,500 + 100 + 120 + 8 = 1,728. (7) 5 × 300 = 1,500, leaving 435; 5 × 80 = 400, leaving 35; 5 × 7 = 35; so 387. (8) 100 ÷ 9 = 11 R 1, so 12 boats. (9) 3 × $0.85 = $2.55, and $5.00 − $2.55 = $2.45.
