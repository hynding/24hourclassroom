# Unit 7 · Angles and Angle Measurement · Unit summary

**Weeks:** 22 to 25

## The big ideas

1. **Geometry has its own building blocks.** A point is an exact spot. A line goes on forever both ways, a segment has two endpoints, and a ray has one endpoint and goes on forever one way. A ray is named endpoint first; an angle is named with its vertex in the middle.
2. **Lines can meet or never meet.** Intersecting lines cross. Perpendicular lines cross at right angles. Parallel lines never cross and stay the same distance apart.
3. **An angle is a turn.** Its size is how far one ray turns from the other, not how long the rays are. A full turn is 360°, and a one-degree angle is 1/360 of a turn.
4. **A protractor measures turns.** Put the center mark on the vertex, line one ray up with a 0, and read the other ray on the row that starts at that 0.
5. **Angle measures add.** When an angle is split into parts that do not overlap, the whole is the sum of the parts. Knowing the whole (90°, 180° or 360°) lets you subtract to find a missing part.

## Strategies at a glance

| Strategy | When to use it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Count the endpoints | Naming a figure | One endpoint, arrow on one end: a ray |
| Vertex in the middle | Naming an angle | Rays from B through A and C: angle ABC |
| Square-corner test | Right, acute or obtuse? | Narrower than a paper corner: acute |
| Fraction of 360 | Angles as turns | 1/6 of a turn: 360 ÷ 6 = 60° |
| 30° per clock step | Clock angles | 12 to 5: 5 × 30 = 150° |
| Same-row reading | Measuring with a protractor | First ray on the inner 0: read the inner row |
| Benchmark check | Choosing between two readings | Obtuse angle reads 50 or 130: it is 130° |
| Subtract on one row | Neither ray on 0 | Rays at 35 and 120: 120 − 35 = 85° |
| Whole minus part | Unknown angle | Straight angle, part 128°: 180 − 128 = 52° |
| Equation with a letter | Unknown angle | 27 + x = 90, so x = 63 |

## Words to know

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Ray | Part of a line with one endpoint, going on forever in one direction. |
| Vertex | The shared endpoint of the two rays of an angle. |
| Perpendicular | Crossing to make right angles. |
| Parallel | Never crossing, always the same distance apart. |
| Degree (°) | The unit for angles; a full turn is 360°. |
| Acute, right, obtuse | Less than 90°, exactly 90°, between 90° and 180°. |
| Straight angle | 180°, two rays that make a straight line. |
| Protractor | A tool for measuring and drawing angles. |

## Worked example: one picture, three questions

A protractor sits on an angle. One ray points right through the 0 on the inner row. The other ray crosses 115 on the inner row and 65 on the outer row.

- **Measure.** The first ray is on the inner 0, so read the inner row: 115°.
- **Kind.** 115° is more than 90° and less than 180°, so the angle is obtuse. That also confirms 115 and not 65.
- **Missing part.** How much more to make a straight angle? 180 − 115 = 65°. Notice that 65 is the other row's reading: the two readings always add to 180.

## Watch out for

- Naming a ray with the wrong letter first, or an angle without its vertex in the middle.
- Calling any two crossing lines perpendicular. They need right angles.
- Thinking longer rays make a bigger angle.
- Counting clock steps as 1° or 10° each instead of 30°.
- Reading the protractor row that starts at 0 on the wrong side.
- Putting the protractor's edge, not its center mark, on the vertex.
- Subtracting from 180 when the whole is a right angle, or from 90 when it is a straight line.
- Adding angles that overlap.

## Quick practice (answers at the end)

1. How many endpoints does a ray have?
2. Two lines never meet. Are they parallel or perpendicular?
3. What is 3/4 of a full turn in degrees?
4. The hands of a clock show 2:00. What is the smaller angle between them?
5. On the same protractor row, the rays of an angle cross 40 and 145. What is the angle?
6. Angles of 90°, 110° and x fill the space around a point. Find x.

*Answers:* (1) One. (2) Parallel. (3) 3 × 90 = 270°. (4) 2 × 30 = 60°. (5) 145 − 40 = 105°. (6) 90 + 110 = 200, and 360 − 200 = 160°.
